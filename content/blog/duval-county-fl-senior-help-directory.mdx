*Free, plain-English help for seniors and families in Duval County, FL. Education, not advice. Verify before you rely on it.*

## Start here (works anywhere)
- 211 (dial 2-1-1), Eldercare Locator 1-800-677-1116, BenefitsCheckUp.org, Medicare 1-800-633-4227, Social Security 1-800-772-1213, VA 1-800-827-1000, Alzheimer's 24/7 Helpline 1-800-272-3900, National Elder Fraud Hotline 1-833-372-8311, Crisis Lifeline 988

**Three local front doors:** **ElderSource**, the Area Agency on Aging and Aging and Disability Resource Center for Northeast Florida, HelpLine **1-888-242-4464** or **904-391-6699**, Monday through Friday 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., at 10688 Old St. Augustine Road, Jacksonville, FL 32257 | the **City of Jacksonville Senior Services Division at 904-255-5400**, adultsvcs@coj.net, at City Hall at St. James, 117 West Duval Street, Jacksonville, FL 32202 | **United Way 211 of Northeast Florida**, answered 24 hours a day (dial **2-1-1**, call **904-632-0600**, or text **Hello to 211904**)

One thing to understand before you make any of those calls, because it changes the answer to almost every question on this page. **In 1968 the City of Jacksonville and Duval County consolidated into one government.** For most purposes the city and the county are the same thing, which is why a county senior program here is run by a city department out of City Hall. But four towns kept their own governments and their own city tax levy: **Atlantic Beach, Jacksonville Beach, Neptune Beach, and Baldwin.** So when someone tells you "the county does not have that program," the honest follow-up question is which government they mean, and whether your address is inside one of those four towns. Give every agency the exact street address, not just "Jacksonville."

That second front door deserves a paragraph. The **Senior Services Division** is not a referral line. It runs **16 senior centers** across Duval County, it runs the county's Older Americans Act meal program, and it is the designated local provider of the elder energy program. It also runs the Foster Grandparent Program, the Senior Companion Program, an Independent Living Program for homebound seniors and their caregivers, a homemaking program, transportation, and the Senior Games. There is **no fee to attend a Jacksonville senior center**, though nominal fees may be charged for some classes.

Two more numbers to write down. The **Florida Abuse Hotline** at **1-800-962-2873** takes reports of known or suspected abuse, neglect, self-neglect, or exploitation of a vulnerable adult, 24 hours a day, with TDD at 1-800-453-5145. And the **Long-Term Care Ombudsman** at **1-888-831-0404** is who you call about a nursing home or an assisted living facility, not the facility's own management.

**Before hurricane season, do this one thing.** The Jacksonville Emergency Preparedness Division keeps a **Special Medical Needs Registry** for people who are medically dependent on electricity, who need oxygen, or who have physical, medical, cognitive, or sensory disabilities requiring professional assistance. Registering gets you on the list for a special medical needs shelter, and **transportation is coordinated for people who need it**, with a call to arrange a pickup time. Register online through the state's Special Needs Registry portal or by calling **904-255-3172**. The catch that trips families up every year: **you must re-register annually**, and renewal notices go out at the start of each calendar year. A caregiver or family member has to go to the shelter with the registrant.

## Property tax relief (apply with the Duval County Property Appraiser, 904-255-5900, 231 E. Forsyth Street, Suite 260)
Florida seniors apply for property tax relief through the county Property Appraiser, not the Tax Collector. In Duval County that is **Joyce Morgan, CFA**, at **231 E. Forsyth Street, Suite 260, Jacksonville, FL 32202**, phone **904-255-5900**, email pacustserv@coj.net, fax 904-255-7963. The Customer Service and Exemptions Division takes mail at Suite 270 in the same building.

**Standard Homestead Exemption (statewide, any qualifying primary residence):** up to **$50,000** off the assessed value of a permanent primary residence you own and occupy as of January 1. It comes in two pieces, the first $25,000 and an additional amount that applies to assessed value above $50,000. Applications are due by **March 1**, though Duval accepts late applications for up to **25 days following the mailing of the Notices of Proposed Property Taxes in mid-August**. After the first year, the annual increase in assessed value is capped, and that cap is **Save Our Homes**, which over a decade is often worth more than any exemption listed below.

Now the two senior exemptions, and Duval County offers **both** of them. Both require that you already have or qualify for the regular homestead exemption, that at least one owner is **65 or older as of January 1**, and that **total household income for the prior year does not exceed $38,686** for the 2026 exemptions. That income figure is set annually by the Florida Department of Revenue and was $37,694 for 2025.

**Limited-Income Additional Homestead Exemption:** up to **$50,000** more off the assessed value. The Property Appraiser is precise about what it touches: it applies to **county and municipal millages only**. It does not reduce school district taxes, which are usually the largest single line on a Florida tax bill. That is not a Duval quirk, it is how Florida wrote the law, but it is the reason the savings are smaller than the headline number suggests.

**Long-Term Residency Senior Exemption:** this is the one worth a phone call, and it has been available in Duval since **2014**. Instead of a flat dollar amount, it **exempts the ad valorem City of Jacksonville portion of the property taxes** entirely. To qualify you must meet the age and income tests above, **and**:
- Have **maintained permanent residency on the property for no less than 25 years, uninterrupted**.
- Have a property **just value of less than $250,000**.

Read that twice if a parent has been in the same Jacksonville house since 2001 or earlier and it is still modestly valued. This exemption is not a discount, it removes the city's slice. The Property Appraiser also notes it applies to **any municipality tax levy that has also adopted the ordinance**, which matters if the address is in Atlantic Beach, Jacksonville Beach, Neptune Beach, or Baldwin.

**Here is what this page will not guess at.** The Property Appraiser publishes the exemptions and the amounts, but it does not publish a list of which of Duval's four other municipalities adopted these ordinances and at what amount. Several Florida counties publish exactly that list, and Duval does not. So if the home is in one of the beach towns or in Baldwin, **do not assume, and do not assume against yourself either**. Call **904-255-5900**, give them the exact street address, and ask which taxing authorities on that specific bill have adopted the senior exemptions. If the home is inside the consolidated city, which is most of Duval County, the answer is straightforward.

**The deadline and the paperwork.** Apply by **March 1** using the **Homestead and Related Tax Exemptions Application (Duval 501)** together with the **Statement of Income (DR-501SC)**. Expect to provide proof of age and proof of all household income, meaning the IRS Form 1040 or, if no return is required, total income less Social Security benefits. Income here is broad: Social Security, pension, VA retirement annuities, interest, and wages all count. Once granted, the exemptions **renew automatically**, but you will get an annual verification card, and that card is not junk mail. Do not throw it out.

**One housekeeping note.** The Property Appraiser has announced that its website is transitioning beginning **March 13, 2026**. If a link you saved goes dead, that is why. The phone number is the stable thing.

**A separate office handles the bill.** The **Duval County Tax Collector** at **231 E. Forsyth Street, Suite 130**, phone **904-255-5700, option 4**, mails property tax bills in November and cannot grant exemptions. The move that helps most on a fixed income is the **Installment Payment Plan**. If your estimated taxes total more than **$100**, you can split the bill into four payments. You enroll on or before **April 30** for that year. The first installment is due June 30 with a **6 percent discount**, the second September 30 at **4.5 percent**, the third December 31 at **3 percent**, and the fourth March 31 with no discount. Two traps: you must pay the first installment to be in the plan at all, and if it is not paid by **July 31** you are removed for the year.

## Energy and utility help
Duval County splits energy help two ways by age, and then splits again by which utility serves the address. Most families call the wrong door first.

- **EHEAP is the 60 and older program, and in Duval County the city itself runs it.** The Florida Department of Elder Affairs lists the **Consolidated City of Jacksonville Senior Services Division**, 117 West Duval Street, City Hall, Suite 280, Jacksonville, FL 32202, as the EHEAP provider for Duval County, at **904-255-5400**. JEA also publishes **904-630-2489** for EHEAP, which is the city's main 630-CITY line. Either gets you to the same government. EHEAP requires at least one person **60 or older** in the household, household income at or below **60 percent of State Median Income**, and a **verifiable home energy crisis**, meaning a delinquent utility bill, a shut-off notice, or a lack of fuel or wood. Eligible households may receive **more than one crisis benefit per program year**, up to a maximum set by FloridaCommerce. Ask the office for the current benefit cap rather than trusting a figure you read elsewhere, because the published amounts vary and the state does not post a per-county number.
- **LIHEAP is the general program**, and in Duval County it is run by the **Northeast Florida Community Action Agency (NFCAA)**, main office **2055 Reyko Road, St. Johns Building, Suite 200, Jacksonville, FL 32207**, phone **904-398-7472**. The detail that matters most: **the appointment line, 904-362-8052, is answered 24 hours a day, seven days a week.** If a shut-off notice lands on a Saturday, you are not waiting until Monday to get on the calendar. You can also apply online at **floridaliheap.com**. NFCAA covers Baker, Clay, Duval, Flagler, Putnam, and St. Johns counties for LIHEAP.
- **NFCAA also runs Weatherization** (WAP) in **Baker, Duval, Flagler, Nassau, and Putnam** counties, which tests air infiltration, improves heating and cooling, and repairs or replaces insulation. That is the permanent fix rather than the one-time bill payment, and it has nothing to do with age or a crisis. Same office, **904-398-7472**.
- **If the address is in a beach town, the utility is probably not JEA.** **Beaches Energy Services**, run by the City of Jacksonville Beach out of 11 N. 3rd Street, serves more than 36,000 electric and natural gas customers in **Jacksonville Beach, Neptune Beach, Ponte Vedra Beach, and Palm Valley**. Beaches Energy runs its own **CARE Program**, funded by customer donations that the utility matches dollar for dollar, and **BEAM (Beaches Emergency Assistance Ministry) delivers one utility voucher to a qualified recipient**. The eligibility categories are named plainly, and the first one is **a customer 60 years of age or older whose household meets low or moderate income guidelines**, followed by medically certified disability, low income, and emergency relief. In 2025 the CARE program helped 256 customers keep the power on. Call Beaches Energy customer care at **904-247-6241**.
- **Beaches Energy also runs a Medical Support Notification Program** for customers who depend on life-supporting electrical medical equipment such as oxygen machines, which flags them for notification of extended scheduled outages. Call **904-247-6281**. Note the utility's own caution: registering does not make anyone exempt from an outage, and you are still responsible for backup power and a plan.
- **JEA** customers can request a payment extension or arrangement through JEA rather than defaulting to a shut-off. JEA's own assistance page also points customers to 211, NFCAA, EHEAP, and the city's emergency financial assistance line.
- For a past-due bill right now, dial **211** or text **Hello to 211904**. They keep the current list of church ministries and local funds that still have utility money this month.

## Medicare, Medicaid, prescriptions
- Florida's SHIP (free Medicare counseling) is **SHINE, Serving Health Insurance Needs of Elders**, and in Northeast Florida it is delivered by **ElderSource**. Counseling is **free**, and the promise ElderSource makes in writing is worth repeating: counselors and volunteers **are not affiliated with any insurance company and will not attempt to sell you insurance**, and all counseling records are confidential. They handle turning 65, benefit and billing questions, plan comparisons, and appeals. Call **1-888-242-4464**. Start here before any Medicare decision and especially before switching plans during open enrollment.
- **SMP, the Senior Medicare Patrol**, is the fraud side of the same house. It helps beneficiaries, families, and caregivers protect, detect, and report health care fraud, errors, and abuse. Report suspected Medicare fraud at **1-800-963-5337**, which is 1-800-96-ELDER.
- **Medicaid** in Florida, including long-term care coverage and the programs that help pay Medicare costs, is applied for through the Department of Children and Families at **myaccess.myflfamilies.com** or **850-300-4323**. The same single application covers SNAP food assistance and Temporary Cash Assistance.
- **Medicare Savings Programs** and **Extra Help**, the Low-Income Subsidy that lowers Part D drug costs, are screened through SHINE (1-888-242-4464), Social Security (1-800-772-1213), and MyACCESS. Florida does not run a general statewide senior drug program, so ask SHINE about Extra Help and use BenefitsCheckUp.org to find manufacturer and nonprofit patient-assistance programs for specific medications.
- **ElderSource also runs the Veteran Directed Care program** in partnership with the VA, which helps a veteran stay home and receive services from **a caregiver of their own choosing**. It is part of the Veterans Health Administration standard medical benefits package. Ask for it by name at 1-888-242-4464.

## Home repair and safety
Duval County's repair money runs in windows, and right now some doors are open and some are shut. All of it goes through the **City of Jacksonville Housing and Community Development Division**, and the person to ask for on the repair programs is **Jackie Jones, Housing Services Specialist, 904-255-8229**, JJones2@coj.net. The general housing line is **904-255-8200**.

- **Emergency Repair Program (ERP), open to applications, up to $12,000 as a grant.** It exists to resolve immediate threats to life and safety, and the eligible work list is specific: replacement or repair of **heating and air conditioning**, falling ceiling repair, **water heaters**, electrical components posing an imminent fire hazard, structural components showing signs of imminent collapse, and **ADA accessibility**. In a Jacksonville August, a dead air conditioner belongs on that list, and this is the program for it.
- **Limited Repair Program (LRP), currently closed to new applicants, up to $55,000.** This is the bigger general repair program, for bringing a home up to code where conditions threaten health or safety or where Municipal Code Compliance has cited the property. Closed is not gone. Call 904-255-8229 and ask when the next application period opens and how you will be notified.
- **Utility Tap-In Program (UTIP), currently closed to new applicants, up to $55,000.** It covers city sewer and water connection, septic repair or replacement, well repair, galvanized pipe replacement, and **ADA bathroom accessibility**. That last item is the one families miss, because a walk-in shower reads as a remodel rather than a repair program.
- **The shared rules on all three:** the home must be **owner occupied** and you must live in it, the **mortgage and property taxes must be current**, **mobile homes are not eligible**, and total household income must not exceed **80 percent of the area median income for Duval County** as established by HUD. Ask for the current income figure when you call, because HUD resets it every year.
- **NFCAA's Weatherization program** at **904-398-7472** is the other real door, and it is not a repair program in name but it replaces insulation and fixes heating and cooling.
- **211** can find a faith-based or volunteer group that does small safety work such as grab bars and ramps when no government program is open.

## Rent, housing, emergency cash
- **The City of Jacksonville Emergency Financial Assistance Program** is the main local door for rent, mortgage, and utility crises, and the way you reach it is specific. **Call the appointment line at 904-255-2469. It opens at 9:30 a.m., Monday through Friday**, and appointments are for the next business day on a first-come basis, with priority to people holding a court-ordered eviction notice. The office is at **Midtown Centre, 3400 Building, 1809 Art Museum Drive, Suite 100, Jacksonville, FL 32207**.
- **Know the rules before you dial, because they are strict.** You must be a **Duval County resident** with a valid Florida ID showing a Duval address, household income must not exceed **200 percent of the federal poverty level**, you need a documentable crisis with a delinquent, eviction, foreclosure, or shut-off notice, and **you must not have received assistance from this program within the past 24 months**. If an application is denied there is a **90-day wait** before reapplying. That 24-month rule is the one that surprises families who used the program during a past emergency.
- **The senior-specific piece inside that program:** wintertime **heating and fuel oil assistance for eligible people ages 64 and older** and for disabled adults. The city also provides indigent burial and cremation assistance, which is a hard thing to have to look up and a real thing this office does.
- **211 Northeast Florida** is answered around the clock and keeps the live list of which church and community funds still have money. Dial **2-1-1**, call **904-632-0600**, or text **Hello to 211904**.
- For Medicaid, SNAP, and Temporary Cash Assistance, apply through MyACCESS at **myaccess.myflfamilies.com** or **850-300-4323**.

## Transportation
**Start with the fact that surprises people: on JTA, riders 65 and older ride free.** Not discounted, free. You show a **Florida driver license, a Florida-issued ID, or a JTA Senior STAR Card**, and you scan or show it on boarding. **Out-of-state IDs are not accepted**, which matters for a parent who just moved down and has not switched their license yet. That is worth doing in the first week.

For context on the rest of the fare table: the regular cash fare including the First Coast Flyer is **$1.75**, the **reduced fare is 75 cents** for people with disabilities and Medicare cardholders holding an ID STAR Card, ReadiRide is **$2.00**, a 31-day pass is **$65** and a reduced 31-day pass is **$32**. JTA customer service is **904-630-3100**, Monday through Friday 5:30 a.m. to 8:30 p.m. and Saturday 8 a.m. to 5 p.m.

**If a disability makes the regular bus impossible, that is Connexion**, JTA's door-to-door paratransit, and you must apply and be approved before riding.
- **Applying** takes three steps: submit the application online or on paper, schedule an in-person assessment by calling the **Eligibility and Training Center at 904-265-6001**, and receive a decision **within 21 days**. Start this before you need it.
- **Two eligibility tracks.** **ADA** certification is for people who cannot independently use fixed-route transit under the Americans with Disabilities Act standard. **TD**, Transportation Disadvantaged, is a separate Florida certification. The fares differ: **$3.00 each way for ADA riders and $3.50 each way for TD riders**. Exact fare is required and drivers cannot make change.
- **Reservations** for Connexion are at **904-265-6999**, daily 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., booked at least one day ahead and up to seven days out. **Connexion Plus**, the on-demand version, books at **904-404-0848** with **at least two hours notice**, 5:30 a.m. to 11:30 p.m. weekdays and 5:30 a.m. to 10 p.m. weekends. Connexion customer service is **904-265-8928**. Both operate within **Duval County**.
- Medicaid long-term care plans cover **transportation** as a plan benefit, so if a parent is enrolled in Statewide Medicaid Managed Care Long-Term Care, ask the plan before paying out of pocket.

## Legal help
- **Jacksonville Area Legal Aid is the first call, and the reason is a rule most families never hear.** JALA generally considers representation for clients within **200 percent of the poverty line**, but several of its program units are **not income sensitive at all**, and one of them is the **Title III unit that serves elderly citizens age 60 and older**. So a retired homeowner with savings and a paid-off house is not automatically disqualified. JALA handles elder law, consumer, housing and fair housing, family, public benefits, health, veterans, and education matters. The Jacksonville office serving Duval County residents is at **126 W. Adams St., Jacksonville, FL 32202**, phone **904-356-8371**. You can also start online with JALA's intake form and expect a call or email back in three to five business days.
- **A warning JALA posts on its own site, and it belongs on a senior page.** JALA's email addresses always end in **jaxlegalaid.org**. An operation calling itself **Legal-Aid-Now is not a civil legal aid firm and is not affiliated with JALA**. If someone contacts a parent claiming to be legal aid and the email does not end in jaxlegalaid.org, call the office directly at 904-356-8371 to check.
- **Florida's Senior Legal Helpline** gives free legal advice and brief services by phone to Florida residents **60 and older** on civil matters including housing, family, government benefits, and contract and consumer problems. Call **1-888-895-7873**, Monday through Friday 9 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. It is operated by Bay Area Legal Services and serves seniors statewide, so it is a second shot if the local office is at capacity. Interpreters are free.
- To report suspected abuse, neglect, self-neglect, or exploitation of a vulnerable adult, call the **Florida Abuse Hotline at 1-800-962-2873**, 24 hours a day. For a problem inside a nursing home or assisted living facility, call the **Long-Term Care Ombudsman at 1-888-831-0404**.

## Caregiver support
ElderSource carries most of the caregiver load in Duval County, and its programs have names worth using when you call **1-888-242-4464**.

- **TCARE, the Tailored Caregiver Assessment and Referral program**, is the burnout program. A caregiver is interviewed to identify the actual stressors, and then a plan is built to address the root causes rather than handing over a brochure. You qualify if you are caring for someone **age 60 or older** who lives in Baker, Clay, Duval, Flagler, St. Johns, Nassau, or Volusia County.
- **Powerful Tools for Caregivers** is a **free** evidence-based program, six classes held once a week, led by trained class leaders. **The Virtual Caregiver Support Group** meets over Zoom in 60-minute sessions with a skilled moderator, built specifically for caregivers who cannot physically leave the person they care for.
- **The Sharing and Caring Closet is the quietest useful thing on this page.** ElderSource started it in 2024 because so many callers could not afford **consumable medical supplies**: disposable incontinence undergarments, waterproof pads, personal hygiene wipes. Insurance rarely covers any of it and the monthly cost is real. Call the HelpLine at **1-888-242-4464** or walk in to 10688 Old St. Augustine Road with a genuine request. Availability depends on inventory, and ElderSource will assist an individual **once every three months**.
- **GUIDE Dementia Care** is new and it is the single best-value program here if it fits. Medicare's **Guiding an Improved Dementia Experience** model, delivered locally through ElderSource, provides a care navigator, a **24/7 clinical support helpline for caregivers**, in-home or facility-based **respite**, care coordination, home safety assessments, medication management, and caregiver training, **at no cost to Traditional Medicare Part A and B beneficiaries**. Eligibility is a confirmed dementia diagnosis, living at home or in the community. **Not eligible:** people with mild cognitive impairment only, **Medicare Advantage enrollees**, people living in a skilled nursing facility, and people already in hospice or a PACE organization. That Medicare Advantage exclusion catches a lot of Florida families, so check the card before you get your hopes up.
- **Florida's Alzheimer's Disease Initiative** funds respite through the Area Agency on Aging for people **18 and older with a diagnosis of probable Alzheimer's disease or a related dementia**: in-home, facility-based, emergency, and extended respite of **up to 30 days**. Caregivers can also access case management, specialized medical equipment and supplies, counseling, support groups, and training. Start at **1-888-242-4464**.
- **For diagnosis, Duval County has a state-designated memory clinic.** Florida's legislature funds a network of **Memory Disorder Clinics** that provide comprehensive diagnostic and referral services, and the one serving this region is the **Mayo Clinic Memory Disorder Clinic, 4500 San Pablo Rd., Jacksonville, FL 32224, 904-953-7103**. If you are chasing a diagnosis and getting nowhere, this is the door.
- The **Alzheimer's Association 24/7 Helpline** (**1-800-272-3900**) is staffed at any hour, and 211 can point you to local support groups.

## Staying at home instead of a nursing home
- Florida Medicaid pays for care at home through the **Statewide Medicaid Managed Care Long-Term Care** program, for people **65 and older who are Medicaid eligible**, or **18 and older and Medicaid eligible by reason of a disability**, who are also determined by the state's **CARES** unit at the Department of Elder Affairs to meet a **nursing home level of care**. Plans must cover, at minimum, adult companion care, adult day care, assistive care, assisted living facility services, attendant care, behavior management, caregiver training, case management, home accessibility adaptation, home delivered meals, homemaker, hospice, skilled nursing, medical equipment and supplies, medication administration and management, nursing facility care, nutritional assessment, occupational, physical, respiratory and speech therapy, personal care, personal emergency response systems, respite care, and transportation. **ElderSource is where you start**, and its intake specialists can walk you through the Medicaid eligibility process. Call **1-888-242-4464**. Ask about the waitlist on that first call, and start early.
- **PACE (the Program of All-Inclusive Care for the Elderly)** wraps all medical care, therapy, an adult day center, home care, and prescriptions into one program for people **55 and older** who meet a nursing home level of care but can live at home with help. Duval County has **one PACE organization: The PACE Place**, run by **PACE Partners of Northeast Florida**.
  - Day center: **5450 Ramona Boulevard, Jacksonville, FL 32205**, phone **904-428-0400**, TDD/TTY 800-955-8771, open **Monday through Friday 8 a.m. to 5 p.m.**
  - **Eligibility:** at least **55 years old**, able to live safely in the community, **approved by the state for nursing home care**, and living in one of their service area ZIP codes.
  - **Cost:** no cost to join if you qualify for both Medicare and Medicaid, or for Medicaid alone, with no deductible or copayment for any drug, service, or care approved by your care team. With Medicare only, or with neither, you can still join for a monthly fee, and private pay participants are accepted. All care must go through the PACE network, so out-of-network care other than emergencies may be your own bill.
- **Check the ZIP code before anything else, because the service area is drawn by ZIP and it does not cover all of Duval County.** The published service area is **32065, 32073, 32202, 32204, 32205, 32206, 32207, 32208, 32209, 32210, 32211, 32212, 32216, 32217, 32218, 32219, 32220, 32221, 32222, 32223, 32224, 32225, 32244, 32246, 32254, 32257, 32258, and 32277**. Two of those, 32065 and 32073, are over the line in Clay County, and several Duval ZIP codes do not appear at all. **Jacksonville Beach, 32250, is not on that list.** If your parent's ZIP is missing, PACE is not an option at that address today, and the conversation should move to Medicaid long-term care through ElderSource instead.
- **Florida's Agency for Health Care Administration** monthly PACE report for **February 2026** lists Duval County with **PACE Partners of NE Florida, status Operational, 94 enrollees** that month and 111 unique individuals served in the fiscal year to date, against **250 slots approved and 250 funded**, with initial AHCA approval on **June 27, 2018**. No other PACE organization appears for Duval, operational or pending. Clay County shows the same organization with zero enrollees.
- **One piece of history you will find if you search, so here it is straight.** On **May 17, 2024**, CMS suspended new Medicare enrollment at PACE Partners of Northeast Florida after deficiencies found in 2021, 2022, and 2023 trial-period audits and a 2024 finding that it lacked contracts with required medical specialists. The organization attested that the violations were corrected as of **March 31, 2025**, CMS required a validation audit, and **effective August 6, 2025, CMS released the sanction and returned the program to normal enrollment status.** So the freeze is over. It is still fair to ask the program directly how staffing and specialist access look now, and CMS has said it expects continued post-sanction corrective action and monitoring.
- The city's own senior centers, meals, homemaking, Senior Companion, and Independent Living programs through **Senior Services at 904-255-5400** also help an older adult stay home longer, and none of them require a Medicaid determination to start.

## Frequently asked questions
**Mom is 79 and has been in the same Jacksonville house since 1994. Is there anything better than the regular senior exemption?** Very likely yes, and this is the one to chase. Duval County offers the **Long-Term Residency Senior Exemption**, available here since 2014, which **exempts the City of Jacksonville portion of the property taxes** rather than knocking a flat amount off the value. She needs to be **65 or older**, have prior-year household income at or below **$38,686**, have kept **permanent residency on the property for at least 25 uninterrupted years**, and the property's **just value must be under $250,000**. She clears the years easily. Call the Property Appraiser at **904-255-5900** and ask them to run her for the Long-Term Residency version, not just the limited-income one. File the **Duval 501** and the **DR-501SC** by **March 1**.

**My parents live in Atlantic Beach. Do they get the same property tax break as their friends across the Intracoastal?** They get the same **county** treatment, because Duval County and the City of Jacksonville are one consolidated government. What is not settled from public information is the **city portion**. Atlantic Beach, Jacksonville Beach, Neptune Beach, and Baldwin kept their own governments and their own municipal tax levies, and the Property Appraiser's senior exemption page says the exemptions apply to any municipality levy **that has also adopted the ordinance** without publishing a list of which ones did. So call **904-255-5900**, give the exact street address, and ask which taxing authorities on that bill have adopted the senior exemptions. Do not skip applying because you assume the answer is no.

**Dad is homebound in Arlington and cannot cook. How do we get him meals?** Call **Aging True at 904-807-1203** for **Meals on Wheels**, which covers Duval and Clay counties for people **60 and older** who are homebound with mobility restrictions. If he does not fully meet the eligibility rules, ask about their **private pay** option rather than giving up. Also call the **City of Jacksonville Senior Services Division at 904-255-5400**, because the **Frances Padgett Arlington Senior Center** at 1078 Rogero Road serves a hot midday meal Monday through Friday and breakfast most days at no charge, and getting him out of the house has its own value. If you are not sure which agency covers an address, the **ElderSource HelpLine at 1-888-242-4464** will route you in one call.

**The air conditioning died and my mother is 82 in Jacksonville. Who helps?** Because she is over 60, start with **EHEAP** through the **City of Jacksonville Senior Services Division at 904-255-5400**, or the city's main line at **904-630-2489**. EHEAP requires a documented home energy crisis and there can be more than one crisis benefit in a program year. In parallel, call **NFCAA's LIHEAP appointment line at 904-362-8052**, which is answered around the clock, or apply at floridaliheap.com. If the unit needs replacing rather than a bill paid, the **Emergency Repair Program** at the city's Housing and Community Development Division is **open** and covers heating and air conditioning replacement up to **$12,000** for owner-occupied homes under 80 percent of area median income. Ask for **Jackie Jones at 904-255-8229**. If she lives in Jacksonville Beach or Neptune Beach, her utility is **Beaches Energy**, and its **CARE program** for customers 60 and older is reached at **904-247-6241**.

**My father can't drive anymore. What does it cost him to get around Jacksonville?** If he can still ride a regular bus, **nothing**. JTA riders **65 and older ride free** with a Florida driver license, a Florida ID, or a JTA Senior STAR Card. Out-of-state IDs are not accepted, so getting a Florida ID is the first errand. If a disability prevents using the fixed-route bus, apply for **Connexion** through the Eligibility and Training Center at **904-265-6001**, expect an in-person assessment and a decision within **21 days**, and then pay **$3.00 each way** as an ADA rider or **$3.50** as a Transportation Disadvantaged rider, exact fare only. Reservations are **904-265-6999** for Connexion and **904-404-0848** for the on-demand Connexion Plus.

**Is my mother's PACE program real, and did it not get shut down?** It is real and it was never shut down. **The PACE Place**, run by PACE Partners of Northeast Florida at **5450 Ramona Boulevard, 904-428-0400**, is listed as **Operational** in Florida AHCA's February 2026 PACE report, the only PACE organization present in Duval County, with 94 enrollees that month. What you probably found was the **CMS enrollment sanction of May 17, 2024**, which suspended new Medicare enrollments after audit findings. **CMS released that sanction effective August 6, 2025** and the program returned to normal enrollment status. Before you call, check the ZIP code against their service area list, because it does not include every ZIP in Duval County.

**Mom needs a lawyer about a contractor who took her money, but she owns her home outright. Does she make too much for legal aid?** Probably not, and this is the most common wrong assumption on this page. **Jacksonville Area Legal Aid's Title III unit for people age 60 and older is not income sensitive.** Most other JALA units apply a 200 percent of poverty test, but the elder unit does not. Call **904-356-8371** or go to 126 W. Adams Street. If they cannot take it, call **Florida's Senior Legal Helpline at 1-888-895-7873**, free to any Florida resident 60 and older. And be careful who calls her back: JALA warns that **Legal-Aid-Now is not affiliated with them**, and genuine JALA email addresses end in jaxlegalaid.org.

**My husband has dementia and I am exhausted. What is actually available in Jacksonville?** Three things, in this order. Call **ElderSource at 1-888-242-4464** and ask about **TCARE**, the caregiver burnout program for people caring for someone 60 or older, and about **GUIDE Dementia Care**, Medicare's new dementia model, which provides a care navigator, a **24/7 caregiver helpline**, and **respite at no cost** to Traditional Medicare Part A and B beneficiaries. GUIDE does not accept Medicare Advantage enrollees, people in skilled nursing, or people in hospice or PACE, so check the card first. Second, if the diagnosis itself is unclear, the **Mayo Clinic Memory Disorder Clinic at 904-953-7103** is Northeast Florida's state-designated memory clinic. Third, ask ElderSource about **Alzheimer's Disease Initiative respite**, which covers in-home, facility, emergency, and extended respite of up to 30 days. The **Alzheimer's Association helpline at 1-800-272-3900** is answered at 3 a.m., which is often when you need it.
