*Free, plain-English help for seniors and families in Hillsborough County, FL. Education, not advice. Verify before you rely on it.*

## Start here (works anywhere)
- 211 (dial 2-1-1), Eldercare Locator 1-800-677-1116, BenefitsCheckUp.org, Medicare 1-800-633-4227, Social Security 1-800-772-1213, VA 1-800-827-1000, Alzheimer's 24/7 Helpline 1-800-272-3900, National Elder Fraud Hotline 1-833-372-8311, Crisis Lifeline 988

**Three local front doors:** 211, answered 24 hours a day for Hillsborough County by the Crisis Center of Tampa Bay (dial 2-1-1, or text your ZIP code to 898211) | the **Elder Helpline at 1-800-963-5337** (1-800-96-ELDER), which reaches **Senior Connection Center**, the Area Agency on Aging and Aging and Disability Resource Center for Hillsborough, Polk, Manatee, Highlands, and Hardee counties | **Hillsborough County Aging Services at 813-272-5250** for county meals and senior in-home services

One note worth knowing before you dial. The county's own Aging Services landing page lists a number labeled "Elder Helpline" that is not the Elder Helpline. The county's Senior In-Home Services page gives the correct one, 1-800-963-5337, and so does Senior Connection Center itself. Use 1-800-963-5337. Senior Connection Center's other published number, 813-740-3888, is its line for service providers and business contacts, not the help line for families.

## Property tax relief (apply with the Hillsborough County Property Appraiser, 813-272-6100, hcpafl.org)
Florida seniors apply for property tax relief through the county Property Appraiser. In Hillsborough that office is at the **15th Floor, County Center, 601 E. Kennedy Boulevard, Tampa, FL 33602-4932**, open 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Monday through Friday, with other locations across the county. The statutory filing deadline is **March 1** each year, extending to the next business day when it lands on a weekend or holiday.

Here is the part most people miss. Florida's senior exemptions are a **local option**. They only come off the taxes of a city or county government that passed an ordinance adopting them, and they never touch the school district portion of your bill. So the answer depends on which side of a city line your house sits on.

**Standard Homestead Exemption (statewide, any qualifying primary residence):** up to $50,000 off the assessed value of a permanent primary residence. The first $25,000 is entirely exempt; the second $25,000 applies to value between $50,000 and $75,000 and does not include a benefit on the school tax. After the first year, the annual increase in assessed value is capped at 3 percent or the change in the Consumer Price Index, whichever is lower. That cap is **Save Our Homes**, and you can carry all or part of it to a new Florida home through **portability**.

**Senior Citizen Over 65 Low Income exemption:** an additional exemption on top of the standard homestead for owners who meet all of these:
- The property already receives the Homestead Exemption.
- At least one owner is 65 or older on January 1.
- Prior-year household income, measured as adjusted gross income under section 62 of the Internal Revenue Code, is at or below the annual limit. The Florida Department of Revenue set that limit at **$38,686 for 2026**. It is adjusted every January by the change in the cost of living index, so it moves each year.

Apply with **Form DR-501SC**. First-time applications should be in by March 1, and the Property Appraiser says to apply even if you do not yet know the household's total income. Once approved, it renews automatically as long as you keep meeting the income limit. In Hillsborough County the governments that adopted it are the **City of Tampa (up to $50,000)**, **Unincorporated Hillsborough County (up to $50,000)**, and **Temple Terrace (up to $25,000)**. Plant City is not on the Property Appraiser's list of adopting municipalities, so a Plant City homeowner would not get a city-portion senior exemption there.

**Long-Term Residency Senior Citizen exemption (25 or more years in the home):** this one can be much larger, because it is an exemption equal to the assessed value of the property against the adopting government's millage. To qualify you must first meet the Homestead and Low Income Senior requirements, plus:
- A just (market) value under **$250,000**, as determined in the first tax year you apply and qualify.
- Twenty-five consecutive years as your permanent residence.

In Hillsborough County only the **City of Tampa** and **Unincorporated Hillsborough County** have adopted the Long-Term Residency version. It is filed with a Long Term Residency Senior Citizen Affidavit.

**Other exemptions worth asking about at the same time:** $5,000 widow or widower, $5,000 personal disability, $5,000 blind, a total and permanent disability exemption based on household gross income (renewed yearly with Form DR-501A), $5,000 for a veteran with a service-connected disability under 100 percent, and a full exemption for a veteran with a service-connected total and permanent disability, which can pass to a qualifying surviving spouse. Florida also gives veterans 65 and older with a permanent, partial service-connected disability a discount equal to their disability percentage. If you built on to the house for a parent or grandparent 62 or older, ask about the **Granny Flat** assessment reduction.

**The one move:** call the Property Appraiser at **813-272-6100** before March 1, tell them your address so they can confirm which government's exemption applies to you, and have them check you for both the Low Income Senior and the Long-Term Residency exemptions at the same time.

## Food
- **Hillsborough County Aging Services** runs two meal programs for residents **60 and older**. **Weekday Lunch** is a hot noon meal in a group setting at nutrition centers around the county. **Home Delivered Meals** go to seniors who cannot leave home because of illness, disability, or frailty. There is no fee, donations are accepted, and there is a waiting list that prioritizes by need. Call the Aging Services Customer Care Team at **813-272-5250** to apply.
- **Meals On Wheels of Tampa** (813-238-8410, mowtampa.org) is a separate nonprofit. Volunteers deliver a freshly prepared hot meal Monday through Friday between 10 a.m. and 2 p.m., with two frozen weekend meals delivered on Fridays. You must be **62 or older, or homebound**, unable to prepare meals, and live in one of the specific Hillsborough ZIP codes it covers, which include much of Tampa plus areas like Riverview, Apollo Beach, and Gibsonton. Recipients make a good-faith contribution toward the meal cost, discussed during intake. Caregivers and spouses may also be eligible.
- **Feeding Tampa Bay** is the regional food bank, serving a ten-county region that includes Hillsborough. Use the pantry and free-grocery finder at ftb.org/findfood, or dial 211. It also runs **Trinity Cafe**, free full-service restaurants serving a three-course, chef-prepared meal 365 days a year, and **Groceries on the Go**, a mobile grocery store with low-cost produce and shelf-stable food.
- **SNAP** (food stamps) is based on income, not age. Apply any time through Florida's ACCESS system at myaccess.myflfamilies.com or 1-866-762-2237.

## Energy and utility help
- **Hillsborough County Social Services** runs both energy programs, and you apply through a county **Community Resource Center**. Call **813-272-5220**.
- **LIHEAP** helps with home energy bills for households at or below 60 percent of the state median income who are responsible for the bill and have a recent energy bill in hand. During peak periods the county prioritizes households with a **senior 60 or older**, a child 5 or under, or a disabled member. Assistance runs as long as program funds last.
- **EHEAP** is the version built for older adults. It helps a low-income household with **at least one person 60 or older** that is in a home energy emergency, meaning a delinquent bill, a shut-off notice, or a lack of fuel or wood. Households can receive more than one crisis benefit per program year, up to a maximum set by FloridaCommerce, and payment goes straight to the utility or by two-party check. Benefit amounts vary and are set by the state, so ask the county or the Elder Helpline (1-800-963-5337) what is available this year rather than counting on a figure you read somewhere.
- **Tampa Electric's Share program** helps customers in crisis pay their energy bills and is administered by **Metropolitan Ministries, the Salvation Army, and the Catholic Charities Diocese of St. Petersburg**. Contact one of those agencies or dial 211 to be routed. TECO also offers payment arrangements and its Due Date Plus program for customers who cannot pay by the due date.
- For a past-due bill, dial 211 to find a church ministry or other local fund with emergency utility money.

## Medicare, Medicaid, prescriptions
- Florida's SHIP (free Medicare counseling) is **SHINE (Serving Health Insurance Needs of Elders)**, delivered locally by Senior Connection Center. Trained volunteers give free, unbiased, confidential help with enrollment, Parts A, B, and D, Medigap and Advantage plans, plan comparisons, claims and billing problems, complaints and appeals, fraud, and the programs that lower Medicare costs. Call **1-800-963-5337** to schedule a counseling session, and see floridashine.org for community events. Start here before any Medicare decision.
- **Medicaid** in Florida, including long-term-care coverage and the programs that help pay Medicare costs, is applied for through the Department of Children and Families ACCESS system: myaccess.myflfamilies.com or **1-866-762-2237**.
- **Medicare Savings Programs** (help paying Part A and B premiums) and **Extra Help / the Low-Income Subsidy** (lower Part D drug costs) are screened through SHINE (1-800-963-5337), Social Security (1-800-772-1213), and ACCESS. Florida does not run a general statewide senior drug program, so ask SHINE about Extra Help and use BenefitsCheckUp.org to find manufacturer and nonprofit patient-assistance programs for specific drugs.

## Home repair and safety
Where the house sits matters here too, and the split runs the opposite way from the tax exemption.

- **Hillsborough County's Owner-Occupied Rehabilitation (OOR) Program**, run by Affordable Housing Services, serves homeowners in **Unincorporated Hillsborough County, Plant City, and Temple Terrace**. It does not cover the City of Tampa. Repairs are prioritized for life, health, and safety: failing roofs, plumbing or sewer and septic, hazardous electrical, no working heat or air conditioning, accessibility improvements, and repairs to doors, walls, and windows. Help comes as a **SHIP interest-free, deferred-payment loan that is forgiven over 5 to 15 years** depending on the amount, as long as you keep living there. You must own a single-family home, villa, or condominium, meet income limits, have had a homestead exemption for the past two years, be current on property taxes, and have no more than one mortgage. Email AffordableHousingServices@HCFL.gov or call **813-635-1610**.
- **City of Tampa homeowners** go through the city's **Housing Rehabilitation and Renovation Program (HRRP)** instead. As of the city's July 8, 2026 page update, HRRP is **not accepting new applications** because of limited funding; applications submitted before January 7, 2026 are still being reviewed, and the city projects reopening during summer 2026 if state and federal funding allows. Call **813-274-7954** to check the current status before you count on it.
- **Weatherization** measures such as insulation, windows, doors, and efficient air conditioning are handled inside these rehabilitation programs rather than as a separate senior program. Ask Affordable Housing Services or dial 211 for the current provider.
- If the home is in a rural area, the **USDA Section 504 Home Repair** program offers low-interest repair loans, and grants for owners 62 and older who cannot repay a loan, to fix health and safety hazards.

## Rent, housing, emergency cash
- **211**, answered around the clock for Hillsborough County by the **Crisis Center of Tampa Bay**, is the fastest route to emergency help with rent and utilities. Specialists work from a database of thousands of local programs and can also provide short-term crisis support. Dial 2-1-1, text your ZIP code to 898211, or search 211atyourfingertips.org. Interpretation is available in more than 150 languages.
- For Medicaid, SNAP, and cash assistance, apply through Florida ACCESS at myaccess.myflfamilies.com or 1-866-762-2237.
- **Metropolitan Ministries**, the **Salvation Army**, and **Catholic Charities Diocese of St. Petersburg** run emergency assistance in the Tampa area and administer utility help; 211 will tell you which one has funds right now.

## Transportation
- **Sunshine Line** is Hillsborough County's door-to-door transportation for seniors, people with disabilities, and low-income residents who do not have or cannot afford their own transportation. It covers medical and social service appointments, grocery trips, health and wellness, and personal business. Eligibility is based on income, age, disability, and available transportation; income qualification is at or below **175 percent of the Federal Poverty Guidelines**. **There are currently no fees for this service.** All vehicles are wheelchair accessible. Enroll by calling **813-272-7272**, Monday through Friday 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Once enrolled, book rides at least two days ahead and up to seven days ahead, and schedule the return trip at the same time.
- **HARTPlus** is HART's ADA paratransit: door-to-door shared van service for people whose physical, cognitive, emotional, visual, or other disability prevents them from using the regular fixed-route bus. A disability by itself does not make you eligible. Eligibility runs through an application and, if needed, an in-person interview and functional evaluation, and takes at least **21 days** after the application is complete; free transportation to and from the evaluation is provided. Certification lasts up to three or five years. Reservations can be made the day before and up to three days in advance, seven days a week. Riders pay exact fare when boarding and operators cannot make change, so call for the current fare. For applications, eligibility, reservations, and fares, call **813-254-4278**.
- **Riders 65 and older** can get a HART discount card for reduced fixed-route fare with proper HART identification; ask at 813-254-4278.

## Legal help
- **Florida's Senior Legal Helpline** gives free legal advice and brief services by phone to Florida residents **60 and older** on civil matters including housing, family matters, government benefits, contracts, and consumer problems. Call **1-888-895-7873**, Monday through Friday, 9 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. Worth knowing locally: the statewide helpline is operated by **Bay Area Legal Services**, the nonprofit law firm headquartered right in Tampa.
- **Bay Area Legal Services** also helps seniors and their family members directly across eight counties including Hillsborough, with family law, housing, caregiver services, public benefits, guardianship, elder abuse prevention, advance planning, wills and estates, and bankruptcy. The Hillsborough helpline is **813-232-1343**; the general Legal Aid Line is **800-625-2257**, Monday through Friday 9 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. TTY is **800-955-8771**. Language interpreters are free. You can also apply online any time for non-emergency matters.
- Bay Area Legal Services has Hillsborough offices in **Ybor City, Plant City, and Ruskin**, plus a Legal Information Center. All offices are **by appointment only, with no walk-ins**, so call first.

## Caregiver support
- Senior Connection Center runs **TCARE (Tailored Caregiver Assessment and Referral System)** free for people caring for someone **60 or older** who live in Hillsborough, Hardee, Highlands, Manatee, or Polk County. A specialist builds a care plan with you, works on the root causes of caregiver burnout, and connects you to resources. Note that TCARE itself does not pay for in-home or paid caregiver services; for that, ask about the Long Term Care Services department at **1-800-336-2226** or **813-740-3888**, option 2. Start at the Elder Helpline, **1-800-963-5337**.
- The **Alzheimer's Association 24/7 Helpline** (1-800-272-3900) is there any hour for dementia caregiving questions, and 211 can point you to local support groups.

## Staying at home instead of a nursing home
- Florida Medicaid pays for in-home care through its **Statewide Medicaid Managed Care Long-Term Care** program for older adults who meet a nursing-home level of care but can live safely at home. **Hillsborough County Senior In-Home Services** is the separate, county-run option for adults over 60 who are functionally impaired: respite for the caregiver, companionship, homemaking, personal care, home delivered meals, consumable medical supplies, emergency alert buttons, and lock boxes for bedbound seniors who live alone. A case manager visits the home, builds a plan, and follows up. Fees are on a sliding scale based on income. Apply by phone through the **Elder Helpline at 1-800-963-5337** or **Aging Services at 813-272-5250**. Applicants most in need are approved first, so start early.
- **PACE (the Program of All-Inclusive Care for the Elderly)** wraps all medical care, therapy, an adult day center, home care, and prescriptions into one program for people **55 and older** who meet a nursing-home level of care but can live at home with help. Hillsborough County has **two operational PACE programs**, both confirmed operational in Florida's official February 2026 AHCA PACE report:
  - **Empath LIFE (Living Independence for the Elderly)**, 5414 Beaumont Center Blvd., Tampa, FL 33634, serves **all of Hillsborough County except ZIP codes 33547, 33567, and 33598**. Call **866-458-2933** (hearing impaired 800-955-8771).
  - **InnovAge Tampa PACE Center**, 4520 Seedling Circle, Tampa, FL 33614, serves **Hernando, Hillsborough, and Pasco** counties. Call **813-816-0378** for enrollment, or 813-697-1350 if you are already a participant.
  If you live in one of the three ZIP codes Empath LIFE excludes, ask InnovAge whether your address falls in its service area. Enrollment usually requires Medicaid eligibility and is set by ZIP-code service area, so confirm with the program before you plan around it. A third organization, BoldAge, has an application pending with the federal Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services for this area and is **not operating yet**, so do not count on it.
- Home-delivered meals, in-home help, and Sunshine Line rides also help an older adult stay in the home longer.

## Frequently asked questions
**Mom is 70 in Tampa on a fixed income. Can she lower her property taxes?** Likely yes, and Tampa is one of the better places in the county for it. On top of the standard homestead exemption (up to $50,000 off plus the Save Our Homes cap), the City of Tampa adopted the Senior Citizen Over 65 Low Income exemption at up to $50,000 for owners 65 and older whose prior-year household income was at or below $38,686 for 2026. If she has lived in the home 25 consecutive years and its just value was under $250,000 the first year she qualified, Tampa also adopted the Long-Term Residency version, which is an exemption equal to the assessed value against the city's millage. Call the Property Appraiser at 813-272-6100 before March 1 and have them check her for both.

**My parents are in Plant City. Do they get the same senior exemption?** Not the city-portion one. The Property Appraiser lists the City of Tampa, Unincorporated Hillsborough County, and Temple Terrace as the governments that adopted the senior exemption, and only Tampa and Unincorporated Hillsborough for Long-Term Residency. Plant City is not on either list. They still get the standard homestead exemption and Save Our Homes, and they should still call 813-272-6100 to confirm what applies at their exact address. There is a flip side: Plant City homeowners **are** eligible for the county's Owner-Occupied Rehabilitation home repair program, which City of Tampa homeowners are not.

**Dad is homebound in Hillsborough and can't cook. How do we get him meals?** Two doors, and it is worth trying both. Call Hillsborough County Aging Services at 813-272-5250 about Home Delivered Meals for residents 60 and older, knowing there is a waiting list ranked by need. Also call Meals On Wheels of Tampa at 813-238-8410, which delivers hot meals Monday through Friday if he is 62 or older or homebound and lives in one of its covered ZIP codes. For food right now, dial 211 or use the Feeding Tampa Bay finder at ftb.org/findfood.

**My parent can't drive and needs rides to the doctor. What is available?** Start with Sunshine Line at 813-272-7272. It is the county's door-to-door service for seniors, people with disabilities, and low-income residents, and there are currently no fees. Book at least two days ahead. If a disability specifically prevents your parent from using the regular HART bus, apply for HARTPlus paratransit at 813-254-4278, but start early because eligibility takes at least 21 days. Riders 65 and older can also get a HART discount card for the regular bus.

**How does mom get help paying her electric bill in Tampa?** Call Hillsborough County Social Services at 813-272-5220 and ask about both LIHEAP and EHEAP, and apply through a Community Resource Center. EHEAP is the one built for households with someone 60 or older who is facing a shut-off notice or a past-due bill, and it can be used more than once in a program year. Tampa Electric's Share program is administered through Metropolitan Ministries, the Salvation Army, and Catholic Charities Diocese of St. Petersburg. Dial 211 to find which agency has funds available now.
