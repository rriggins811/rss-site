*Free, plain-English help for seniors and families in Jefferson County, AL. Education, not advice. Verify before you rely on it.*

## Start here (works anywhere)
- 211 (dial 2-1-1), Eldercare Locator 1-800-677-1116, BenefitsCheckUp.org, Medicare 1-800-633-4227, Social Security 1-800-772-1213, VA 1-800-827-1000, Alzheimer's 24/7 Helpline 1-800-272-3900, National Elder Fraud Hotline 1-833-372-8311, Crisis Lifeline 988

**Three local front doors:** **United Way Area Agency on Aging of Jefferson County**, whose **Aging and Disability Resource Center answers 1-800-AGE-LINE (1-800-243-5463)**, Monday through Friday **8 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.**, at **3600 8th Avenue South, Birmingham, AL 35232**, direct line **205-458-3330** | **Jefferson County Senior Services Division**, Department of Community Services, **716 Richard Arrington Jr. Boulevard North, Birmingham, AL 35203**, Information Specialist **Dedra Lewis at 205-325-5567**, Coordinator **Janice L. Williams at 205-325-5572** | **2-1-1**, also reachable at **888-421-1266**, or **text your ZIP code to 898-211**, run by United Way of Central Alabama.

**Start with the structural fact that explains half the confusion in this county: the aging network here is a United Way, not a government agency, and its charter is narrower than you would guess.** In most Alabama counties the Area Agency on Aging is a regional council of governments. In Jefferson County it is the **United Way Area Agency on Aging**, and the Alabama Department of Senior Services lists it with a parenthetical that matters: **"Older Americans Act only."** That is not a knock on the agency. It is a map. Meals on Wheels, congregate meals at senior centers, SHIP Medicare counseling, SenioRx prescription help, free legal assistance, the Long-Term Care Ombudsman, homemaker and personal care, and caregiver support all run through **1-800-AGE-LINE**. But the **Medicaid Elderly and Disabled Waiver**, the track that pays for care at home instead of a nursing home, does not. In Jefferson County that program is administered by the **Regional Planning Commission of Greater Birmingham**. If you call the Area Agency about a Medicaid waiver, you will be referred out. Knowing that in advance saves a week.

**The second thing to understand is that Jefferson County government is split into two divisions, and it is not a formality.** The county operates a **Birmingham Division** downtown and a **Bessemer Division**, often called the **Bessemer Cut-Off**, out of a separate courthouse in Bessemer. The Tax Assessor has an office in each. So does the Department of Human Resources. So does the Community Action Agency that runs LIHEAP. Which office holds your parcel and your case file depends on where in the county you live, and the boundary is a legal division line, not a straight guess from a map. Rather than assume, give your address to the office when you call and ask which division you are in. It is a thirty-second question that prevents a wasted trip to the wrong courthouse.

**The third pattern worth naming up front: several programs serving Birmingham are administered from somewhere else.** **LIHEAP** for Jefferson County is run by an agency headquartered in **Rainsville**, in the far northeast corner of the state, though it does staff two offices here. The **statewide respite reimbursement program** is run out of Huntsville. **PACE**, the all-inclusive alternative to a nursing home, does not operate in this county at all, and the nearest program is in Mobile. None of that means you cannot get help. It means the phone and the online application are your tools, and it means you should always confirm that the office you found online is the one that actually serves your address this year.

Three more numbers belong on the refrigerator. The **Alabama Adult Abuse Hotline at 1-800-458-7214** takes reports of suspected abuse, neglect or exploitation of an elderly or disabled adult. The **Long-Term Care Ombudsman**, reached through **1-800-AGE-LINE** or **ombudsman@uwaaa.org**, is who you call about a nursing home, assisted living facility or boarding home, rather than that facility's own management, and complaints can be made anonymously by a resident, a family member, a friend, or any concerned third party. And **Cooper Green Mercy Health Services at 205-930-3200** is the county's safety-net health system for uninsured and underinsured residents.

## Property tax relief (apply with the Jefferson County Tax Assessor, 205-325-5300 Birmingham or 205-481-4125 Bessemer)
Alabama seniors claim property tax relief through the **county Tax Assessor or tax-assessing official**, not the Tax Collector or the Revenue Commissioner, and in Jefferson County there are two offices:

- **Birmingham Division.** Tax Assessor Gaynell Hendricks, **Suite 170, Jefferson County Courthouse, 716 Richard Arrington Jr. Boulevard North, Birmingham, AL 35203**. The county website lists **205-325-5300**; the Special Senior Exemption form itself lists **(205) 325-5505**. Either will get you to the office.
- **Bessemer Division.** **1801 3rd Avenue North, Room 209, Courthouse, Bessemer, AL 35020**, phone **205-481-4125**, fax **205-481-4128**, serving the Bessemer Cut-Off District.

**The claim window is the part people miss, and it is not tax season.** The county's own FAQ states it plainly: **"Exemptions must be claimed by December 31 for next tax year."** For the age and disability exemptions it goes further: **"To apply for senior citizens or disability exemption you must bring proof of age (birth certificate or drivers license) and most recent Federal and State income tax returns to the Tax Assessor's office between October 1 and December 31 to be eligible for exemption on the next years tax."** You must be **65 years of age or considered disabled as of October 1**. And the county states that **"This exemption must be renewed annually."** That last sentence is the one that quietly costs people money, and it matters more here than in most counties, for a reason explained below.

**The statewide tiers, from the Alabama Department of Revenue.** Alabama sorts homestead exemptions into four categories, and the differences are worth real money:

- **H-1.** Taxpayers under age 65 who are not disabled: **$4,000 assessed value** off state tax and **$2,000 assessed value** off county tax.
- **H-2.** Taxpayers **age 65 and older with an annual adjusted gross income of less than $12,000**, or those permanently and totally disabled: exempt from **all of the state portion**, plus **$5,000 of assessed value** on the county portion.
- **H-3, age 65 and older.** Taxpayers **65 and older with net taxable income of $12,000 or less on the combined taxpayer and spouse federal income tax return**: **exempt from all ad valorem taxes**. Not a discount. All of it.
- **H-3, disabled.** A taxpayer who is **permanently and totally disabled** is **exempt from all ad valorem taxes**, and the Department of Revenue is explicit that **there is no income limitation** on this one.
- **H-4.** Age 65 and older with **income exceeding $12,000**: exempt from the state portion, plus the regular **$2,000** county homestead exemption.

Read those tiers against a real tax return before you decide you do not qualify. The gap between H-4 and H-3 is the gap between a small trim and a zero bill, and the deciding number is **net taxable income on the federal return**, not gross income and not Social Security. Bring the return to the office and let them read it.

**Jefferson County uses its own exemption codes, so the letters may not match what you read online.** The Tax Assessor publishes an exemption-code table that reads like this: code **2--2**, homestead or primary residence, 10 percent assessment rate. Code **6--6**, disability, **"EXEMPT FROM ALL PORTIONS OF REAL PROPERTY TAXES."** Code **5--5**, age-based, **"65 YEARS OF AGE OR OLDER EXEMPT FROM ALL PORTIONS OF REAL PROPERTY TAXES."** Code **3--2**, **"65 YEARS OF AGE OR OLDER EXEMPTED FROM STATE PORTION ONLY."** Code **3--3**, **"65 YEARS OF AGE OR OLDER EXEMPTED FROM COUNTY AND SCHOOL DISTRICT TAX."** And code **SCE**, Senior Citizen Exemption, **"EXEMPTION THAT WILL FREEZE THE TAXES FOR THE NEXT APPLICABLE TAX YEAR."** If a clerk says "you are a 3-2," that is the state-portion-only exemption, and it is worth asking whether you should be a 5-5 instead.

**Now the exemption that is specific to this county, and the one most worth a phone call.** Under **Act 2021-300**, approved by Jefferson County voters on **November 8, 2022**, the county offers a **Special Senior Property Tax Exemption**. It is a **freeze**, not a write-off. The Tax Assessor's own FAQ answers the obvious question first: **"Is the Special Senior Exemption a full exemption for everyone 65 years of age or older?"** Answer: **"No, it is an exemption that is in addition to the Over-65 exemption that will freeze the taxes for the next applicable tax year once claimed."**

**What it requires,** per the county's own application form: you must be **65 or older**, it must be your **primary residence**, and the property must have been **assessed in the taxpayer's name for a period of 5 consecutive years**. The form also asks whether you were the **owner of the property as of October 1st**.

**Five things the county's FAQ answers that people get wrong:**
1. **Income is irrelevant.** Asked what happens if you do not file income taxes, the county answers: **"It does not, taxable income is not a qualification for the SSPE."** Almost every other exemption on this page turns on income. This one does not.
2. **Primary residence only.** **"The Special Senior Exemption can only be claimed on the property that is your primary residence or any adjacent properties that are considered part of your primary residence."**
3. **A younger spouse does not disqualify you.** **"Yes, you can still claim the exemption even though your spouse is not 65 years of age or older at this time. If your name is on the deed and the property is your primary residence, the exemption can be claimed."**
4. **The freeze depends on the annual renewal.** **"The exemption will remain on the property if you renew the Over-65 Exemption each year."** Miss the renewal and you can lose the freeze along with it.
5. **If your value drops, you can lock in the lower number.** **"Yes, you will only need to come in and reclaim the exemption at the lower rate."** Most people never think to do this.

**Two more things to hold in mind.** A freeze on assessed value is not a freeze on your bill: **while the assessed value is frozen, the property remains subject to any millage rate increases**. And the application carries teeth. It states that a fraudulent statement **"is subject to a penalty for perjury and shall be ordered to pay twice the amount of any ad valorem taxes which would have been due retroactive for a period of 10 years plus interest."** Fill it out carefully and honestly, and if you are unsure whether five consecutive years of assessment applies to your situation, ask the office rather than guessing on the form.

**One caution on deadlines.** Several law-firm and accounting posts circulating online cite an **April 30** date for claiming the Special Senior exemption, a leftover from the first year the exemption existed. The county's current published guidance is the October 1 to December 31 window described above. If you are reading this in the spring and someone tells you that you missed it, call **205-325-5300** or **205-481-4125** and ask directly rather than waiting a year on the strength of a blog post.

## Energy and utility help
**LIHEAP, and the agency you would never guess.** Alabama runs the Low-Income Home Energy Assistance Program through **ADECA**, which contracts with regional community action agencies. ADECA is blunt about its own role: **"ADECA does not schedule appointments and does not accept or process applications."** For **Region 10**, which covers **Blount, Cherokee, DeKalb, Jackson, Jefferson, Marshall and St. Clair counties**, the agency is the **Community Action Agency of Northeast Alabama, Inc.**, headquartered at **1481 McCurdy Avenue South, Rainsville, AL 35986**, call center **855-287-1730**, main office **256-638-4430**.

If you remember JCCEO handling this, that is why the address looks wrong. The old Jefferson County community action agency stopped administering these federal programs in 2021, and the work moved. The good news is that the current agency staffs two offices right here:

- **Birmingham office**, **900 38th Street North, Birmingham, AL 35222**, **205-502-2108**
- **Bessemer office**, **1612 3rd Avenue North, Bessemer, AL 35020**, **205-502-2108**

**What it takes to qualify.** Household income for the **previous month** must be at or below **150 percent of the federal poverty level**. The agency currently publishes these monthly figures: **1 person $1,883; 2 people $2,555; 3 people $3,228; 4 people $3,900; 5 people $4,573; 6 people $5,245; 7 people $5,918; 8 people $6,590**, adding **$643** for each additional member beyond eight. Limits are updated annually, so confirm the current numbers when you call.

**What to bring to the appointment**, per the agency: **photo ID of the applicant; Social Security cards for all household members; proof of income for the previous month for all adults over the age of 18, or a Social Security award letter; a utility bill in the applicant's name; and a lease if you live in government housing.** Applicants schedule an appointment rather than walking in.

**The same agency also runs other help worth asking about.** Its **GROW Program** offers housing counseling, credit and budget counseling, and can include rental assistance, mortgage assistance and fuel vouchers. Its **CSBG Direct Aid** programs vary by year and can include rental assistance, food cards, fuel gift cards, public transportation vouchers and prescription vouchers, and the agency says a full calendar of available resources goes up on its website **starting November 1**. Mark that date if money is tight going into winter.

**Project SHARE, which exists specifically for people 60 and older.** Since 2017, Alabama Power and the rural electric cooperatives have partnered with **The Salvation Army Greater Birmingham Area Command** to run **Project SHARE**, Service to Help Alabamians with Relief on Energy, providing emergency energy assistance **"to elderly and disabled Alabamians in peak winter and summer months"** across 57 counties. The requirement is simple: **"In order to qualify for Project share the applicant must be 60 years of age or older or a disabled Alabamian."**

Jefferson County has **three** Project SHARE community partners, more than most counties in the state:
- **Positive Maturity**, **205-803-1665**
- **The Salvation Army Bessemer Service Center**, **205-425-4303**

You can also call **205-328-2420 and press extension 2** to be pointed to the right local office.

## Medicare, Medicaid, prescriptions
**Free Medicare counseling (SHIP).** The **State Health Insurance Assistance Program** is administered locally by the United Way Area Agency on Aging and reached at **1-800-AGE-LINE (1-800-243-5463)**. Counselors provide **"personalized, unbiased counseling to help you understand your options,"** guidance on programs that help pay Medicare costs, and **"assistance with claims and appeals."** They do not sell insurance. The agency is direct about why the choice matters: **"Original Medicare is accepted by nearly all doctors and hospitals in the country. But with Medicare Advantage Plans, there are network restrictions that could limit your access to certain doctors and hospitals."** The same agency runs the **Senior Medicare Patrol**, which is where to take a suspicious bill or a caller who wants your Medicare number.

**SenioRx, Alabama's own prescription program.** Funded by the Alabama Legislature and delivered through the Area Agencies on Aging, SenioRx helps people get **free or reduced-cost prescription medications for chronic health conditions** through pharmaceutical patient-assistance programs. Two eligibility paths, per the local agency:
- **Individuals age 55 or older** with a chronic medical condition requiring prescription drug management **who have Medicare or no prescription insurance**.
- **Individuals of any age** deemed disabled by Social Security, or who have applied and are awaiting a decision, or who have a doctor's declaration of disability, or who are in the 24-month Medicare waiting period.

One important limit, stated by the program: **"SenioRx does not provide short-term medications, including antibiotics, for treatment of acute conditions."** It is for the maintenance drugs, and that is where the money is anyway. Call **1-800-AGE-LINE**.

**Medicaid, and the three applications seniors actually need.** The **Alabama Medicaid Recipient Call Center** is **1-800-362-1504**, Monday through Friday **8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.** For anything long-term-care related, the office that handles Jefferson County is the **Birmingham District Office**, **Beacon Ridge Tower, Suite 300, 600 Beacon Parkway West, Birmingham, AL 35209**, toll-free **866-371-4073**, fax **877-814-8302**, serving **Jefferson, Shelby and St. Clair counties**. Box 5624, Montgomery, AL 36103-5624. Ask for **Form 204/205** for a waiver or nursing home, and **Form 211** if what you want is help with your Medicare premiums.

**The Elderly and Disabled Waiver, and who actually runs it here.** This is the program that pays for services at home for someone who would otherwise need a nursing home: case management, personal care, homemaker services, companion services, respite, adult day services, meals, private duty nursing, medical supplies and assistive technology. In Jefferson County it is **"a program of the Alabama Department of Senior Services (ADSS) and the Alabama Medicaid Agency and administered by the Regional Planning Commission of Greater Birmingham."** That office is at **Two Twentieth Street North, Suite 1200, Birmingham, AL 35203**, phone **205-251-8139**. **If there is no insurance at all.** **Cooper Green Mercy Health Services**, the county's safety-net system, is at **1509 6th Avenue South, Birmingham, AL 35233**, main line **205-930-3200**. To become a patient you must be **18 years of age, a resident of Alabama for 1 year, and a resident of Jefferson County for at least 30 days.** It accepts Medicare, Medicaid and many third-party plans, and it operates a financial assistance program that gives reduced co-payments **"for all services, including prescriptions filled through our on-site pharmacy."** Services on site include primary care, cardiology, neurology, nephrology, ophthalmology, orthopaedics, podiatry, rheumatology, urology, behavioral health, wound care, radiology, a lab and a pharmacy. ## Home repair and safety
**Which office you call depends entirely on your address, and this is the single most common wasted phone call in the county.** Jefferson County runs Community Development Block Grant repair programs, but it states the boundary flatly: **"Properties must be located within the Jefferson County CDBG Consortium which is all of Jefferson County outside the city limits of Birmingham, Bessemer, and the towns of Sumiton and Helena."** And then, in the county's own words: **"City of Birmingham homeowners, please contact the City of Birmingham Housing Division at 205-254-2312."**

**If you are in the consortium**, meaning outside those four municipalities, the county's programs are:
- **Housing Emergency Grant Program (CDBG).** **"This program provides grants for emergency repairs to the homes of low and very low-income households."**
- **Housing Rehabilitation.** Larger-scope work. Call **205-325-5761** for the program and application process.
- **Volunteer Based Housing Rehabilitation Programs (CDBG).** The county **"works with volunteer-based organizations to make repairs such as painting to the homes of lower income citizens."**
- **Housing Accessibility Program (CDBG).** Grants for **"ramps, enlarged doorways, bathroom grab bars and other similar items"** for qualified low- and moderate-income families. If a parent is coming home from a hospital stay and the front steps are the problem, this is the call.

**Weatherization.** The Community Action Agency of Northeast Alabama also runs the **Weatherization Assistance Program**, described as designed **"to lower the energy usage of a client's home and improve the applicants' health and safety by tightening up their homes and preventing outside air infiltration."** Same offices as LIHEAP: Birmingham **900 38th Street North**, Bessemer **1612 3rd Avenue North**, both **205-502-2108**.

**A safety program that is free here and is not free everywhere.** If someone in the home has dementia and is at risk of wandering, **Alzheimer's of Central Alabama provides Project Lifesaver radio-tracking bracelets for individuals with dementia living in Jefferson County, in partnership with the Jefferson County Sheriff's Department.** The organization states the equipment costs **$450** and that it **"is committed to helping families across Alabama and will provide a bracelet regardless of ability to pay."** Two eligibility conditions: **the person with dementia must live in the home with a caregiver who can provide continuous supervision, and should no longer be driving.** These bracelets are radio-frequency, trackable by sheriff's departments rather than by an app, and the organization reports a 100 percent success rate for safe returns in Alabama. Call **205-871-7970**.

**Free memory screenings.** The Jefferson County Senior Services Division hosts the **Alzheimer's Foundation of America's National Memory Screening Program**, described as **"a simple and safe 'healthy brain check-up'"** that takes **less than 10 minutes** and **"can indicate if someone might benefit from a comprehensive medical evaluation."** It does not diagnose anything and does not replace a doctor. Ask about scheduling at **205-325-5567**.

## Transportation
**Two systems, two different eligibility rules, and people mix them up constantly.**

**ClasTran is the age-based option.** It provides **curb-to-curb demand-response service in Jefferson and Shelby counties**. The eligibility split is worth quoting: **"For riders living in urban areas of Jefferson and Shelby counties, you must either be 60 years of age or older OR have a disability to qualify for service. ## Legal help
**Start with the aging network, because it is free and it is built for this.** The **Alabama Legal Assistance Program**, administered locally by the United Way Area Agency on Aging, is **"available through UWAAA at no charge to persons age 60 and older."** You start with a call to **1-800-AGE-LINE (1-800-243-5463)** and are connected to a legal services specialist who gathers the facts and determines next steps. The program covers counseling, advice and representation in civil, non-fee-generating cases including:

- Access to public benefits such as Social Security, SSI, SSDI, Medicare, veterans' benefits and unemployment compensation
- Protections related to elder abuse, including fraud and financial exploitation
- Advance directives for financial affairs, medical and end-of-life decision-making, simple wills, and guardianship issues
- Access to housing options that allow seniors to remain independent at home
- **Eviction or foreclosure issues** that impede a senior's ability to remain in the home
- Access to healthcare and long-term care

**Legal Services Alabama is the other door, and there is a detail here most people never hear.** The Birmingham office is at **1820 7th Avenue North, Suite 200, Birmingham, AL 35203**, phone **205-328-3540**. Statewide intake is **1-866-456-4995** in English and **1-888-835-3505** in Spanish, **Monday through Friday 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.** LSA normally screens household income, but it publishes this: **"LSA also has special grants that are not income-based such as elder grants to assist clients 60 and over."** If someone told you that you make too much for legal aid, that sentence is worth reading to them over the phone.

**For a nursing home or assisted living problem, call the Ombudsman, not the facility.** The Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program of the United Way Area Agency on Aging **"provides assistance to residents of all long-term care facilities in Jefferson County,"** including nursing homes, assisted-living facilities, specialty-care facilities and boarding homes. Ombudsmen investigate complaints, make unannounced visits, and mediate between families and facilities. **"The resident or a family member, friend or any concerned third party may make a complaint with an Ombudsman at any time and may remain anonymous if desired,"** and **"There is no charge for using Ombudsman services."** Reach them at **1-800-AGE-LINE** or **ombudsman@uwaaa.org**.

**To report suspected abuse, neglect or exploitation** of an elderly or disabled adult, call the Alabama Department of Human Resources **Adult Abuse Hotline at 1-800-458-7214**. Reports may also be made to the county DHR office or local law enforcement, and may be made anonymously.

## Caregiver support
**Alabama Cares is the front door, and it is at the same number as everything else.** A program of the United Way Area Agency on Aging, it provides **information**, **support groups**, **short-term case management** to help families access services, **training** to improve caregiving skills, **supplemental services** on a limited basis, and **respite**, described as **"temporary, substitute support to relieve family caregivers from their daily caregiving responsibilities."** Be aware of the rationing language the agency publishes: **"Due to limited funding, this service is prioritized based on those with the greatest burden and highest need of relief."** Call **1-800-AGE-LINE (1-800-243-5463)** and describe the situation honestly, including how many hours you are covering and what you have already had to give up.

**Alzheimer's of Central Alabama is the local dementia specialist, and it writes checks.** Based at **300 Office Park Drive, Suite 225, Birmingham, AL 35223**, phone **(205) 871-7970**, toll-free **(866) 806-7255**, email aca@alzca.org, open **Monday through Friday, 9 a.m. to 4 p.m.** It describes itself as a local nonprofit where **"100% of the money we raise stays in the state."** Three programs are worth an application:

- **Adult Day Care Scholarships.** **"Scholarships will be for up to $300 per month"** at participating centers, awarded for one year with the possibility of renewal, and the organization says **"On average, forty to fifty scholarships are awarded each year."** Eligibility: the person with dementia must reside in Alabama, have a dementing illness, and **reside at home and have a caregiver**. ACA pays the day care center directly for days attended.
- **Continence Products Program.** A set amount of products shipped to the home monthly from a medical supply company.
- **Nutritional Supplement Program.** Home shipment of **Ensure** and **Glucerna** for diabetics, on their own or alongside continence products.

**Support groups you can walk into.** ACA runs a **virtual caregiver support group on Zoom at 11 a.m. every Tuesday**. In-person Birmingham-area groups it lists include the **United Way Area Agency on Aging of Jefferson County group, 3rd Tuesday of each month, 11:30 to 12:30**, contact Valarie Lawson at vlawson@uwaaa.org; **Trussville Public Library, 3rd Tuesday, 6:00 p.m.**; **Holy Apostles Church in Hoover, 2nd and 4th Thursdays, 10:30 to noon**; **St. Mark's UMC in Vestavia Hills, Fridays at 1 p.m.**; and **Asbury United Methodist Church, 1st and 3rd Thursdays at 1:00**. Listings change, so confirm before you drive.

**The statewide respite reimbursement program, and its current status.** **Alabama Lifespan Respite**, run through UCP Huntsville at **256-859-8300** or al.respite@ucphuntsville.org, reimburses unpaid family caregivers who pay a third-party respite provider out of pocket and submit a timesheet, with a check issued within 30 days. **As of late August 2026 the program states: "Applications are closed at this time," and "Please check back in October."** If you applied and received reimbursement after July 2025, the program says there is no need to reapply. Its rules to know before you plan around it: you **cannot** be receiving respite through another program, **including Medicaid Home and Community Based Services waivers**; only **one caregiver per household** may apply; you cannot be both the primary caregiver and the paid respite provider; and the provider must be **at least 18 and live outside the care recipient's home**.

**If what you need is a few hours of housekeeping rather than dementia care.** The Area Agency's **Homemaker and Personal Care** program covers light housekeeping, laundry, simple meal preparation, and dressing and grooming assistance. Read the gate before you call, because it is narrower than people expect: it is **"available to individuals who are age 60 or older, live alone and need help for a minimum of two hours per week with two or more activities of daily living."** Living alone is a requirement, not a preference. Call **1-800-AGE-LINE**.

## PACE
**There is no PACE program serving Jefferson County, and that is worth stating plainly so you stop looking for one.**

PACE, the Program of All-Inclusive Care for the Elderly, bundles medical care, prescriptions, adult day health, therapy, transportation and in-home help for people who need nursing-home-level care but want to stay at home. It is excellent where it exists. Alabama Medicaid's own PACE fact sheet lists the eligibility requirements, and one of them settles the question: an applicant **"Must live in service area (currently Mobile and Baldwin counties)."** Alabama has a single PACE organization, **Mercy LIFE of Alabama**, based in Mobile, roughly 250 miles from Birmingham.

**One warning, because this trap is easy to fall into.** Search for PACE and Jefferson County and you will find an organization called **Senior CommUnity Care of Jefferson County**. It is at 1700 Old Bluegrass Avenue in **Louisville, Kentucky**. Kentucky has a Jefferson County too. It has nothing to do with Alabama and cannot enroll you.

**What to ask for instead.** The closest equivalent here is the **Medicaid Elderly and Disabled Waiver**, which covers case management, personal care, homemaker and companion services, respite, adult day services, meals, private duty nursing, medical supplies and assistive technology for people who would otherwise need a nursing facility. In Jefferson County, call the **Regional Planning Commission of Greater Birmingham at 205-251-8139**, or start with the **Alabama Medicaid Birmingham District Office at 866-371-4073** and ask for Form 204/205. Then call **1-800-AGE-LINE** and ask what the Area Agency can layer on top while the waiver application is pending, because the waits are real and Alabama Cares, Homemaker and Personal Care, and Meals on Wheels can start sooner.

## Frequently asked questions
**Dad is 79, owns the same house in Center Point he bought in 1988, and lives on Social Security. What is he actually entitled to?** Possibly a zero property tax bill, and he should call before December 31. Under the Alabama Department of Revenue's **H-3** category, a taxpayer 65 or older with **net taxable income of $12,000 or less on the combined federal return** is **exempt from all ad valorem taxes**. Separately, he almost certainly qualifies for Jefferson County's **Special Senior Property Tax Exemption**, since he is over 65, it is his primary residence, and the property has been assessed in his name for far more than five consecutive years, and that one does not look at income at all. He should take his driver's license and his most recent federal and state returns to the Tax Assessor at **716 Richard Arrington Jr. Boulevard North, Suite 170**, or call **205-325-5300** first to confirm which division serves Center Point.

**Mom's power bill is about to go past due and she is 84 on a fixed income. Who actually helps pay it?** Make two calls, not one. First, **205-502-2108**, the Community Action Agency of Northeast Alabama's Birmingham office at 900 38th Street North, and ask for a **LIHEAP** appointment. Have the last month's income, a photo ID, Social Security cards for the household and a utility bill in her name ready. Second, **Project SHARE**, which exists specifically for people 60 and older: in Jefferson County call **Positive Maturity at 205-803-1665**, **The Salvation Army Bessemer Service Center at 205-425-4303**, or **Urban Ministry at 205-781-0517**. These are different pots of money and applying to one does not use up the other.

**My mother stopped driving. She is 73 and lives in Hueytown. How does she get around?** Two options, and she may want both. **ClasTran** is the age-based door-to-door service: at 60 or older she qualifies, the fare is a flat **$4.00 one way**, and she books by calling **205-325-8787** by **11:00 a.m. the day before**. She should apply now rather than when she needs it, since processing takes about ten days. Separately, the county pays for **ClasTran round trips to the Hueytown Senior Center**, which serves a weekday meal and is at 2501 Brooklane Drive, **205-491-7052**. And if she can manage a bus, **MAX Transit** cuts her fare to **$0.75** at age 62 with valid ID, with a **$25.00 Monthly Access Pass**.

**My husband has dementia and I have not had a real break in months. What is in Jefferson County?** Three calls, in this order. **Alzheimer's of Central Alabama at 205-871-7970** about an **adult day care scholarship of up to $300 per month**, which is the fastest route to actual hours away, plus a free **Project Lifesaver** bracelet if wandering is a worry. **1-800-AGE-LINE** for **Alabama Cares**, which provides respite, training and short-term case management, though respite there is prioritized by greatest burden, so describe your situation fully. And **256-859-8300** for **Alabama Lifespan Respite**, keeping in mind that as of late August 2026 its applications were closed with a note to check back in **October**. Meanwhile ACA's Zoom support group meets **Tuesdays at 11 a.m.** and costs nothing.

**We are looking at a nursing home for my mother and cannot afford it. Is there an alternative?** Yes, but it is not PACE, because PACE does not operate in this county. Ask about the **Medicaid Elderly and Disabled Waiver**. Call the **Regional Planning Commission of Greater Birmingham at 205-251-8139**, and the **Alabama Medicaid Birmingham District Office at 866-371-4073** for Form 204/205. The financial screen the commission publishes is income not exceeding **$2,829 monthly** with no more than **$2,000 in resources**, and her physician has to certify that she needs nursing-home-level care. Start the paperwork before a crisis, because approval is not fast.

**Mom is 71 and I think she qualifies for food stamps, but does she have to sit through an interview?** She may not have to, but check her age against the right rule. Alabama's **Elderly Simplified Application** is a short form with **no interview**, and the current state form says it is for households where **everyone is age 65 or older** with no earnings from work. At 71 she fits. Call **1-800-438-2958** for help filling it out. If she is closer to 65 than 71 and unsure, the **Benefits Enrollment Center at 1-877-833-2550** will screen her for free, and the Jefferson County DHR Food Assistance line is **205-423-4200**.

**My father is in a nursing home in Birmingham and something is wrong. Can I complain without him facing retaliation?** Call the **Long-Term Care Ombudsman** at **1-800-AGE-LINE** or email **ombudsman@uwaaa.org**. The program covers every long-term care facility in Jefferson County, complaints can be made by **any concerned third party**, you **may remain anonymous** if you choose, and there is **no charge**. Ombudsmen also make unannounced visits on a routine basis, so a pattern you report can be checked without your name attached to it. If what you are describing is abuse, neglect or financial exploitation, also call the **Adult Abuse Hotline at 1-800-458-7214**.

**My mother lives in Bessemer, not Birmingham. Does any of this apply to her?** All of it, and in several cases her office is closer than a Birmingham resident's. The **Tax Assessor Bessemer Division** is at **1801 3rd Avenue North, Room 209**, **205-481-4125**. The **Community Action Agency** has a Bessemer office at **1612 3rd Avenue North**, **205-502-2108**. **DHR** has a Bessemer office at **2014 1st Avenue North**. Project SHARE runs through **The Salvation Army Bessemer Service Center at 205-425-4303**. The **Exeter/Fairfax Senior Center** at 1500 Exeter Avenue serves meals at **205-481-4420 extension 218**. One thing to note: because Bessemer is one of the four municipalities excluded from the Jefferson County CDBG Consortium, the county's home repair grants do **not** cover her address, so ask the county at **205-325-5761** where Bessemer residents should apply instead.

**Dad needs a doctor, has no insurance and is not yet on Medicare. Where does he go?** **Cooper Green Mercy Health Services**, **1509 6th Avenue South**, **205-930-3200**. To enroll he must be **18 or older, an Alabama resident for one year, and a Jefferson County resident for at least 30 days**, and if he qualifies for financial assistance based on income he gets reduced co-payments on everything including prescriptions from the on-site pharmacy. Call **205-930-3377** between **7:00 a.m. and 4:30 p.m.** to make an enrollment appointment, and bring documents that verify residency, identity and income.
