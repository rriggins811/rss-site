*Free, plain-English help for seniors and families in New Hanover County, NC. Education, not advice. Verify before you rely on it.*

## Start here (works anywhere)
- 211 (dial 2-1-1), Eldercare Locator 1-800-677-1116, Medicare 1-800-633-4227, Social Security 1-800-772-1213, VA 1-800-827-1000, Alzheimer's 24/7 Helpline 1-800-272-3900, National Elder Fraud Hotline 1-833-372-8311, Crisis Lifeline 988

**Three local front doors:** **New Hanover County Senior Resource Center**, **2222 South College Road, Wilmington, NC 28403**, **910-798-6400** | **New Hanover County Social Services (Health and Human Services)**, **1650 Greenfield Street, Wilmington, NC 28401**, **910-798-3500** | **Cape Fear Council of Governments Area Agency on Aging**, **1480 Harbour Drive, Wilmington, NC 28401**, **910-395-4553**. And **2-1-1**, or **1-888-892-1162**.

**If you only make one call from this page, make it the Senior Resource Center.** Unlike many counties, New Hanover runs its senior center itself, as a county department. The Senior Resource Center (people call it the SRC) handles senior lunches and **Meals on Wheels**, **free rides to medical visits**, **SHIIP Medicare counseling**, prescription cost help, **caregiver respite grants**, support groups, counseling for older adults, and a team of social workers who do **home visits** and help families plan. It is open **Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m.**, and until 7 p.m. on Thursdays for some activities. For a question about a parent's situation, ask for the **social worker on call at 910-798-6437**.

**The Senior Resource Center's three meal sites.**
- **Senior Resource Center (Ohana Café)**, 2222 South College Road, Wilmington 28403. Lunch is served at **11 a.m. and noon**, Monday to Friday.
- **Castle Hayne Community Church**, 4544 Parmele Road, Castle Hayne 28429, for northern New Hanover. Monday to Friday, 9:30 a.m. to 1:30 p.m.
- **Katie B. Hines Senior Center**, 308 Cape Fear Boulevard, Carolina Beach 28428, for southern New Hanover. Lunch at noon.

The SRC's own pages list slightly different hours and lunch times for the two satellite sites, so call **910-798-6400** before a first visit.

**Social Services is the benefits office.** Medicaid, Food and Nutrition Services, energy assistance, Special Assistance, adult day care funds, in-home aides, guardianship and Adult Protective Services all run out of **1650 Greenfield Street, Wilmington**. Hours are **Monday, Wednesday, Thursday and Friday 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., and Tuesday 8 a.m. to 6 p.m.** Main line **910-798-3500**; **option 4** reaches the economic services team for Medicaid and food benefits. You can also email applications and documents to **DSSinfo@nhcgov.com**.

**Cape Fear Council of Governments is the regional layer.** It is the **Area Agency on Aging for Region O**, covering **Brunswick, Columbus, New Hanover and Pender** counties. It plans and funds aging services across the four counties, runs **Project C.A.R.E.** for families living with dementia and the **Long-Term Care Ombudsman** for nursing home and assisted living residents, and is piloting a **Caregiver Mentorship Program** with UNCW.

## Property tax relief (apply with the New Hanover County Tax Department, 910-798-7300)

**The office.** Senior and disability relief is handled by **New Hanover County Tax Administration**, **230 Government Center Drive, Suite 190, Wilmington, NC 28403**, phone **910-798-7300**, **Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m.** The state's official list of county assessors gives the same address and phone. The county has an online "Email the Tax Department" form.

**The three programs.** All three are set by the state, filed on the state's **Form AV-9**, and applied for with the county Tax Department.

- **Elderly or Disabled Exclusion.** Takes the **greater of $25,000 or 50 percent** off the assessed value of your home **and up to one acre** of land. You must be a North Carolina resident, **65 or older or totally and permanently disabled on or before January 1**, own and live in the home, and have income within the limit: **$38,800 for 2026, $39,900 for 2027**. Income means all money from every source except gifts and inheritances, and a married couple living together counts **both spouses' income** even if only one is on the deed. **You apply once.** The county says you do not refile each year, but you must file again if income, use or ownership changes, and it will periodically ask for updated income to keep the benefit. For disability, the county wants a certificate from a North Carolina licensed physician or a government agency (Form **AV-9A**); it says a Social Security disability letter alone is not accepted unless it meets the statute.
- **Circuit Breaker Tax Deferment.** Limits the tax to a percentage of income for owners who meet the exclusion rules **and have owned and lived in the home five consecutive years** as of January 1, with incomes up to **$58,200 for 2026** or **$59,850 for 2027**. **This is a deferral, not forgiveness.** The county says interest accrues on deferred taxes as if they had been paid on time, the deferred taxes **are a lien on your property**, and on a disqualifying event, **the last three years of deferred taxes plus interest become due and payable**. Disqualifying events are **the owner's death, transfer of the property, or the owner no longer living there** (a pass to a co-owner or spouse is the exception). And unlike the exclusion, **the Circuit Breaker needs a new application every year.** Understand the lien before your parent signs, especially if the plan is to sell the house or leave it to family.
- **Disabled Veteran Exclusion.** Takes **$45,000** off the appraised value of the home of an honorably discharged veteran with a **100 percent total and permanent service-connected disability** (or who receives the VA's specially adapted housing benefit), or the veteran's unmarried surviving spouse. **No age or income limit.** It uses the AV-9 plus **Form NCDVA-9**. **New Hanover County Veteran Services, 1650 Greenfield Street, 910-798-7611**, helps with VA paperwork: walk-in hours **Monday to Thursday, 8:30 a.m. to 3 p.m.**, Fridays by appointment.

**Each owner can use only one of the three programs.**

**Deadlines.** The county asks for exclusion applications during the January listing period, **January 1 to 31**, but accepts them **up to June 1**. The Circuit Breaker's yearly application is also due **by June 1**. The state form says an application must be filed by June 1 to be timely; a late one needs a showing of good cause.

**A nursing home stay does not end the exclusion.** The county says a qualified owner keeps it through a temporary absence for health reasons or an extended stay in a rest home or nursing home, **as long as the house sits empty or only a spouse or dependent lives there**. If the home is **sold or the owner dies between January 1 and July 1**, the exclusion comes off that year's bill; after July 1, it comes off the next year's.

**Values and rates.** New Hanover County revalues every **four years**, twice as often as state law requires. The current values took effect **January 1, 2025**, and the next revaluation is scheduled for **January 1, 2029**. The county rate for 2026 is **30.20 cents per $100** of value. Towns add their own: Wilmington **33.15 cents** plus a 5 cent downtown service district where it applies, Carolina Beach **17.12**, Kure Beach **19.50** and Wrightsville Beach **5.31**, and unincorporated areas pay a **7.25 cent** fire district rate.

**Paying this year's bill.** 2026 bills were due **September 1, 2026** and can be paid **without penalty or interest through January 5, 2027**. If a parent cannot pay in full, the Tax Department has a **Property Tax Payment Agreement Request Form** on its website, or call **910-798-7300**.

**Two protections worth ten minutes.**
- The **Register of Deeds** runs a free **Fraud Alert**: if a document is recorded in your name, you get an email **with a copy of the document at no cost**. Sign up on the Register of Deeds website, or call **910-798-4530**. The office is at **320 Chestnut Street, Wilmington**.
- The Register of Deeds also warns families not to overpay for a copy of a house deed. Copies come from the Register of Deeds itself, not from companies that mail offers.

## Food

**Meals on Wheels.** The Senior Resource Center delivers **home-delivered lunches Monday to Friday** to New Hanover residents **60 and older** who are **homebound** (unable to leave home unassisted), **living alone and unable to have their meals met by family, friends or others**, and **unable to cook or shop** because of ill health, no working stove or refrigerator, or a special diet. It starts with a **telephone screening**, then **a social worker visits the home** to enroll, and people are reassessed at least every six months. Call **910-798-6400**. A separate local nonprofit, **Meals on Wheels, Inc.**, works with the SRC to deliver **weekend meals** to eligible clients: **910-612-0676**. The SRC does not publish waitlist status, so ask when you call.

**Lunch at the senior centers.** Meals are **free for people 60 and older** (contributions are encouraged); anyone under 60 pays **$5**. **Registration is required** but **reservations are not**: fill out the lunch registration form, or complete it in person on your first day at any of the three sites above. Lunches are first come, first served. There is also a **drive-up lunch**, Monday to Friday **noon to 1 p.m.**, at the Senior Resource Center and at Castle Hayne Community Church.

**Farmers markets and food boxes.** With the Food Bank of Central and Eastern North Carolina and Feast Down East, the SRC runs **monthly farmers markets and a commodity food box program**. Its nutritionist also offers free one-on-one counseling to any New Hanover senior: **910-798-6420**.

**Groceries and prescriptions delivered: the FAM pilot.** New Hanover County's **Food and Medication (FAM) Delivery Program** is a two-year pilot for households in the county's **eight food desert areas**. Eligible residents get a free **Instacart+ membership** (no delivery fee on orders of $10 or more) and **$120 a year in "Fresh Funds"** for produce, and can order groceries and prescriptions to the door. Income must be **under 300 percent of the federal poverty level**, which is **$47,880 a year for one person** and **$64,920 for two**. You must show you live in one of the food desert areas; the county has an interactive map. People in assisted living or skilled nursing do not qualify, and participants still pay for their groceries and Instacart service fees. Apply online, or get help in person at Health and Human Services, 1650 Greenfield Street, **Monday to Friday 9 a.m. to 4 p.m.**

**The regional food bank.** The **Food Bank of Central and Eastern North Carolina's Wilmington branch**, **1000 Greenfield Street, Wilmington, 910-251-1465**, serves Brunswick, Columbus, Duplin, New Hanover and Pender counties. It runs a **No-Cost Market on Greenfield**: Monday and Thursday 10 a.m. to 1 p.m., Tuesday and Friday 1 to 4 p.m., and the 2nd and 4th Saturday 10 a.m. to 1 p.m. Its "find food" page lists pantries by ZIP code.

**Food and Nutrition Services (SNAP).** Apply on **ePASS** at epass.nc.gov, by phone at **910-798-3500, option 4**, by email at **DSSinfo@nhcgov.com**, by mail or fax, or in person at 1650 Greenfield Street. **An interview is required** for every application, by phone, video or in person.

## Energy and utility help

**Low Income Energy Assistance (LIEAP).** A once-a-year payment sent straight to the heating company. **The county and the state disagree on the age for the early window.** New Hanover County's page says households with **someone 65 or older**, or a disabled person getting services through the Division of Aging and Adult Services, can apply **December 1 to 31**. The state's page says **60 or older**. If a parent is 60 to 64, apply in December anyway and ask. Everyone else applies **January 1 to March 31** or until the money runs out. The county's table puts the general income limit at **$1,695 a month for one person** (130 percent of poverty) and **$1,956** (150 percent) for households with someone 65 or older or disabled. Apply on **ePASS**, by phone at **910-798-3500**, by mail or fax, or in person.

**Crisis Intervention Program (CIP).** For a **heating or cooling emergency**: no heat or cooling, or a disconnect notice, when a household member's health would be at risk. Income must be **at or below 150 percent** of poverty, and you need the utility statement showing what is owed. Apply on **ePASS**, by phone at **910-798-3500, option 4**, or in person. The county says help with **other financial crises** may be available depending on funding; ask at the same number.

**If the power bill says Duke Energy.** Duke Energy's **Share the Light Fund** helps customers with a **past-due balance, unpaid deposit or unpaid reconnection charge**. Apply through the **NCDHHS CARELINE at 800-662-7030, press 3**, through Social Services, or at **epass.nc.gov**.

**Help with any heating fuel.** Piedmont Natural Gas's **Share the Warmth** fund is handed out through county social services in counties with Piedmont residential customers. State policy says a household **does not have to heat with gas or be a Piedmont customer**, income must be **at or below 150 percent** of poverty, and the benefit is capped at **$200 per state fiscal year**. Ask Social Services at **910-798-3500** whether money is available this season.

## Medicare, Medicaid, prescriptions

**Free Medicare counseling.** North Carolina's **Seniors' Health Insurance Information Program (SHIIP)**, run by the NC Department of Insurance, gives free, unbiased Medicare counseling. In New Hanover County it runs through the **Senior Resource Center**, by appointment: call **910-798-6442** or use the SRC's online SHIIP request form. For a quick Medicare question, the SRC has a separate **Medicare information line, 910-620-6714**, which is not for booking appointments. The SRC also holds a free **"Getting Started with Medicare"** class on the **third Wednesday of every month at noon**. The statewide SHIIP line is **1-855-408-1212**. Call before any Medicare Advantage or Part D decision, especially during open enrollment, **October 15 to December 7, 2026**.

**Help paying Medicare costs and prescriptions.** The **Medicare Savings Programs** can pay Medicare premiums, and **Extra Help** lowers Part D drug costs. Apply for the Medicare Savings Programs through Social Services at **910-798-3500**, and for Extra Help through SHIIP or Social Security at **1-800-772-1213**. The SRC's **prescription assistance** specialist works one on one to find the lowest cost for costly medicines: **910-798-6422**. **NC MedAssist (1-866-331-1348)** is a free mail-order pharmacy for uninsured North Carolinians with income up to 300 percent of poverty; it says it may help **some Medicare patients on a case-by-case basis**. We are not printing income limits for the Medicare programs; ask SHIIP to check them against your parent's numbers.

**Medicaid, and a reason to apply before December 31.** Apply on ePASS, by phone at **910-798-3500, option 4**, by mail, fax or email, or in person at 1650 Greenfield Street. The **NC Medicaid Contact Center** is **888-245-0179**. NC Medicaid says that for **adults 65 and older and people with a disability**, if you **apply by December 31, 2026**, Medicaid will cover approved costs for **up to 3 months before you applied**. Starting **January 1, 2027**, that look-back shrinks to **2 months**. If a parent is heading toward a nursing home or already paying for care, that difference can be real money. The new work requirements and six-month renewals starting January 1, 2027 are aimed at adults **19 to 64**, not people 65 and older.

**Long-term care Medicaid.** Social Services takes applications for **nursing home Medicaid, CAP and PACE (Elderhaus)** at the same number, **910-798-3500, option 4**. Appointments are not required but are available.

**Staying home on Medicaid: CAP/DA.** The **Community Alternatives Program for Disabled Adults (CAP/DA)** is North Carolina's Medicaid waiver that brings in-home aide help, respite and case management so someone who would otherwise qualify for a nursing home can stay home. NC Medicaid's list of case management agencies for New Hanover County (dated May 2025) names **Novant Health, NHRMC, 910-667-8303**; **Pender Adult Services, 910-259-9119**; **Primary Health Choice, 910-865-3500**; **RHA Management Services, Inc., 888-207-7828**; and **Walkers Home Care CAPDA, 252-432-6308**. The list is more than a year old, so if a line does not answer, ask the Medicaid Contact Center.

**Special Assistance In-Home.** For an adult who **fully qualifies for Medicaid** and has an **FL-2 saying they need care in an adult care home** but wants to live at home, Special Assistance In-Home pays **a monthly cash supplement** and builds a care plan with in-home aides and supplies. It starts with a face-to-face assessment; call **910-798-3500, option 4**.

**Two things most families don't know about.**
- **Free dental care at 55.** New Hanover County's **Older Adult Dental Program**, run with Access Dental Care, Inc., offers exams, X-rays, cleanings, fillings, extractions, some root canals, **dentures and partials** to county residents **55 and older with income under 300 percent of poverty**. Having Medicare Advantage, employer dental coverage or Medicaid does not disqualify you. Apply online or pick up a paper application at the SRC or at 1650 Greenfield Street. Questions: **910-798-3774**.
- **Counseling for older adults.** The SRC's two licensed counselors, both certified in older adult mental health, see people at the center, at home or by telehealth, for depression, anxiety, grief and family stress. They take Medicare, Medicaid and several private plans, with **sliding scale fees**. **910-798-6404.** The SRC also gives out **free incontinence products**, in limited quantities, through a partnership with the Diaper Bank of North Carolina; call **910-798-6400** and ask for the social worker on coverage.

## Home repair and safety

- **WARM NC.** Does **free home repairs** for owners in Bladen, Brunswick, Columbus, Duplin, **New Hanover**, Onslow and Pender counties. You must **own the home and have lived in it at least three years**, with income **at or below 50 percent** of the county median (some funders allow up to 80 percent). Its funding includes the state's **Urgent Repair Program**. **910-399-7563.**
- **Cape Fear Habitat for Humanity Home Repair Program.** Critical repairs that affect health and safety, such as roofs, HVAC, kitchens, bathrooms and ramps, for owners in **New Hanover, Pender and Duplin** counties. The owner must have **lived in the home at least five years**, own no other residential property, be current on taxes and any mortgage, and **contribute something toward the cost** based on what the household can afford. The home must be in **flood Zone X**, and Habitat cannot currently work on homes in **Wilmington's Historic District**. If a parent inherited the house, Habitat asks for proof of who has an ownership interest. **910-762-4744, extension 100.**
- **City of Wilmington repair loans, inside city limits only.** The city's **Minor Housing Repair Forgivable Loan** covers up to **$10,000** for things like a roof, air conditioning or paint, with **no payments** and a deed of trust for **three years**. Its **Major Housing Rehabilitation Forgivable Loan** goes up to a **$90,000 base** at **0 percent interest**, with no application fee and a free inspection, for owners who have lived in the home **at least 12 months** and meet income limits. **Read the fine print on the major loan:** the city says the outstanding balance is **due on the borrower's death, after 20 years, or on sale or transfer**, which matters if the house is meant for heirs. **910-341-7836.** Addresses outside the city limits do not qualify; the city has an online lookup.
- **Weatherization and heating repair.** North Carolina's weatherization provider for New Hanover County is **Coastal Community Action**, based in Newport, **252-223-1630**. Its **Heating and Air Repair and Replacement Program (HARRP)** fixes or replaces failing heating systems for households **at or below 200 percent** of poverty, with **priority for the elderly and people with disabilities**, and renters can qualify with the landlord's written permission. Have income proof, Social Security numbers, proof of ownership and **12 months of power and fuel bills** ready.

**The hurricane registry.** If a parent is **on oxygen, has a disability that would make evacuating hard, or has other special needs**, sign them up for the county's **Access and Functional Needs Registry** through Emergency Management. A trained Retired and Senior Volunteer Program volunteer **calls 36 to 72 hours before a hurricane** to review their disaster plan and flags anyone who needs more help. The SRC will help you sign up: **910-798-6400**.

**Project Lifesaver.** The **New Hanover County Sheriff's Office** fits people who wander because of **Alzheimer's, dementia, autism or a similar condition** with a small radio transmitter bracelet, and trained deputies track it if they go missing. The application is on the Sheriff's page of the county website. Sheriff's Office main line **910-798-4200**, 3950 Juvenile Center Road, Castle Hayne.

When it reopens, WHA announces it on its website and in local media.

## Transportation

**Senior Resource Center rides.** The SRC gives **non-emergency medical rides** to New Hanover residents **60 and older who are not on Medicaid**, to appointments **inside New Hanover County**, and coordinates rides to and from the SRC and monthly day trips. There is an **application** first, rides must be **requested at least 7 days ahead**, and there is **no required fee**; contributions are welcome. **910-798-6401.**

**Wave Transit buses.** Wave's regular fare is **$2**, and it is **$1 for riders 65 and older** who show a **red, white and blue Medicare card or a valid photo ID**. People with disabilities also ride for half price with a Medicare card or a Wave Reduced Fare Card, and veterans ride half price with a free **New Hanover County Veteran ID** from the Register of Deeds. Reduced-fare multi-ride passes are half price too. **Route 107 stops at the Senior Resource Center.** Wave's main line is **910-343-0106**.

**DART, Wave's paratransit.** Lift-equipped vans for people approved through an application that a **healthcare professional** helps complete; decisions come within 21 days. DART serves addresses **within three quarters of a mile of a Wave bus route**, during the same hours the buses run, and costs **$4 each way**. Book by **the day before**, at **910-202-2053**. Drivers wait only **three minutes**, and Wave does not provide aides, though a personal care attendant rides free.

**RideMICRO, for areas the buses miss.** Wave's on-demand vans cover **Zone 2, Northeast New Hanover; Zone 3, Southern New Hanover; and Zone 4, Northern New Hanover**, and connect to the bus system. Anyone can ride, no application needed. Weekdays 6 a.m. to 8 p.m., Saturdays 8 a.m. to 6 p.m., Sundays 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. The fare is **$2** with **no senior discount**. Book at **910-861-0661** or in the app, up to a week ahead.

**Medicaid rides to appointments.** Call Social Services at **910-798-3500, option 7**. Give **3 days' notice** for trips inside the county and **5 days** for trips outside it, and out-of-county trips must be confirmed by a doctor as necessary.

## Legal help

**Senior Legal Helpline.** **Legal Aid of North Carolina's Senior Legal Helpline** gives **free civil legal help to North Carolinians 60 and older**: **1-877-579-7562**, Monday to Friday **9 to 11 a.m. and 1 to 3 p.m.**

**Legal Aid, Wilmington office.** **272 North Front Street, Suite 220, Wilmington, 910-763-6207**, Monday to Friday 9 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., serves **Bladen, Brunswick, Columbus, Duplin, New Hanover, Onslow and Pender** counties. To apply for help, Legal Aid points people to its statewide helpline, **1-866-219-5262**, Monday to Friday 9 a.m. to 3 p.m.

**Protecting an older adult.**
- **New Hanover County Adult Protective Services:** **910-798-3500** during business hours, Monday to Friday 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. **In an emergency, call 911.** After hours, the APS page says to call 911 and ask for the social worker on duty, and the Social Services main page gives the county's non-emergency number, **910-452-6120**. ## PACE

**New Hanover County has a PACE program, and its own list covers the whole county.**

PACE, the **Program of All-Inclusive Care for the Elderly**, takes over a person's whole care plan, doctors, a day center, therapy, personal care, prescriptions and transportation, so someone who needs a nursing home level of care can keep living at home. **Elderhaus PACE**, operated by EveryAge, runs its PACE center at **1380 North College Road, Wilmington, NC 28405**: **910-343-8209**, toll free **1-888-343-8209**, TTY **1-800-735-2962**.

## Frequently asked questions
**Mom is 70, lives in Ogden, and gets about $3,000 a month from Social Security and a small pension. Does she qualify for a tax break?** Likely. About $3,000 a month is about $36,000 a year, under the **$38,800** limit for 2026 and the **$39,900** limit for 2027. The **Elderly or Disabled Exclusion** takes the greater of **$25,000 or half** her home's value off the bill. She applies once, ideally in **January**, and no later than **June 1**. Call the Tax Department at **910-798-7300**.

**Dad is in a nursing home for rehab and his house in Wilmington is empty. Does he lose his exclusion?** Not for that reason. New Hanover County says the exclusion continues through a temporary absence for health, or an extended stay in a rest home or nursing home, **as long as the house is empty or only a spouse or dependent lives there**. If a relative moves in, or the house is sold, ask the Tax Department how that changes things.

**Should my father use the Circuit Breaker instead?** Go in with open eyes. It limits his tax to a percentage of income, but the rest is **deferred, not forgiven**. It is a lien, interest runs on it, and when he dies, the home is transferred, or he stops living there, **the last three years of deferred taxes come due with interest**. He must **reapply every year** and needs five years of ownership and occupancy first. If his income is under the exclusion limit, the exclusion is usually simpler, and each owner can use only one program. Ask the Tax Department to run both, and talk to the **Senior Legal Helpline (1-877-579-7562)** if the house is part of an estate plan.

**Dad's savings are running out and he may need Medicaid for care. Does timing matter?** It can. NC Medicaid says people 65 and older who **apply by December 31, 2026** can get approved costs covered for **up to 3 months before they applied**; from January 1, 2027, the look-back is **2 months**. Call Social Services at **910-798-3500, option 4**, or apply on ePASS, and talk to the Senior Legal Helpline before moving any assets.

**Mom lives alone in Carolina Beach and hurricane season scares me. What can I set up today?** Three things. Put her on the county's **Access and Functional Needs Registry** so a volunteer calls **36 to 72 hours before a storm** (the SRC helps at **910-798-6400**). Sign her house up for the **Register of Deeds Fraud Alert**. And register her for lunch at the **Katie B. Hines Senior Center** in Carolina Beach, so she is known to the SRC's social workers before a storm, not after.

**My mother has dementia and wandered last week. What should I do first?** Call the **Sheriff's Office (910-798-4200)** about **Project Lifesaver**, call **Project C.A.R.E. (910-408-6365)** for dementia-specific help and respite vouchers, and ask the SRC about its caregiver respite grant (**910-798-6402**). If she needs a nursing home level of care, ask **Elderhaus PACE (910-343-8209)** whether she qualifies to stay home.
