*Free, plain-English help for seniors and families in Mobile County, AL. Education, not advice. Verify before you rely on it.*

## Start here (works anywhere)
- 211 (dial 2-1-1), Eldercare Locator 1-800-677-1116, BenefitsCheckUp.org, Medicare 1-800-633-4227, Social Security 1-800-772-1213, VA 1-800-827-1000, Alzheimer's 24/7 Helpline 1-800-272-3900, National Elder Fraud Hotline 1-833-372-8311, Crisis Lifeline 988

**Three local front doors:** **South Alabama Regional Planning Commission (SARPC), Area Agency on Aging**, **110 Beauregard Street, Mobile, AL 36602**, mailing **P.O. Box 1665, Mobile, AL 36633**, phone **251-706-4680**, or the statewide **1-800-AGELINE (1-800-243-5463)** | **Via Health, Fitness and Enrichment Center**, **1717 Dauphin Street, Mobile, AL 36604**, **251-478-3311** | **2-1-1**, also **1-888-421-1266**, or **text your ZIP code to 898-211**, free and confidential.

**Start with the one thing that makes this county different from every other county in Alabama: PACE is real here.** Everywhere else in this state, a family asking about the Program of All-Inclusive Care for the Elderly gets told it does not operate in their area and sent to a Medicaid waiver instead. Not here. **Alabama Medicaid's own eligibility list ends with the line that settles it: an applicant must live in a designated PACE service area, currently Mobile and Baldwin counties.** The provider is **Mercy LIFE of Alabama** on Springhill Avenue. If someone 55 or older in this county is being pushed toward a nursing home, there is a real alternative here that most of the state does not have, and it is covered in full further down this page.

**The second thing to understand is the size and shape of this county.** Mobile County is not one city with suburbs. It runs from **Citronelle** and **Mt. Vernon** in the north to **Bayou La Batre** and **Grand Bay** on the water, with **Prichard**, **Saraland**, **Creola**, **Wilmer**, **Tillman's Corner** and the **City of Mobile** in between. The aging network is built for that: there are **fifteen senior nutrition centers**, not one, and several of them are forty-five minutes from downtown. But the **bus system is not built for that**. The Wave serves a defined service area, and a family in Wilmer or Bayou La Batre planning around a 60 cent bus fare is planning around something that does not reach them. Meals come to the whole county. Transit does not. Sort out which side of that line an address falls on before you build a plan around it.

**The third pattern is about the tax office, and it is the one that costs real money.** Alabama does not use a single name for the county property tax job, and Mobile County uses **Revenue Commissioner**, currently **Kim Hastie**. More importantly, this county draws a line that several others do not: the **basic homestead exemption does not require annual renewal**, but the **age, income and disability exemptions must be reclaimed on a yearly basis**, with **renewal forms mailed in October and due back by December 31**. That means a household paying **zero** property tax under Homestead Exemption 3 can lose the whole thing by not opening an envelope. If October passes and no form arrives, that is not good news. That is a phone call.

**And one practical warning before you start dialing: the tax office is closed on Wednesdays.** All Mobile County Revenue Commission offices are. The satellite locations in **Theodore, Eight Mile, Citronelle and downtown Mobile** are also **not open year-round**, and as of this writing the county's published notice states that all satellite offices are closed until October 1 and directs everyone to the **Michael Square** location. Call before you drive.

Three more numbers belong on the refrigerator. The **Alabama Adult Abuse Hotline at 1-800-458-7214** takes reports of suspected abuse, neglect or exploitation of an elderly or disabled adult, and it is run by the Adult Protective Services Division of the Alabama Department of Human Resources. The **Long-Term Care Ombudsman**, reached through the **Area Agency on Aging at 251-706-4680** or the state office at **334-242-5753**, is who you call about a nursing home or assisted living facility rather than that facility's own management. And **2-1-1**, which here is answered by **Lifelines** staff under the **United Way of Southwest Alabama** and covers Mobile, Clarke, Washington, Baldwin, Escambia, Conecuh, Choctaw and Monroe counties, will tell you which programs currently have money.

## Property tax relief (apply with the Mobile County Revenue Commissioner, 251-574-8530)
**Get the office title right first, because Alabama does not use one name for this job.** Some counties have a Tax Assessor. Some have a Property Tax Commissioner. The Alabama Department of Revenue's official county office list shows Mobile County with a **Revenue Commissioner**, currently **Kim Hastie**. The main office is at **3925 Michael Blvd, Suite G, Mobile, AL 36609**, mailing address **PO Box 1169, Mobile, AL 36633-1169**, phone **251-574-8530**, with an additional published line at **251-574-4714**. The office describes itself as centrally located with ample free parking and handicap access, and its hours are **Mondays, Tuesdays, Thursdays and Fridays from 7 a.m. to 5 p.m.** Note what is missing from that list. **Every Mobile County Revenue Commission office is closed on Wednesdays.**

**What the exemptions actually are, in the county's own words.**

- **Standard homestead.** Property owners may deduct **up to $4,000.00 in assessed value** on the **state** portion and **up to $2,000 in assessed value** on the **county** portion. This one **does not require annual renewal, however the exemption is non-transferrable**.
- **Homestead Exemption 3, the one worth the most.** The county states that **all property owners aged 65 or older may be eligible to claim Homestead Exemption 3, under which the property owner or owners pays no taxes**. To qualify you must be **65 years old or older, with proof of age required**, **own and occupy the property as your primary residence**, and your **taxable income, as indicated on your Federal Income Tax Return, cannot exceed $12,000**. Not a discount. The whole bill.
- **Homestead Exemption 2, the partial version.** Same age and residency requirements, with the **$12,000** limit measured on the **State** return, and it **covers all the State and up to $5,000 of the County taxes owed**.
- **Disability exemption.** The applicant must be **retired due to being totally and permanently disabled**. The county names the acceptable proof: a **Social Security disability letter plus a physician's affidavit**, a **military or pension letter confirming disability retirement**, or **two physician letters** stating the disability status. Its own questionnaire lists six acceptable documentation routes, including **Department of Veterans Affairs documentation at 100 percent service connected or unemployable**, **State of Alabama Retirement documents**, a **Department of Revenue certification**, and **private company disability annuity documents**.
- **Blind exemption.** The county's questionnaire also asks whether you are **blind, as defined by the Code of Alabama 1975, Section 1-1-3, vision acuity of 20/200 or less**, with supporting documents attached. People rarely think to ask about this one.

**Now the rule that separates Mobile County from its neighbors, and it is a calendar rule.** The Revenue Commission is explicit that all age, income and disability exemptions **must be reclaimed on a yearly basis**. Property owners **receive renewal forms in October** and must return them **no later than December 31**, with **current tax returns** attached where income verification is required. Read that against the paragraph above about the standard homestead, which never renews, and you can see how a family gets caught: nothing changed at the house, nobody moved, and the exemption lapsed anyway because the renewal form went in a pile. Put a reminder on the calendar for the first week of October and treat it like a bill.

**The statewide tiers behind the county's language.** Alabama sorts homestead exemptions into four lettered categories, and the county's Homestead Exemption 3 corresponds to the state's **H-3**:

- **H-1.** Taxpayers under age 65 and not disabled: **$4,000 assessed value** state and **$2,000 assessed value** county.
- **H-2.** Taxpayers **65 and older with annual adjusted gross income of less than $12,000**, or retired due to permanent and total disability regardless of age: exempt from **all of the state portion**, plus **$5,000 of assessed value** on the county portion **including school district ad valorem taxes**.
- **H-3, age 65 and older.** Taxpayers **65 and older with net taxable income of $12,000 or less on the combined taxpayer and spouse federal return**: **exempt from all ad valorem taxes**.
- **H-3, disabled.** A taxpayer who is **permanently and totally disabled** is **exempt from all ad valorem taxes**, and the state is explicit that **there is no income limitation**.
- **H-4.** Age 65 and older with **income greater than $12,000**: exempt from **all of the state portion**, plus the regular **$2,000** county homestead exemption.

The distance between H-4 and H-3 is the distance between a trim and a zero bill, and the deciding figure is **taxable income on the return**, not gross income and not the size of a Social Security deposit. Take the actual return to the office before deciding you do not qualify.

**Read the affidavit before you sign it.** Mobile County's Affidavit for Homestead Exemption carries a penalty clause most counties do not put in front of you, and it is worth quoting: knowingly and willfully giving false information to claim a homestead exemption, or to help another person claim one, is **punishable by penalty of twice the amount of any ad valorem tax which would have been due, retroactive for up to 10 years plus interest at a rate of 15 percent per year**. The form also requires you to attest that you **have no active homestead exemptions for any other property in this or any other county or state**, and the accompanying questionnaire states that **any other homestead must be canceled before this application can be processed**. If a parent kept a homestead on a house in another state after moving in with family here, deal with that first. The affidavit must be **notarized if signed out of office**, and the office also asks for an accepted form of ID for each residing owner along with proof of ownership and occupancy.

**A newer statewide protection that helps whether or not you qualify on age.** Under **Act 2024-344**, Alabama caps the annual increase in taxable assessed value for **Class II and Class III** real property, which includes owner-occupied residential property, at **7 percent**. The base year was **October 1, 2024**, for collections beginning **October 1, 2025**, and the limitation continues **through the fiscal year beginning October 1, 2027**. The cap comes off when the property changes ownership, with exceptions for family transfers and transfers because of death, when the classification changes, or when significant improvements are added.

**What we are routing rather than stating.** The Revenue Commission's exemption pages direct applicants to **visit our office to apply** and do not publish a complete online filing path for the senior exemptions, so we are not going to promise you can do this from a kitchen table. We also found no published local Mobile County senior exemption enacted on top of the state tiers. Ask about both in the same call to **251-574-8530**, and ask a third question while you have someone: which exemption code am I currently on. People are often on H-1 when they qualify for H-3.

## Energy and utility help
**LIHEAP and weatherization are the same agency here, under two different state region numbers.** ADECA, which funds both, assigns Mobile County's **LIHEAP** to **Region 11**, **Mobile Community Action, Inc.**, **461 Donald Street, Mobile, AL 36617-3327**, with a **call center at 251-206-6117** and a **main office at 251-457-5700**, covering **Mobile and Washington counties**. ADECA assigns Mobile County's **weatherization** to **Region 14**, which is **the same agency at the same address**, coordinator **Tracy Rhodes** at **251-457-5700**, except that the weatherization service area is wider and covers **Mobile, Washington and Choctaw** counties. One agency, one street address, two programs. Use the **call center number for energy bills** and ask for **weatherization by name** if what you want is insulation rather than a payment.

**How LIHEAP works here.** ADECA sets the income ceiling at **150 percent of the federal poverty level** and prioritizes households with **elderly persons, individuals with disabilities and young children**. The state is blunt that **ADECA does not schedule appointments and does not accept or process applications**, and that if you are seeking assistance, an update on an outstanding application, or want to file a complaint, **you must contact the Community Action Agency in your county**. Mobile Community Action takes appointments through an **online scheduler** as well as by phone. The applicant must be **the head of the household or the spouse of the head of the household**, and you will need **a current utility bill in the name of the head of household or spouse**, **current picture identification**, **Social Security cards for all household members**, and **income verification for the previous month for all members 18 and older**. The agency notes that **LIHEAP assistance is provided based on funding availability and is not guaranteed**, and that **all services provided by MCA are free of charge**.

**Weatherization, which is the one that lowers the bill permanently.** Mobile Community Action's program states that **eligibility is based on household income**, that recipients **must own or be purchasing their home**, and that they must **not have received prior weatherization assistance**. Critically for this page, the program **prioritizes services to the elderly, people with disabilities, and families with children**. Work can include **attic and wall insulation, air sealing, window repairs, HVAC tune-ups, appliance replacement and lighting upgrades**. Contacts published by the agency are **Tracy Rhodes, Weatherization Coordinator, 251-457-5700**, and **Brenda Nettles at 251-829-6362 or 251-829-5418**.

**Project SHARE, and yes it applies in this county.** Mobile is **Alabama Power** territory, which is not true of every Alabama city, so the utility's own assistance program is available here. **In partnership with the Salvation Army, Project SHARE helps low-income Alabamians who are 60+ or disabled pay winter energy bills**, and Alabama Power notes that **summer assistance for medical emergencies may be available if funds exist**. The program is administered statewide by the **Salvation Army of Greater Birmingham**, reachable at **205-328-2420**, but you **apply at your local Salvation Army office**. In Mobile that is the **Salvation Army Coastal Alabama Area Command, 1009 Dauphin Street, Mobile, AL**, phone **251-438-1625**, open weekdays **8:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m.**, which also provides **emergency shelter, hot meals, social services, seasonal assistance and utility bill assistance**.

**Two more Alabama Power routes worth knowing.** The utility publishes an **Energy Assistant** tool that walks you through anonymous questions to identify which programs match your situation, and it points customers with **SSI and Medicaid** to **low income assistance programs**, plus **special needs programs** offering **alternate payment dates** and accessible billing including **Braille and large-print bills**. The **Alabama Business Charitable Trust Fund**, known as the **ABC Trust**, also funds energy assistance through community action agencies, which in this county means back to **Mobile Community Action**.

## Medicare, Medicaid, prescriptions
**Free Medicare counseling, and nobody is selling anything.** **SHIP**, Alabama's State Health Insurance Assistance Program, is funded by the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services and administered through the Area Agencies on Aging. In this county that is the **South Alabama Regional Planning Commission Area Agency on Aging at 251-706-4680**. It runs a **monthly Medicare educational seminar** for people new to Medicare on the **first Tuesday of each month**, which is the single best use of two hours for anyone approaching 65 or facing an open enrollment decision. The agency also operates an **Aging and Disability Resource Center** providing **information and assistance, benefit enrollment, options counseling** and an **Alzheimer's and Dementia Helpline**.

**Medicaid, and where the office actually is.** The **Alabama Medicaid Mobile District Office** is at **2800 Dauphin Street, Suite 105, Mobile, AL 36606-2400**, phone **866-371-4078**, open **Monday through Friday, 8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.**, and it serves **Baldwin, Clarke, Escambia, Mobile and Washington** counties. District offices handle applications for elderly and disabled programs, applications for nursing home care, and applications for programs that help pay Medicare premiums. The statewide **Recipient Call Center is 1-800-362-1504**.

**The Elderly and Disabled Waiver.** The **E&D Waiver provides services to the elderly and individuals with disabilities at risk for nursing facility placement**, so that people whose needs would otherwise require care in a nursing facility can remain in the community. Depending on the plan of care, participants may receive **personal care, homemaker, respite, adult day health, companion services, and home-delivered frozen meals**, with **case managers working with clients to develop a person-centered plan of care based on medical needs**. Income must **not exceed 300 percent of the federal poverty level**, with limited assets. In Mobile County the Area Agency on Aging is the case management arm, so **251-706-4680** starts it. Note that in this county the waiver is not your only option, which is unusual. Read the PACE section below before choosing.

**Prescription help.** **SenioRx** is Alabama's prescription assistance program, and it has helped thousands of Alabamians receive **free or low-cost prescription medications from pharmaceutical companies**. Eligible clients may receive a **three-month supply**, with **refills permitted as long as the participant remains eligible**. You qualify if you are **at least 55 years of age with a chronic medical condition, have no prescription drug insurance coverage, and meet certain income guidelines**. Separately, **Alabamians with disabilities are eligible regardless of age**, including those who are Social Security disabled, who have applied for disability, or who have a doctor's declaration of disability and are in the **24-month Medicare waiting period**. Apply through the Area Agency on Aging at **251-706-4680** or the statewide **1-800-AGELINE (1-800-243-5463)**.

## Home repair and safety
**The City of Mobile's Housing Rehab Program.** The city publishes a **Housing Rehab Program** that **provides a grant of up to $15,000 for home repair for qualified homeowners**. Only **single family, owner-occupied residences** are eligible, and eligibility is **based upon the number of household members and total household income**, which **must be less than or equal to 80 percent of Area Median Income** as determined by HUD. The program is administered by the city's **Neighborhood Development** function and is traditionally funded through federal **Community Development Block Grant** dollars from HUD. The city has publicly announced an expansion of its critical home repair work with additional discretionary funding, aimed at growing the number of households served, with seniors and residents on fixed incomes named as the target. The city routes inquiries through **Mobile 311**. Because the published page does not print an application deadline or a direct program line, call **311** inside the city, or **251-208-5311**, and ask for Neighborhood Development by name. Mobile 311 is staffed **7 a.m. to 6 p.m. Monday through Friday**, with calls after 6 p.m., on weekends or on city holidays going to voicemail, and requests can also be emailed to **Mobile311@CityofMobile.gov**.

**Outside the city limits.** There is no equivalent countywide repair grant published for addresses outside the City of Mobile, so the working route for a homeowner in **Wilmer, Grand Bay, Citronelle or Bayou La Batre** is **weatherization** through **Mobile Community Action at 251-457-5700**, which prioritizes the elderly and people with disabilities and does real building-envelope work, plus **2-1-1** for church and civic repair ministries that come and go with funding.

**Free wandering protection.** The **Mobile County Sheriff's Office** runs **Project Lifesaver**, described on its own site as a **FREE Program offered by Sheriff Paul Burch** for the **caregiver of an individual with Alzheimer's, Autism, Down Syndrome or a related disorder**. The participant wears a **wristband transmitter that is picked up by special receivers with directional antennas**, and when someone wanders the office deploys a search team with specialized equipment. The Sheriff's Office reports the **average rescue takes less than 20 minutes**. Enroll through **Sgt. Kristen Barclay**, office **251-574-8049**, cell **251-331-5113**, or **projectlifesaver@mobileso.com**. Do this before there is an incident, not after.

## Transportation
**The Wave Transit System, and the senior fare is 60 cents.** The regular one-way fare is **$1.25**, with **one free transfer if using a MyWAVE card or the MyWAVE Mobile App**. **Senior Citizens: riders who are 65 or older qualify for reduced fares**, at **$0.60**, and **Disabled and Medicare Recipients** pay the same **$0.60**. If you pay cash rather than using the card or app, **you must provide proper documentation to the operator**. Passes are where the savings compound: a **monthly pass** is **$40.00** for the general public but **$20.00** for seniors, disabled riders and Medicare recipients, and a **weekly pass** is **$10.00** against **$5.00**. A **day pass** is **$3.00** and the **Moda! downtown circulator** is **$0.50**. Reduced-fare identification is issued at the **GM&O Building**, and recertification requirements vary by category.

**Door-to-door service.** The Wave's **Mobility Assistance Program**, or **MAP**, is the ADA complementary paratransit service. Eligibility is not about age: **persons with a physical or functional disability which limits their capacity to use accessible fixed route public transportation may be eligible for demand-response transportation service**. That means certification comes first, and certification takes time, so start it before you need it. Call the **MAP Department at 251-344-5656**, where **customer service personnel are available between the hours of 8 a.m. and 5 p.m., Monday through Friday**, or email the Mobility Eligibility Specialist at **mwalters@thewavetransit.com**. MAP fares and the exact service boundary are set by the Wave and are not published on the program's landing page, so confirm both on that call rather than assuming.

**If the bus does not reach you.** Most **senior nutrition centers provide transportation**, which is the practical answer for a lot of rural Mobile County. Ask the center nearest you, using the list in the Food section, and ask the Area Agency on Aging at **251-706-4680** what is running in your specific community.

## Legal help
**Start with Legal Services Alabama.** The **Mobile office** is at **104 St. Francis Street, Suite 700, Mobile, AL 36602**, **local 251-433-6560**, **toll free 1-800-403-4872**, with the statewide call center at **1-866-456-4995**, weekdays **8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.** The Mobile office serves **Baldwin, Choctaw, Clarke, Conecuh, Escambia, Mobile, Monroe and Washington** counties, and the published income test is **at or below 125 percent of the federal poverty level**. Call before you go.

**Then ask about Leaving a Legacy, which most people have never heard of.** The City of Mobile publishes it plainly: **Legal Services Alabama, through a Partnership with the City of Mobile provides free drafting, execution, and probate of Wills to low-to-moderate income property owners.** Consider what that is worth in a county where a great deal of land has passed down through families informally. A house with no will becomes heirs' property, and heirs' property is how families lose land they have held for generations, often to a forced sale none of them wanted. Free will drafting is not a small nicety. Ask about it by name through Legal Services Alabama at **251-433-6560** or through **Mobile 311**.

**Free legal counseling through the aging network.** The Area Agency on Aging offers **free legal counseling for older persons** as one of its published programs, reachable at **251-706-4680**. That is a different resource from Legal Services Alabama, with different capacity, and it is worth asking both.

**And the number for when it is not a legal question but a safety one.** Suspected abuse, neglect or exploitation of an older or disabled adult goes to the **Alabama Adult Abuse Hotline at 1-800-458-7214**, which is **accessible twenty-four hours a day, seven days a week**. Complaints about a nursing home or assisted living facility go to the **Long-Term Care Ombudsman**, through the Area Agency on Aging at **251-706-4680** or the state ombudsman line at **334-242-5753**, where **complainants do not have to give their name and all information is confidential**.

## Caregiver support
**Alabama Cares, through the Area Agency on Aging.** The agency runs **Alabama Cares Caregiver Programs** including **Caregiver Colleges**, described as a **free one day course** to **enhance your skills and knowledge of caring for a loved one**. Start at **251-706-4680**. Ask specifically about **respite**, which is the piece most caregivers need first and ask for last.

**GrandFriends adult day program at Via.** This is the practical daytime answer in this county. **GrandFriends** is a community service program of the Via Health, Fitness and Enrichment Center **designed to offer caregivers an affordable opportunity for respite while providing supervised activities within a safe, healthy and comfortable environment**, and it is **for any adult over the age of 50 in need of moderate assistance and supervision**. Staff includes **Certified Nursing Assistants, senior aides, senior companions and trained volunteers**. Published eligibility is specific and worth reading before you apply, because it screens for a particular level of need: the participant must be **50 years of age or older**, **require supervision**, be **able to eat independently with minimum assistance**, be **non-combative and will accept direction**, have **no wandering or exit-seeking behaviors**, **not be completely incontinent**, be **able to transfer independently or with minimum assistance of one person**, arrive **clean and well groomed**, be **able to take own medications correctly or not need medications while attending**, and be **able to stay out of bed 6-8 hours a day**. Admission runs through an application, a **confidential interview** the prospective participant attends, and a **half-day trial day**, with a **non-refundable $25.00 assessment fee** payable at the interview. Coordinator **Linda Bush**, **251-470-5228**, **lbush@viamobile.org**, or the Via Center main line **251-478-3311**. Because the published program sheet predates this year, confirm current daily rates and hours on that call rather than relying on a figure you found online.

**Note what the wandering rule means.** GrandFriends screens out exit-seeking behavior. If that describes your situation, the combination to ask about is the **Medicaid Elderly and Disabled Waiver** at **251-706-4680**, **Project Lifesaver** at **251-574-8049**, and **PACE**, below, which is built for exactly this level of need.

**Statewide, at any hour.** The **Alzheimer's Association 24/7 Helpline at 1-800-272-3900** is free, staffed around the clock every day of the year, and can point you to current local support groups.

**County-run volunteer programs, all in one building.** Mobile County operates a cluster of senior programs at **1150 Government Street**: the **Retired and Senior Volunteer Program (RSVP)**, Suite 209, **251-574-7787**; the **Senior Companion Program**, **251-574-3232**; and the **Foster Grandparents Program**, **251-574-6488**. These are usually filed under volunteering rather than under help, which is why families miss them. For an older adult who is isolated rather than ill, and for one who could use a companion visiting, this is the right call.

## PACE
**Mobile County has a PACE program, and almost nowhere else in Alabama does.**

**PACE**, the **Program of All-Inclusive Care for the Elderly**, bundles **primary care including doctor and nursing services, hospital care, medical specialty services, prescription drugs, dentistry, nursing home care, personal care, physical therapy, adult day care, nutritional counseling, laboratory and x-ray services, social services, and transportation** into a single program for people who need nursing-home-level care but want to remain at home.

**Who qualifies.** Alabama Medicaid publishes four criteria, and an applicant must meet **each** of them: **meet the nursing home level of care**, be **able to live safely in the community at the time of enrollment**, be **55 years of age and older**, and **live in a designated PACE service area, currently Mobile and Baldwin counties**. That last line is the one that rules out most of this state and includes this county. On payment, Alabama Medicaid notes that **PACE participants may be Medicaid-only, Medicare-only, Medicaid and Medicare (dually eligible), or private pay**, so being on Medicare without Medicaid does not automatically disqualify a family.

**Who runs it.** **Mercy LIFE of Alabama**, **2900 Springhill Avenue, Mobile, AL 36607**, phone **251-287-8420**, **TTY 711**, **referrals 855-287-8427**, open **8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. with 24/7 on-call support for participants**. Formed in 2011, it was the first PACE organization in the state of Alabama and serves residents of Mobile and Baldwin counties.

**Why this matters more than anything else on this page.** For a family here that is being told the next step is a nursing home, there are genuinely two doors, not one. The **Medicaid Elderly and Disabled Waiver** at **251-706-4680** assembles services around a person at home. **PACE** replaces the whole arrangement with one organization that becomes responsible for the care, the doctor, the drugs, the day program and the ride there. They are not the same thing and a family cannot be in both. Call **Mercy LIFE at 251-287-8420** and the **Area Agency on Aging at 251-706-4680**, ask each what enrollment would actually look like, and compare the two before deciding.

## Frequently asked questions
**Dad is 78, has owned the same house off Old Shell Road since 1979, and lives on Social Security and a small pension. What is he actually entitled to?** Possibly a zero property tax bill. Mobile County's own exemption guidance says **all property owners aged 65 or older may be eligible to claim Homestead Exemption 3, under which the property owner pays no taxes**, provided he is **65 or older with proof of age**, **owns and occupies the property as his primary residence**, and his **taxable income as indicated on his Federal Income Tax Return does not exceed $12,000**. The deciding number is taxable income on the return, not gross and not the Social Security deposit, so take the actual return to the **Revenue Commissioner at 3925 Michael Blvd, Suite G, 251-574-8530**. Go on a Monday, Tuesday, Thursday or Friday. **The office is closed Wednesdays.**

**Mom had the senior exemption and this year a tax bill showed up anyway. What happened?** Almost certainly a renewal that lapsed. In Mobile County the **standard homestead exemption does not require annual renewal**, but the **age, income and disability exemptions must be reclaimed on a yearly basis**. **Renewal forms go out in October** and must come back **no later than December 31**, with **current tax returns** attached where income has to be verified. Nothing has to change at the house for the exemption to fall off; the envelope just has to go unopened. Call **251-574-8530**, ask what exemption code she is on now, ask whether a renewal was mailed and to what address, and get the mailing address corrected while you have someone on the phone.

**We are looking at a nursing home for my mother and cannot afford it. Is there an alternative?** In this county, two of them, which is not true in most of Alabama. Ask about **PACE** through **Mercy LIFE of Alabama at 251-287-8420**, which requires that she **meet the nursing home level of care**, be **able to live safely in the community at the time of enrollment**, be **55 or older**, and **live in Mobile or Baldwin county**, and which then covers primary care, hospital care, specialists, prescriptions, dentistry, nursing home care, personal care, physical therapy, adult day care, nutrition, labs, social services and transportation as one program. Also ask about the **Medicaid Elderly and Disabled Waiver** through the Area Agency on Aging at **251-706-4680**, which builds **personal care, homemaker, respite, adult day health, companion services and home-delivered frozen meals** around her at home, with income **not exceeding 300 percent of the federal poverty level**. Then call the **Alabama Medicaid Mobile District Office at 866-371-4078** about paperwork. Compare the two. They are different models, not different names for the same thing.

**Mom's power bill is past due and she is 81 on a fixed income. Who actually helps?** Three calls, in this order. First **251-206-6117**, the **LIHEAP call center** at Mobile Community Action, and have ready a **current utility bill in her name**, **photo ID**, **Social Security cards for the household**, and **last month's income for everyone 18 and older**. Second, because she is over 60 and Mobile is **Alabama Power** territory, ask about **Project SHARE** at the **Salvation Army Coastal Alabama Area Command, 1009 Dauphin Street, 251-438-1625**. Third, while you have Mobile Community Action on the phone, ask about **weatherization by name** at **251-457-5700**, because that is the one that lowers next winter's bill instead of this month's. And **2-1-1** will tell you who currently has funds.

**My father lives out in Wilmer and stopped driving. How does he eat and how does he get to a doctor?** Meals are the easy part. The **Wilmer Center at 13251 Avenue B, 251-645-9209** does **congregate and homebound meals** for anyone **60 and older on a donation only basis**, and **most centers provide transportation**. Transit is the harder part, because the Wave's fixed routes and its **MAP** paratransit are built around the urban service area, not the far west end of the county. So the practical sequence is: call the Wilmer center first and ask what transportation it runs, then call the **Area Agency on Aging at 251-706-4680** and ask what else operates in that community. If he is at nursing-home level of need, **PACE at 251-287-8420** includes transportation as part of the program.

**My husband has dementia and I have not slept properly in months. What is in Mobile County?** Four calls. **251-706-4680** for **Alabama Cares** and its **Caregiver Colleges**, and ask directly about **respite** rather than waiting to be offered it. **251-470-5228** for **GrandFriends** adult day at Via, but read the eligibility first, because it screens out **wandering or exit-seeking behaviors** and requires a **confidential interview** and a **half-day trial** with a **$25.00 assessment fee**. **251-574-8049** or **projectlifesaver@mobileso.com** for a **free Project Lifesaver bracelet** from the Sheriff's Office, before there is an incident. And **1-800-272-3900**, the Alzheimer's Association's free 24/7 helpline, at whatever hour you are actually awake. If the wandering is the central problem, that combination points toward **PACE** or the **Medicaid waiver** rather than toward adult day care.

**Mom is 74 and I think she qualifies for food stamps, but she will not sit through an interview.** Ask for the **Alabama Elderly Simplified Application Project** by name. The state's current form, **revised October 2025**, is for applicants where **everyone in the SNAP household is age 65 or older**, or where all household members are 65 or older and **purchase and prepare food separately from the other people in the home**, and where **no SNAP household member receives earnings from work**. At 74 with no wages, she likely fits. The form says you **may file this application by completing at least your name, address, and signing the form**. Take it to **Mobile County DHR Food Assistance, 2970 Cottage Hill Road, Suite 150, 251-450-6955**, or call **1-800-438-2958** for help filling it out. While you are at it, ask **Feeding the Gulf Coast at (888) 704-3663** about the **Senior Box**, which is a separate monthly USDA food package for people **60 or older at or below 130 percent of the federal poverty level**.

**My parents have owned family land here for fifty years and there is no will. Is there anything free?** Yes, and it is unusual. The City of Mobile publishes **Leaving a Legacy**, under which **Legal Services Alabama, through a Partnership with the City of Mobile provides free drafting, execution, and probate of Wills to low-to-moderate income property owners**. This is the most valuable thing on this page for a family in that position. Land that passes with no will becomes heirs' property, split among more and more relatives each generation until any one of them can force a sale of the whole thing. Getting a will drafted while your parents are able to sign one prevents a problem that is nearly impossible to fix afterward. Call **Legal Services Alabama at 251-433-6560**, or the statewide intake at **1-866-456-4995**, and ask about Leaving a Legacy specifically.

**Is there anything for a Mobile County veteran or a veteran's widow?** Yes, and it is free. The **Mobile County Veterans Affairs office** is at **1150 Government Street, Mobile, AL 36604**, phone **251-574-8578**. County veterans service offices help veterans, dependents and survivors file and process claims for benefits from both the federal VA and the state of Alabama, at no charge. There is also a **Mobile Vet Center** operated by the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs, and the national **VA benefits line is 1-800-827-1000**. If a widow is living on very little and her husband served, the claim worth asking about by name is **survivor benefits and Aid and Attendance**.

**Is there anything in this county that is just a good place to go?** Yes. The **City of Mobile Parks and Recreation Department** runs senior programming for adults **aged 55 and older** at the **Connie Hudson Senior Center, 3201 Hillcrest Road**, the **Parkway Senior Center, 1600 Boykin Boulevard**, the **Tricksey Senior Center, 3055 Bank Avenue**, and the **Hillsdale** and **Thomas Sullivan** community centers, with published programs including **Bridging Generations Through Art** and a **Brain Boost Cafe**. The department is at **48 North Sage Avenue, Mobile, AL 36607**, **MPRD@cityofmobile.org**. Separately, the **Via Health, Fitness and Enrichment Center at 1717 Dauphin Street, 251-478-3311** offers fitness, continuing education and social programming along with the **Dauphin Street Cafe**, open to the public for lunch **Monday through Friday, 11:00 a.m. to 1:00 p.m.** If a parent is more isolated than unwell, one of these is often the better first call than anything else on this page.
