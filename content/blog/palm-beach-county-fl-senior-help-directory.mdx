*Free, plain-English help for seniors and families in Palm Beach County, FL. Education, not advice. Verify before you rely on it.*

## Start here (works anywhere)
- 211 (dial 2-1-1), Eldercare Locator 1-800-677-1116, BenefitsCheckUp.org, Medicare 1-800-633-4227, Social Security 1-800-772-1213, VA 1-800-827-1000, Alzheimer's 24/7 Helpline 1-800-272-3900, National Elder Fraud Hotline 1-833-372-8311, Crisis Lifeline 988

**Three local front doors:** **211 Palm Beach and Treasure Coast**, answered 24 hours a day, every day (dial 2-1-1, call **561-383-1112**, email Help@211pbtc.org, or chat on their site) | the **Elder Helpline at 1-866-684-5885**, which reaches the **Area Agency on Aging of Palm Beach/Treasure Coast**, the Area Agency on Aging and Aging and Disability Resource Center for Palm Beach, Martin, Okeechobee, St. Lucie, and Indian River counties, Monday through Friday 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., at 701 Northpoint Parkway, Suite 115, West Palm Beach, FL 33407, with a direct line of **561-684-5885** | the **Palm Beach County Division of Senior Services at 561-355-4746**, at 810 Datura Street, Suite 300, West Palm Beach, FL 33401

That third door deserves a paragraph, because a lot of counties do not have one. Palm Beach County government runs its own **Division of Senior Services**, separate from the Area Agency on Aging, and it is the designated lead agency for older adult programs in the county. It employs its own case managers, who hold bachelor's or master's degrees and work in Creole, Spanish, and English, and they do home visits to figure out what a person actually needs rather than handing you a list. The division runs congregate meal sites, home delivered meals, adult day care, senior centers, and the elder energy program, and it operates from four locations: the administration office at **810 Datura Street** in West Palm Beach (**561-355-4746**), the **Mid County Senior Center at 3680 Lake Worth Road** in Lake Worth (**561-357-7100**), the **North County Senior Center at 5217 Northlake Blvd.** in Palm Beach Gardens (**561-694-5435**), and the **West County Senior Center at 2916 State Road 15** in Belle Glade (**561-996-4808**). The senior centers require no membership and charge no fee to walk in, hot lunch is served daily, and they run everything from chair exercise to free AARP tax preparation.

One local program worth knowing by name: **Sunshine Calls**. It is a free telephone reassurance program run through 211 that calls to check on seniors 60 and older, homebound or disabled people of any age, and isolated family caregivers 18 and older. Calls go out **seven days a week between 7:30 a.m. and 5 p.m.**, it covers Palm Beach plus the four Treasure Coast counties, and it costs nothing. Sign up by dialing 2-1-1 and asking for Sunshine Calls, or emailing sunshine@211pbtc.org. If you live out of state and worry about a parent who lives alone down here, this is the cheapest peace of mind available.

One more number to write down. The **Florida Abuse Hotline** at **1-800-962-2873** takes reports of known or suspected abuse, neglect, or exploitation of a vulnerable adult, 24 hours a day.

## Property tax relief (apply with the Palm Beach County Property Appraiser, 561-355-2866, pbcpao.gov)
Florida seniors apply for property tax relief through the county Property Appraiser, not the Tax Collector. In Palm Beach County that office is at **301 N. Olive Ave., 1st Floor, West Palm Beach, FL 33401**, phone **561-355-2866**, email myexemption@pbcpao.gov.

Now here is the local story, and Palm Beach County has the most complicated version of it in Florida. The senior exemptions are a **local option**. Every county and every city has to pass its own ordinance, the exemption only reduces the millage of the government that passed it, and it never touches school district taxes. Most Florida counties end up with a tidy answer. Palm Beach County has **39 incorporated municipalities**, and they did not agree on much.

**Standard Homestead Exemption (statewide, any qualifying primary residence):** up to **$50,000** off the assessed value of a permanent primary residence you own and occupy as of January 1. It comes in two pieces, the first $25,000 and an additional amount that phases in on assessed value above $50,000. Applications are due by **March 1**. After the first year, the annual increase in assessed value is capped, and that cap is **Save Our Homes**, which in a county where values have run hard is often worth more over time than any exemption on this page.

**Limited Income Senior Citizen Exemption (65+):** an additional exemption on top of the standard homestead for owners who meet all of these:
- At least one owner is **65 or older**.
- The owner already has or qualifies for the regular homestead exemption.
- **Household income does not exceed $38,686** for the 2026 exemption, based on the prior year's income. The Property Appraiser notes this figure does not include tax-exempt bond interest or non-taxable Social Security income. The limit is set annually by the Florida Department of Revenue and was $37,694 for 2025.

**Here is what each government in Palm Beach County actually adopted**, per the Property Appraiser's own list:

- **$50,000:** Haverhill, Juno Beach, Jupiter, Jupiter Inlet Colony, Loxahatchee Groves, Riviera Beach, Wellington
- **$25,000:** **Palm Beach County**, Boynton Beach, Delray Beach, Greenacres, Lake Worth Beach, Lantana, North Palm Beach, Palm Beach Gardens, Palm Springs, South Palm Beach, Tequesta, West Palm Beach
- **$10,000:** Lake Park
- **$5,000:** Royal Palm Beach

Count them and you get 20 municipalities plus the county. That leaves **19 of the county's 39 municipalities that have not adopted it at all**, and the list includes some big names: **Boca Raton**, the **Town of Palm Beach**, **Highland Beach**, **Atlantis**, **Lake Clarke Shores**, **Ocean Ridge**, **Manalapan**, **Gulf Stream**, **Hypoluxo**, **Mangonia Park**, **Palm Beach Shores**, **Briny Breezes**, **Cloud Lake**, **Glen Ridge**, **Golf**, **Westlake**, and the three Glades cities, **Belle Glade**, **Pahokee**, and **South Bay**.

Read that list twice if your parents live in one of those places. **Boca Raton is the one that surprises people most**, because it is the second largest city in the county and it skews old. A qualifying 78-year-old in Boca Raton does not get a Boca Raton senior exemption, because there is no Boca Raton ordinance to get. What that person **does** still get is the standard homestead exemption, the Save Our Homes cap, and the **$25,000 Palm Beach County portion**, because the county adopted separately from the cities. That is real money and it is worth applying for. The same is true in Belle Glade, Pahokee, and South Bay, which is worth saying plainly because those are the lowest-income communities in the county and they get only the county piece.

The flip side: a qualifying senior in **Jupiter, Wellington, or Riviera Beach** gets $50,000 off that city's portion **plus** $25,000 off the county portion. Same income, same age, very different bill.

**Super Senior Citizen Exemption:** this is the one worth a phone call, and in Palm Beach County only **two cities offer it, Boynton Beach and Delray Beach**. You must already meet the limited income senior qualifications, plus:
- Have **maintained ownership and permanent residence at the property for at least 25 years**.
- Have a **market value of less than $250,000** at the time of application.

Under Florida law this exemption equals the **assessed value** of the property rather than a flat dollar amount, which is why it is so much larger than the regular senior exemption. For a long-time Boynton Beach or Delray Beach homeowner still under the $250,000 value line, this can remove the city's portion of the bill. If a parent has been in the same Boynton or Delray house since 2001 or earlier, make the call.

**The deadline and the paperwork.** Apply by **March 1** using form **DR-501SC**, and expect to provide a copy of the federal 1040 or the Social Security 1099. First-time applicants also need proof of age. Do not let missing paperwork stop you from filing by the deadline; call and ask what they will accept later.

**Other exemptions worth asking about in the same call:** $5,000 widow or widower (a death certificate is required); $5,000 for civilian total and permanent disability or legal blindness, documented on form DR-416B or by a VA or Social Security letter; a **full exemption for total and permanent disability with an income test** that is separate from the senior income test, so ask for the current year's figure; $5,000 for a veteran with a service-connected disability rated 10 percent or higher; a full exemption for a veteran with a service-connected total and permanent disability; a **combat-related disability discount for veterans 65 and older**, where the discount equals the disability percentage; a full exemption for a first responder totally and permanently disabled in the line of duty; and a full exemption for the surviving spouse of a veteran or first responder who died in the line of duty. Because several of these income limits change every year, ask the Property Appraiser for the current figure rather than trusting a number you read online.

**A separate office handles the bill.** The **Constitutional Tax Collector, Serving Palm Beach County** (**561-355-2264**) sends and collects the bill and cannot grant exemptions. Two things there help people on fixed incomes. Paying early earns a discount: **4 percent in November, 3 percent in December, 2 percent in January, and 1 percent in February**. And the **Installment Payment Plan** breaks the bill into four payments if your estimated taxes total more than $100. You enroll between **November 1 and April 30** for the following year. The first quarter is due in June with a **6 percent discount** (pay by July 31 with no discount or you are dropped from the plan), the second quarter is due September 30 at **4.5 percent**, the third is due December 31 at **3 percent**, and the balance is due March 31.

**The one move:** call **561-355-2866** before March 1, give them the exact address so they can confirm which municipality you are actually in and what that municipality adopted, and if the home has been the permanent residence for 25 years or more and sits in Boynton Beach or Delray Beach, ask them specifically about the **Super Senior** exemption.

## Food
Palm Beach County has three separate meal doors and they cover different parts of a county that runs about 45 miles north to south. Calling the wrong one costs you a week.

- **Palm Beach County Division of Senior Services** (**561-355-4746**) runs the county's Older Americans Act nutrition program: **congregate meals** served Monday through Friday at senior meal sites, and **home delivered meals** for older adults who cannot prepare their own food, cannot shop, and have no one to shop or cook for them. Case managers do an in-home assessment. This is the countywide door and the one to start with if you are not sure.
- **Meals on Wheels of the Palm Beaches** (**561-802-6979, extension 7** for eligibility) serves people **65 and older** who are homebound, defined as spending at least 80 percent of their time at home and being there for weekday deliveries. There is **no income requirement**, but there is a cost: meals run on a sliding scale from **$1.75 to $7.00 each**, which works out to roughly $35 to $140 a month. The service area is specific, running **from Lantana Road north through Jupiter**, plus some senior communities in Delray Beach, so confirm your address when you call. Office hours are Monday through Thursday 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. and Friday 9 a.m. to 3 p.m. Service typically starts within two weeks of completed paperwork.
- **The Volen Center** (**561-395-8920**), formerly the Mae Volen Senior Center, at 1515 West Palmetto Park Road in Boca Raton, is the **south county** agency. It runs home delivered meals and senior dining sites, plus adult day care at 850 North Congress Avenue in Delray Beach (**561-265-3667**), companion care, respite, homemaking, and personal care, coordinated through the Area Agency on Aging.
- **Palm Beach County Food Bank**, at **701 Boutwell Road, Suites A-2, Lake Worth, FL 33461**, phone **561-670-2518**, distributes through more than 200 partner pantries, churches, and mobile food pantries across the county. Use the Find Food tool at pbcfoodbank.org. It also runs two programs built specifically for older adults: **Produce for Seniors**, which delivers biweekly boxes of fresh produce with recipes, and **Groceries for Seniors**, which delivers shelf-stable food and recipe cards to low-income seniors every two weeks through partner organizations. Ask for them by name.
- **SNAP** (food stamps) is based on income, not age. Apply any time through Florida's MyACCESS portal at **myaccess.myflfamilies.com**, or call the state customer call center at **850-300-4323**.

## Energy and utility help
Palm Beach County has two separate energy doors, and which one you use depends on age. Most families call the wrong one first.

- **EHEAP is the elder-only program, and in Palm Beach County the county itself runs it.** That is unusual and it is good news, because it means you call the same Division of Senior Services that handles meals and case management. The **Emergency Home Energy Assistance for the Elderly Program** requires at least one person **60 or older** in the household, low income, and a **verifiable home energy crisis**. Households get **one summer cooling benefit between April 1 and September 30 and one winter heating benefit between October 1 and March 31, not to exceed $400 each**. You cannot use it if you live in government subsidized housing where heating and cooling are entirely included in the rent. Apply at any of the four offices: administration at **561-355-4746**, **Mid County Senior Center 561-357-7100**, **North County Senior Center 561-694-5435**, or **West County Senior Center 561-996-4808**. The county notes that applications are processed while funds remain available, so call before you drive over.
- **LIHEAP is the general program**, run by the county's **Community Action** program. Household income must be at or below **60 percent of State Median Income or 150 percent of the federal poverty guidelines**, you must live in Palm Beach County, and the utility bill must be in a household member's name. The published benefit limits are **up to $1,000 for a Crisis benefit** (winter and summer) and **up to $700 for regular Home Energy** help. Apply online at **floridaliheap.com**, or call the call center at **1-833-CSD-WILL, which is 1-833-273-9455**, or schedule an in-person appointment at **561-355-4792**. Community Action service centers are at 810 Datura Street in West Palm Beach, 1440 Martin Luther King Blvd in Riviera Beach, 1699 Wingfield Street in Lake Worth, 225 South Congress Avenue in Delray Beach, and 38754 State Road 80 in Belle Glade. The county says particular effort is made to serve the elderly and people with special needs.
- **Do not assume the income is too high.** Sixty percent of State Median Income is a higher bar than most retirees expect, and the LIHEAP and EHEAP tests are different from the property tax income test. Being over one does not mean you are over the other.
- For a past-due bill right now, dial **211**. They keep the current list of church ministries and local funds that still have utility money this month.

## Medicare, Medicaid, prescriptions
- Florida's SHIP (free Medicare counseling) is **SHINE, Serving Health Insurance Needs of Elders**, a volunteer program of the Florida Department of Elder Affairs delivered locally by the **Area Agency on Aging of Palm Beach/Treasure Coast**. Counseling is **free, unbiased, and confidential**, and counselors are trained volunteers who do not sell insurance. They handle Medicare eligibility and enrollment, plan choices, appeals, Medigap policies, long-term care insurance, prescription drug plans, fraud, and the programs that lower Medicare costs. Call **1-866-684-5885** or **561-684-5885**, Monday through Friday 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Start here before any Medicare decision, and especially before switching plans during open enrollment.
- **Medicaid** in Florida, including long-term care coverage and the programs that help pay Medicare costs, is applied for through the Department of Children and Families at **myaccess.myflfamilies.com** or **850-300-4323**.
- **Medicare Savings Programs** (help paying Part A and B premiums) and **Extra Help**, also called the Low-Income Subsidy, which lowers Part D drug costs, are screened through SHINE (1-866-684-5885), Social Security (1-800-772-1213), and MyACCESS. Florida does not run a general statewide senior drug program, so ask SHINE about Extra Help and use BenefitsCheckUp.org to find manufacturer and nonprofit patient-assistance programs for specific medications.

## Home repair and safety
This is the section where Palm Beach County requires the most patience, because the money moves in narrow windows rather than sitting open all year.

- **Palm Beach County Department of Housing and Economic Development**, at **100 Australian Avenue, 5th Floor, West Palm Beach, FL 33406**, phone **561-233-3600**, runs the county's owner-occupied repair and rehabilitation programs. Its **Emergency Repair Program** most recently opened for applications on **June 8, 2026 and closed June 26, 2026**. That is a nineteen-day window for the whole year. If you are reading this outside that window, the move is to call **561-233-3600** and ask when the next application period opens and how you will be notified, then set a reminder. Do not assume a closed page means the program is gone.
- The county also runs **owner-occupied housing rehabilitation** for substantial repairs that correct code violations and conditions threatening the health and safety of the people living there, generally funded as a deferred loan secured by a recorded mortgage and forgiven after a term of years. Award amounts, income limits, and which parts of the county qualify change with the funding source, and several of the county's program pages were offline when this page was checked, so get the current terms from **561-233-3600** or hedcomments1@pbcgov.org rather than from a summary.
- **Check whether the address is inside a city.** Some county housing programs are limited to the unincorporated area, and Palm Beach County has 39 municipalities, so a great many addresses that read as "West Palm Beach" or "Boca Raton" in the mail are one thing for postal purposes and another for program eligibility. Ask the county which pot your address falls under, and ask your city whether it runs its own housing rehabilitation program, because several of the larger ones do.
- **For a cooling emergency specifically**, do not wait on a rehabilitation program. In South Florida a dead air conditioner is a health emergency. Call the Division of Senior Services about **EHEAP** at **561-355-4746** and Community Action about **LIHEAP** at **1-833-273-9455** the same day.
- **211** can find a faith-based or volunteer group that does small repairs and safety work such as grab bars and ramps when no government program is open.

## Rent, housing, emergency cash
- **211 Palm Beach and Treasure Coast** is the fastest route, answered around the clock. Dial 2-1-1 or call **561-383-1112**.
- **County rental and relocation assistance runs on a monthly capacity limit, not a permanent waitlist.** The county's own portal states that intake for rental and relocation assistance is closed when it has reached the maximum capacity for the current month, and that the next opening is announced on the site. That means checking back at the start of a month is a real strategy rather than a brush-off. Apply through the county's service eligibility portal, or call **1-833-CSD-WILL, which is 1-833-273-9455**, 8 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.
- **Utility assistance is a separate line and it was open** when this page was checked, so if the rent door is closed the utility door may not be. Same phone number, or floridaliheap.com.
- **211 also runs Elder Crisis Outreach**, which exists for vulnerable seniors who do not have the physical strength, money, or family support to work through a crisis alone. If a parent is overwhelmed rather than merely short of one payment, say that when you call.
- For Medicaid, SNAP, and Temporary Cash Assistance, apply through MyACCESS at **myaccess.myflfamilies.com** or **850-300-4323**.

## Transportation
**The rules changed on January 18, 2026, so ignore older advice.** On April 8, 2025 the Palm Beach County Board of County Commissioners approved new paratransit guidelines, and as of January 18, 2026 Palm Tran Connection is split into two programs:

- **Connection ADA** covers trips where both the pick-up and drop-off are within three-quarters of a mile of Palm Tran's fixed-route network.
- **Connection Plus** covers trips where the pick-up or drop-off falls outside that three-quarter-mile band. In a county with as much low-density western development as this one, that distinction decides a lot of trips.

You must apply and be approved before riding either. Call **561-649-9838, option 7**, or toll free **1-877-870-9849**, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. any day of the week. After approval you can book online through PassWeb. Palm Tran lists the Connection single ride fare at **$4.00**, payable through the Connection app or with Tickets to Ride bought in person at 50 S. Military Trail, Room 122, Palm Springs. Because the two-program structure is new, confirm the fare that applies to your specific trip type when you are approved.

**If your parent can still ride a regular bus, the half fare is the better deal and it takes one registration.** Palm Tran's reduced fare covers **seniors 65 and over**, Medicare card holders, people with disabilities, students 21 and under, and veterans with a service-connected VA card. Regular fares are $2 for a single trip, $5 for an unlimited one-day pass, and $70 for an unlimited 31-day pass. **Reduced fares are $1 single, $3.50 day pass, and $55 for the 31-day pass.** Seniors register with a valid driver license or Florida ID card by emailing PTdiscount@pbcgov.org or visiting the Palm Tran Central County Facility at 1200 S. Congress Ave., Palm Springs, FL 33406. Two cautions: there is no age at which seniors ride entirely free here, and a Medicaid card does not qualify you for the discount.

- **In the south county**, The Volen Center runs its own **Community Coach** group rides in wheelchair-equipped vehicles for a nominal fee, serving from **Boynton Beach south to north Broward**, going to medical appointments, food stores, and social and religious activities. Rides are Monday through Friday only and reservations are required. Call **561-395-8920, option 5**.
- Medicaid long-term care plans cover **transportation** as a plan benefit, so if a parent is enrolled in Statewide Medicaid Managed Care Long-Term Care, ask the plan before paying out of pocket.
- General Palm Tran customer service is **561-841-4200**.

## Legal help
- **The Elder Law Project at the Legal Aid Society of Palm Beach County is the single most underused resource on this page, because it has no income test.** Services are free to any resident **60 or older** of Palm Beach, Martin, St. Lucie, Okeechobee, or Indian River County **regardless of income**, and caregivers of seniors and grandparents or relatives raising minor children also qualify. It covers abuse, neglect, or exploitation by a family member or caregiver, domestic violence protective orders, landlord and tenant disputes, public and subsidized housing problems, eviction defense, SSI and Social Security, Medicaid including Qualified Income Trusts, Medicare, managed care and long-term care, debt collection and garnishment defense, **mortgage and reverse mortgage foreclosure defense**, bankruptcy for debt relief or home preservation, and scams and fraud. Call **561-655-8944** or toll free **800-403-9353**. The office is at 423 Fern Street, Suite 200, West Palm Beach, FL 33401, open Monday through Thursday 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. and Friday 9 a.m. to 4 p.m.
- That reverse mortgage line matters more than it looks. If a parent took a reverse mortgage and is now facing foreclosure over unpaid taxes or insurance, that is a specific, fixable legal problem and there is a free lawyer in this county for it.
- **Florida's Senior Legal Helpline** gives free legal advice and brief services by phone to Florida residents **60 and older** on civil matters including housing, family matters, government benefits, and contract and consumer problems. Call **1-888-895-7873**, Monday through Friday 9 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. It is operated by Bay Area Legal Services and serves seniors statewide, so it is a second shot if the local office is at capacity. Interpreters are free.
- To report suspected abuse, neglect, or exploitation of a vulnerable adult, call the **Florida Abuse Hotline at 1-800-962-2873**, 24 hours a day.

## Caregiver support
- The Area Agency on Aging runs the Older Americans Act **caregiver support** program along with respite, adult day care, chore, companionship, counseling, emergency alert response, homemaker, personal care, and specialized medical equipment. Start at the **Elder Helpline, 1-866-684-5885**.
- **The Alzheimer's Disease Initiative** is the one to ask for by name if there is a dementia diagnosis. Through the Area Agency on Aging it serves people **18 and older with a diagnosis of Alzheimer's disease or a related disorder**, and their caregivers 18 and older.
- **Community Care for the Elderly (CCE)** serves functionally impaired adults **60 and older** to preserve independence and prevent or delay costlier institutional care. **Home Care for the Elderly (HCE)** pays a subsidy to a caregiver 18 or older living in the home with a care recipient 60 or older, for up to three elders. Both start at **1-866-684-5885**.
- **Alzheimer's Community Care** is the Palm Beach County dementia specialist and it is worth calling directly. ## Staying at home instead of a nursing home
- Florida Medicaid pays for care at home through the **Statewide Medicaid Managed Care Long-Term Care** program, for people **65 and older who are Medicaid eligible**, or **18 and older and Medicaid eligible by reason of a disability**, who are also determined by the state's **CARES** unit at the Department of Elder Affairs to be at a **nursing home level of care**. Plans must cover, at minimum, adult companion care, adult day care, assisted living facility services, attendant care, caregiver training, case management, home accessibility adaptation, home delivered meals, homemaker, hospice, nursing care, medical equipment and supplies, nursing facility care, occupational, physical, respiratory and speech therapy, personal care, emergency response systems, respite care, and transportation. Start the screening through the local Aging and Disability Resource Center, which here is the **Area Agency on Aging of Palm Beach/Treasure Coast, 1-866-684-5885**. Ask about the waitlist when you call, and start early.
- **PACE (the Program of All-Inclusive Care for the Elderly)** wraps all medical care, therapy, an adult day center, home care, and prescriptions into one program for people **55 and older** who meet a nursing home level of care but can live at home with help. Palm Beach County has **one operational PACE organization, MorseLife PACE**, also branded as Palm Beach PACE, and it runs **three centers**:
  - **West Palm Beach**, 4847 David S. Mack Drive, West Palm Beach, FL 33417, **561-529-9829**
  - **Delray Beach**, 2475 West Atlantic Avenue, Delray Beach, FL 33445, **561-325-6439**
  - **Greenacres**, 4550 Jog Road, Greenacres, FL 33467, **561-332-1762**

  All three are open Monday through Friday 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. To enroll you must be 55 or older, live in a PACE center's service area, meet Florida's medical eligibility rules including a Department of Elder Affairs determination that you qualify for nursing home care, be able to live safely in the community, and be eligible for Medicaid and Medicare or Medicaid only.

  Florida's Agency for Health Care Administration monthly PACE report for **February 2026** lists **MorseLife PACE as the only operational PACE organization present in Palm Beach County**, with **1,006 enrollees** that month and 1,121 unique individuals served in the fiscal year to that point, against 1,206 funded slots. The same report lists a second organization, **Kinship Palm Beach PACE, as undergoing review**. Its application was received on June 30, 2025 and was still pending initial state approval, which means **it is not operating and should not be counted on**. If you find it advertised somewhere, that is a program in the application process, not an option available to your parent today.
- The county's own case management, home delivered meals, adult day care, and in-home services through the **Division of Senior Services at 561-355-4746** also help an older adult stay home longer, and none of them require a Medicaid determination to start.

## Frequently asked questions
**Mom is 74 in Wellington on a fixed income. Can she lower her property taxes?** Yes, and Wellington is one of the better places in the county to be for this. Wellington adopted the Limited Income Senior Exemption at the **top amount of $50,000**, and Palm Beach County adopted $25,000, so both portions of her bill can come down. Her prior-year household income has to have been at or below **$38,686**, and she needs to already have or qualify for the regular homestead exemption. File form **DR-501SC** with the Property Appraiser by **March 1** and expect to show a 1040 or a Social Security 1099. Call **561-355-2866** with the exact address.

**My parents live in Boca Raton and my aunt lives in Jupiter. Do they get the same break?** No, and this is the biggest gap in Palm Beach County. **Jupiter adopted the senior exemption at $50,000. Boca Raton does not appear on the Property Appraiser's list of adopting authorities at all.** Your parents in Boca Raton still get the standard homestead exemption, the Save Our Homes cap, and the **$25,000 Palm Beach County portion**, because the county passed its own ordinance, but there is no Boca Raton city-portion senior exemption to apply for. Your aunt in Jupiter gets both the county and the town portions. Both households should still call 561-355-2866, because the county piece alone is worth having.

**Dad has been in the same Delray Beach house since 1998 and it is worth about $230,000. Is there anything better than the regular senior exemption?** Very likely yes. Delray Beach is one of only two Palm Beach County cities, along with Boynton Beach, that adopted the **Super Senior Citizen Exemption**. He needs to meet the limited income senior qualifications, have owned and lived there at least **25 years**, and have a market value **under $250,000** at the time of application. He clears the years and looks likely on value. Because this exemption equals the assessed value rather than a flat amount, it can eliminate the Delray Beach portion of his bill. Call **561-355-2866** and ask them to run him for the Super Senior version, not just the regular one.

**Dad is homebound in Boca Raton and can't cook. How do we get him meals?** Call **The Volen Center at 561-395-8920**, which is the south county agency, at 1515 West Palmetto Park Road in Boca Raton. Also call the **Palm Beach County Division of Senior Services at 561-355-4746**, which runs the county's home delivered meals program and will do a case management assessment. Do not start with Meals on Wheels of the Palm Beaches for a Boca address: their delivery area runs from **Lantana Road north through Jupiter** plus some Delray Beach senior communities, so Boca Raton is outside it. If you are not sure which agency covers an address, the **Elder Helpline at 1-866-684-5885** will route you in one call.

**The air conditioning died and my mother is 81 in Lake Worth. Who helps?** Because she is over 60, start with the county's own **EHEAP** through the Division of Senior Services. Her closest office is the **Mid County Senior Center at 3680 Lake Worth Road, 561-357-7100**. EHEAP covers one cooling benefit between April 1 and September 30, **not to exceed $400**, and it requires a verifiable energy crisis. Call before you go, because it runs while funds last. In parallel, apply for **LIHEAP** at floridaliheap.com or **1-833-273-9455**, which pays up to **$1,000** for a crisis. If the unit needs replacing rather than paying a bill, ask the county's Department of Housing and Economic Development at **561-233-3600** when its repair application window next opens.

**My parent can't drive anymore. What rides are available in Palm Beach County?** Two main options, and the paratransit rules just changed. If a disability prevents using the regular bus, apply for **Palm Tran Connection** at **561-649-9838, option 7**. Since **January 18, 2026** it splits into **Connection ADA** for trips within three-quarters of a mile of a bus route and **Connection Plus** for trips outside that band, so ask which one fits the trips your parent actually takes. If your parent can still ride a regular bus, the **half fare** is the bargain: **$55 for a 31-day pass** instead of $70, or $1 a ride, for anyone **65 and over** with a driver license or Florida ID. Register at PTdiscount@pbcgov.org. In the south county, The Volen Center's **Community Coach** at **561-395-8920, option 5** covers Boynton Beach south to north Broward for a nominal fee.

**Someone is trying to foreclose on my mother's reverse mortgage. Can she get a free lawyer?** Yes, and she does not have to prove she is poor to get one. The **Elder Law Project at the Legal Aid Society of Palm Beach County** serves residents **60 and older regardless of income**, and reverse mortgage foreclosure defense is specifically on its list, along with regular mortgage foreclosure, debt collection defense, garnishment, Medicaid, and exploitation by a family member or caregiver. Call **561-655-8944** or **800-403-9353**. If they are at capacity, call **Florida's Senior Legal Helpline at 1-888-895-7873**, which serves Florida residents 60 and older statewide.

**Is my father's PACE program real? I saw two listed for Palm Beach County.** Only one is operating. **MorseLife PACE**, also called Palm Beach PACE, runs centers in West Palm Beach, Delray Beach, and Greenacres and is listed as **Operational** in Florida AHCA's February 2026 PACE report with 1,006 enrollees. The second name, **Kinship Palm Beach PACE**, appears in that same report as **undergoing review** with an application still pending state approval, which means it cannot enroll anyone yet. Call MorseLife at **561-529-9829** for West Palm Beach, **561-325-6439** for Delray Beach, or **561-332-1762** for Greenacres.
