*Free, plain-English help for seniors and families in Madison County, AL. Education, not advice. Verify before you rely on it.*

## Start here (works anywhere)
- 211 (dial 2-1-1), Eldercare Locator 1-800-677-1116, BenefitsCheckUp.org, Medicare 1-800-633-4227, Social Security 1-800-772-1213, VA 1-800-827-1000, Alzheimer's 24/7 Helpline 1-800-272-3900, National Elder Fraud Hotline 1-833-372-8311, Crisis Lifeline 988

**Three local front doors:** **Top of Alabama Regional Council of Governments (TARCOG), Area Agency on Aging**, **5075 Research Drive NW, Huntsville, AL 35805**, phone **256-830-0818**, or through the statewide **1-800-AGE-LINE (1-800-243-5463)** | **STUDIO 60 Senior Center**, formerly the Huntsville-Madison County Senior Center, **2200 Drake Avenue SW, Huntsville, AL 35805**, **256-880-7080** | **2-1-1**, also **1-888-421-1266**, or **text your ZIP code to 898-211**, free and confidential.

**Start with what makes this county unusually easy to navigate: almost everything is run from inside it.** In a lot of Alabama counties the aging network sits in one city, the energy office in another, and the Medicaid long-term care network somewhere else again, and a family loses a week to referrals. Madison County is close to the opposite. **TARCOG**, the Area Agency on Aging for **DeKalb, Jackson, Limestone, Madison and Marshall counties**, is on Research Drive in Huntsville, and its own published service list runs from **Medicare and insurance counseling, home-delivered meals, nutrition, caregiver support, senior employment, prescription assistance, information and referral and Long-Term Care Ombudsman services** all the way through the **Medicaid Elderly and Disabled Waiver**, which it administers. The **community action agency** that runs **LIHEAP** also runs **weatherization** here, from a central office in north Huntsville plus seven neighborhood sites. The **Alabama Medicaid district office** for Jackson, Limestone and Madison counties is on University Drive. The **regional food bank** is on Finney Drive. Even the **statewide respite program** is headquartered here. One area code covers nearly all of it.

**The second thing to understand is the line that runs through everything, which is the Huntsville city limits.** This is the single most useful fact on this page. **Meals on Wheels** delivers inside the city; outside it, you go to a **neighborhood nutrition center**. The **Orbit** bus and **Access** paratransit serve the city; the rural half of the county is served by **TRAM**, a separate county program at four times the fare. The **CDBG home repair program** is a City of Huntsville program and a Gurley address will not qualify. There are **two Project Lifesaver programs**, one run by the Sheriff and one by the Huntsville Police Department. None of this is a mistake or a gap, but a family that calls the wrong one first loses days. Before you dial anything below, settle whether the address is inside the city.

**The third pattern is about the power bill, and it catches transplants.** Much of Alabama is Alabama Power territory, and Alabama Power runs a well-known winter bill program for people 60 and older. **Huntsville is not an Alabama Power town.** The city is served by **Huntsville Utilities**, which sells electricity, natural gas and water, and Huntsville Utilities runs **its own Project Share**, launched in December 1988, with a different age floor: **62**. If you read about a program elsewhere in the state and could not find it here, that is why. The Huntsville Utilities program is real, it is screened by the Salvation Army, and it has helped **32,419 families since 1988**.

**And one warning specific to this county's name, because it will cost you time.** There is a **Madison County**, and inside it there is a **City of Madison**, and they are not the same government. The **Madison Senior Center** is a City of Madison Parks and Recreation facility. **Madison Crossroads Senior Center** is a different place entirely, up in **Toney** in the far north of the county. **Triana** is its own town with its own municipal building. On top of that, Madison County exists in many other states, several of them with well-optimized senior agencies. If a phone number you found does not start with **256**, you are probably looking at the wrong Madison County.

Three more numbers belong on the refrigerator. The **Alabama Adult Abuse Hotline at 1-800-458-7214** takes reports of suspected abuse, neglect or exploitation of an elderly or disabled adult, and it is run by the Adult Protective Services Division of the Alabama Department of Human Resources. The **Long-Term Care Ombudsman**, reached through **TARCOG at 256-830-0818**, is who you call about a nursing home or assisted living facility rather than that facility's own management. And **2-1-1**, which in Madison County is operated by **Crisis Services of North Alabama** under contract with the **United Way of Madison County**, will tell you which programs currently have money, which changes month to month.

## Property tax relief (apply with the Madison County Tax Assessor, 256-532-3350)
**Get the office title right first, because Alabama does not use one name for this job.** Some counties have a Revenue Commissioner. Some have a Property Tax Commissioner. The Alabama Department of Revenue's official county office list shows Madison County with a **Tax Assessor**, currently **Cliff Mann**, at the **Madison County Service Center, 1918 North Memorial Parkway, Huntsville, AL 35801**, phone **256-532-3350**, email CMann@MadisonCountyAl.gov. Madison County also has a separate **Tax Collector**, so if you call the wrong one you will be transferred. Exemptions are claimed with the **Assessor**.

**The two dates, in the county's own words.** A homestead exemption applies if you own a single-family residence and occupy it as your primary residence **on October 1 of the tax year for which you are applying**, and the **deadline to apply is December 31st**. On the additional senior and disability exemptions the county is equally specific: you **must be eligible on October 1 and claim it no later than December 31, and it will apply to the following year's taxes**. There is also a **File Homestead Exemption Online** option, and a **satellite office at the Madison County Taxpayer Service Center, 100 Plaza Boulevard, Madison**, open 8:30 a.m. to 4:45 p.m. Monday through Friday and closed for lunch from noon to 12:30.

**What the exemptions actually are.** The county publishes them alongside the state's lettered tiers:

- **Basic homestead.** Places the property in the **10 percent tax class** and produces **approximately a $48 per year savings**.
- **Over 65.** Under Code of Alabama 1975 sections 40-9-19(d) and 40-9-21, **if you are sixty-five years old or older, you are entitled to an exemption from the STATE portion of your property taxes**. You may also be entitled to **additional exemptions if the income of the person claiming the exemption and that of his or her spouse is twelve thousand dollars ($12,000) or less**.
- **Disability.** Under section 40-9-21, **if you are totally and permanently disabled, you are entitled to an exemption regardless of income or age**. The county names acceptable proof: an **award letter from Social Security**, a **benefits verification letter from the VA**, or **two PT-PA forms**.

**The statewide tiers behind that, which is where the real money is.** Alabama sorts homestead exemptions into four lettered categories:

- **H-1.** Taxpayers under age 65 and not disabled: **$4,000 assessed value** state and **$2,000 assessed value** county.
- **H-2.** Taxpayers **65 and older with annual adjusted gross income of less than $12,000**, or retired due to permanent and total disability regardless of age: exempt from **all of the state portion**, plus **$5,000 of assessed value** on the county portion **including school district ad valorem taxes**.
- **H-3, age 65 and older.** Taxpayers **65 and older with net taxable income of $12,000 or less on the combined taxpayer and spouse federal return**: **exempt from all ad valorem taxes**. Not a discount. All of it.
- **H-3, disabled.** A taxpayer who is **permanently and totally disabled** is **exempt from all ad valorem taxes**, and the state is explicit that **there is no income limitation**.
- **H-4.** Age 65 and older with **income greater than $12,000** on the most recent Alabama return: exempt from **all of the state portion**, plus the regular **$2,000** county homestead exemption.

Read those against an actual tax return before deciding you do not qualify. The distance between H-4 and H-3 is the distance between a trim and a zero bill, and the deciding figure is **net taxable income on the combined federal return**, not gross income and not the size of a Social Security deposit.

**Now the Madison County paperwork rule that trips up more people here than anywhere else, and it is about your driver's license.** The county's homestead policy states that the **address on your driver's license must match the property address and must have been issued on or before October 1**. If you changed the address on your license **between October 1 and December 31**, the county requires **both a homeowner's insurance policy AND a utility set date letter from Huntsville Utilities**, and it publishes the number to get one: **Huntsville Utilities customer service at 256-535-1200**. Until the license is corrected the office can grant **second home status at the 10 percent rate** on those two documents, and once the license matches you come back for the rest. If you hold an **out-of-state license**, the county wants a statement **from the prior taxing official, on their letterhead, that you no longer claim a homestead in their county**. An **active duty military ID** substitutes for the license change with the insurance policy and set date letter. Given how many people move into this county for work and never change their license, this is worth checking before December.

**Two more documentation notes.** For the income-tested exemptions the county states that **handwritten tax returns are not accepted**, and that if you are not required to file, you must submit **a verification of non-filing letter from the IRS or a copy of your transcript**. If the property is held **in a trust**, bring a copy of the trust for review.

**A newer statewide protection that helps whether or not you qualify on age.** Under **Act 2024-344**, Alabama caps the annual increase in taxable assessed value for **Class II and Class III** real property, which includes owner-occupied residential property, at **7 percent**. The base year was **October 1, 2024**, for collections beginning **October 1, 2025**, and the limitation continues **through the fiscal year beginning October 1, 2027**. The cap comes off when the property changes ownership, with exceptions for family transfers and transfers because of death, when the classification changes, or when significant improvements are added. In a county whose values have moved the way Huntsville's have, that is worth understanding before you open the notice.

**What we are routing rather than stating.** Madison County's published pages do not say that the senior exemption must be refiled every year, and some other Alabama counties do say exactly that about theirs. What Madison County does publish is that **a homestead exemption must be claimed, exemptions are not automatic**, and that **if any change is made to your deed, you may need to re-claim your homestead exemption**. We are not going to guess at an annual renewal rule the county has not published. Call **256-532-3350** and ask two questions in one breath: which exemption code am I on, and does anything I hold have to be refiled. We also found no published local Madison County senior exemption enacted on top of the state tiers, so ask about that in the same call rather than assuming either way.

**How LIHEAP works here.** Households are eligible for **one regular heating payment and one regular cooling payment per year**, and payments go **directly to the vendor**. Households with members who are **elderly, disabled, or with children 18 or younger may qualify for additional crisis assistance if funding is available**. ADECA sets the income ceiling at **150 percent of the federal poverty level** and states plainly that **ADECA does not accept or process applications**, so the local agency is the only real door. You can apply **face to face** by scheduling through **256-907-1550**, or **contactlessly** through the state's LITT Lite portal by uploading photos of your documents and signing electronically. Bring **Social Security cards for everyone in the household**, a **current valid photo ID**, a **current utility or gas bill in the head of household's or spouse's name**, a **lease** if you live in income-based housing, and **proof of gross income for the previous month for everyone in the household**.

**Huntsville Utilities Project Share, which starts at 62.** This is Huntsville Utilities' own contributor-funded program, launched in **December 1988**, and it is a different thing from the similarly named program run by the state's investor-owned utility. It qualifies **utility customers aged 62 or older** and **utility customers certified as disabled or handicapped**, who must be on a **low or fixed income which does not exceed the state household income eligibility guidelines as specified by the Alabama Department of Human Resources**. **The Salvation Army screens potential recipients, determines eligibility and decides who will receive assistance.** The scale is real: in **January, February and March of 2026 alone, Project Share assisted 844 families**, awarding **$192,860.15**, an average of **$228.51 per family**. Huntsville Utilities is at **112 Spragins Street, Huntsville, AL 35801**, main number **256-535-1200**, outages **256-535-4448**.

**One piece of Project Share most people never hear about.** **Thirty percent of Project Share donations go toward home improvements to create long-term energy savings**, combined with grant awards and TVA contributions, and **participants for energy improvements are qualified through HUD criteria for very low-income residents of Madison County**. That is a home-repair benefit hiding inside a bill-payment program. Ask about it by name.

**If both are out of funds this month.** The **Huntsville Assistance Program** grants **help paying a portion of a utility bill**, **only once within a 12-month period**, and the logistics are strict: the **Financial Assistance Line is 256-539-2320, and it is answered ONLY from 9:30 to 11 a.m. on Mondays**, until all available appointments are filled. You will attend an appointment with a trained volunteer and must bring **proof of Madison County residency and a photo ID**. The **Salvation Army Huntsville Corps**, **2114 Oakwood Avenue NW, Huntsville, AL 35810**, also provides emergency rent and utility assistance. And **Community Action Partnership** itself provides **emergency assistance on a case by case basis, depending on funding and eligibility**, which may cover food vouchers, utilities, rent, mortgage assistance, medical or dental care, prescription medicine, bus tickets or fuel.

## Medicare, Medicaid, prescriptions
**Free Medicare counseling from two places in this county, neither of which sells anything.** **SHIP**, Alabama's State Health Insurance Assistance Program, is funded by the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services and administered by the Alabama Department of Senior Services through the Area Agencies on Aging. **TARCOG at 256-830-0818** runs it for the region, and **STUDIO 60 at 256-880-7080** runs it locally. Both state the same guarantee in nearly the same words: **counselors and volunteers are not affiliated with any insurance company and will not attempt to sell insurance, and all counseling records are strictly confidential**. If you only make one call in October, make it this one.

**Medicaid, and where the office actually is.** The **Alabama Medicaid Huntsville District Office** is at **6515 University Drive NW, Suite B, Huntsville, AL 35806-1775**, phone **855-733-3160**, fax **877-295-8813**, and it **serves Jackson, Limestone and Madison counties**. District offices process **applications for elderly and disabled programs, applications for nursing home care, and applications for programs that help pay Medicare premiums**. The statewide **Recipient Call Center is 1-800-362-1504**, Monday through Friday 8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.

**The Elderly and Disabled Waiver, and the reason it matters more here than PACE.** The **E&D Waiver provides home and community-based services to the elderly and individuals with disabilities at risk for nursing facility placement**. **Prescription help.** **SenioRx** is Alabama's prescription assistance program, and its reach is wider than the name suggests. It covers Alabamians who are **age 55 or older with a chronic medical condition and no or limited prescription drug insurance meeting certain income limits**, **any age with a disability**, or anyone who has **reached the Medicare Part D coverage gap**. Apply through your Area Agency on Aging, which here is **TARCOG at 256-830-0818**, or the statewide **1-800-AGE-LINE (1-800-243-5463)**.

## Home repair and safety
**The City of Huntsville program, and the age floor is 62 rather than 65.** The **CDBG Home Rehabilitation Program** rehabs owner-occupied homes to improve living conditions through exterior modifications. The published eligibility is four things: you **must own the home**, and **rental or leased property is not eligible**; you **must be 62 years of age or older and/or certified disabled**; you **must reside at the property**; and **total gross household income from all sources cannot exceed 80 percent of the area median income, adjusted for family size, as currently determined by HUD**. Request assistance at **comdev@huntsvilleal.gov**. Community Development is on the **3rd floor of Huntsville City Hall, 305 Fountain Circle, Huntsville, AL 35801**, phone **256-427-5400**, manager Scott Erwin. Volunteer crews from local companies and organizations do a good deal of the work, including **yard cleanup, exterior painting, siding, roofing and securing doors and windows**.

**Weatherization, which covers the whole county.** Administered by **Community Action Partnership at 256-851-9800**. Income **must not exceed 200 percent of the federally established poverty level**, and the program is aimed **particularly at the elderly, people with disabilities and families with children**. Understand how the queue works before you call: **qualifying applicants are assigned priority points based on state-regulated guidelines and placed on a waiting list**, and selection follows those points and funding availability. A **licensed assessor** then determines which measures the house gets. Work can include **sealing outside air infiltration, attic, wall and floor insulation, duct sealing, HVAC tune-ups and repairs, repairing leaky windows and doors, LED bulb replacement, and health and safety checks**. Apply early, because the list is the bottleneck, not the work.

**Two routes for a rural Madison County homeowner.** There is no published county-run senior home repair grant outside the city, so the honest answer is that this takes two calls rather than one: **weatherization at 256-851-9800**, and the **energy improvement side of Huntsville Utilities' Project Share**, which qualifies participants through **HUD criteria for very low-income residents of Madison County**, reached at **256-535-1200**. If neither fits, **2-1-1** tracks which local church and volunteer programs currently have capacity.

**Homemaker services, for the house rather than the structure.** **STUDIO 60's Homemaker Program**, funded by **Title III-B of the Older Americans Act through the Alabama Department of Senior Services and the TARCOG Area Agency on Aging**, provides **in-home light housekeeping to promote independent living** for **adults 60 and over with health or physical limitations that prevent them from doing household chores**. Routine services are **sweeping, vacuuming and mopping, dusting, cleaning the bathroom and bedroom, and cleaning the kitchen and living room**, with other chores pre-approved. Two conditions matter: the program states plainly that **this is not a maid service**, and **clients must provide their own cleaning supplies**. There is **no cost**, though donations are encouraged. Contact **Victoria Watkins, Homemaker Supervisor, at 256-513-8324** or hmksup@seniorview.com.

## Transportation
**Inside the Huntsville city limits: Orbit for the bus, Access for door to door.** The city's fixed-route system is **Orbit**, which runs **Monday through Friday 6 a.m. to 9 p.m. and Saturday 8 a.m. to 5 p.m.** The fare structure is where the senior discount lives. A **one-way fare is $1**, but it is **50 cents for seniors 65 and older, disabled citizens, Medicare or Medicaid cardholders with valid ID, students with a valid student ID, and children 6 and under**. You **must show qualifying ID** to use the discounted fare. A **book of 20 one-way tickets is $18** and a **monthly pass is $30**, with **free transfers**. Tickets are sold at the **Huntsville Transit Center, 521 Pratt Avenue**, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. weekdays, or through the **Token Transit** app. Service animals are welcome on all city transit vehicles.

**Access, the paratransit side.** The city runs **19 radio-dispatched, ADA-accessible vehicles** providing **specialized, door-to-door, demand-response** service. Two groups are eligible: **persons with disabilities who, because of their disability, are unable to ride regular city buses**, and **senior citizens who are over 65 and do not live near a bus route**, the second **when space is available**. An application must be completed and returned before any trip can be scheduled, and **certification can take up to 21 days**, though someone with a disability and immediate needs may receive **conditional eligibility inside the ADA service area** during that time. **One Personal Care Attendant travels free of charge** with an ADA-eligible passenger, and it is important to indicate the need for one when you apply.

**The Access logistics that decide whether it works for you.** **Tickets are $2.00 each, one per boarding, and drivers accept no cash or checks.** Reserve by emailing Access@HuntsvilleAL.gov or calling **256-427-6857 extension 1** between **7 a.m. and 5 p.m. Monday through Friday**; trips may be scheduled the day before or up to a month ahead, and reservations are required **by 5 p.m. the day before**. On the day, **be ready one hour before the time you are scheduled to arrive**, and the **vehicle will wait no more than five minutes**. Drivers assist you from the main entrance door onto the van and from the van to the main entrance of your destination, but **will not enter a house or building** and **will not carry parcels over 10 pounds**. Bring **no more than two bags per person**. A pattern of no-shows, defined as **more than three in a month and 7 percent over the past 12 months**, can cost you **ten business days** of service. Call **256-427-6811** with questions, or **256-427-6829** about the free **Access Huntsville** app for booking and real-time tracking.

**Outside the city: TRAM, which has no age test at all.** **Transportation for Rural Areas of Madison County** is a federally funded **FTA Section 5311** public transportation program **open to the general public who reside in rural Madison County**, with the Madison County Commission providing matching funds. There is no disability requirement and no age requirement. The office is at **819 Cook Avenue, Suite 137, Huntsville, AL 35801**, transit manager **Katrina Banks-Love**, phone **256-532-3792**, email tram@madisoncountyal.gov, office hours **Monday through Friday 8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.**

**TRAM's schedule is the thing to plan around, and it is tighter than a city bus.** Transportation runs **Monday through Thursday 6:30 a.m. to 3:30 p.m. and Friday 6:30 a.m. to 2:30 p.m.**, with **morning pickups from 7:00 to 8:00 a.m.**, and **final pickups leaving Huntsville begin at 1:00 p.m. Monday through Thursday and noon on Friday**. A 2:30 p.m. specialist appointment in Huntsville does not work on TRAM. **Round-trip tickets are $10.00 and one-way tickets are $8.00**, tickets are required to board, they are **non-refundable and do not expire**, and drivers do not handle money. Riders are urged to **schedule 24 hours in advance**, and **reservations are accepted weekdays between 8:00 a.m. and 3:00 p.m.**, with none taken after 3:00 for the following day. **Cancellations must be made at least one hour before pickup**, the driver waits **five minutes**, and **two no-shows within 60 days means a five-day suspension**. If the **Madison County Courthouse is closed, TRAM is closed**.

**A few local rides that are not public transit.** The **Madison Senior Center** publishes a **bus transportation request form** for its members. **STUDIO 60's Center for Adult Day Care** provides **transportation to and from the Center for those living within the city limits**. And **TARCOG's advocacy and support services** at **256-830-0818** include transportation assistance, which is worth asking about when the trip is medical.

## Legal help
**Start with TARCOG, because this county's Area Agency on Aging employs its own lawyer.** That is not true everywhere, and it is the best legal front door here. **TARCOG employs a full-time, licensed attorney to provide legal assistance to senior adults age 60 and over living in DeKalb, Jackson, Limestone, Madison and Marshall counties.** The subjects are exactly what goes wrong in later life: **Medicare and Medicaid issues, income maintenance, long-term care, individual rights, consumer matters, powers of attorney and health directives, and basic estate planning**. **TARCOG does not charge a set fee for its services**, though voluntary donations are encouraged against a published suggested contribution table.

**How to get an appointment, and what the attorney will not take.** For **Madison County**, call **256-830-0818**, and **appointments generally take place at the TARCOG office in Huntsville**. You can also complete the **Request for Legal Assistance** form and email it to **legalintake@tarcog.us**, or mail it to **TARCOG, ATTN: LEGAL, P.O. Box 1087, Huntsville, AL 35805**, and TARCOG recommends encrypting any email containing personal information. TARCOG's own downloadable forms include an **Advance Directive for Health Care** and an **Alabama Durable Power of Attorney**. The attorney **does not assist with criminal matters, filing bankruptcy, or fee-generating cases**. Two useful extras: **home visits can occasionally be scheduled** for people receiving services through **Hospice, Home Health, Alabama Cares and Medicaid Waiver programs**, and **facility visits** can sometimes be arranged for seniors living in nursing homes.

**Then Legal Services Alabama, which has an income test.** LSA's statewide intake is **1-866-456-4995** in English and **1-888-835-3505** en Espanol, with the **call center open Monday through Friday 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.**, and you can apply online at legalservicesalabama.org. AlabamaLegalHelp.org, which is a project of Legal Services Alabama and the Legal Services Corporation, lists the **Huntsville office at 610 Airport Road, Suite 200, Huntsville, AL 35802**, local **256-536-9645**, toll free **1-888-741-7129**, weekdays 8:30 a.m. to 5:00 p.m., serving **Madison County** along with Colbert, Cullman, Franklin, Jackson, Lauderdale, Lawrence, Limestone, Marshall and Morgan counties. **And the two protective lines.** The **Alabama Adult Abuse Hotline is 1-800-458-7214**, run by the Adult Protective Services Division of the Alabama Department of Human Resources under the APS Act of 1976, which requires DHR to receive and investigate reports of suspected abuse, neglect or exploitation of adults who cannot protect themselves. For a problem inside a facility rather than a home, the **Long-Term Care Ombudsman** through **TARCOG at 256-830-0818** is the right call, and it is a different function from filing an abuse report.

## Caregiver support
**Alabama Cares, and the eligibility rule that surprises people.** TARCOG administers **Alabama Cares**, part of the National Family Caregiver Support Program, at **256-716-2442**. Three groups have access: **primary family caregivers of frail older adults aged 60 or older**; **older relative caregivers, not parents, including grandparents age 55 or older, caring for children ages 18 and younger with or without disabilities**; and **older relative caregivers and parents, including grandparents age 55 or older, caring for adults ages 19 to 59 with disabilities**. The services are **information, counseling, support groups, training, assistance through short-term case management, supplemental services, and respite**.

**Read the eligibility conditions before you get your hopes up on respite.** TARCOG publishes three: there **must be a caregiver, family member, friend or church member, who provides care two or three times per week**; the **care recipient must be 60 years or older and need help with at least three activities of daily living** such as bathing, cooking, household chores, transportation, taking medicines, transferring or walking; and **there is no income limit, but there is a cost share based on the care recipient's income**. On respite specifically, TARCOG is candid that **due to limited funding, this service is available for family caregivers who have the greatest burden and highest need for relief**. Income will not prevent you from receiving services, but it is used when prioritizing.

**Two Project Lifesaver programs, both free, and which one you call depends on your address.** The **Madison County Sheriff's Office** runs **Project Lifesaver of Madison County** in partnership with the **Pilot Club of Huntsville**, providing **wristband transmitters at no cost to people with Alzheimer's, dementia, autism or other cognitive disorders who have a history of wandering and becoming lost**. It is an active radio system, not a passive ID bracelet: when a caregiver reports the client missing, a **specially trained search and rescue team** searches with a mobile locator, and **recovery times average less than 30 minutes**. The county program currently has **over 60 people enrolled**. Contact **Sgt. Travion Ragland at tragland@madisoncountyal.gov**, **Deputy Kadarius McBride at kmcbride@madisoncountyal.gov**, or **Deputy Jerry Johnson at jjohnson2@madisoncountyal.gov**, or call the non-emergency line **256-722-7181**.

Inside the city, the **Huntsville Police Department** runs its own Project Lifesaver for people with **Alzheimer's disease, dementia, autism, Down syndrome, traumatic brain injury and other cognitive challenges**. Participants wear a small radio-frequency device on the **wrist or ankle**. HPD states there are **no fees for participation**, that the program is funded through **private donations, corporate support and grants**, and that **program volunteers conduct monthly check-ins to ensure equipment is functioning and provide needed supplies**. Enroll by emailing **PoliceChief@huntsvilleal.gov**. HPD's **Crisis Intervention Team** is at **256-705-3089**.

**Adult day care, which is usually what an exhausted caregiver actually needs.** **STUDIO 60's Center for Adult Day Care** has operated **since 1981** and provides **care and companionship for adults 18 and older who need assistance or supervision during the day**. It is open **7:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. Monday through Friday**, with **full or part-time openings**, a **complete hot balanced lunch and afternoon snack**, supervised gardening and outdoor activities, health and fitness programs, and monthly parties. **Long-term care insurance is accepted**, and **transportation to and from the Center is available to those living within the city limits**. Cancellations require **two weeks' notice**. Call **256-880-7094** to arrange a tour and a **free one day visit**, director Julie Born.

**Alabama Lifespan Respite is headquartered in this county, and right now it is closed.** The statewide respite reimbursement program is **a program of UCP Huntsville**, at **256-859-8300** or al.respite@ucphuntsville.org. As of this writing its own application page states: **applications are closed at this time**, and **please check back in October**. If you received respite reimbursement after July 2025 there is **no need to reapply**, since re-enrollment information is sent to recipients annually. When it reopens, read the conditions first: you **cannot currently be receiving respite services through any other program, including Medicaid Home and Community Based Services waivers**; **only one caregiver per household is eligible**; you **cannot be both the primary caregiver and the respite provider**, because the program reimburses you after you have paid a third party; the provider must be **at least 18 and live outside the care recipient's home**; you must submit **proof of diagnosis**; and there is a **30-day processing period** before the reimbursement check arrives. If you are already on the Medicaid waiver, use the waiver's respite through TARCOG instead of waiting.

**Support groups, in person, in this county.** STUDIO 60 hosts a **Diabetic Support Group** on the **first Tuesday at 9:30 a.m.** in the cafeteria, information at **256-880-7080**, and a **Bereavement Support Group** run by Hospice Family Care in the Art Room on the **second and fourth Wednesdays at 1:15 p.m.**, information from Anthony Ford at **256-650-1212**. ## PACE
**There is no PACE program serving Madison County, and it is worth saying plainly so you stop looking.**

PACE, the Program of All-Inclusive Care for the Elderly, bundles primary care, hospital care, medical specialty services, prescription drugs, dentistry, nursing home care, personal care, physical therapy, adult day care, nutritional counseling, lab and x-ray services, social services and transportation for people who need nursing-home-level care but want to remain at home. It is excellent where it exists. Alabama Medicaid's own material lists the eligibility criteria, and one of them settles the question: an applicant must **meet the nursing home level of care**, be **able to live safely in the community at the time of enrollment**, be **55 years of age and older**, and **live in a designated PACE service area, currently Mobile and Baldwin counties**. Those are on the Gulf Coast. There is no PACE site anywhere in north Alabama.

**What to ask for instead.** The nearest equivalent available here is the **Medicaid Elderly and Disabled Waiver**, and the phone number is one you already have. **TARCOG administers the waiver in Madison County**, so **256-830-0818** reaches the waiver, the meal programs, the SHIP counselor, the caregiver program and the staff attorney in a single call. Then contact the **Alabama Medicaid Huntsville District Office at 855-733-3160** about the eligibility paperwork. And ask TARCOG the follow-up question that saves families weeks: what can start now, while the waiver application is pending.

## Frequently asked questions
**Dad is 79, has owned the same place near Gurley since 1988, and lives on Social Security and a small pension. What is he actually entitled to?** Possibly a zero property tax bill. The Alabama Department of Revenue's **H-3** category for age 65 and older with **net taxable income of $12,000 or less on the combined federal return** is **exempt from all ad valorem taxes**, and Madison County's own guidance says the same thing in county terms. The deciding number is net taxable income on the return, not gross and not the Social Security deposit, so take the return to the **Tax Assessor at the Madison County Service Center, 1918 North Memorial Parkway, 256-532-3350**. Check the driver's license first: the county requires the **license address to match the property and to have been issued on or before October 1**. And note the calendar. You must be **eligible on October 1** and **claim it no later than December 31**, and it applies to the **following year's** taxes.

**We just moved here from out of state for my son's job and my mother came with us. Can she claim anything this year?** Possibly, but the paperwork is the hurdle rather than the age. Madison County requires homeowners with an **out-of-state driver's license** to produce **a statement from the prior taxing official, on their letterhead, that you no longer claim a homestead in their county**. Until the license is changed, the office can grant the **10 percent assessment rate** on the strength of a **homeowner's insurance declaration page covering October 1** plus a **utility set date letter from Huntsville Utilities**, which you get by calling **256-535-1200**. Once the license is updated, come back for the rest. Out-of-state license transfers are handled at the **Department of Public Safety at 1115 Church Street in Huntsville**, with offices also in Athens, Decatur, Scottsboro and Guntersville. Start this in October, not on December 30.

**Mom's power bill is past due and she is 84 on a fixed income. Who actually helps?** Three calls, in this order. First **256-907-1550** to schedule a **LIHEAP** appointment with Community Action Partnership, or apply contactlessly through the state's LITT Lite portal. Second, because she is over 62, ask about **Huntsville Utilities Project Share**, screened by the Salvation Army, through **256-535-1200**. Third, if both are out of funds this month, the **Huntsville Assistance Program at 256-539-2320**, remembering that the line is answered **only from 9:30 to 11 a.m. on Mondays**. And **2-1-1** will tell you who currently has money, which changes week to week.

**My mother stopped driving. She is 77 and lives out past New Market. How does she get to the doctor?** **TRAM**, at **256-532-3792**, because she is outside the Huntsville city limits. There is no age test and no disability test, since TRAM is open to the general public in rural Madison County. Fares are **$8.00 one way and $10.00 round trip**, tickets must be bought in advance from the TRAM office, and drivers handle no money. Book **24 hours ahead** by calling between **8:00 a.m. and 3:00 p.m.** on a weekday. The critical detail is the return trip: **final pickups leaving Huntsville begin at 1:00 p.m. Monday through Thursday and noon on Friday**, so schedule the appointment in the morning. If she lived inside the city, the answer would be **Access** at **256-427-6857 extension 1** at **$2.00 a ticket** instead.

**My husband has dementia and I have not slept properly in months. What is in Madison County?** Four calls, in this order. **256-716-2442** for **Alabama Cares** through TARCOG, but read the eligibility first: the care recipient must be **60 or older and need help with at least three activities of daily living**, and although there is no income limit there **is a cost share** based on his income. **256-880-7094** for **STUDIO 60's Center for Adult Day Care**, which is open 7:30 to 4:30 weekdays, accepts long-term care insurance, provides transportation inside the city limits, and will give you a **free one day visit** to try it. **256-722-7181** or **PoliceChief@huntsvilleal.gov** for a **free Project Lifesaver bracelet**, before there is an incident rather than after. And **1-800-272-3900**, the Alzheimer's Association's free 24/7 helpline, at whatever hour you are actually awake.

**We are looking at a nursing home for my mother and cannot afford it. Is there an alternative?** Yes, and it is not PACE, because PACE does not operate anywhere near this county. Ask about the **Medicaid Elderly and Disabled Waiver**, which covers **personal care, homemaker services, respite, adult day health, companion services, case management and home-delivered frozen meals** for people at risk of nursing facility placement. Eligibility requires full Medicaid or eligibility through deeming, with income **not exceeding 300 percent of the federal poverty level** and limited assets. In Madison County that waiver is run by **TARCOG at 256-830-0818**, the same agency that handles meals, Medicare counseling and the staff attorney. Then call the **Alabama Medicaid Huntsville District Office at 855-733-3160**, which serves Jackson, Limestone and Madison counties, about the paperwork.

**Mom is 73 and I think she qualifies for food stamps, but she will not sit through an interview.** She may not have to, but check her age against the right rule. Alabama's **Elderly Simplified Application Project** uses a short form, and the state's current version, **revised October 2025**, is for households where **all members are age 65 or older**, who **purchase and prepare food separately from others in the home**, with **no earnings from work**. At 73 with no wages, she likely fits. Ask for it **by name** at the **Madison County DHR office, 2206 Oakwood Avenue NW, Huntsville, 256-427-6000**. For help filling out the form, the state publishes **1-800-438-2958**.

**My father is in a nursing home here and something is wrong. Can I raise it without him facing retaliation?** Call the **Long-Term Care Ombudsman** through **TARCOG at 256-830-0818**. The ombudsman program exists specifically to investigate and resolve complaints on behalf of residents of long-term care facilities, and it is a separate function from the facility's own management. If what you are describing is abuse, neglect or exploitation rather than a care dispute, that goes to the **Alabama Adult Abuse Hotline at 1-800-458-7214** instead.

**I keep finding a "Madison Senior Center" and a "Madison Crossroads Senior Center" and I cannot tell them apart.** They are two different places about twenty miles apart. The **Madison Senior Center** is a **City of Madison** Parks and Recreation facility at **1329 Browns Ferry Road, Madison, AL 35758**, **256-772-6242**, open weekdays 8 a.m. to 4 p.m., with its own membership packet, caregiver packet and monthly program guide. **Madison Crossroads Senior Center** is in **Toney**, in the far north of the county near the Tennessee line, at **256-813-3036**. Both are Madison County. Neither is the county government. And if a number you found starts with anything other than **256**, you have found one of the Madison Counties in another state.

**Is there anything in this county that is just a good place to go?** Yes, and it is county-owned. The **Robert "Bob" Harrison Senior Wellness and Advocacy Center** at **6156 Pulaski Pike, Huntsville, AL 35810**, **256-519-2040**, sits on a campus in the foothills of Wade and Drake mountains, and Madison County's own facility directory lists what is inside: a **heated indoor pool**, a **jacuzzi**, an **indoor walking track**, a **fitness gym**, a **game room**, **computer stations**, a **sewing room**, an **outdoor walking trail**, a **nutrition site** and a **Little Pantry site**. Membership is **open to adults 40 and older at $180 per year**, which is an unusually low age floor, and the center runs water aerobics, chair yoga, Zumba Gold, strength training, quilting, gardening, monthly health screenings and weekday nutritional lunches. If a parent is isolated more than they are unwell, this is often the better first call.

**Is there anything for a Madison County veteran or a veteran's widow?** Yes, and it is free. The **Madison County Veterans Service Office**, service officer **Terrence Peoples**, is at **819 Cook Avenue NW, Suite 137, Huntsville, AL 35801**, phone **256-532-1662**, fax 256-532-1562, open **Monday through Friday 8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.** County veterans service offices help veterans, dependents and survivors file and process claims for benefits from both the federal VA and the state of Alabama. Madison County also has one of Alabama's five state veterans homes inside it, the **Floyd E. "Tut" Fann State Veterans Home, 2701 Meridian Street, Huntsville, AL 35811**, **256-851-2807**. Eligibility requires an **honorable discharge with a minimum of 90 continuous days of active duty**, with a 24-month rule for those who enlisted after September 7, 1980 or were commissioned after October 16, 1981; qualification under **U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs criteria for skilled nursing care or domiciliary and assisted living**; and **residence in Alabama during the immediate past 12 months**. Applications come from the county veterans service office or from va.alabama.gov. Read the state's own list of what the homes do not provide before you plan around one: the first item on it is **free nursing home care**.
