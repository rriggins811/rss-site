You know how Mom's Social Security letter shows up in December, and she sets it on the counter next to the Christmas cards, and nobody reads past the first line? That letter is the one piece of mail every year that tells you, in one number, what she actually has to live on. And most families never open it until the month they're sitting in a hospital hallway trying to figure out how to pay for what comes next.

This year the first line will say 3.6 percent. The Social Security Administration announced it on October 14. For the average retirement check, that's about $75 a month more starting in January. It's the biggest raise since 2023, and it's going to feel like good news.

It is good news. It's also smaller than it sounds once you put it next to what care costs, and it's going to arrive at the same time as a Medicare premium that comes out of the check before she ever sees it.

I spent more than eight years buying houses from families in transition, and I can tell you exactly when most of them called me: after the money math was already done for them by an emergency. A fall, a discharge planner, a facility with one bed open and a deposit due Friday. The house became the solution because nobody had sat down with the actual income number six months earlier, when there was still time to choose.

I walked away from the buying side. The job now is helping you see the whole board while you still have moves. The December COLA notice is the easiest place on the board to start, because it's the one number you don't have to go looking for. It comes to you.

So here's what the raise is, what eats it, and what to do with it in the next 60 days.

## What Social Security Actually Announced

Here are the figures, straight from the Social Security Administration's October 14 announcement and its 2027 COLA page.

**The raise is 3.6 percent.** Social Security and Supplemental Security Income (SSI) payments for 76 million Americans will go up 3.6 percent in 2027. SSA says that on average, Social Security retirement benefits will increase by about $75 per month starting in January.

**When it lands.** Nearly 72 million Social Security beneficiaries see the increase beginning with January 2027 payments. The roughly 7.3 million people on SSI see theirs starting December 31, 2026. Some people get both.

**How it compares.** SSA's own history table shows 2.8 percent for 2026, 2.5 percent for 2025, 3.2 percent for 2024, and 8.7 percent for 2023. So 3.6 is the largest adjustment since 2023, and the last time it was exactly 3.6 was January 2012. The adjustment is set by law from the change in the Consumer Price Index for Urban Wage Earners and Clerical Workers, third quarter to third quarter, as measured by the Bureau of Labor Statistics.

**The SSI number, which matters more than most people realize.** Per SSA, the maximum federal SSI payment for 2027 is $1,029 a month for an individual and $1,544 for a couple, up from $994 for an individual in 2026. That figure is the one a lot of state programs key off of, including the North Carolina program that helps pay for assisted living. More on that below.

**The notice.** SSA says COLA notices will be available online in late November through the Message Center of a personal my Social Security account, for people who create the account by November 18, 2026. Everyone else gets a mailed notice in December. For beneficiaries on Medicare, SSA says the new 2027 benefit amount, meaning what's left after the premium, will be in that same notice.

**Medicare.** SSA's announcement points to Medicare.gov for 2027 Medicare changes. As of this writing, the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services has not published the 2027 Part B premium. The 2026 standard premium, per Medicare.gov, is $202.90 a month.

## What the Raise Means When a Parent Needs Care

### It's real money, and it's small money

Do the arithmetic on your own parent's check, not the average. A $1,500 check goes up about $54. A $2,000 check goes up about $72. A $2,800 check goes up about $101. Those are my calculations on the 3.6 percent figure, not SSA statements, and SSA will send the exact amount.

Now put it next to care. AARP's March 2026 long-term care affordability report says home care and assisted living costs have gone up nearly 50 percent since 2019, while median household income for someone 65 or older grew by less than half that amount. The same report puts the median 65-plus household income at about $60,000 in 2024 and the annual median cost of home care services at more than $50,000.

Read that again slowly. A 3.6 percent raise on the income side doesn't change which of those two numbers is bigger.

AARP also says the median household 75 and older has about $50,000 in financial assets, which it describes as roughly one year of home care or a few months of nursing home care. That's the version I saw at kitchen tables over and over. Not broke. Not rich. One year of runway, and a house.

### Part B comes out before the raise reaches the bank

Medicare.gov says most people don't get a bill from Medicare because their Part B premium is deducted automatically from their Social Security benefit. So the number that hits your parent's account in January is the gross benefit, minus Part B, minus any Part D or Medicare Advantage premium they've asked to have withheld.

CMS hasn't announced the 2027 Part B premium yet, so don't let anyone, including a blog, tell you what it will be. What you can do is pull the 2026 premium, $202.90 a month, off the current check to see what Mom actually nets today. Then watch the December notice for the 2027 net. SSA has said that for beneficiaries on Medicare, the notice will show the new benefit amount after the change.

### Medicare does not pay for the care you're worried about

This is the sentence most families learn in the hospital hallway, so learn it here instead. Medicare.gov says plainly: "Medicare doesn't pay for long-term care." It says Medicare and most health insurance, including Medigap, don't pay for long-term care services, including care in a nursing home or in the community. The custodial care that keeps a parent safe, help with bathing, dressing, meals and supervision, is on the family or Medicaid.

What Medicare does cover is short and conditional. Per Medicare.gov, skilled nursing facility care after a qualifying hospital stay of at least three days as an inpatient costs $0 a day for days 1 through 20 in 2026, after the $1,736 Part A deductible. Days 21 through 100 cost $217 a day. After day 100 you pay all costs. Time spent in observation status or the emergency room doesn't count toward the three days.

So the honest picture is this: the 3.6 percent raise is the income side. Medicare is the acute side. The long-term care side has no federal check behind it unless your parent qualifies for Medicaid under North Carolina's rules.

### In North Carolina, the COLA moves the assisted living program too

North Carolina's Special Assistance program helps pay for adult care homes, which is what the state calls most assisted living. Here's the part that matters this month: the NC Department of Health and Human Services tells county social services directors that Special Assistance rates are adjusted every year using the federally approved Social Security cost-of-living change. For 2026, with a 2.8 percent COLA, the Basic Rate was set at $1,397 a month and the Basic Maintenance Amount at $1,467, per the December 9, 2025 letter from the Division of Social Services. The Enhanced Rate for special care units was $1,792, with a maintenance amount of $1,862.

The 2027 rates haven't been published as of this writing. If the state applies the 3.6 percent figure the same way, they'll move up. Don't write a number down until the state does.

There's a wrinkle in that same letter you should know about. NC FAST, the state's eligibility system, recalculates every Special Assistance case by raising the person's Social Security, SSI and VA income by the COLA percentage, and the state tells counties to review a report of cases that are "potentially terminating due to the COLA increase." In plain English: a raise on the income side can push a parent across an income line on the benefit side. If your parent is on Special Assistance or close to applying, January is the month to check with the county, not assume.

### What this means for the aging-in-place question

If you're deciding whether Mom stays in the house with paid help or moves to a community, the COLA doesn't settle it. It does something more useful. It gives you a clean, dated, official income number to start from, and it arrives in the same 30-day window every year.

Families who stay in the house usually do it on a mix of the parent's income, the parent's savings, and the adult children's time. Families who move usually do it on the parent's income plus the house. Either way, the question is the same: how many months does this income and this savings cover at this cost of care? Nobody can answer that without the income number. December hands it to you.

## What to Do in the Next 60 Days

### 1. Get the notice early, and get it in writing

SSA says that if your parent creates a personal my Social Security account by November 18, 2026, the COLA notice shows up in the account's Message Center in late November, about three weeks ahead of the mail. If your parent isn't online, the paper notice comes in December. Either way, make sure it ends up in your hands, not under the Christmas cards. If you have power of attorney or you manage her finances, ask her now where that letter goes.

### 2. Write down the net, not the gross

Take the 2027 benefit amount from the notice. Confirm what's being withheld for Part B and any drug or Advantage plan premium. The number that's left is what your parent lives on each month. Write it on one sheet, with the date. That sheet is the first page of every conversation you're about to have, with a facility, a home care agency, a county caseworker, or a sibling.

### 3. Run the runway

Put the net monthly income on one side. Put the real monthly cost of the care you're considering on the other, from an actual quote, not an average. Subtract. Divide your parent's liquid savings by the gap. The result is months of runway. The free Care Runway Calculator on this site does that arithmetic for you and shows what changes if the cost or the help changes. The point isn't precision. The point is knowing whether you're looking at 40 months or 14, because those two families make different decisions.

### 4. Check the January thresholds with the county

If your parent is on, or near, Special Assistance or Medicaid, call the county Department of Social Services in January once the 2027 rates post. Ask two questions: what are the 2027 maintenance amounts, and does my parent's new benefit amount change anything. Bring the COLA notice. The state's own instructions say the COLA can push cases over a line, and the counties are told to review those cases. Be the family that calls first.

### 5. Have the house conversation before the clock starts

Here's the thing nobody tells you about the runway number. If it says 14 months, the house is coming into the conversation whether you plan for it or not. The difference is whether the family decides when, at the kitchen table, or whether a discharge planner decides when, on a Friday.

Calling a real estate agent starts a clock. That's not the agent being pushy, that's the job: they want the house listed and sold. If you're a year or two from that stage, the market only offers you one door, and it's onto a process you're not ready for. That's the door I used to stand in. The conversation most families need first is the one where nobody's trying to list anything: what the house is actually worth, what it would net, what it would cost to hold, and what the runway looks like with and without it. Have that one in December, with the notice on the table. When the time is right, finding the right agent is the easy part, and nobody has to audition three of them.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much is the Social Security COLA for 2027?

The Social Security Administration announced a 3.6 percent cost-of-living adjustment on October 14, 2026. It applies to benefits payable starting January 2027 for nearly 72 million Social Security beneficiaries, and SSI payments go up starting December 31, 2026. SSA says the average retirement benefit will rise by about $75 a month.

### Will the 2027 Medicare Part B premium eat the COLA?

The 2027 Part B premium hasn't been announced by CMS as of this writing, so nobody can say yet. Medicare.gov says most people have the Part B premium deducted automatically from their Social Security benefit, and the 2026 standard premium is $202.90 a month. SSA says the December COLA notice will show the 2027 benefit amount for beneficiaries on Medicare.

### Does the COLA help pay for assisted living or home care?

Only on the income side. Medicare.gov states that Medicare doesn't pay for long-term care, including care in a nursing home or in the community. The raise adds about 3.6 percent to your parent's monthly check, while AARP's March 2026 report says home care and assisted living costs have risen nearly 50 percent since 2019.

### What is the SSI payment amount for 2027?

Per SSA, the maximum federal SSI payment for 2027 is $1,029 a month for an eligible individual and $1,544 for an eligible couple, up from $994 for an individual in 2026. The higher amounts begin December 31, 2026. Some states, including North Carolina, use the SSI rate and the COLA in their own programs.

### Does the COLA change North Carolina Special Assistance for adult care homes?

Yes. NC DHHS tells counties that Special Assistance rates are adjusted every year using the Social Security COLA. For 2026 the Basic Rate was $1,397 and the Basic Maintenance Amount $1,467. The 2027 rates haven't been published yet, and the state's own instructions say the COLA can push some cases over an income limit, so check with the county in January.
