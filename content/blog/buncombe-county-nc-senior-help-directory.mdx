*Free, plain-English help for seniors and families in Buncombe County, NC. Education, not advice. Verify before you rely on it.*

## Start here (works anywhere)
- 211 (dial 2-1-1), Eldercare Locator 1-800-677-1116, Medicare 1-800-633-4227, Social Security 1-800-772-1213, VA 1-800-827-1000, Alzheimer's 24/7 Helpline 1-800-272-3900, National Elder Fraud Hotline 1-833-372-8311, Crisis Lifeline 988

**Three local front doors:** **Council on Aging of Buncombe County**, **46 Sheffield Circle, Asheville, NC 28803**, **828-277-8288** | **Land of Sky Regional Council Area Agency on Aging**, **339 New Leicester Highway, Suite 140, Asheville, NC 28806**, **828-251-6622** | **Buncombe County Health and Human Services**, **40 Coxe Avenue, Asheville, NC 28801**, **828-250-5500**. And **2-1-1**, or **1-888-892-1162**, free and open 24 hours a day, every day of the year.

**If you only make one call from this page, make it the Council on Aging.** In a lot of counties the Area Agency on Aging does the hands-on work. In Buncombe County, much of it sits with the **Council on Aging of Buncombe County**, a local nonprofit on Sheffield Circle. It is the county's **SHIIP Medicare counseling site**, it runs a **Benefits Enrollment Center** for Extra Help, SNAP and the Medicare Savings Programs, it sends **volunteer drivers** to people 60 and older, it runs the **senior dining sites**, it delivers a **monthly bag of shelf-stable food** to homebound seniors, it installs **grab bars and shower wands**, and it hands out **Family Caregiver Support Program respite funds** for Buncombe and Madison counties. Hours are **Monday to Friday, 9:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.**, and the email is **info@coabc.org**.

**Land of Sky is the regional layer.** **Land of Sky Regional Council's Area Agency on Aging** covers **Buncombe, Henderson, Madison and Transylvania** counties. It provides **options counseling**, the regional **Family Caregiver Support Program**, **Project C.A.R.E.** for families living with dementia, the **Senior Companion** and **Foster Grandparent** programs, and the **Long-Term Care Ombudsman** for nursing home and assisted living residents. It also funds a good share of the local senior services you will see below.

**Health and Human Services is the benefits office.** Medicaid, Food and Nutrition Services, Special Assistance, energy assistance, Adult Protective Services and the county's General Assistance Program all run out of **40 Coxe Avenue**, **Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m.** The main line is **828-250-5500**. The county also runs a **One Buncombe Call Center at 828-250-6100**.

**A word on Helene.** Tropical Storm Helene reshaped almost every program on this page. Some storm programs are still running, some have quietly closed, and a few county web pages have not caught up. Where an official page is out of date, this guide says so and tells you which source we went by.

## Property tax relief (apply with Buncombe County Tax Assessment, 828-250-4915)

**The office.** Buncombe County's senior and disability relief is handled by **Tax Assessment**, part of the county Tax Department. The county's forms call it the **Buncombe County Property Assessor, Exemption Division**. The office is at **182 College Street, Asheville, NC 28801**, which is also the mailing address. The **senior citizen and disability assistance line is 828-250-4915**. The main Tax Assessment line is **828-250-4920**, and Real Estate Assessment is **828-250-4940**. Hours are **Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m.** If you go in person, the county says parking is available at the **164 College Street Parking Deck**; bring your ticket in for validation. The county does not publish an exemptions email address, so call.

**Read this before you look up the income limit.** Buncombe County's two relief web pages still show an income limit of **$36,700** and a Circuit Breaker ceiling of **$55,050**. The county's own application packet labels $36,700 as the **2024** limit, and a FAQ on the reappraisal page quotes an even older **$29,500**. The current numbers come from the North Carolina Department of Revenue's form AV-9: **$38,800 for the 2026 tax year** and **$39,900 for the 2027 tax year**. If your parent's income sits between the old and new figures, do not let an outdated web page talk you out of applying.

**The three programs.** All three are set by the state, filed with the county, and never sent to the Department of Revenue.

- **Elderly or Disabled Exclusion.** Takes the **greater of the first $25,000 or 50 percent** off the appraised value of the permanent residence. The owner must be **65 or older, or totally and permanently disabled**, as of **January 1**, and income must be within the limit: **$38,800 for 2026, $39,900 for 2027**. If both spouses are under 65 and one is totally and permanently disabled, you also file **Form AV-9A**, which only a physician licensed in North Carolina or a government agency can complete. Buncombe County says that **once you are in the program, an annual application is not required**, but the county **audits accounts receiving tax relief periodically**.
- **Circuit Breaker Tax Deferment.** Caps the tax at **4 percent of income** for incomes up to the base limit, or **5 percent** for incomes up to **$58,200 for 2026** or **$59,850 for 2027**. It needs **five full years of ownership and five years of occupancy** before January 1, and **every owner must qualify and elect it**, or no one gets the benefit. **This is a deferral, not forgiveness.** The taxes above the cap stay a **lien** on the home, and when a disqualifying event happens, such as **the owner's death, a transfer of the property, or the home no longer being the owner's permanent residence**, the **last three years of deferred taxes come due with interest**. And unlike the exclusion, **you must file a new application every year**. Understand the lien before your parent signs, especially if the plan is to leave the house to family or sell it later.
- **Disabled Veteran Exclusion.** Takes up to **$45,000** off the appraised value, with **no age or income limit**. An unremarried surviving spouse may also qualify. It starts with **Form NCDVA-9**, which must first be completed by a Veterans Service Officer. In Buncombe County that is **Buncombe County Veterans Services, 40 Coxe Avenue, Asheville, NC 28801, 828-250-5726**, Monday to Friday 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. (One county relief page labels that number as the NC Division of Veterans Affairs; the county's Veterans Services page shows it is the county office.)

**Each owner can use only one of the three programs.** When a married couple owns the home, one application covers both, and one full exclusion is available even if only one spouse qualifies.

**Deadlines and paperwork.** Relief applications are open **January 1 to June 1**. Income proof is the **first two pages and Schedule 1 of the prior year's federal return**, and the county's forms warn that applications returned without income information will be denied. If you miss June 1, the state form allows a late application **for good cause**, but it applies only to that year's taxes. Call **828-250-4915** and ask.

**The reappraisal was pushed back again.** Buncombe County's reappraisal was originally delayed after Helene from 2025 to 2026, and then state law placed the 2026 reappraisal in a **moratorium until January 1, 2027**. That means **2026 tax bills still use the 2021 values**, adjusted for any Helene damage the county already recorded, at the county rate of **61.54 cents per $100** of value. You can still **appeal the value on your current bill through December 31, 2026**. Expect new values in 2027, which is a good reason to get an eligible parent into the exclusion before then.

**Paying this year's bill.** 2026 bills were due September 1 and can be paid **without penalty through January 5, 2027**. If a parent cannot pay in full, the county's collection specialists can talk about a payment plan at **828-250-4910**.

**The grant that ended, and the program that replaced it.** For several years Buncombe County and the City of Asheville ran a **Homeowner Grant** that sent eligible owners a few hundred dollars toward their tax bill. The county now states plainly: **as of July 1, 2026, the Homeowner Grant program has been discontinued.** Older city and county news stories about it still turn up in searches; the city's page is marked as archived. What exists now is the **Buncombe County HHS General Assistance Program (GAP)**, funded by the county and the City of Asheville:

- It can pay **up to $500**, sent **directly to the bill holder**, for a **primary residence**.
- You must live in Buncombe County, have income **at or below 200 percent of the federal poverty limits**, and have **no more than $5,000 in reserve**.
- **People already receiving a tax exemption are now eligible**, which was not true under the old grant.
- **Last year's recipients must reapply.**
- Call **828-250-5500**.

**Watch for the fake federal senior exemption.** In August 2026 Buncombe County warned residents about YouTube videos that **appear to use artificial intelligence** to promote a federal senior property tax exemption. There is no such federal program. If an older parent mentions one, the county's tax office is the place to check, and anyone asking for a fee to "file" it is a red flag.

## Food

**Meals on Wheels.** **Meals on Wheels of Asheville and Buncombe County**, **146 Victoria Road, Asheville, NC 28801**, **828-253-5286**, serves **any resident of Buncombe County 60 or older who is homebound**. Meals are delivered **Monday through Friday between 9:30 a.m. and 12:30 p.m.**, up to five a week. A **$3.00 contribution per meal** is requested, and the program states that **no eligible senior is denied service due to an inability to pay**. Start with its referral form on mowabc.org or a phone call. The program does not publish waitlist status, so ask when you call.

**Senior dining sites.** The **Council on Aging's Senior Dining and Wellness** program is **free for Buncombe County residents 60 and older**. You must **pre-register**; call **828-277-8288, extension 1323**. The Council on Aging lists four sites:

- **Grove Street Community Center**, 36 Grove Street, Asheville (formerly the Senior Opportunity Center), Monday to Friday, **828-350-2062**
- **Shiloh Community Center**, 121 Shiloh Road, Asheville, Wednesday and Friday, **828-274-7739**
- **Lakeview Center for Active Aging**, 401 Laurel Circle Drive, Black Mountain, Monday to Friday
- **Weaverville First Baptist Church**, 63 North Main Street, Weaverville, Monday to Friday, **828-645-6720**

Land of Sky's older listing shows different days, hours and a different Weaverville phone for some of these sites. The Council on Aging runs them, so we went by its schedule; confirm hours when you register. The Town of Black Mountain says Lakeview **reopened its upstairs for congregate meal site dining and programs on March 9, 2026**.

**Homebound food delivery.** Separately from Meals on Wheels, the Council on Aging delivers a **monthly bag of shelf-stable food** to Buncombe residents who are **60 or older and homebound**. A home visit is required to enroll. Call **828-277-8288**.

**MANNA FoodBank.** MANNA supplies the pantries across Western North Carolina. Its **Food Helpline** helps with finding a pantry and with SNAP applications: **call or text 828-290-9749**, Monday to Friday 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. The pantry locator is at mannafoodbank.org/find-food. Note that MANNA's headquarters now lists **99 Broadpointe Drive, Mills River**, in Henderson County, so older Asheville addresses you may find are out of date. Main line **828-299-3663**.

**Food and Nutrition Services (SNAP).** Apply online through **ePASS** at epass.nc.gov, or through Buncombe County HHS at **828-250-5500**. The Council on Aging's Benefits Enrollment Center also helps older adults apply; call **828-277-8288, extension 1327**.

## Energy and utility help

**Low Income Energy Assistance (LIEAP).** LIEAP is a once-a-year heating payment. The state's rule is that **households with a person 60 or older**, or disabled persons receiving services through the NC Division of Aging and Adult Services, **can sign up December 1 to 31**, and all other households **January 1 to March 31** or until funds run out. The income limit is **130 percent of the federal poverty level**. The state has not yet posted 2026 to 2027 season dates, and last winter it pushed the ePASS opening to December 10, so check with the county in late November. Apply on **ePASS** or through **Buncombe County HHS, Public Assistance / Economic Services, 40 Coxe Avenue, 828-250-5500**.

**Crisis Intervention Program (CIP).** CIP is for a **heating or cooling emergency** and runs from **July 1 through June 30** or until the county's money runs out, so it is open now. Buncombe County says it covers households **below 150 percent of the poverty level** and can pay **electric bills, deposits, fuel oil, kerosene, gas, propane, wood, coal, heaters, furnace repairs and fans**. It does **not** pay rent, mortgage, water or prescriptions. For households with someone 60 or older or disabled, the state's resource limit is **$4,500**. The county's page says CIP requires a final notice for electric and gas, while the state page says disconnect notices are not required at this time, so apply anyway and ask. Apply on **ePASS** or call **828-250-5500**; the county's page reads as if walk-in applications are not taken, so call before you go.

**If the power bill says Duke Energy.** Duke Energy's **Share the Light Fund** helps customers with a **past-due balance, unpaid deposit or unpaid reconnection charge**. Apply through the **NCDHHS CARELINE at 800-662-7030, press 3**, or online at **epass.nc.gov**. State policy caps the benefit at **$2,500 per application and per state fiscal year**.

**If the power bill says Haywood EMC.** Part of Buncombe County is served by Haywood Electric Membership Corporation, which bought the Hominy Power Company here in 1943. Its **Project HEMC** gives **one $200 credit per program period**, winter (November through April) and summer (May through October), for members under its income limits, for example **$30,120 for one person** or **$40,880 for two**. It asks for **tax returns**, not pay stubs. **Haywood EMC, 376 Grindstone Road, Waynesville, 828-452-2281.**

**If the power bill says French Broad EMC.** French Broad Electric Membership Corporation serves part of northern Buncombe County. Its website **does not list a bill assistance fund**, so for a French Broad bill, start with LIEAP, CIP and 2-1-1.

**Natural gas.** The gas utility here is **Enbridge Gas North Carolina**. It sponsors the **Salvation Army's Heat Care Fund** for customers who need help with a gas bill, and its customer line is **1-877-776-2427**, Monday to Friday 7 a.m. to 6 p.m. Enbridge also offers **free weatherization** for income-qualified customers who heat or warm water with gas: **1-855-905-1278**. Note that Enbridge says the old account.dominionenergync.com address retires on **September 25, 2026**.

**Local crisis help with utilities.**
- **ABCCM Crisis Ministry** provides **utility and rental assistance**. Main site **24 Cumberland Avenue, Asheville, 828-259-5300**; South, 10 Buck Shoals Road, Arden, 828-259-5302; Hominy Valley, 1914 Smokey Park Highway, Candler, 828-259-5301; North, 403 Weaverville Highway, 828-259-5303. All open Monday to Friday, 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.
- **Eblen Charities**, **23 Hamilton Street, Asheville**, **828-255-3066**, says it is **currently accepting requests** for rent, utility and medical help, and that **mortgage assistance is no longer available**.

**A storm program that looks open but isn't.** The state's energy assistance page still says **Disaster Energy Applications** for Helene would be accepted **beginning January 15, 2025**, with nothing saying they are still open. Do not plan around it; ask HHS.

## Medicare, Medicaid, prescriptions

**Free Medicare counseling.** North Carolina's **Seniors' Health Insurance Information Program (SHIIP)**, run by the NC Department of Insurance, gives free, unbiased Medicare counseling. In Buncombe County, the state's counselor locator lists the **Council on Aging of Buncombe County Medicare Center, 46 Sheffield Circle, 828-277-8288**, extension **1310**, **by appointment only**. The statewide SHIIP line is **1-855-408-1212**, Monday to Friday 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Call before any Medicare Advantage or Part D decision, especially during open enrollment from October 15 to December 7.

**Help paying Medicare costs.** The **Medicare Savings Programs** (QMB, SLMB, QI and QDWI) can pay Medicare premiums and, for QMB, cost-sharing. **Extra Help** lowers Part D drug costs. Apply for the Medicare Savings Programs through Buncombe County HHS at **828-250-5500**; apply for Extra Help through SHIIP, through Social Security at **1-800-772-1213**, or online at socialsecurity.gov/i1020. The Council on Aging's **Benefits Enrollment Center (828-277-8288, extension 1327)** will help with either. We are not printing income limits here: the state pages we checked still showed prior-year figures.

**Medicaid.** Apply on ePASS or at **Buncombe County HHS, 40 Coxe Avenue, 828-250-5500**. The **NC Medicaid Contact Center** is **888-245-0179**. NC Medicaid says work requirements for adults **19 to 64** start January 1, 2027, and renewal moves to every six months; the work rule does not apply to people 65 and older.

**Staying home on Medicaid: CAP/DA.** The **Community Alternatives Program for Disabled Adults (CAP/DA)** is North Carolina's Medicaid waiver that brings in-home aide help, respite and case management so a person who would otherwise qualify for a nursing home can stay home. NC Medicaid no longer names a single lead agency per county; it says to **contact a local CAP/DA case management entity in the county of residence**. Its list for Buncombe County (dated May 2025) names **C and B Support Services Inc., 828-654-0644**; **CarePartners Private Duty Services, 828-277-4800**; **Healthkeeperz Inc., 910-522-0009**; **Primary Health Choice, 910-865-3500**; **RHA Management Services, Inc., 888-207-7828**; and **Walkers Home Care CAPDA, 252-432-6308**. CarePartners' own site lists a different Buncombe number, so if one line does not answer, try the other or ask the Medicaid Contact Center.

**Prescriptions.** Start with Extra Help above, and ask SHIIP to run a Part D plan comparison against the actual drug list. **NC MedAssist (1-866-331-1348)** is a free pharmacy program, but it serves **uninsured** North Carolinians, so most people on Medicare will not qualify.

## Home repair and safety

**Start with the programs that are actually taking families.**

- **Mountain Housing Opportunities, Essential Home Repair.** For homeowners who are **62 or older, disabled, a veteran**, a single parent, or a family with three or more dependent children. **53 South French Broad Avenue, Suite 100, Asheville, 828-254-4030**, or apply at mtnhousing.org. Woodfin homeowners have a separate partnership through MHO.
- **Asheville Area Habitat for Humanity, Home Repair.** For Buncombe or Madison County homes that have been **owner occupied for at least one year**, with a **building tax value of $450,000 or less** and income **below 80 percent** of the HUD median. **31 Meadow Road, Asheville, 828-251-5702.** Habitat says it has **restarted non-storm repairs**. Its Helene disaster repairs run through a coalition intake whose current status we could not confirm, so ask.
- **Council on Aging, Minor Home Repair.** Installs **grab bars and shower wands** for clients **60 and older in Buncombe County**, and staff help families apply to Habitat, Community Action Opportunities, Independent Living and MHO. The Council on Aging warns that **all programs have a waiting list**. **828-277-8288.**
- **Weatherization.** North Carolina's weatherization provider for Buncombe is **Community Action Opportunities, 25 Gaston Street, Asheville, 828-252-2495**. It serves households **at or below 200 percent** of the federal income guidelines, with automatic eligibility for anyone who received SSI, TANF or SNAP in the prior 12 months, and **priority for the elderly and disabled**. Its **Heating and Air Repair and Replacement Program (HARRP)** can repair or replace a furnace or air system that is failing.
- **USDA Section 504.** For very-low-income homeowners in eligible rural areas: loans up to **$40,000** at a fixed **1 percent** over 20 years, and grants up to **$10,000** for owners **62 or older**, combinable up to $50,000. Grants must be repaid if the home is sold within three years. Much of Asheville will not qualify as rural, so check the address on USDA's eligibility map first.

**Helene programs: what is open and what is not.**
- **Renew NC Single-Family Housing Program: closed.** The state says **the application period is now closed**, and Renew NC says applications are **no longer being accepted**. A Buncombe County Helene housing page still says homeowners can apply. That page is out of date. The program is now run by the **NC Department of Commerce, Division of Community Revitalization**; questions go to helene.recovery@commerce.nc.gov. If your parent already applied and was denied, **Pisgah Legal Services** handles Renew NC appeals (see Legal help).
- **City of Asheville Helene repair money** was folded into Renew NC, and the city's recovery site lists **no upcoming deadlines**.
- **Buncombe County Hazard Mitigation Grant Program: closed.** The county says applications are no longer being accepted.
- **Reduce to Rebuild: open through June 30, 2027.** Buncombe County cuts **certain permit and inspection fees by 50 percent** for rebuilding a Helene-damaged primary home. Ask the county's Permits and Inspections Department.
- **City of Asheville Home Repair Program: coming, city limits only.** The city says a call for proposals will be issued, and **applications will open in September 2026**, taken through partner agencies, for homes **within the city limits**. Questions go to affordablehousing@ashevillenc.gov. If your parent lives in Black Mountain, Weaverville, Woodfin or unincorporated Buncombe, this one will not apply.

**A check-in call for seniors living alone.** The **Buncombe County Sheriff's Office Senior Resource Program** enrolls seniors for **phone check-ins during holidays and severe weather**. The Sheriff's Office is clear that it is **not a home security check**. **828-250-4447**, Monday to Friday 8:30 a.m. to 5:00 p.m., or seniors@buncombesheriff.org. After Helene, that is worth ten minutes to set up for a parent who lives alone up a mountain road.

## Transportation

**Mountain Mobility** is Buncombe County's community transportation service, administered through Land of Sky and run by McDonald Transit. Call **828-250-6750**: **option 5** for information and eligibility, **option 4** to schedule. TTY users dial 711.
- **Most county residents are eligible**; eligibility can depend on where you live, age, trip needs and Medicaid status. Sign-up asks for a Social Security number and date of birth.
- Trips are booked **one to 30 days ahead**, reservations Monday to Friday 8 a.m. to 3 p.m., rides Monday to Saturday 5:30 a.m. to 7:30 p.m. There is **no same-day service**. Off-peak rides between 10 a.m. and 2 p.m. are the easiest to get.
- Shopping and errand trips run only on set community routes and days. Rides to the Council on Aging's dining sites are available.
- The county does not publish fare amounts; you are told at sign-up whether a fare applies.

## PACE

**Buncombe County has a PACE program, and for many families it is the strongest option on this page.**

PACE, the **Program of All-Inclusive Care for the Elderly**, takes over a person's whole care plan, doctors, a day center, therapy, personal care, prescriptions and transportation, so someone who needs a nursing home level of care can keep living at home. NC Medicaid's official service area list names **CarePartners PACE, 286 Overlook Road, Asheville, NC 28803, 828-213-8442**, serving **Buncombe, Henderson, and portions of McDowell and Transylvania** counties. CarePartners is now part of Mission Health.

**Who qualifies.** People **55 and older** who meet a **nursing home level of care** and can live safely in the community with PACE's support, inside the service area.

**Check the ZIP code.** The state list names these Buncombe ZIP codes: **28701, 28704, 28709, 28711, 28715, 28730, 28748, 28757, 28778, 28787, 28801, 28803, 28804, 28805, 28806 and 28810**. If your parent's ZIP code is not there, call CarePartners and ask before you build a plan around it.

## Frequently asked questions
**Mom is 74, lives alone in West Asheville on Social Security of about $2,900 a month, and heard about a "federal senior tax exemption" on YouTube. What is real?** The YouTube exemption is not. Buncombe County has warned that those videos appear to use artificial intelligence. What is real is North Carolina's **Elderly or Disabled Exclusion**. About $2,900 a month is about $34,800 a year, which is under the **$38,800** limit for 2026, and the exclusion takes the greater of **$25,000 or half** of her home's value off the tax bill. Her application window for the 2027 tax year is **January 1 to June 1, 2027**, when the limit is **$39,900**. Call Tax Assessment at **828-250-4915** in January. She may also qualify for up to **$500** from the county's General Assistance Program toward this year's bill; call **828-250-5500**.

**We applied for the Homeowner Grant last year. Why hasn't anything come?** Because the program ended. Buncombe County says the Homeowner Grant was discontinued as of July 1, 2026. The **General Assistance Program** replaced it, last year's recipients must **reapply**, and people who already get a tax exemption can now apply too. Call **828-250-5500**.

**Dad's roof was damaged by Helene. Can he still apply to Renew NC?** Not as a new applicant. The state says the Single-Family Housing Program application period is **closed**, even though a county page still says apply. If he applied and was turned down, **Pisgah Legal Services (828-253-0406)** handles Renew NC appeals. For a new request, try **Mountain Housing Opportunities (828-254-4030)** at 62 or older, **Asheville Area Habitat for Humanity (828-251-5702)**, and weatherization through **Community Action Opportunities (828-252-2495)**. If he lives inside Asheville's city limits, watch for the city's **Home Repair Program**, which says applications open in September 2026. And if he is rebuilding, ask the county about **Reduce to Rebuild**, which halves certain permit fees through June 30, 2027.

**Is the Circuit Breaker a good idea if we plan to sell the house after Dad passes?** Go in with open eyes. It caps his tax at 4 or 5 percent of income, but the rest is **deferred, not forgiven**. The deferred taxes stay a lien, and when he dies, the home is transferred, or it stops being his permanent residence, the **last three years of deferred taxes come due with interest**, typically out of the sale. It also must be **reapplied for every year**. If his income is under the exclusion limit, the **Elderly or Disabled Exclusion** is usually simpler, and each owner can only use one program. Ask Tax Assessment at **828-250-4915** to run both, and talk to an attorney through the **Senior Legal Helpline (1-877-579-7562)** if the house is part of an estate plan.

**My mother in Weaverville needs rides to the doctor and her Medicaid just started. Who do I call?** First the Medicaid ride. Buncombe County says Medicaid members call **828-552-5486** to set up medical trips; NC Medicaid says plan members should request rides at least **2 days** ahead and Medicaid Direct members at least **4 days** ahead. For everything else, **Mountain Mobility (828-250-6750)** serves the whole county, and the **North Buncombe Trailblazer** through Weaverville and Woodfin is fare-free. The Council on Aging's volunteer **Call-A-Ride** requires Medicaid members to use Medicaid transportation first.

**Can PACE replace a nursing home for my father in Candler?** Possibly. **CarePartners PACE (828-213-8442)** serves Buncombe County, and Candler's **28715** is on the state's service area list. He must be 55 or older, meet a nursing home level of care, and be able to live safely at home with PACE's support. Ask about Medicaid and private-pay costs on the first call.
