*Free, plain-English help for seniors and families in Sumter County, FL. Education, not advice. Verify before you rely on it.*

## Start here (works anywhere)
- 211 (dial 2-1-1), Eldercare Locator 1-800-677-1116, BenefitsCheckUp.org, Medicare 1-800-633-4227, Social Security 1-800-772-1213, VA 1-800-827-1000, Alzheimer's 24/7 Helpline 1-800-272-3900, National Elder Fraud Hotline 1-833-372-8311, Crisis Lifeline 988

**Three local front doors:** **Elder Options**, the Area Agency on Aging for this region, **100 SW 75th Street, Suite 301, Gainesville, FL 32607**, whose **Elder Helpline is 1-800-262-2243**, options@agingresources.org | **You Thrive Florida**, the agency that delivers senior services on the ground here, **Sumter County Senior Services 352-326-3540**, main office 820 Kennedy Blvd., Brooksville, FL 34601, 1-888-802-7483 | **2-1-1**, plus **United Way of Lake and Sumter Counties** directly at **352-787-7530**, 32644 Blossom Lane, Leesburg, FL 34788. Sumter County's own **Citizens' Information Line is 352-689-4400**.

**Start with the fact that makes this county different from every other county page on this site.** The Census Bureau's July 2025 estimate puts Sumter County at **157,772 people, and 57.6 percent of them are 65 or older**. Only 7.6 percent are under 18. There is no other county in the United States built like this. The Villages, which drives almost all of it, is a census-designated place that **spans three counties: Sumter, Lake and Marion**. That last sentence is the single most useful thing on this page, because nearly every program below is administered county by county. Two neighbors on the same street can be routed to two different offices, two different memory clinics and two different tax bills depending on which county line their house sits behind. Before you make any call, know which county the property is actually in. The Property Appraiser can tell you in thirty seconds.

**The second thing to understand is that Sumter County has very little senior-services infrastructure of its own, and that is not a criticism, it is a routing problem.** For a county this old, the aging network here is run from outside it. **Elder Options**, formally the Mid-Florida Area Agency on Aging, is headquartered in **Gainesville**, about an hour and a half north, and covers sixteen counties: Alachua, Bradford, Citrus, Columbia, Dixie, Gilchrist, Hamilton, Hernando, Lafayette, Lake, Levy, Marion, Putnam, Sumter, Suwannee and Union. It is the state's designated Aging and Disability Resource Center for the region. That one number, **1-800-262-2243**, is where eligibility gets determined for home delivered meals, where SHINE Medicare counseling is scheduled, where the caregiver programs live, where the elder energy crisis program is run, and where the Medicaid long-term care process begins.

**You Thrive Florida is the other half of the answer.** Until recently it was called Mid Florida Community Services, and you will still see the old name on directories, referral sheets and the door of more than one office. It is the community action agency for this area, headquartered in Brooksville, and it serves Hernando, Sumter, Pasco, Lake, Volusia, Citrus, Polk and Hillsborough counties. It delivers the meals, the in-home respite, the homemaker and personal care services, the energy assistance and the weatherization. Its Sumter County lines are direct and worth writing down separately, because the main switchboard will only slow you down: **Senior Services 352-326-3540, Energy Assistance 352-796-1425, Weatherization 352-796-1427**.

Two more numbers belong on the refrigerator. The **Florida Abuse Hotline at 1-800-962-2873** takes reports of known or suspected abuse, neglect or exploitation of a vulnerable adult, 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. And the **Long-Term Care Ombudsman at 1-888-831-0404** is who you call about a nursing home, assisted living facility, adult family care home or continuing care retirement community, rather than the facility's own management.

**Before hurricane season, do this one thing, and do it early.** Sumter County Emergency Management is required by state law to maintain a registry of people who need extra help evacuating, and the registry is operated by the Florida Department of Health in Sumter County. You register two ways: print the Special Needs Registry Form and mail it to **ATTN: Special Needs Registry Coordinator, P.O. Box 98, Bushnell, FL 33513**, addressed to "Emergency Management," or register online through the Florida Special Needs Registry. If you meet the criteria you are assigned to the **Special Needs Shelter at the Gloria Rowe Hayward complex, 227 Gloria Rowe Hayward Way, Sumterville, FL 33585**, and if your needs exceed what a shelter can handle, the health department identifies a temporary healthcare facility instead. Here is the detail worth reading twice, especially for anyone who has stopped driving: **when an evacuation is called, the health department notifies you, asks whether you want to evacuate, and a transportation representative picks you up and takes you to the shelter.** That is a door-to-door ride out of a storm, and it only works if you registered in advance. The county states plainly that it **reserves the right to cease application processing during an emergency or disaster**, so a form filed the week the cone appears on the news is a form filed too late. Questions go to the Citizens' Information Center at **352-689-4400**.

## Property tax relief (apply with the Sumter County Property Appraiser, 352-569-6800, 218 E McCollum Avenue, Bushnell)
Florida seniors apply for property tax relief through the county **Property Appraiser**, not the Tax Collector. In Sumter County the main office is at **218 E McCollum Avenue, Bushnell, FL 33513**, phone **352-569-6800**, open **8:30 a.m. to 5 p.m. Monday through Friday**. There is a **branch office at 7375 Powell Road, Wildwood, FL 34785**, same phone, same hours, except that it **closes for lunch from 12:30 to 1:30**, which is exactly when a lot of people drive over. Plan around it.

**Standard Homestead Exemption (statewide):** you must be a Florida resident who owns and occupies the property as a permanent residence on **January 1** of the year you are claiming, and the filing deadline is **March 1**. The exemption is up to $50,000, of which the first $25,000 applies to all property taxes and the second $25,000 applies to non-school taxes on assessed value above $50,000. After the first year the annual increase in assessed value is capped, and that cap is **Save Our Homes**, which over a decade is usually worth more than any exemption below.

**Now the senior exemption, and this is where Sumter County is genuinely worse than most of Florida, so set expectations before you spend a day on paperwork.** Florida law lets each county and each city decide, by local ordinance, whether to grant one or both additional homestead exemptions to low-income seniors, and each one applies **only to the millage of the government that adopted it**, never to school taxes. The first can go **up to $50,000**. The second is the long-term residency exemption, worth **the entire assessed value** of a home under $250,000 for an owner who has lived there 25 years or more.

**Sumter County adopted only the first, and it set the amount at $15,000.** Section 14-6(b) of the Sumter County Code reads, in the county's own words, "There is hereby authorized an additional homestead exemption of fifteen thousand dollars ($15,000.00) for any person who has the legal or equitable title to real estate and maintains thereon the permanent residence of the owner, who has attained age sixty-five (65), and whose household income does not exceed twenty thousand dollars ($20,000.00)." That $20,000 is the year-2001 base figure written into the ordinance, and subsection (c) indexes it to the cost-of-living index every January 1. **The Florida Department of Revenue publishes the current adjusted number, and for 2026 it is $38,686.** It was $37,694 for 2025 and $36,614 for 2024.

**Two limits on that $15,000 that decide whether it is worth anything to you.** First, subsection (d) says the exemption "applies only to taxes levied by the board of county commissioners," which the county has confirmed means all commission-levied taxes including dependent districts and municipal service taxing units, but **not city taxes and not school taxes**. Second, the county's own January 2025 workshop item put the county's budgeted millage at **4.8900 mills**. Do that arithmetic and a $15,000 exemption removes roughly **$73 a year** from the county's share of the bill, somewhat more if you also sit inside a county MSTU. It is real money on a fixed income and it is worth filing for. It is not the transformation that families who moved here from a county with a $50,000 exemption are expecting.

**The county has looked at raising it, and you should ask whether it did.** In July 2024 a commissioner formally asked the board to raise the cap from $15,000 toward the state maximum of $50,000. The county attorney supplied the statutory procedure in August 2024, and the board took it up as a **workshop, report only, on January 21, 2025**. The staff summary estimated roughly **1,778 parcels** could qualify and put the revenue impact of the increase at about **$289,090**. The version of the county code published online is codified through Ordinance No. 2025-25, adopted July 22, 2025, and it still reads $15,000. Under Florida law a change has to reach the Property Appraiser by **December 1** to take effect the following tax year, so **call 352-569-6800 and ask what the exemption amount is for the current year before you assume it is still $15,000.** That is a thirty-second call that could be worth several hundred dollars.

**Ask the same office about your city, separately.** Wildwood, Bushnell, Center Hill, Webster and Coleman each have their own authority to adopt a senior exemption against their own millage, and a city ordinance is a different question from the county's. We could not verify from a current official source which Sumter municipalities have adopted one, so rather than guess, do this: give the Property Appraiser your **exact street address** and ask which taxing authorities appear on that specific bill and which of them granted a senior exemption. If your house is inside The Villages, ask in the same breath whether the parcel is in Sumter, Lake or Marion County, because the answer changes everything above.

**The paperwork, and the deadline people miss is the second one.** The form is **DR-501SC, the Sworn Statement of Adjusted Gross Income of Household**, and the Property Appraiser lists it on the **March 1** deadline list along with the standard homestead exemption, the widow's and widower's exemptions, the disabled veteran exemption and the deployed military exemption. Then, separately, the office lists a **June 1** deadline for the financial information that backs it up. The ordinance spells out what that means: copies of the prior year's federal income tax returns, the W-2 forms, and any other documents the Property Appraiser needs, **for each member of the household**, submitted by June 1. The county code states flatly that the property appraiser **may not grant the exemption without the required documentation**. Clearing March and missing June is a real way to lose the whole thing.

**Do not overlook the other exemptions on the same counter, because several of them are worth far more than $15,000.** The Property Appraiser publishes the full list. A **$5,000 widow's or widower's exemption**, lost on remarriage. A **$5,000 exemption for a Florida resident who is totally and permanently disabled**, and another **$5,000 for legal blindness** at 20/200 or less in the better eye with correcting glasses. A **$5,000 disabled veteran exemption** for a permanent resident disabled at least 10 percent in war or by service-connected misfortune. For veterans **65 and older with a permanent combat-related disability**, a homestead **discount equal to the disability percentage**. And a **full exemption from taxation** for an honorably discharged veteran with a service-connected total and permanent disability, which also passes to a surviving spouse, and to the spouse of a veteran who died from service-connected causes on active duty. In a county with **18,301 veterans**, those last three deserve a phone call more than the $15,000 senior exemption does.

**One more that almost nobody knows about, and it is aimed squarely at multigenerational families.** Section 14-8 of the Sumter County Code provides a **reduction in the assessed value of homestead property for construction or reconstruction that creates living quarters for a parent or grandparent** of the owner or the owner's spouse, when at least one of those parents or grandparents is **62 or older**. The value excluded is the lesser of the increase in assessed value from the construction or **20 percent of the total assessed value of the property as improved**. It applies only while that parent or grandparent actually lives there, the work has to be consistent with local land development regulations, and it must be **applied for annually before March 1**. If you are building a mother-in-law suite so a parent can move in, ask the Property Appraiser about this before the permit, not after.

**A separate office handles the bill.** The **Sumter County Tax Collector, Randy Mask**, has four locations: **Bushnell, 220 E. McCollum Avenue, 352-569-6740**; **Wildwood, 7375 Powell Road, Suite 135, 352-689-4540**, with a drive-thru; **The Villages, 8033 E CR 466, 352-689-4645**, with a drive-thru; and **Sumterville, 2722 E C 470, 352-689-4630**. All are open **8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Monday through Friday**. The office cannot grant exemptions, but it controls two things that help on a fixed income. First, the **early payment discounts: 4 percent in November, 3 percent in December, 2 percent in January, 1 percent in February**, gross amount due in March, and **taxes become delinquent April 1 with a 3 percent penalty added**. Second, the **installment plan**, available when estimated taxes exceed **$100**, which requires an application returned to the Tax Collector **before May 1** with the **first payment made no later than June 30**. Call 352-569-6740 and ask them to walk you through the four due dates and the discount on each before you commit.

## Food

The program provides **home-delivered meals**, congregate meals with nutrition education, **transportation to the meal sites**, in-home respite care, homemaker and personal care services, telephone reassurance, an emergency alert response program, and information and referral. Case-managed clients may have **co-payments based on ability to pay**. Funding comes through the Older Americans Act, Community Care for the Elderly, the Alzheimer's Disease Initiative, the Florida Department of Elder Affairs and the Area Agencies on Aging, which is another way of saying the Elder Helpline at **1-800-262-2243** can also start the assessment.

Look at that list against a map. **All four sit in the older, western half of the county, and none of them is inside The Villages.** In the oldest county in America, the congregate dining program is built around the towns that were here before the retirement community was. If your parent lives in The Villages, do not assume there is a lunch site down the road. Ask about **home delivery** or about the transportation to a site, both of which the agency provides.

It is the Feeding Florida member whose service area includes Citrus, Hardee, Hernando, Highlands, Hillsborough, Manatee, Pasco, Pinellas, Polk and Sumter. In practice you will not drive to Tampa. You will use one of its partner pantries, which is what the next entry is for.

Two of those are restricted to Sumter County residents, which matters if the address in question is on the Lake or Marion side of The Villages.

## Energy and utility help
**Before anything else, find out which electric company serves the house, because Sumter County has four and the answer decides who you call.** The county's own economic development office lists all of them: **SECO Energy**, **Duke Energy**, **Withlacoochee River Electric Cooperative**, which serves south Sumter along with Pasco, Hernando and Citrus, and the **City of Bushnell**, which runs its own municipal electric service inside the city. Pull out the bill and look at the top of it before you make a single call.

- **SECO Energy is the big one here, and its headquarters is inside Sumter County**, at **330 South U.S. Highway 301, Sumterville, FL 33585**. Member services is **352-793-3801**, **Monday through Friday 8 a.m. to 5 p.m.** SECO is a not-for-profit cooperative serving Citrus, Hernando, Lake, Levy, Marion, Pasco and Sumter counties. It runs the **Angel Fund**, its humanitarian program, but the money is **administered through area social service agencies rather than paid by SECO directly**, so the co-op's instruction is to call, email or text **Florida 2-1-1** to find the agency that has funds now, or use the interactive agency map on its Angel Fund page. Ask about **budget billing** in the same call: it levels your bill by averaging the most recent 12 months of use, which is the single most underused tool for a fixed-income household on summer air conditioning.
- **EHEAP is the 60 and older crisis program, and Sumter County is unusual: there is no local EHEAP office.** The Florida Department of Elder Affairs 2025-2026 provider list shows **Elder Options running EHEAP directly** for **Hernando, Lake and Sumter** counties, out of **100 SW 75 Street, Suite 301, Gainesville**, contact **EHEAEP@agingresources.org**, phone the **Elder Helpline at 1-800-262-2243**. Most Florida counties have a hometown agency for this. Here you are calling Gainesville. EHEAP requires at least one person **60 or older** in the household, household income at or below **60 percent of State Median Income**, and a **documented heating or cooling emergency**, meaning a delinquent bill, a shut-off notice, or lack of fuel or wood. **Payment goes directly to the utility**, not to you, and eligible households **may receive more than one crisis benefit per program year** up to a cap set by FloridaCommerce.
- **Do not put a dollar figure for EHEAP in your plan.** No reliable current per-county benefit amount is published anywhere, and the figures that circulate are years out of date. Ask the office what the cap is this program year.
- **LIHEAP is the general electric bill program, and in Sumter County it runs through You Thrive Florida Energy Assistance at 352-796-1425.** Florida's rules, published by FloridaCommerce, require total income no more than **60 percent of the Florida State Median Income or 150 percent of the federal poverty level**, that you be responsible for paying the home heating or cooling bill, that you be a Florida resident, and that you be a U.S. citizen, qualified alien or permanent resident. The published household income limits are **$32,155 for one person, $42,049 for two, $51,943 for three and $61,837 for four**, rising to $85,335 for eight.
- **Three LIHEAP rules that catch people.** The local provider **pays your utility company directly**. The program **cannot pay for water, sewer or telephone service**. And LIHEAP may help with a **natural gas or propane bill only in the winter, and only if gas or propane is your primary source of heat**: if you use it only for hot water or cooking, the program cannot pay that bill. Depending on funds available in your county you may be able to apply **more than once a year, but not every month**.
- **What to bring when you apply for LIHEAP:** your most recent home energy bill, any final utility termination notice, proof of income for every household member, photo identification, Social Security numbers for all household members, and proof of citizenship or permanent residence. The state also warns that local agency phone lines run heavy and asks callers to keep trying, so call early in the day.
- **Weatherization is the permanent fix and it is a different phone number: 352-796-1427.** You Thrive Florida is the Weatherization Assistance Program provider here. FloridaCommerce sets the eligibility: total household income **at or below 200 percent of the federal poverty level**, published as **$30,120 a year for one person, $40,880 for two, $51,640 for three and $62,400 for four**, adding $10,760 for each person beyond eight. **Preference is given to residents 60 and older**, people with disabilities, families with children, and households with a high energy burden. **Renters qualify** with landlord authorization. There is **no cost** to the household.
- **What weatherization actually installs, and what it does not.** Based on an energy audit: air sealing with weather stripping, caulking and thresholds, window and door replacement, attic insulation, duct sealing and repair, HVAC repairs or replacement when justified, attic ventilation, solar reflective coating on manufactured homes, solar screens, water heater repair or replacement, and refrigerator replacement when it is cost-effective. It is **not a home repair program**. For a failing roof, see the home repair section below. Bring photo ID, proof of income for all household members, Social Security cards, a recent utility bill, proof of homeownership or landlord authorization, and documentation of age, disability or a child in the home.
- **If the power is about to be shut off and someone in the home is a senior or has a disability**, the state's own instruction is to call **2-1-1** for agencies that can help, and **9-1-1** if it is genuinely life threatening. 211 keeps the current list of church ministries and local funds that still have utility money this month, and in this county that list changes weekly.

## Medicare, Medicaid, prescriptions
- **Florida's SHIP, the free Medicare counseling program, is SHINE, and in this region Elder Options delivers it.** Counseling is **free, personalized, unbiased and confidential**. The counselors are specially trained volunteers who are **not affiliated with any insurance companies or agencies** and who **neither sell nor recommend insurance of any type**. They handle Medicare options, supplement plans, Medicare Advantage plans, prescription drug coverage, financial assistance programs, appeals, fraud concerns and long-term care planning. Call **1-800-262-2243** or email **shine@agingresources.org**. Counseling is available in person, by phone and by Zoom.
- **Sumter County has one of the densest sets of in-person SHINE sites in Florida, and four of the five are inside The Villages.** Florida's state SHINE counseling site list shows: **Chula Vista Recreation Center, 1011 Rio Grande Avenue, The Villages**, 1st and 3rd Friday, 3:00 to 5:30 p.m. **Eisenhower Recreation Center, 3560 Buena Vista Blvd., The Villages**, 1st and 3rd Wednesday, 9:00 to 11:30 a.m. **Ezell Recreation Center, 769 Marilee Place, The Villages**, 1st and 3rd Tuesday, 9:00 to 11:30 a.m. **Lake Miona Recreation Center, 1526 Buena Vista Blvd., Defender Room, The Villages**, 2nd and 4th Tuesday, 9:00 to 11:30 a.m. And the **Lady Lake Public Library, 225 W Guava Street, Lady Lake**, 2nd and 4th Wednesday, 2:00 to 3:30 p.m., which is **just over the line in Lake County** but is listed on Sumter's page because it serves the same families.

## Home repair and safety
**Sumter County runs its own repair programs out of one small office, and the terms are the part to read before you get your hopes up.** The **Sumter County Housing Division** is at **404 East Anderson Avenue, Bushnell, FL 33513**, phone **352-689-4400**, and the Housing Coordinator is **Denna Lafferty**. Note the hours, which are earlier than most county offices: **Monday through Friday, 7:30 a.m. to 4:00 p.m.**

**Emergency Home Repair Assistance** is the one most families need. Eligible repairs are **septic system repair or replacement, roof replacement, well repair or replacement including necessary wiring and plumbing, and HVAC system repair or replacement**. The **maximum award is $15,000**.

**Read this sentence carefully, because it is the difference between this program and the equivalent program in most Florida counties.** The county states that "assistance shall be in the form of a zero percent interest loan secured by a recorded mortgage and promissory note **with monthly payments required**." Not a grant. Not a deferred mortgage forgiven if you stay in the home. A recorded lien with a monthly payment attached. For a 78-year-old on Social Security whose roof is failing, that is a genuine trade and it deserves a real conversation with the family before anyone signs. It may still be the right answer at zero percent. It is not free money, and a lot of people assume it is.

**The eligibility requirements are specific.** The home must be **owner-occupied and used as a primary residence**. **Proof of Homestead Exemption is required, and all property taxes must be current.** The house **must be in good condition other than the emergency repair needs**, which means a home that needs six things is likely to be routed elsewhere. Mortgage restrictions apply. **Applications are picked up in person at 404 E Anderson Avenue, Bushnell**, or call **352-689-4400** and ask for Housing Services to schedule an appointment.

**The income limits are unusually generous, and this is the good news on this page.** The county's own Housing Repair page publishes the figures: **one person $87,480, two persons $99,960, three persons $112,440, four persons $124,960**, rising to $165,000 for eight. Compare that to the **$38,686** ceiling on the senior property tax exemption and you can see how differently these two programs are built. Sumter County's median household income is **$76,508**. A great many households that are far over the line for the tax exemption are comfortably **under** the line for home repair. If you were told no on the tax side, that tells you nothing about this side. Apply.

**The county runs two more housing programs worth asking about in the same call:** **Home Replacement Assistance** for homes past repairing, and **Disaster Mitigation Assistance**. Both go through the same office at **352-689-4400**.

**Two things that apply no matter what.** **You Thrive Florida's weatherization program at 352-796-1427** can install insulation, duct sealing, air sealing, window and door replacement and HVAC work at no cost, on its own timeline, entirely independent of the county's repair program, and it gives **preference to applicants 60 and older**. And **2-1-1** can find a faith-based or volunteer group that does small safety work such as grab bars and ramps when no government program is open.

## Transportation
**Sumter County Transit charges some of the lowest senior fares in Florida, and the discount starts at 60 rather than 65.** The catch is entirely in the process: you have to be approved before you ride, and the reservation window is narrow.

The office is at **7375 Powell Road, Wildwood, FL 34785**, open **Monday through Friday 8 a.m. to 4 p.m.** **Reservations are taken at 352-568-6683 or toll-free 1-866-568-6606, but only between 8:00 a.m. and 1:00 p.m., Monday through Friday.** The system asks for at least **48 hours notice** and states that **voicemail reservations are not accepted**. If you call at 2 p.m. you have missed the window for the day.

The fare table is short and the senior column is the whole point:

- **Shuttle route, regular fare age 16 and up: 50 cents**
- **Shuttle route, senior 60 and older: 25 cents**
- **Shuttle route deviation surcharge: $1.00 regular, 50 cents senior**
- **Door-to-door inside the service area: $1.00 regular, 50 cents senior**
- **Door-to-door out of county: $4.00 regular, $2.00 senior**
- **Children riding with a parent: free**

**How the shuttles actually work.** Two routes run **Mondays, Wednesdays and Fridays**: the **Orange / South Sumter Route** and the **Wildwood Circulator**, which has a morning and an afternoon version. The shuttles **can deviate off the route up to three quarters of a mile** to pick you up or drop you off, which is the feature that makes them usable for someone who cannot walk to a stop. Deviations require a reservation, and the county recommends **at least three days notice** for those. **Door-to-door trips run Monday through Friday.**

**Getting approved is the first step, not the second.** Anyone who wants to use the door-to-door or route deviation service must **complete an eligibility application**, evaluated under **Chapter 427, Florida Statutes**, which is the state's Transportation Disadvantaged law. Applications are available online or by calling for a paper form. The program is funded through the Florida Department of Transportation, the Commission for the Transportation Disadvantaged and the county commission, and the county states that **all trips are subject to funding as well as vehicle and driver availability**.

**Know the trip priority order before you book, because it explains the answers you get.** The county ranks trips: **medical first, employment second, nutritional third, education fourth, then everything else.** A dialysis appointment and a trip to the grocery store are not competing on equal terms. If a parent needs standing rides to treatment, say the word "medical" in the first sentence of the call.

## Caregiver support
- **The most valuable thing on this page for a dementia caregiver is a Medicare program most people have never heard of, and it comes with real money for respite.** **GUIDE**, the dementia care model Elder Options delivers, is available **at no cost** and provides a **24/7 helpline** for caregivers at any hour, **care coordination** including home safety assessments and transportation help, individualized caregiver support with regular check-ins, and, the part that changes lives, **a $2,500 annual respite allowance for trained professionals to care for your loved one at home or in a facility**.
- **Read the eligibility carefully, because the exclusion list is where Villages families get caught.** GUIDE requires **Traditional Medicare, Part A or Part B**, a **confirmed dementia diagnosis** such as Alzheimer's, Lewy body dementia, frontotemporal dementia or vascular dementia, and that the person be **living at home or in the community**. It **excludes people with mild cognitive impairment, anyone enrolled in Medicare Advantage, skilled nursing facility residents, and hospice or PACE participants**. In a county where a great many people are enrolled in Medicare Advantage plans, that one line decides whether this program is available to your family. Find out which kind of Medicare your parent actually has before you get your hopes up, and if you are not sure, that is exactly what a free SHINE appointment is for. Approval takes roughly **30 days** after the assessment. Start with the Elder Helpline at **1-800-262-2243**.
- **Elder Options carries the structured caregiver training, all free, all at 1-800-262-2243 or CaregiverSupport@agingresources.org.** **Savvy Caregiver** is a **seven-week course** built specifically for people caring for a family member with Alzheimer's or another dementia, covering disease progression, managing behavior and getting the rest of the family involved. **Coping with Caregiving I and II** are free weekly two-hour programs, the first for caregivers of any adult and the second aimed at dementia caregivers, covering stress management, self-care, communication and behavioral challenges. **TCARE**, the Tailored Caregiver Assessment and Referral program, pairs you with someone who does a full assessment and builds an actual plan rather than handing you a brochure.
- **For in-home respite, the local provider is You Thrive Florida at 352-326-3540**, which offers in-home respite care, homemaker services and personal care alongside the meals. Case-managed clients may have a co-payment based on ability to pay.
- **For a diagnosis, know which county line you are on, because it changes the clinic.** Florida's legislature funds a network of **17 memory disorder clinics operating in 13 service areas**, providing comprehensive diagnostic and referral services for Alzheimer's disease and related dementia. **Sumter County falls in service area 5**, which is served by two Orlando clinics: the **Orlando Health Neuroscience Institute's Center for Aging and Memory Disorder Clinic, 76 W. Underwood St., 2nd Floor, Orlando, FL 32806, (321) 841-2800**, and the **AdventHealth Memory Disorder Clinic, 265 E. Rollins Street, 6th Floor, Orlando, FL 32803, (407) 392-9237**. Both are roughly an hour from The Villages. **The Marion County portion of The Villages falls in service area 4 instead**, served by the **University of Florida Memory Disorder Clinic, 3009 SW Williston Rd., Gainesville, FL 32608, (352) 294-5400**. Same community, same street grid, different clinic, purely because of a county line.
- **For adult day care, use the state's own licensing database rather than a lead-generation site.** Adult day care centers in Florida are **licensed and surveyed by the state**, and the Agency for Health Care Administration publishes every licensed center in its FloridaHealthFinder facility locator, searchable by county. Adult day care basic services include **social activities, self-care training, nutritional meals, a place to rest and respite care**. The Elder Helpline at **1-800-262-2243** can also tell you which centers accept the funding streams the agency administers, which is the question that actually determines cost.
- The **Alzheimer's Association 24/7 Helpline** at **1-800-272-3900** is staffed at any hour, in over 200 languages, and 211 can point you to local support groups.

## Veterans and their families
**This section exists on this page and not on most county pages for one reason: the Census Bureau counts 18,301 veterans in Sumter County.** In a county of 157,772 people where nearly six in ten residents are over 65, veterans benefits are not a niche topic here, they are one of the largest untapped sources of money for long-term care in the county.

- **Sumter County Veteran Services is free by statute and it is in Wildwood.** The **Villages Sumter County Service Center, 7375 Powell Road, Wildwood, FL 34785**, phone **352-689-4400**, open **Monday through Friday 8 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.**, **appointments only**. The county describes it as an agency created to help former, present and future members of the Armed Forces and **their dependents** prepare claims for compensation, hospitalization, vocational training and other benefits. All services under Florida Statute **292.05** are provided **without charge to the claimant**. Anyone charging your parent a fee to file a VA claim is a reason to call this office instead.
- **Ask specifically about VA Pension.** The county's own page describes it as **a needs-based benefit for wartime veterans with limited or no income who are age 65 or older**, or who have a permanent and total non-service-connected disability. That is the doorway to Aid and Attendance, which is the benefit most often missed by families paying out of pocket for in-home or assisted living care. Ask about it by name.
- **The Villages VA Clinic is at 8900 Southeast 165th Mulberry Lane, The Villages, FL 32162**, open **Monday through Friday, 7:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.** It is one of the multi-specialty outpatient clinics of **VA North Florida/South Georgia**, and it offers primary care, mental health care, **social work**, pharmacy, physical and occupational therapy, podiatry, audiology, cardiology and more. VA's own facility listing for this clinic does not currently display a direct phone number, so route through the county Veteran Service Office at **352-689-4400** or the VA benefits line at **1-800-827-1000**.
- **The program almost nobody knows about: Veterans Directed Care.** Elder Options administers it, and it is for **veterans who need a nursing home level of care, regardless of service connection or wartime era**, who receive primary care through the **North Florida/South Georgia Veterans Health System**, which is the system The Villages VA Clinic belongs to. The veteran gets a **flexible budget** and decides what mix of services meets their needs, including **hiring personal care aides, and family members or neighbors can be hired**. A person-centered counselor from Elder Options provides monthly support with spending plans, hiring and timecards. To start it, a **VA primary care provider** identifies that the veteran needs nursing home level care but wants to stay home, or you call the **Malcolm Randall VA Medical Center at 352-264-7300 and select Option 3**. Elder Options is at **1-800-262-2243**.

## Staying at home instead of a nursing home
- Florida Medicaid pays for care at home through the **Statewide Medicaid Managed Care Long-Term Care** program. Eligibility is **age 65 and over and eligible for Medicaid**, or **age 18 and over and eligible for Medicaid by reason of a disability**, plus a determination by the state's **CARES** unit that the person is at a **nursing home level of care** and meets the clinical criteria. Plans must cover a minimum of **27 services**, including skilled nursing, personal care, assisted living facility services, adult day care, hospice, occupational, physical, respiratory and speech therapy, and medical equipment, all based on medical necessity or on delaying or preventing nursing facility placement.
- **Ask about the waitlist on the first call, not the fifth.** The state's own program page says the local Aging and Disability Resource Center is where you get waitlist information, and that ADRC is **Elder Options, 1-800-262-2243**. There is also a statewide **Elder Helpline at 1-800-963-5337** for immediate assistance. Getting on the list early costs nothing and the list is the whole game.
- **PACE, the Program of All-Inclusive Care for the Elderly**, wraps medical care, therapy, an adult day center, home care and prescriptions into one program for people **55 and older** who meet a nursing home level of care but can live at home with help. **In Sumter County, PACE exists on paper and not yet in practice, and the numbers say so plainly.**
- **Florida's Agency for Health Care Administration** monthly PACE report for **February 2026** lists Sumter County with **InnovAge PACE, status Operational, and zero enrollees**. The report explains its own notation: for counties that do have PACE coverage, "a 'zero' indicates that there are no participants in the identified PACE organization from that particular county." So the authorization exists and nobody in this county is using it.
- **The provider's own page draws a narrower map than the state's.** InnovAge's Orlando PACE center, at **8410 S Orange Blossom Trl, Orlando, FL 32809**, phone **689-284-0896**, states that it "serves Lake, Orange, Osceola, and Seminole counties." **Sumter is not on that list**, even though AHCA's authorized service area for that organization includes it. That gap between the state's authorization and the provider's published coverage is exactly the thing to resolve on the phone before you plan around it.
- **A second organization has applied and has not been approved.** AHCA's report shows **Hope** applying on **November 4, 2024** to serve **Citrus, Levy and Sumter** with 100 slots, status **Pending Federal Approval**, with the note that **CMS found deficiencies and sent a request for additional information on June 25, 2025**, and the organization "continues to work with CMS to address their feedback." That is not an available program, and anyone presenting it as one is ahead of the paperwork. Watch for it. Do not wait for it.
- **What should a family actually do with this?** Call InnovAge at **689-284-0896** and ask directly whether they will enroll someone at a Sumter County address, because the state says the service area covers you and the provider's website does not. Then, in the same week, start the **Medicaid long-term care** track through the Elder Helpline at **1-800-262-2243** and ask about the waitlist. Do both. Do not let one wait on the other.
- **Do not confuse this PACE with the other one.** Across Florida, **Property Assessed Clean Energy** is a home improvement financing program for solar, impact windows, roofs and air conditioning, repaid as an assessment **on your property tax bill**. Same four letters, entirely different thing, and it is sold door to door to older homeowners. If someone knocks talking about PACE, find out which one they mean before anyone signs anything.
- **The infrastructure that helps someone stay home without any Medicaid determination at all** is the You Thrive Florida side: home delivered meals, homemaker and personal care, in-home respite, telephone reassurance and emergency alert response, all starting at **352-326-3540**, and all funded through the Older Americans Act, Community Care for the Elderly and the Alzheimer's Disease Initiative rather than Medicaid.

## Frequently asked questions
**Mom is 81 and owns her home in The Villages. What is the Sumter County senior property tax exemption actually worth to her?** Probably around $73 a year, and it is worth knowing that number before you spend a weekend on the paperwork. Florida law allows a county to grant up to $50,000. **Section 14-6 of the Sumter County Code grants $15,000**, and subsection (d) limits it to **taxes levied by the board of county commissioners**, so it never touches school taxes and does not automatically reach city taxes. Against the county's budgeted millage of **4.8900 mills**, cited in the county's own January 2025 workshop item, a $15,000 exemption removes roughly **$73** from the county's share, a bit more if the property is inside a county MSTU. She also has to be **65 or older** and meet the household income limit, which the Florida Department of Revenue set at **$38,686 for 2026**. File **DR-501SC** by **March 1** with the Property Appraiser at **352-569-6800**, then send the tax returns and W-2s for everyone in the household by **June 1**. And call first to confirm this year's amount, because the commission workshopped raising it to $50,000 in January 2025.

**I lived in a Florida county that gave seniors a $50,000 exemption and one that erased the whole bill after 25 years. Why does Sumter not?** Because those are local ordinances, not state law, and each county and city chooses for itself. Florida Statute 196.075 allows either or both: an exemption up to **$50,000**, and a second one equal to **the entire assessed value** for an owner 65 or older with a just value under $250,000 who has lived there at least **25 years**. Sumter County's code contains only the first, set at **$15,000**, and the county's own workshop documents describe it the same way. There is no long-term residency exemption on the books here. This is not something the Property Appraiser can waive: it takes a vote of the county commission, and under state law any change must reach the Property Appraiser by **December 1** to apply the following tax year.

**My parents live in The Villages but I keep getting sent to different offices. What is going on?** The Villages is a census-designated place that **spans Sumter, Lake and Marion counties**, and almost every program on this page is administered county by county. Property tax, home repair, transit eligibility, the special needs shelter registry, even which memory disorder clinic is assigned to you all change at the county line. Sumter County falls in memory disorder clinic **service area 5**, served from **Orlando**. The Marion County side falls in **service area 4**, served by the **University of Florida clinic in Gainesville**. Before you call anything, call the **Sumter County Property Appraiser at 352-569-6800** with the exact street address and ask which county the parcel is in. That one answer saves weeks.

**Dad is homebound in Wildwood and cannot cook. Who brings him meals?** Call **You Thrive Florida Senior Services at 352-326-3540**. It is the agency formerly known as Mid Florida Community Services, and it delivers meals to homebound adults **60 and older** as well as running congregate dining sites. Ask about **home delivery** specifically, and ask about **transportation to a meal site**, which the agency also provides. The four Sumter County sites are **407 E. Belt Avenue in Bushnell**, **52 CR 572N in Lake Panasoffkee**, **98 S Warfield Avenue in Wildwood**, and the **MLK Community Center at 101 Martin Luther King Jr. Avenue in Wildwood**. You can also start the assessment through the **Elder Helpline at 1-800-262-2243**. For an immediate gap, the **Caring Hands pantry at 307 Hall St., Wildwood, 352-748-2553** distributes every Tuesday from 9:30 a.m. to 2:30 p.m.

**The electric bill is past due and my mother is 84. Who do I call first?** Two calls, and start by looking at the bill, because Sumter County has **four electric providers**: SECO Energy, Duke Energy, Withlacoochee River Electric Cooperative in south Sumter, and the City of Bushnell's own municipal system. Because she is over 60, **EHEAP** is first, and in this county the state's provider list shows **Elder Options running it directly**, so call **1-800-262-2243** or email **EHEAEP@agingresources.org**. EHEAP needs a documented crisis such as a shut-off notice, pays the utility directly, and a household can receive **more than one crisis benefit in a program year**. In parallel apply for **LIHEAP through You Thrive Florida at 352-796-1425**, where the income limit is **$32,155 for one person** and **$42,049 for two**. If she is a SECO member, ask about the **Angel Fund and budget billing at 352-793-3801**. And do not write any EHEAP dollar figure into your plan, because no current per-county amount is published. Ask what the cap is this year.

**My father's roof is failing. He is 79 and owns his home free and clear. Where does he apply?** **Sumter County Housing Services, 404 East Anderson Avenue, Bushnell, 352-689-4400**, open **7:30 a.m. to 4:00 p.m.** The Emergency Home Repair Assistance program covers **roof replacement, septic, well and HVAC** up to **$15,000**. But read the terms out loud to him before he applies: the county states the assistance is a **zero percent interest loan secured by a recorded mortgage and promissory note with monthly payments required**. That is a lien and a monthly bill, not a grant. He must show **proof of Homestead Exemption**, be **current on all property taxes**, and the house must be in good condition apart from the emergency repair. The income limits are generous, **$87,480 for one person**, so income is unlikely to be the obstacle. The monthly payment might be. Also call **You Thrive Florida weatherization at 352-796-1427**, which is free, has no lien, and prioritizes applicants 60 and older, though it handles efficiency work rather than a roof.

**Mom needs a Section 8 voucher. I applied to Sumter County and heard nothing.** That is because Sumter County does not run the program. **The Section 8 Housing Choice Voucher program was transferred to Citrus County Housing Services effective October 1, 2011.** Call **Citrus County Housing Services at 1-352-527-7520**, main office at **2804 W. Marc Knighton Court, Key #12, Lecanto, FL 34461**. Their staff take appointments inside Sumter County, sharing space with Sumter County Housing Services, so you can be seen locally. Understand the rule before you get to the top of the list: the county states that **eligible waiting list applicants are offered vouchers to live in Citrus County**, and if the applicant declines to live in Citrus County the voucher passes to the next family. In the meantime, ask about the county's list of subsidized apartment communities, including **Wildwood Commons at 352-748-0047** and **Wildwood Terrace at 352-748-0013**, and call **2-1-1** and **The Salvation Army at 352-568-2284** for one-time emergency help.

**My mother stopped driving. She is 74 and lives in Bushnell. What are her options?** **Sumter County Transit**, and the fares are very low: on the shuttle routes a rider **60 and older pays 25 cents**, and door-to-door inside the service area is **50 cents** for a senior. But she has to be approved first. The eligibility application is evaluated under **Chapter 427, Florida Statutes**, and it can be completed online or on a paper form. After that, **reservations are taken only between 8 a.m. and 1 p.m., Monday through Friday, at 352-568-6683 or 1-866-568-6606**, with at least **48 hours notice** and **no voicemail reservations**. Shuttles run **Mondays, Wednesdays and Fridays** and can deviate up to **three quarters of a mile** off the route with a reservation, which the county suggests booking three days ahead. One more thing to know: trips are prioritized **medical first, then employment, then nutritional, then education**, so lead with the word "medical" when it applies.

**My husband has dementia and I have not had a full day off in a year. What is available in Sumter County?** Start with **GUIDE**, because it is the only program here that comes with money attached. Call the **Elder Helpline at 1-800-262-2243** and ask about it. It provides a **24/7 caregiver helpline**, care coordination, and **a $2,500 annual respite allowance** for trained professionals to care for him at home or in a facility, at no cost. The catch is the eligibility: it requires **Traditional Medicare Part A or B** and a **confirmed dementia diagnosis**, and it **excludes Medicare Advantage enrollees**, so find out which he has first. In the same call, ask about **Savvy Caregiver**, the free seven-week course built for dementia caregivers, and **TCARE**. For in-home respite outside of GUIDE, call **You Thrive Florida at 352-326-3540**. For a stalled or unclear diagnosis, Sumter County's assigned memory disorder clinics are in Orlando: **Orlando Health at (321) 841-2800** and **AdventHealth at (407) 392-9237**. And the **Alzheimer's Association helpline at 1-800-272-3900** is answered at 3 a.m., which is usually when you need it.

**Dad is a Korean War veteran, 88, and we are about to start paying for aides out of pocket. Is there anything?** Two things, and both are free to pursue. First, call **Sumter County Veteran Services at 352-689-4400** for an appointment, which is free by Florida statute, and ask specifically about **VA Pension**, described by the county as a needs-based benefit for **wartime veterans with limited or no income who are 65 or older**. That is the doorway to Aid and Attendance, and it is the most commonly missed benefit for exactly this situation. Second, ask **Elder Options at 1-800-262-2243** about **Veterans Directed Care**, which gives a veteran needing a nursing home level of care a flexible budget he controls, **regardless of service connection or wartime era**, and which explicitly allows **hiring family members or neighbors as paid aides**. It requires primary care through **VA North Florida/South Georgia**, which is the system The Villages VA Clinic belongs to. His VA primary care provider can start the referral, or call **Malcolm Randall VA Medical Center at 352-264-7300, Option 3**.
