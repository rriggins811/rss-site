**Start with the single best piece of news about this county: your Area Agency on Aging is not in another city, and it does not hand you off halfway through.** M4A serves Blount, Chilton, Shelby, St. Clair and Walker counties, and its office is at 209 Cloverdale Circle in **Alabaster**, inside this county. That matters more than the drive time. In several Alabama counties the aging network and the Medicaid long-term care network are two different organizations, and a family that calls the aging agency about a Medicaid waiver gets referred somewhere else and loses a week. Here, M4A's own published service list includes both sides: **home-delivered meals, SHIP Medicare counseling, SenioRx, legal assistance, the Long-Term Care Ombudsman, Alabama Cares caregiver support and senior centers**, and also the **Medicaid Elderly and Disabled Waiver**, which covers case management, homemaker services, personal care, respite, adult day health, adult companion services and home-delivered meals for people who would otherwise need a nursing facility. One phone number, **866-570-2998**, opens both tracks. Very few counties can say that.

**The second thing to understand is that Shelby County's help is administered from three different cities, and none of the three is obvious.** Aging services run from **Alabaster**, inside the county. **LIHEAP**, the energy assistance program, is run by an agency headquartered in **Clanton**, in Chilton County, because the federal energy region groups Shelby with Autauga, Chilton and Elmore rather than with Birmingham. **Weatherization** is run from **Montgomery**, by a regional planning commission whose service area also reaches down to Russell and Macon counties. Meanwhile **Medicaid eligibility**, **legal aid** and the **regional food bank** all route north to **Birmingham**. So a Shelby County family can be sent toward the River Region for one thing and toward Jefferson County for the next, on the same afternoon, and neither routing is a mistake. Knowing which direction a given program points saves a lot of frustration.

**The third pattern is a geographic one that shows up on tax bills and in search results.** Parts of **Hoover, Vestavia Hills and Birmingham** extend across the county line into Shelby County, and Shelby County's own millage documentation lists all three as taxing municipalities here. That means a household can have a **Birmingham mailing address and a 35242 ZIP code, sit inside a Jefferson County city government, and still be a Shelby County taxpayer** who files exemptions in Columbiana. If you are not sure which county your parcel is actually in, ask before you drive anywhere. The Property Tax Commissioner's office at **205-670-6900** can settle it in one call.

**And one warning specific to this county's name, because it will waste your time otherwise.** There are Shelby Counties in several states, and at least two of them have well-optimized senior agencies. **Shelby County Senior Services**, at shelbyseniorservices.org, is in **Shelbyville, Indiana**, and runs a transit service called ShelbyGo. The **Aging Commission of the Mid-South** is the Area Agency on Aging and Disability for Fayette, Lauderdale, Shelby and Tipton counties in **West Tennessee**, and sits at 160 North Main Street in **Memphis**. Neither can enroll anyone in Alabama. If a phone number you found starts with 317 or 901, you are in the wrong Shelby County.

Three more numbers belong on the refrigerator. The **Alabama Adult Abuse Hotline at 1-800-458-7214** takes reports of suspected abuse, neglect or exploitation of an elderly or disabled adult. The **Long-Term Care Ombudsman**, reached through M4A at **205-670-5770**, is who you call about a nursing home or assisted living facility rather than that facility's own management, and the program is available to anyone, including residents, their friends or family, or employees of the facility. And Shelby County publishes a **Senior Talk Line at 205-328-8255**, described as a free telephone reassurance service for senior citizens, their caregivers, retirees and widowed persons, whose counselors call clients at least three times a week and often daily, with additional questions going to Ana Sullivan at **205-458-8987**.

## Property tax relief (apply with the Shelby County Property Tax Commissioner, 205-670-6900)
**Get the office title right first, because Alabama does not use one name for this job.** Some counties have a Revenue Commissioner. Some have a Tax Assessor. The Alabama Department of Revenue's official county office list shows Shelby County with a **Property Tax Commissioner**, currently **Jacob Tidmore**, at the **Shelby County Property Tax Office, 102 Depot Street, Columbiana, AL 35051**, phone **205-670-6900**, fax **205-670-6915**, mailing address P.O. Box 1269, Columbiana, AL 35051. That is where exemptions are claimed. Asking a neighboring county's office, or a title company, will get you a confidently wrong answer.

**What the county itself publishes.** Shelby County's own explainer on how property tax is calculated defines a homestead exemption as **a single-family owner-occupied dwelling and the land thereto, not exceeding 160 acres**, and says the owner may be entitled to it if he or she **owns a single-family residence and occupies it as their primary residence on the first day of the tax year for which they are applying**. It then lists four exemptions in the county's own numbering:

1. **Any owner-occupant under 65 years of age** is allowed homestead exemption on state taxes not to exceed **$4,000** and on county taxes not to exceed **$2,000** assessed value, **both excluding all school and municipal taxes**.
2. **Any owner-occupant over 65 years of age, having a joint net annual taxable income of $12,000 or less, is exempt from taxes on the principal residence.** You must claim this exemption, and proof of age and income is required.
3. **Any owner-occupant who is retired due to 100 percent total and permanent disability** whose joint federal taxable income is $12,000 or less receives a **total exemption**. You must claim it, and proof of disability and income is required.
4. **Any owner-occupant who is blind** as defined in section 1-1-3 gets a homestead exemption **not to exceed $5,000** of assessed value. You must claim it, and proof is required.

**Then comes the sentence that quietly costs people money, in the county's own words: special exemptions, meaning numbers 2, 3 and 4 above, must be filed ANNUALLY with the Property Tax Commissioner.** The basic under-65 homestead is not the one at risk. The senior exemption, the disability exemption and the blind exemption are. If a year goes by without a filing, the relief can come off the bill, and the first many families hear about it is when the bill arrives looking wrong.

**The statewide tiers behind that, from the Alabama Department of Revenue.** Alabama sorts homestead exemptions into four lettered categories, and the differences are worth real money:

- **H-1.** Taxpayers under age 65 who are not disabled: **$4,000 assessed value** state and **$2,000 assessed value** county.
- **H-2.** Taxpayers **age 65 and older with an annual adjusted gross income of less than $12,000**, or who are retired due to permanent and total disability regardless of age: exempt from **all of the state portion**, plus **$5,000 of assessed value** on the county portion.
- **H-3, age 65 and older.** Taxpayers **65 and older with net taxable income of $12,000 or less on the combined federal income tax return**: **exempt from all ad valorem taxes**. Not a discount. All of it.
- **H-3, disabled.** A taxpayer who is **permanently and totally disabled** is **exempt from all ad valorem taxes**, and the Department of Revenue is explicit that **there is no income limitation** on this one.
- **H-4.** Age 65 and older with **income greater than $12,000** on the most recent Alabama income tax return: exempt from **all of the state portion**, plus the regular **$2,000** county homestead exemption.

Read those against an actual tax return before you decide you do not qualify. The distance between H-4 and H-3 is the distance between a trim and a zero bill, and the deciding figure is **net taxable income on the combined federal return**, not gross income and not the size of a Social Security check. Take the return to Columbiana and let the office read it.

**A newer statewide protection that helps whether or not you qualify for an age exemption.** Under **Act 2024-344**, the Alabama Department of Revenue caps the annual increase in taxable assessed value for **Class II** and **Class III** real property, which includes owner-occupied residential property, at **7 percent**. The base year was **October 1, 2024**, for tax collections beginning **October 1, 2025**, and the limitation continues **through the fiscal year beginning October 1, 2027**. When the cap applies, taxable assessed value is set at **1.07 times the prior year's taxable assessed value**. The cap comes off when the property changes ownership, with exceptions for family transfers and transfers because of death, when the classification changes, or when significant improvements are added. In a county where values have moved as fast as Shelby's, this is worth understanding before you open the notice.

**One honest caveat about millage rates.** Shelby County's published tax-calculation document includes a municipal millage table, but that table is dated **October 2012**. The structure it shows is still useful, and it is where the fact that Hoover, Vestavia Hills and Birmingham are taxing municipalities inside Shelby County comes from. The **numbers** should not be relied on for a current estimate. Call **205-670-6900** and ask for the current millage for your address rather than working from that page.

**What we are routing rather than stating.** Several Alabama counties have added a local senior exemption by local act, on top of the state tiers, and those are county-specific by design. The Shelby County Property Tax Commissioner's exemption pages run inside a portal that will not render without JavaScript, so we could not read them directly this run and we are not going to guess at what they say. If you are 65 or older and own here, call **205-670-6900** and ask two questions in one breath: which exemption code am I on, and is there any local Shelby County exemption I have not claimed.

## Energy and utility help
**LIHEAP for Shelby County is not run from Birmingham, and that surprises almost everyone.** The federal energy assistance regions place Shelby with **Autauga, Chilton and Elmore counties**, which means the administering agency is **Community Action Partnership of Middle Alabama, Inc.**, whose main office is **116 Chilton Place, Clanton, AL 35045**, main line **205-258-5198**.

**You do not have to drive to Clanton.** The agency staffs a **Shelby County site at 56 Kelley Lane, Columbiana, AL 35051, phone 205-669-3836**. Shelby County's own food resource guide lists the same operation at 54 Kelly Lane, Building B-03, with the same phone, so ask for the suite when you go.

**The logistics matter more than usual here, because the hours are unusual.** All of this agency's offices are open **Monday through Thursday 8 a.m. to 4 p.m., closed Fridays**, and **closed for lunch from noon to 1 p.m.** Appointments are booked by calling **205-287-0139** or online. **Walk-ins are taken only from 1 to 2 p.m. each day, and no appointments are scheduled on Fridays.** Showing up at 3 p.m. on a Thursday without an appointment does not work. Showing up at 1:15 p.m. does.

**Eligibility.** ADECA, the state agency that funds LIHEAP, states that **household income must not exceed 150 percent of the federally established poverty level**, and that ADECA itself neither schedules appointments nor processes applications, so the local agency is the only real door. LIHEAP covers both **heating and cooling**, which matters in an Alabama July as much as a January.

**Project SHARE, which is specifically an older-adult program.** Alabama Power runs Project SHARE in partnership with the Salvation Army, and it **helps low-income Alabamians who are 60 and older or disabled pay winter energy bills**, with summer help available for medical emergencies when funds allow. Apply at your local Salvation Army office or call **205-328-2420**. In Shelby County the Salvation Army office is at **105 Plaza Circle, Alabaster, AL 35007, 205-663-6105**, by appointment only.

**Two smaller Alabama Power programs worth asking about by name.** Customers receiving **Supplemental Security Income (SSI) or Medicaid for Low Income Families** may qualify for a discount that includes **$14.50 toward the customer charge** and exemption from certain state utility license tax fees. And the **Alabama Business Charitable Trust Fund**, the ABC Trust, supports energy assistance for low-income families and households facing temporary hardship, delivered through the same community action agencies. Neither is automatic. Both are worth naming when you call.

**And for chronic bill trouble rather than a one-time crisis,** **Shelby Emergency Assistance at 205-665-1942** and **Community Action Partnership at 205-669-3836** both handle utility help, and **2-1-1** will tell you which one currently has funds. Calling 211 first is not a detour here. It is the fastest way to avoid a wasted trip.

## Medicare, Medicaid, prescriptions
**Free, unbiased Medicare help, from an office inside the county.** **SHIP**, Alabama's **State Health Insurance Assistance Program**, is delivered locally by **M4A**. The Alabama Department of Senior Services is direct about what SHIP is not: **counselors and volunteers are not affiliated with any insurance company and will not attempt to sell insurance**. They help you understand Medicare benefits and work out which Medicare prescription drug plan fits. Reach a counselor through **M4A at 205-670-5770 or 866-570-2998**, or the statewide **1-800-AGE-LINE (1-800-243-5463)**. M4A also runs the **Senior Medicare Patrol** for billing-fraud and scam questions.

**Prescription help through SenioRx.** The state describes SenioRx as a program that has helped thousands of Alabamians receive **free or low-cost prescription medications from pharmaceutical companies**. There are three doors in. **Category A** is **age 55 or older**, with a chronic medical condition, **no prescription drug insurance or limited prescription drug insurance**, and income within limits. **Category B** is any age with a disability. **Category C** is having Medicare and having **reached the Part D coverage gap**. What you get is a **three-month supply**, free or low cost, refillable as long as you remain eligible. Apply through M4A at **1-800-AGE-LINE**, or reach the state program at **334-242-5743** or ageline@adss.alabama.gov.

**Medicaid, and which office holds a Shelby County file.** The **Alabama Medicaid Birmingham District Office** serves **Jefferson, Shelby and St. Clair counties** from **Beacon Ridge Tower, Suite 300, 600 Beacon Parkway West, Birmingham, AL 35209**, phone **866-371-4073**, fax 877-814-8302.

**Help paying Medicare premiums, which is the most-missed benefit on this page.** Alabama Medicaid runs three **Medicare Savings Programs**. **QMB**, the Qualified Medicare Beneficiary program, **pays the Medicare Part B premium, covers the Medicare deductible, and pays the Part A premium when certain conditions are met**. **SLMB**, Specified Low Income Medicare Beneficiary, **pays the Part B premium only**. **QI-1**, Qualifying Individual, also **pays the Part B premium only**. You apply on **Form 211**, and you mail it to the district office serving your county, which for Shelby County is Birmingham. Income limits change every year, so rather than print a number that will be stale by spring, call **866-371-4073** and ask what this year's limits are for your household size. Paying a Part B premium you did not have to pay is a quiet, recurring loss, and this is a short form.

**The long-term care track.** The **Medicaid Elderly and Disabled Waiver** is the program that pays for care at home instead of a nursing facility, and it covers **case management, homemaker services, personal care, respite care both skilled and unskilled, adult day health, adult companion services and home-delivered meals**. In Shelby County it is administered by **M4A**, the same agency that handles Meals on Wheels and Medicare counseling. Call **866-570-2998**. Ask, in the same call, what M4A can start immediately while a waiver application is pending, because Alabama Cares respite, homemaker and personal care, and home-delivered meals can often begin sooner than the waiver itself.

## Home repair and safety
**Be honest about what this county does and does not have.** Shelby County is a **CDBG entitlement jurisdiction**, meaning it receives Community Development Block Grant money directly from HUD, and it operates as the **Shelby Urban County** with cooperation agreements covering **Alabaster, Calera, Chelsea, Columbiana, Harpersville, Helena, Indian Springs Village, Montevallo, Pelham, Vincent, Westover, Wilsonville and Wilton**, plus unincorporated areas. But the county's published CDBG pages describe recent projects in infrastructure and recreation rather than a standing senior home repair grant. **We are not going to invent one.** Start with the three routes that are verified instead.

**Weatherization, which is free and is the biggest of the three.** For Shelby County this runs through the **Central Alabama Regional Planning and Development Commission**, **430 South Court Street, Montgomery, AL 36104**, weatherization coordinator **Cade Gunnells**, phone **334-262-4300**, email cgunnells@carpdc.com. The state's rule is that the applicant's **income must not exceed 200 percent of the federally established poverty level**, which is a considerably wider door than LIHEAP's 150 percent. What it pays for is **minor alterations to the home based on an assessment and energy audit conducted by trained professionals**, chosen for the most cost-effective reduction in energy use, and **health and safety checks are also performed**. For an older homeowner in a 1970s house with an original HVAC system, that audit is worth having even if nothing else comes of it.

**USDA Section 504, which is the one built for older rural homeowners.** USDA Rural Development makes **loans to very-low-income homeowners to repair, improve or modernize their homes, and grants to elderly very-low-income homeowners to remove health and safety hazards**. To qualify for a **grant** you must be **62 or older**. The **maximum lifetime grant is $10,000**, and the **maximum combined loan and grant is $50,000**. Grant funds may be used **only for repairs and improvements that remove health and safety hazards, or to repair or remodel a dwelling to make it accessible and usable for a household member with a disability**. Whether a specific Shelby County address counts as rural is a question with a real answer, and it varies street by street in a county this suburban at the north end and this rural at the south. Ask the **USDA Rural Development Alabama State Office, 4121 Carmichael Road, Suite 601, Montgomery, AL 36106, 334-279-3400**, or Single Family Housing at **334-279-3440**, before you conclude either way.

**Home modifications through the aging network, which most families never think to ask about.** Two M4A programs pay for physical changes to a house. The **Medicaid Elderly and Disabled Waiver** covers assistive technology and supports for staying at home. And the **PANDA project**, for households living with Alzheimer's or another dementia, explicitly includes **home modifications** and **supplemental supplies** among its six services, at **no cost to the consumer**. Grab bars, a ramp, a bathroom change: these are the modifications that keep someone out of a facility, and they are cheaper than a month of one. Call **205-670-5770** or email panda@m4a.org.

**And the free safety program that belongs in this section as much as the caregiver one.** Project Lifesaver bracelets from the **Shelby County Sheriff's Office** cost the family nothing, because the Sheriff covers the program. Details are below under caregiver support.

## Transportation
**ClasTran is the answer in this county, and the rural rule is the part worth reading twice.** ClasTran **provides curb-to-curb demand-response service in Jefferson and Shelby counties**. Eligibility splits by where you live:

- **In the urban areas of Jefferson and Shelby counties, you must either be 60 years of age or older OR have a disability to qualify for service.**
- **In the rural areas of Jefferson and Shelby counties, anyone may qualify for service regardless of age or disability.**

Given how much of southern and eastern Shelby County is rural, that second rule reaches a lot of households that assume they are not eligible for anything.

**The practical details.** The fare is **$4.00 one way**. Service runs **Monday through Friday, 7:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m.**, and you must reach your final destination by 5:00. **Reservations can be made a maximum of 14 days in advance, and reservations for next-day service must be made by 11:00 a.m.** To cancel, call **205-325-8787 at least two hours** before pickup. The vehicle may arrive **from 15 minutes before to 15 minutes after** your scheduled time, so be ready early. **A rider may travel with one companion for the same fare**, and a **personal care attendant rides without charge** when accompanying the eligible rider between the same origin and destination. **Excessive cancellations or three no-shows in three months may result in a 30-day suspension.** On holidays, only dialysis trips are provided, and none at all on Thanksgiving or Christmas.

**To get certified,** call **205-325-8787**, or email certification@clastran.com, or mail to ClasTran, P.O. Box 10386, Birmingham, AL 35202, or go in person to 2100 16th Avenue South, Suite 55, Birmingham, AL 35205. **Allow ten days for processing.** Start that paperwork before you need the ride, not the week of the appointment.

**One thing not to count on.** The Birmingham-area fixed-route bus system and its reduced senior fare operate in Birmingham and Jefferson County, not in Shelby County. If you have read about a discounted city bus pass starting at 62, that is a Jefferson County benefit and it does not follow you across the county line. In Shelby County, ClasTran is the age-based option.

## Legal help
**Start with the aging network, because the first hour is free and it is aimed at exactly these problems.** M4A's **Legal Assistance Program provides no-cost legal assistance in non-fee-generating cases as well as non-criminal matters for people 60 years or older**. What it covers is a good list of what actually goes wrong in later life: **access to public benefits including Social Security, Medicaid, Medicare, veterans benefits and unemployment compensation**; **drafting advance directives and designating surrogate decision-makers**; **guardianship**; **housing access and foreclosure or eviction proceedings**; **long-term care financing and financial solvency**; ensuring **elder rights protections transfer from long-term care facilities to the home**; and **helping those who have experienced elder abuse, including consumer fraud and financial exploitation**. Reach it at **866-570-2998** or **205-670-5770**, or through M4A's online referral form.

**Then Legal Services Alabama, which has an income test but also a door around it.** LSA's Birmingham office serves **Shelby County** along with Blount, Calhoun, Cherokee, Chilton, Clay, Cleburne, Coosa, DeKalb, Etowah, Jefferson, Randolph, St. Clair, Talladega and Walker counties, from **1820 Seventh Avenue North, Suite 200, Birmingham, AL 35203**, local **205-328-3540**, toll-free **1-800-819-7685**. Statewide intake is **1-866-456-4995** in English and **1-888-835-3505** en Español, call center hours **Monday through Friday 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.** LSA screens household income, but it also states that it has **special grants that are not income-based, such as elder grants to assist clients 60 and over**. If you were turned away on income before you turned 60, that sentence is worth quoting back to them. The office handles civil cases only, in consumer law, debt and bankruptcy, elder law, family and juvenile law, foreclosure, housing and public benefits.

**And the two protective lines.** The **Alabama Adult Abuse Hotline is 1-800-458-7214**, run by the Adult Protective Services Division of the Alabama Department of Human Resources, whose mission is to protect elderly and disabled adults from abuse, neglect and exploitation. For a problem inside a facility rather than a home, the **Long-Term Care Ombudsman** through **M4A at 205-670-5770** investigates and resolves complaints on behalf of residents, visits each facility to evaluate conditions, and acts as a mediator between residents, family members and facility staff. The ombudsman program is **available to anyone, including residents, their friends or family, or employees of long-term care facilities**.

## Caregiver support
**Project Lifesaver here is free, and that is not true in most Alabama counties.** The **Shelby County Sheriff's Office** has participated in Project Lifesaver since 2005. The program uses a wrist-worn radio transmitter that trained deputies can track when someone with **Alzheimer's, dementia, autism or another condition that causes wandering** goes missing. The signal is only tracked when a person is reported missing, not continuously. **Sheriff John Samaniego has chosen to cover all costs for this program so that any Shelby County resident needing this service can freely and voluntarily participate.** The requirements are three: the participant **cannot operate a motor vehicle**, must have **24/7 supervision and cannot live alone**, and **a deputy must be allowed monthly home access to change the device battery**. Contact **projectlifesaver@shelbyso.com** or **205-670-6096**, mailing address P.O. Box 1095, Columbiana, AL 35051.

This is worth stating clearly because the statewide charity that supplies these bracelets elsewhere in central Alabama partners specifically with the Jefferson County Sheriff's Department, and its own guidance tells families outside Jefferson County to **reach out to your local Sheriff's Department to see whether they offer Project Lifesaver bracelets in your area**. In Shelby County, the answer is yes, and the price is zero.

**PANDA, the dementia program that comes to the house.** M4A administers the **PANDA project, Providing Alzheimer's N' Dementia Assistance**, funded by the federal Administration for Community Living and the Alabama Department of Senior Services, which provides **supportive services for people living with dementia and their caregivers at no cost to the consumer**. It uses a person and family-centered approach and delivers six things: **one-on-one caregiver training, caregiver intervention, home modifications, supplemental supplies, training and education, and self-care counseling**. Its community education arm also runs **first responder dementia-friendly training** and **elder justice advocacy** sessions. Contact **205-670-5770** or **panda@m4a.org**.

**Alabama Cares, for caregivers generally.** M4A describes it as **a caregiver support program providing supportive services for unpaid caregivers providing at least 20 hours of service per week**, offering information and referral, case management, counseling, training, and **respite care in-home, out-of-home day, and overnight**, plus supplemental services such as home modifications and assistive technology. Eligibility reaches further than most people expect. It covers caregivers of **individuals aged 60 and older**, of **people of any age with a dementia diagnosis**, of **adults aged 19 to 59 with severe disabilities where the caregiver is 55 or older**, and of **older relatives, not parents, aged 55 and older who are informally raising children under 18**. Call **866-570-2998**.

**Alzheimer's of Central Alabama, whose help is statewide.** Its **adult day care scholarships** pay **up to $300 per month**, and eligibility requires only that the **person with dementia reside in Alabama**, have a dementing illness, and **reside at home and have a caregiver**. Awards run **one year with the possibility of renewal**, and on average **forty to fifty are awarded each year**. The same organization ships **continence products** and **nutritional supplements** to the home. Reach it at **205-871-7970** or toll-free **866-806-7255**, or aca@alzca.org. A day-care scholarship is the fastest route to actual hours away from the house, which is usually what a worn-out caregiver needs before anything else.

**Alabama Lifespan Respite, with a timing warning.** The statewide respite reimbursement program was, as of this writing, **not accepting applications**, with its own page stating: **applications are closed at this time** and **please check back in October**. When it reopens, note its restrictions before you apply: you **cannot currently be receiving respite services through any other program**, including Medicaid Home and Community Based Services waivers, **only one caregiver per household is eligible**, the respite provider must be **at least 18 and live outside the care recipient's home**, and **reimbursement will be issued to the caregiver within 30 days**. Contact **256-859-8300** or al.respite@ucphuntsville.org. If you are already on the Medicaid waiver, use the waiver's respite through M4A rather than waiting for this one.

## PACE
**There is no PACE program serving Shelby County, and it is worth saying plainly so you stop looking.**

PACE, the Program of All-Inclusive Care for the Elderly, bundles primary care, hospital care, specialty services, prescriptions, dentistry, nursing home care, personal care, physical therapy, adult day care, nutritional counseling, lab and x-ray, social services and transportation for people who need nursing-home-level care but want to stay at home. It is excellent where it exists. Alabama Medicaid's own PACE fact sheet lists eligibility, and one line settles the question for this county: an applicant **must live in service area (currently Mobile and Baldwin counties)**. Those are on the Gulf Coast, at the opposite end of the state. The fact sheet also notes enrollment is capped at **up to 185 enrollees per program**.

**What to ask for instead.** The nearest equivalent available in Shelby County is the **Medicaid Elderly and Disabled Waiver**, and the good news is the phone number is one you already have. **M4A administers the E&D waiver here**, so **866-570-2998** reaches the waiver, the Meals on Wheels intake, the SHIP counselor and the caregiver program in a single call. Start there, then ask the **Alabama Medicaid Birmingham District Office at 866-371-4073** about eligibility paperwork. And ask M4A the follow-up question that saves families weeks: what can start now, while the waiver application is pending.

## Frequently asked questions
**Dad is 78, has owned the same house outside Columbiana since 1985, and lives on Social Security and a small pension. What is he actually entitled to?** Possibly a zero property tax bill. Shelby County's own published guidance says any owner-occupant over 65 with a **joint net annual taxable income of $12,000 or less is exempt from taxes on the principal residence**, and the Alabama Department of Revenue's **H-3** category says the same thing in state terms: 65 or older with net taxable income of $12,000 or less on the combined federal return is **exempt from all ad valorem taxes**. The deciding figure is net taxable income on the return, not gross and not the Social Security deposit, so bring the return to the **Property Tax Commissioner at 102 Depot Street, Columbiana, 205-670-6900** and let them read it. Then put a reminder on the calendar, because the county states that special exemptions **must be filed annually**.

**Mom's power bill is past due and she is 82 on a fixed income. Who actually helps?** Make two calls rather than one. First, **205-287-0139** to book a **LIHEAP** appointment with Community Action Partnership of Middle Alabama, and go to their Shelby County site at **56 Kelley Lane in Columbiana, 205-669-3836**. Remember the hours: Monday through Thursday only, closed noon to 1, and walk-ins accepted only between 1 and 2 p.m. Second, because she is over 60, ask about **Project SHARE** through the Salvation Army at **105 Plaza Circle, Alabaster, 205-663-6105**, or Alabama Power at 205-328-2420. If both are out of funds this month, **Shelby Emergency Assistance at 205-665-1942** handles utilities too, and **2-1-1** will tell you who currently has money.

**My mother stopped driving. She is 76 and lives near Wilsonville. How does she get to the doctor?** **ClasTran**, at **205-325-8787**. At 76 she qualifies anywhere in the county, and if she is in a rural part of Shelby County the age test does not even apply, since ClasTran states that in rural areas **anyone may qualify for service regardless of age or disability**. The fare is **$4.00 one way**, service runs 7 a.m. to 5 p.m. weekdays, next-day trips must be booked by **11 a.m.**, and a companion rides for the same fare while a personal care attendant rides free. Start the certification now and allow **ten days**, because you do not want to be doing paperwork the week of a cardiology appointment.

**My husband has dementia and I have not slept properly in months. What is in Shelby County?** Three calls, in this order. **205-670-5770** and ask for **PANDA**, which sends help into the home for caregiver training, home modifications and self-care counseling at no cost. **205-871-7970**, Alzheimer's of Central Alabama, about an **adult day care scholarship of up to $300 per month**, which is the fastest route to real hours off. And **866-570-2998** for **Alabama Cares**, which provides respite in-home, as day care, or overnight for caregivers giving at least 20 hours a week. Separately, call the **Sheriff's Office at 205-670-6096** about a free **Project Lifesaver** bracelet before there is an incident rather than after.

**We are looking at a nursing home for my mother and cannot afford it. Is there an alternative?** Yes, and it is not PACE, because PACE does not operate anywhere near this county. Ask about the **Medicaid Elderly and Disabled Waiver**, which covers case management, homemaker services, personal care, respite, adult day health, companion services and home-delivered meals for people who would otherwise need a nursing facility. In Shelby County that waiver is run by **M4A at 866-570-2998**, the same agency that handles meals and Medicare counseling. Then call the **Alabama Medicaid Birmingham District Office at 866-371-4073**, which serves Jefferson, Shelby and St. Clair counties.

**Mom is 72 and I think she qualifies for food stamps, but she will not sit through an interview.** She may not have to, but check her age against the right rule. Alabama's **Elderly Simplified Application Project** is a short form for households where **all members are age 65 or older and have no earned income**. At 72 with no wages, she likely fits. Ask for it by name at the **Shelby County DHR office, 987 Highway 70, Columbiana, 205-669-3000**. If she would rather have help with the form, the food bank's **Benefits Enrollment Center at 1-877-833-2550** will do it over the phone.

**My father is in a nursing home in Pelham and something is wrong. Can I complain without him facing retaliation?** Call the **Long-Term Care Ombudsman** through **M4A at 205-670-5770**. The ombudsman investigates and resolves complaints on behalf of residents, visits facilities to evaluate conditions, and mediates between residents, families and staff. M4A states the program is **available to anyone, including residents, their friends or family, or employees** of the facility, so you do not need his permission to raise a concern. If what you are describing is abuse, neglect or exploitation rather than a care dispute, that goes to the **Alabama Adult Abuse Hotline at 1-800-458-7214**.

**I keep finding a "Shelby County Senior Services" with a transit program, but the phone number looks wrong.** It probably is. **Shelby County Senior Services** at shelbyseniorservices.org is at 2120 Intelliplex Drive in **Shelbyville, Indiana**, phone 317-398-0127, and its ShelbyGo transit serves Indiana. The **Aging Commission of the Mid-South**, at 160 North Main Street in **Memphis**, is the Area Agency on Aging for Shelby County, **Tennessee**. For Shelby County, Alabama, the agency you want is **M4A in Alabaster at 205-670-5770**, and the county government is at **200 West College Street, Columbiana**.

**My mother's mailing address says Birmingham and her city is Hoover, but someone told me she is in Shelby County. Which is it?** Both can be true at once, and this is one of the most common sources of confusion here. Shelby County's own tax documentation lists **Hoover, Vestavia Hills and Birmingham** among the municipalities that levy taxes inside Shelby County, and the county's own **Mary Ellen Estes Senior Community Center** carries a Birmingham 35242 address while being a Shelby County senior center. A mailing address is a postal convenience, not a jurisdiction. Call the **Property Tax Commissioner at 205-670-6900** with the address and ask which county the parcel sits in, then use that answer for everything else on this page.

**Is there anything for a Shelby County veteran's widow?** Yes, and it is a free service most families never use. The **Shelby County Veterans Service Office**, service officer **John D. Jones**, is at **202 West College Street, Columbiana, 205-669-3835**, open Monday through Friday **8:00 a.m. to 3:30 p.m.** Alabama's county veterans service offices exist to help veterans, dependents and survivors file and process claims for benefits from both the federal VA and the state of Alabama. Survivor benefits are frequently unclaimed simply because no one ever asked.
