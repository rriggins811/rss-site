Main line **252-752-1717**, email info@pittcoa.com.

## Property tax relief (apply with the Pitt County Tax Assessor, 252-902-3400)

**The office.** Senior and disability relief is handled by the **Pitt County Tax Assessor's Office**, **110 Evans Street, Greenville, NC 27858**, **Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m.**, phone **252-902-3400**. The county also takes relief questions by email at taxadmin.taxrelief@pittcountync.gov. The state's official list of county assessors (revised September 23, 2026) names **Russell Hill** as Pitt's assessor at **252-902-3400**. The county posts one shared "Disabled & Senior Application" for the programs below.

**The three programs.** All three are set by the state and filed on the state's **Form AV-9** with the county.

- **Elderly or Disabled Exclusion** (the county also calls it the Homestead Exemption). It **excludes the greater of $25,000 or 50 percent of the home's appraised value**, and the county says that covers the **dwelling and up to one acre**. You must be **65 or older as of January 1, or totally and permanently disabled**, and own and live in the home on January 1. Income must be within the limit: **$38,800 for the 2026 tax year, $39,900 for 2027** (2026 income). Include the first page of the federal return or the Social Security 1099. The county says it is a **one-time application unless income goes over the limit or the residence changes**, and that periodic audits may apply. If your parent is under 65 and disabled, add **Form AV-9A**.
- **Circuit Breaker Tax Deferment.** Caps the tax at **4 percent of income up to $39,900** and **5 percent from $39,901 to $59,850** for 2027 (for 2026 the ceilings were $38,800 and **$58,200**). The owner must be 65 or older or totally and permanently disabled and must have **owned and lived in the home at least five consecutive years**. **This is a deferral, not forgiveness.** The county says taxes above the cap become a lien, and **up to three years of deferred taxes, plus interest, come due if the home is sold, the owner dies, or it is no longer the permanent residence**. It **requires reapplication every year**, and **all owners on the deed must qualify**. Understand that before your parent signs, especially if the plan is to sell the house or leave it to family.
- **Disabled Veteran Exclusion.** **Excludes the first $45,000** of the appraised value for an honorably discharged veteran with a **100 percent total and permanent service-connected disability**, or an unmarried surviving spouse. File the AV-9 plus **Form NCDVA-9**, certified through a Veterans Service Officer. Pitt County Veteran Services (below) can help. The county says it is a one-time application unless the residence changes.

**Deadline.** The county takes applications **January 1 to June 1** of the tax year. Late applications filed **June 2 to December 31** may be considered **with a valid, documented reason**, and are reviewed by the Board of Equalization and Review; nothing filed after December 31 counts for that year. The county also notes that the Post Office now requires an in-person request for a same-day postmark, so mail early or deliver it.

**The 2028 reappraisal.** Pitt County reappraises **every four years**, more often than the eight years state law requires. Today's values took effect **January 1, 2024**, and the county says **the next reappraisal will be effective January 1, 2028**. In a reappraisal year, **appeals can only be filed after the Notice of Value arrives**, and you have until the later of the Board of Equalization and Review's adjournment or the "Last Date to Appeal" on the notice. In other years, the county generally carries last year's value forward. You can ask for an informal review from January 1 until the Board convenes. The Board's remaining scheduled meetings are **November 5 and December 17, 2026, and January 28, 2027**, at 4 p.m. in the Pitt County Administration Building, 1717 West Fifth Street.

The county's pages do not say how a reappraisal affects relief that is already approved, so call **252-902-3400** when the 2028 value arrives to confirm your parent's status. If a parent's income is under the 2027 limit and they are not yet on the exclusion, **June 1, 2027** is the date to circle.

**Rates and bills.** The county rate for **2026 is 56.63 cents per $100** of value. A countywide **EMS rate of 5.95 cents** applies everywhere **except the City of Greenville**. Towns add their own: **Greenville 39.54 cents**, **Ayden 54**, **Bethel 45**, **Falkland 52**, **Farmville 49**, **Fountain 58.75**, **Grifton 55**, **Grimesland 43**, **Simpson 45** and **Winterville 45**. Fire district rates vary by area, and every household pays a **$144 landfill fee**. The county collects town taxes for Ayden, Bethel, Falkland, Farmville, Greenville, Grifton, Grimesland and Simpson; if your parent lives in Fountain or Winterville, ask the town how its tax is billed.

Bills are mailed in **July** and **due September 1**. The county says 2026 bills must be **paid in full by January 5, 2027** to avoid interest; **interest of 2 percent starts January 6, 2027**, plus **three quarters of a percent each month** after. Payment coupons come with the bill for anyone who wants to pay over time. The **Tax Collector** is at **111 South Washington Street, Greenville**, **252-902-3425**; pay by card by phone at **252-300-3552**.

**One protection worth ten minutes.** The **Pitt County Register of Deeds** offers **Property Check Alerts**, which let you know when documents are filed under a name or address you choose. The office is at **100 East First Street, Greenville**, **252-902-1650**, Monday to Friday 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. It also keeps veterans' military discharge records on file.

## Energy and utility help

**Where to apply: Pitt County Social Services.** North Carolina's heating programs are applied for through **ePASS** or Pitt DSS. The county's energy line is **252-902-1171**, at the **Human Services Center, 203 Government Circle**, Monday to Friday 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. You can apply online, by phone, in person, or by mail or fax (**252-902-1868**). Last season the county also took applications at several community sites around the county; ask which ones are open this winter.

**LIEAP.** A **once-a-year payment** toward heating bills, sent straight to the vendor. The state says households with **someone 60 or older**, or a disabled person getting services through the NC Division of Aging and Adult Services, can sign up **December 1 to 31**; everyone else applies **January 1 to March 31** or until the money runs out. Income is based on **130 percent** of the poverty level. Pitt County's own page still shows last season's dates, so confirm this season's start with DSS.

**CIP.** The **Crisis Intervention Program** is for heating or cooling emergencies, taken **July 1 to June 30** or until the money runs out. The state sets income at **150 percent** of poverty, and households with a member **60 or older** or disabled can have **resources at or below $4,500**. The state says a disconnect notice is **not required at this time**; a final notice or past-due bill is accepted with a health-related or life-threatening emergency, and **no face-to-face interview is required**. Pitt County does not post a dollar cap, so ask DSS.

**If the bill says Greenville Utilities (GUC).** GUC's **Neighbor to Neighbor** program helps low-income Pitt County households with heating bills. **Pitt DSS screens applicants and hands out the money**, **up to $200 a year per eligible household**, during the heating season, **December to March**, for any kind of heating fuel. GUC says it is meant for households, many of them elderly or disabled, whose income is just above the level other programs serve. Apply through Pitt DSS. GUC also lets eligible customers request a **10-day payment extension** at **252-752-7166**.

**Water bills.** The state's **Low Income Household Water Assistance Program** is **not open for applications** right now, the county says. When it reopens, apply through ePASS or call **252-902-1353**.

**Too hot, not just too cold.** The state's 2026 **Operation Fan Heat Relief** contact list does not name a Pitt County provider. The list says that if you cannot reach a local provider, contact the Area Agency on Aging, so call **Mid-East Commission at 252-974-1835**.

## Medicare, Medicaid, prescriptions

**Free Medicare counseling.** North Carolina's **Seniors' Health Insurance Information Program (SHIIP)**, run by the NC Department of Insurance, gives free, unbiased Medicare counseling. The **Council on Aging is the Pitt County coordinating site** for SHIIP; its trained counselors offer **private appointments** on enrollment, Medicare Advantage, Part D and the cost savings programs. Call **252-752-1717**. The statewide SHIIP line is **1-855-408-1212**, Monday to Friday 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Call before any Medicare Advantage or Part D decision, especially during open enrollment, **October 15 to December 7, 2026**.

**Help paying Medicare costs and prescriptions.** The **Medicare Savings Programs** can pay Medicare premiums, and **Extra Help** lowers Part D drug costs. The Council on Aging's SHIIP counselors cover both, and Social Security (**1-800-772-1213**) handles Extra Help. **NC MedAssist (1-866-331-1348)** gives free prescription medicine to **uninsured** North Carolinians with household income **below 300 percent of poverty**. We are not printing income limits for the Medicare programs; ask SHIIP to check them against your parent's numbers.

**Medicaid, and a reason to apply before December 31.** Apply online through ePASS or through Pitt DSS at **252-902-1110**; adult Medicaid is handled at the Human Services Center, 203 Government Circle. The county says a decision typically takes **45 to 90 days**. The **NC Medicaid Contact Center** is **888-245-0179**. NC Medicaid says that **if you apply by December 31, 2026, Medicaid will cover approved costs for up to 3 months before you applied**. Starting January 1, 2027, that window shrinks to **up to 2 months for adults 65 and older** and people with a disability. If a parent is heading toward a nursing home or already paying for care, that difference can be real money.

**Staying home on Medicaid: CAP/DA.** The **Community Alternatives Program for Disabled Adults (CAP/DA)** is North Carolina's Medicaid waiver that brings in-home aide help, respite and case management so someone who would otherwise qualify for a nursing home can stay home. NC Medicaid's list of case management agencies for Pitt County (dated May 2025) names **Pitt County DSS, 252-902-1110**; **Primary Health Choice, 910-865-3500**; **RHA Management Services, 888-207-7828**; **Spectrum Support Services, 919-954-1171**; and **Walkers Home Care CAPDA, 252-432-6308**. The list is more than a year old, so if a line does not answer, start with DSS.

**Mental health.** Pitt County's public mental health plan is **Trillium Health Resources**, whose office is at **1019 WH Smith Boulevard, Greenville**. Its **Behavioral Health Crisis Line is 888-302-0738**; member and recipient services is **877-685-2415**. The county also lists **mobile crisis through Integrated Family Services, 1-866-437-1821, 24 hours a day**, which can come to the home.

## Home repair and safety

- **Home safety checks and grab bars.** The **Council on Aging's** Injury Prevention Coordinator will come to the house **for a nominal fee** and assess it for safety problems, with a written report. The Council also installs **grab bars and hand railings**, inside and out, with **private pay and grant options based on eligibility**. Call **252-752-1717** and ask for the Injury Prevention Coordinator.
- **Weatherization and heating repair.** North Carolina's weatherization provider for Pitt County is **WAGES (Wayne Action Group for Economic Solvency)**, **601 East Royall Avenue, Goldsboro**, **919-734-1178**, which serves **Greene, Lenoir, Pitt, Wayne and Wilson** counties. The state says weatherization is free to households with income **below 200 percent** of poverty, and households on **Work First or SSI** qualify; it can include insulation, air sealing and heating and cooling repair or replacement, and the program puts **the elderly and people with disabilities** first. We are not printing dollar limits; ask WAGES to check your parent's numbers.
- **Other repairs.** We found no county-run home repair program on Pitt County's pages. Ask the Council on Aging's information desk (**252-752-1717**) or **2-1-1** what repair help is open this year.

**Emergency alerts.** Pitt County sends emergency alerts through **Hyper-Reach**; sign up from the Emergency Management page. Pitt County Emergency Management is **252-902-3950**. The county runs **medical shelters** in an emergency but calls them **a last resort**: a **caregiver must come and stay** with the person, only basic care is available, and the county asks people to bring a five-day supply of medical supplies and arrange extra oxygen ahead of time. If your parent would need help in a storm, make that plan now.

## Transportation

**Council on Aging rides, at 60.** As funding allows, the Council gives rides to **doctor appointments, pharmacy visits for prescription pickup, its nutrition sites and special events** for people **60 and older who do not get Medicaid**. Call **252-752-1717** to check eligibility.

**Outside Greenville: Pitt Area Transit System (PATS).** **1717 West Fifth Street, Greenville**, **252-902-2002** (direct) or **252-902-2001** (automated). PATS serves people who live **outside Greenville's city limits**, with **curb-to-curb** rides **Monday to Saturday, 6 a.m. to 6 p.m.** New riders fill out an intake form first; the county says to expect an answer **within two business days**. Rides must be booked **at least one business day ahead** and up to 90 days ahead, by calling **Monday to Friday between 9 a.m. and 2 p.m.** The county lists a fare of **$9.00 one way or $18.00 round trip**, paid to the driver in **cash or check, exact amount**. Ask whether that fare applies to your parent's program. Drivers wait **up to five minutes**.

**Inside Greenville: GREAT.** **Greenville Area Transit**, **600 South Pitt Street** (the G.K. Butterfield Transportation Center), **252-329-4532**. Buses run **Monday to Friday, 6:25 a.m. to 8:15 p.m.**, and **Saturday, 9:25 a.m. to 4:15 p.m.**, with no Sunday service. Every bus has a wheelchair ramp and reserved seating for older riders. The city's current site does not post fares, so call **252-329-4532** and ask about the discount fare for older riders. **GREAT+** on-demand service covers the Route 4 zone.

**GREAT paratransit.** For people whose disability keeps them from riding the bus, PATS runs curb-to-curb van service for the city **within three quarters of a mile** of GREAT's routes, during the same hours as the buses. **A physician or health care professional must certify** the disability. Call **252-329-4532**.

**Medicaid rides to appointments.** Medicaid members ask Pitt Social Services about Medicaid transportation. The county's transit page lists **252-902-1112**; the Council on Aging lists **252-902-1110 or 252-902-1111**. Ask how much notice to give.

## PACE

**No PACE program serves Pitt County today.**

PACE, the **Program of All-Inclusive Care for the Elderly**, takes over a person's whole care plan, doctors, a day center, therapy, personal care, prescriptions and transportation, so someone who needs a nursing home level of care can keep living at home. NC Medicaid's official PACE list (October 2025) **does not include Pitt County** or any of its ZIP codes. The closest program on the list, **Carolina SeniorCare's New Bern center (3681 Neuse Boulevard)**, covers **Craven, Jones, Lenoir and Pamlico** counties and parts of a few others, but not Pitt.

**What to do instead.** Ask Pitt DSS (**252-902-1110**) about **CAP/DA** (above), which brings in-home aide help, respite and case management to people who would otherwise qualify for a nursing home. Ask the Council on Aging (**252-752-1717**) and Mid-East Commission (**252-974-1835**) what else can keep your parent at home. If a PACE program opens in Pitt County, it will appear on NC Medicaid's list first.

## Frequently asked questions
**Mom is 70, lives in Winterville, and gets about $2,900 a month from Social Security and a small pension. Does she qualify for a tax break?** Likely. About $2,900 a month is about $34,800 a year, under the **$38,800** limit for 2026 and the **$39,900** limit for 2027, as long as that is the whole household's income. The **Elderly or Disabled Exclusion** takes the greater of **$25,000 or half** her home's appraised value off. The county takes applications **January 1 to June 1**. Call the Assessor at **252-902-3400**.

**Dad's house gets a new value in 2028. What should we watch for?** The county says the next reappraisal is effective **January 1, 2028**. Appeals in a reappraisal year can only be filed after the **Notice of Value** arrives, so open that envelope right away and call **252-902-3400** if the value looks wrong. If he uses the **Circuit Breaker**, he files again every year anyway.

**Should my father use the Circuit Breaker instead?** Go in with open eyes. It caps his tax at 4 or 5 percent of income, but the rest is **deferred, not forgiven**: if he sells, dies, or moves out, **up to three years come due with interest**. He needs **five consecutive years** of ownership and occupancy, everyone on the deed must qualify, and he must **reapply every year**. If his income is under the exclusion limit, the exclusion is usually simpler. Ask the Assessor's office to run both, and talk to the **Senior Legal Helpline (1-877-579-7562)** if the house is part of an estate plan.

**Mom's savings are running out and she may need Medicaid for care. Does timing matter?** It can. NC Medicaid says applications filed **by December 31, 2026** can get approved costs covered for **up to 3 months before** the application; from January 1, 2027 that drops to **2 months** for adults 65 and older. Call Pitt DSS at **252-902-1110**, and talk to the Senior Legal Helpline before moving any assets.

**Mom lives alone in Greenville and I live out of state. What can I set up today?** Sign up for **Property Check Alerts** at the Register of Deeds and for **Hyper-Reach** emergency alerts. Call the **Council on Aging (252-752-1717)** about Meals on Wheels, a Senior Wellness Center lunch, rides to the doctor and a Medicare counseling appointment. Ask about a **home safety check**. And if she uses GUC for heat and money is tight this winter, ask Pitt DSS about **Neighbor to Neighbor**.
