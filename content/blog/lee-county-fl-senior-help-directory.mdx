*Free, plain-English help for seniors and families in Lee County, FL. Education, not advice. Verify before you rely on it.*

## Start here (works anywhere)
- 211 (dial 2-1-1), Eldercare Locator 1-800-677-1116, BenefitsCheckUp.org, Medicare 1-800-633-4227, Social Security 1-800-772-1213, VA 1-800-827-1000, Alzheimer's 24/7 Helpline 1-800-272-3900, National Elder Fraud Hotline 1-833-372-8311, Crisis Lifeline 988

**Three local front doors:** the **Area Agency on Aging for Southwest Florida**, **2830 Winkler Avenue, Suite 112, Fort Myers, FL 33916**, main line **239-652-6900**, whose **Elder Helpline is 1-866-413-5337** (1-866-41-ELDER), Monday through Friday 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. | **Senior Friendship Centers of Lee County**, **12734 Kenwood Lane, Unit 85, Fort Myers, FL 33907**, **239-275-1881**, Monday through Friday 8 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. | **United Way 211 of Lee, Hendry and Glades**, **dial 2-1-1** or **239-433-3900**, answered **24 hours a day, 365 days a year**, English and Spanish

Lee County has an advantage most Florida counties do not, and it is worth understanding before you start dialing. **The Area Agency on Aging for Southwest Florida is headquartered here, in Fort Myers.** It covers Charlotte, Collier, DeSoto, Glades, Hendry, Lee and Sarasota counties, and it is the state's designated Aging and Disability Resource Center for the region. Families in the other six counties are calling into Lee County when they dial that helpline. For you it is a local office. That one number, **1-866-413-5337**, is where eligibility gets determined for home delivered meals, where SHINE Medicare counseling is scheduled, where the caregiver programs live, and where the Medicaid long-term care process begins.

**Senior Friendship Centers of Lee County is the other half of that answer, and the relationship between the two is the thing to understand.** Each Area Agency on Aging designates lead agencies that actually deliver services in each county, chosen through a competitive process and monitored against state standards. **For Lee County, that lead agency is Senior Friendship Centers**, at 12734 Kenwood Lane, Unit 85 in Fort Myers. So the Area Agency determines and oversees, and Senior Friendship Centers does. Case management, home delivered meals, dining sites, caregiver respite, and the elder energy program all run out of **239-275-1881**. If you only write down two numbers from this page, write down those two.

Lee County's own front counter for government services is **Lee County Human and Veteran Services**, **2440 Thompson Street, Fort Myers, FL 33901**, **239-533-7900**. That is the office behind the electric bill program, the home repair program, and the housing programs further down this page.

Two more numbers belong on the refrigerator. The **Florida Abuse Hotline at 1-800-962-2873** takes reports of abuse, neglect, exploitation or self-neglect of a vulnerable adult, 24 hours a day, TTY 711 or 1-800-955-8771. **Press 2 for the vulnerable adult line.** And the **Long-Term Care Ombudsman at 1-888-831-0404** is who you call about a nursing home or assisted living facility, rather than the facility's own management.

**Before hurricane season, do this one thing, and do it early.** Lee County Emergency Management runs a **Special Medical Needs Program**, free to all county residents, that provides shelter for people whose home is not safe from surge or wind and **transportation to a shelter for those with no way to get there**. Apply online at shelterapp.leegov.com or call **239-533-0640**. Read these rules before you assume you are covered. **The county stops processing applications once Lee County enters the seven-day forecast cone of a storm**, so a form filed when the news gets scary is a form filed too late. **You must update your registration every year by calling 239-533-0640.** Registering does not guarantee a space, because the Department of Health reviews each application and assigns you to a shelter that can manage your needs. **You need a companion or caregiver with you the entire time you shelter**, because staffing is limited. The Special Medical Needs Shelter is built for people whose medical needs require electricity, such as oxygen, dialysis, ventilators or nebulizers. And here is the detail that changes minds: **you can bring your dog or cat with you to the Special Medical Needs Shelter.** They are housed in a separate area, but you have access to them for feeding, walking and company. If a parent has been refusing to evacuate because of a pet, that is the sentence to read out loud.

## Property tax relief (apply with the Lee County Property Appraiser, 239-533-6100, 2480 Thompson Street)
Florida seniors apply for property tax relief through the county Property Appraiser, not the Tax Collector. In Lee County that is **Matt Caldwell**, **2480 Thompson Street, 4th Floor, Fort Myers, FL 33901**, mailing address P.O. Box 1546, Fort Myers, FL 33902, phone **239-533-6100**, open **8:30 a.m. to 5 p.m. Monday through Friday**.

**Standard Homestead Exemption (statewide):** you must be a Florida resident who owns and occupies the property as a permanent residence **on January 1** of the year you are claiming, and the filing deadline is **March 1**. After the first year the annual increase in assessed value is capped, and that cap is **Save Our Homes**, which over a decade is usually worth more than any exemption below.

**One statewide change worth knowing.** Florida voters approved **Amendment 5 in November 2024**, which indexes the second $25,000 slice of the standard homestead exemption to inflation for every levy other than school districts. The Property Appraiser published the figure as **$25,722 for the 2025 tax year**, based on a 2.9 percent CPI adjustment. It is recalculated every January 1 and by law never falls below $25,000. Ask the office for the current year's number rather than trusting an old one, and note that no application is required. If you already have homestead, you already have it.

**Now the senior exemptions, and this is where Lee County is genuinely better than most.** Florida law lets each county and each city decide, by local ordinance, whether to grant one or both additional homestead exemptions to low-income seniors. The exemption applies **only to the millage of the government that adopted it**, and never to school taxes. Most counties adopt one. **Lee County adopted both.** The Property Appraiser publishes the list plainly:

- **All Lee County millages: $50,000 and up to $250,000**
- **Town of Fort Myers Beach: $50,000 and up to $250,000**
- **Village of Estero: $50,000 and up to $250,000**
- **City of Cape Coral: $50,000**
- **City of Fort Myers: $25,000**
- **City of Bonita Springs: $25,000**
- **City of Sanibel: $25,000**

Read that first line carefully, because it applies to every homesteaded property in the county regardless of city. **The "up to $250,000" is the long-term residency exemption, and it is not a $250,000 deduction. It is an exemption equal to the entire assessed value of the property**, available to an owner **65 or older** who has **maintained permanent residence on the property for at least 25 years** and whose home has a **just value under $250,000**. Where it applies, it can wipe out that government's entire levy. Against the county's own millage, a qualifying 25-year Lee County homeowner can go to zero.

**So a Fort Myers homeowner and a Fort Myers Beach homeowner, same age, same income, same 25 years in place, get different results on the city half of the bill and the same result on the county half.** The Fort Myers Beach resident gets both exemptions against the town levy. The City of Fort Myers resident gets $25,000 against the city levy. Both get the county's full treatment against county millages. Give the Property Appraiser your exact street address and ask which taxing authorities on that specific bill adopted which version.

**The requirements and the paperwork.** You must already qualify for or receive homestead exemption, **at least one owner must be 65 or older**, and **total household adjusted gross income must not exceed $38,686 for 2026**. That figure is set annually by the Florida Department of Revenue and was $37,694 for 2025 and $36,614 for 2024. The income limitation is adjusted every January 1 by the consumer price index.

Two deadlines, not one, and the second is the one people miss. **The Sworn Statement of Adjusted Gross Income of Household, form DR-501SC, is due to the Property Appraiser on or before March 1.** Then **a copy of your current year's tax return and W-2 for all persons residing in the home, excluding renters and boarders, must be submitted no later than June 1.** Missing that June date after clearing the March one is a real way to lose the exemption.

**If nobody in the household files a tax return, there is a separate path and it is easy to get wrong.** Complete the same Sworn Statement, then **complete lines 1 through 4 of IRS Form 4506 and sign it**, which verifies non-filing at no fee. Submit the Sworn Statement, proof of age, and the Form 4506 by **March 1**, then submit **a copy of your Social Security Statement, the SSA-1099, by June 1**. Social Security mails the SSA-1099 by February.

**And if you already missed March 1, do not give up on the year.** The Property Appraiser states that statutory provisions govern late filing and income verification, and its instruction is to **file as soon as possible** so the office can approve the application in accordance with Florida law. Waiting until next year when you could have filed late is the more expensive mistake.

**A separate office handles the bill.** The **Lee County Tax Collector**, **Noelle Branning**, **2480 Thompson Street, Fort Myers, FL 33901**, **239-533-6000**, cannot grant exemptions but runs the move that helps most on a fixed income: the **quarterly installment plan**. If estimated taxes exceed **$100** per notice, you can split the bill into four payments by applying **between November 1 and April 30**. The first installment is due **June 30** with a **6 percent discount**, the second **September 30** at **4.5 percent**, the third **December 31** at **3 percent**, and the fourth **March 31** with no discount. **Lee County gives you a grace period other counties do not:** if you miss that first June 30 payment, you may still pay **through July 31**, losing the discount but keeping your place. Miss July 31 and you are dropped from the plan. Once enrolled, **you do not reapply each year** as long as the first installment is paid annually. Office hours are 8:30 a.m. to 5 p.m. Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday and Friday, and 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. Thursday.

## Energy and utility help
Lee County splits energy help by age, and it also splits by which electric company serves your house. Sort out both before you make a call.

- **EHEAP is the 60 and older program.** The Florida Department of Elder Affairs provider list names **Senior Friendship Center for Lee County**, at **12734 Kenwood Lane, Unit 85, Fort Myers, FL 33907**, contact Christin Jones, **eheaplee@friendshipcenters.org**, phone **239-387-5642**. FPL's own assistance-by-county page lists the **Area Agency on Aging for Southwest Florida, 1-866-413-5337**, as the EHEAP contact for Lee County. Either route gets you into the same program. EHEAP requires at least one person **60 or older** in the household, household income at or below **60 percent of State Median Income**, and a **documented heating or cooling emergency**, meaning a delinquent bill, a shut-off notice, or lack of fuel or wood. **Payment goes directly to the utility**, not to you, and eligible households **may receive more than one crisis benefit per program year** up to a cap set by FloridaCommerce.
- **Do not trust any dollar figure you read for EHEAP, including on the provider's own website.** Senior Friendship Centers still publishes a benefit description with a COVID-era note that expired in September 2021. No reliable current per-county amount is published anywhere. Ask the office what the cap is this year.
- **LIHEAP is the general electric bill program, and in Lee County the county government runs it directly.** Apply through **Lee County Human and Veteran Services, 2440 Thompson Street, Fort Myers, FL 33901, 239-533-7900**, or email LIHEAP@leegov.com. Applications are submitted **online**. Eligibility is Lee County residency, U.S. citizenship or qualified immigration status, an obligation to pay a utility bill directly or non-subsidized rent that includes utilities, and gross countable household income not exceeding **60 percent of the current Florida State Median Income** for households of 1 to 8 people, or 150 percent of the federal poverty level for households of 9 or more.
- **Know your utility before you open the application, because the first screen asks.** Lee County has two electric providers and the county's own instructions name both: select **"Lee County Electric Cooperative"** for an **LCEC** account, **"Florida Power and Light Company"** for an **FPL** account, and **"My utility is not listed here"** for anything else. LCEC customer service is **239-656-2300**. Pull out the bill and look before you start.
- **FPL Care To Share** is administered locally by **the Salvation Army at 239-334-3745**, which is the number FPL publishes for Lee County. It is a hardship fund separate from LIHEAP and worth asking about in the same call. The **Salvation Army's Lehigh Acres center handles emergency power assistance for both FPL and LCEC accounts, by appointment**, at **239-491-6071**.
- **Fraud warning straight from the county.** Lee County Human and Veteran Services states that **no payments of any kind, money, cash or checks, will ever be requested or accepted by an office employee**. If any staff member asks you for money, report it immediately to the Family Impact Program Manager at **239-533-2906**. Older applicants are the ones targeted by people impersonating benefit workers.
- **Weatherization is the permanent fix, and in Lee County it is run by an agency four hours away.** The Florida Weatherization Assistance Program subgrantee covering Lee County is **Centro Campesino**, which weatherizes homes in Lee along with thirteen other counties. Contact **Marixa Figueroa at 305-245-7738, ext. 236**, mfigueroa@ccfcfl.org, or Carolina Orezzoli at ext. 235. The 305 area code is correct and is not a wrong number. Work is done by Florida-licensed contractors and inspected by both Centro Campesino and local code enforcement, at **no cost** to eligible low-income homeowners or tenants. Household income may not exceed **200 percent of the federal poverty level**, and **preference is given to residents 60 and older**, people with disabilities, families with children under 12, and households with a high energy burden.
- **Be clear about what weatherization is not.** Centro Campesino states it plainly: weatherization is not home repairs or renovations, and is only intended to reduce the household's energy burden through efficiency measures such as insulation and solar reflective coating. For a leaking roof, see the home repair section below. Also note the income limits posted on its page reference the 2024-2025 program year, so ask for the current figures rather than ruling yourself out on a stale number.
- For a past-due bill right now, dial **2-1-1** or **239-433-3900**, answered around the clock. They keep the current list of church ministries and local funds that still have utility money this month.

## Medicare, Medicaid, prescriptions
- Florida's SHIP, the free Medicare counseling program, is **SHINE, Serving Health Insurance Needs of Elders**, and in this region it is delivered by the **Area Agency on Aging for Southwest Florida**. Counseling is **free, personalized, unbiased and confidential**, and the counselors are trained volunteers who are **not affiliated with any insurance company and neither sell nor recommend insurance of any type**. They handle enrollment decisions, coverage evaluations, screening for cost-savings programs, problem solving, and claims and appeals. Call **1-866-413-5337**. Counselors are available year-round, in person and by webinar. The statewide program site is floridashine.org.
- **Use them before Fall Open Enrollment, not during the panic.** Open enrollment runs **October 15 to December 7**. Around September you will start receiving notices about changes to your coverage for the coming year. Read those notices, then call SHINE, rather than waiting for a mailer from a plan to make the decision for you.
- **SMP, the Senior Medicare Patrol**, is the fraud side of the same house. Report suspected Medicare fraud, errors or abuse through the statewide Elder Helpline at **1-800-963-5337**, which is 1-800-96-ELDER.
- **Medicaid** in Florida, including long-term care coverage and the programs that help pay Medicare costs, is applied for through the Department of Children and Families at **myaccess.myflfamilies.com** or **850-300-4323**. The same single application covers SNAP food assistance and Temporary Cash Assistance.
- **Medicare Savings Programs** and **Extra Help**, the Low-Income Subsidy that lowers Part D drug costs, are screened through SHINE (1-866-413-5337), Social Security (1-800-772-1213), and MyACCESS. Florida does not run a general statewide senior drug program, so ask SHINE about Extra Help and use BenefitsCheckUp.org to find manufacturer and nonprofit patient-assistance programs for specific medications.

## Home repair and safety
**This is the section where Lee County addresses matter more than anywhere else on this page. There are three separate home repair programs, and which one you can use is decided by which side of a city line your parent's house sits on.** Get this wrong and you spend a month in the wrong queue.

**If the home is in unincorporated Lee County:** the county's **Home Repair Assistance Program**, through Human and Veteran Services, **239-533-7900**, HVSHousing@leegov.com. Apply online and **select the "Owner-Occupied Housing Rehabilitation" option**, which is the step people miss. Eligible repairs are **roof repair or replacement, plumbing, septic repair or replacement, heating and air conditioning, well repair or replacement, window replacement, electrical, and door repair or replacement**. The **minimum assistance amount is $1,000**, and the money is secured as a deferred mortgage you must own and occupy the home through.

The county's requirements are specific and worth reading before you apply. You must **live in unincorporated Lee County, not within a city**. You must have **owned and continuously lived in the home for at least the past 12 months**, have annual income at or below HUD's income levels for Lee County, be **current on all mortgages, property taxes and homeowner association fees**, obtain hazard and flood insurance if applicable, and the property must be **homesteaded and free of excessive clutter**. It cannot be **listed for sale**, cannot be in **bankruptcy, foreclosure or probate**, and **everyone listed on the deed must live there**. Excluded: **maintenance-type repairs**, married couples living separately, **mobile homes on rented or leased lots**, and mold damage exceeding one square foot.

**If the home is in the City of Fort Myers:** the city runs its own **Home Repair and Rehabilitation** program out of the Housing Division, **1825 Hendry St., Fort Myers, FL 33901**, **239-321-7970**. It is aimed at repairs that improve health, safety and structural integrity, **emergency repairs receive priority**, and the **maximum assistance is $15,000 as a grant**. A grant, not a deferred loan, is a meaningfully different thing for an older homeowner, and it is the reason a Fort Myers address is worth confirming before you assume the county program is the answer. Requirements mirror the county's: city residency, HUD income limits for Lee County, occupancy, current mortgage and taxes, hazard and flood insurance, homesteaded, free of excessive clutter, no bankruptcy, foreclosure or probate. Maintenance-type repairs are not eligible.

**If the home is in Cape Coral:** there are two doors and neither is the county. **The City of Cape Coral partners with Rebuilding Together Greater Florida** to provide **no-cost home repairs** for income-eligible homeowners, including **essential repairs, accessibility upgrades, roofing, plumbing and electrical work**, plus disaster readiness and recovery support. Check eligibility and apply at **RTGFL.org**, or call **239-229-6392** for help with the application. To qualify you must own and live in the home, **not own other residential property**, meet income requirements, and be **current on mortgage and property taxes**.

The second Cape Coral door is the **Emergency Owner Occupied Home Repair Program**, run by **Habitat for Humanity of Lee and Hendry Counties** in partnership with the city, **239-652-4663**, homerepairs@habitat4humanity.org. Qualifying repairs are **roof, HVAC, plumbing, electrical, well and septic**. The home must be your **primary residence in Cape Coral with homestead status**, mortgage and property taxes current, homeowner's and flood insurance in place where applicable, and home value within HUD's maximum price limits. **Note one disqualifier that catches seniors specifically: no reverse mortgages, property tax liens, IRS liens or judgments.** If a parent took a reverse mortgage, this program is closed to them and you should find that out now rather than after an inspection.

**Two things that apply no matter the address.** **Centro Campesino's weatherization program** at **305-245-7738 ext. 236** can install insulation and efficiency measures at no cost, on its own timeline, independent of any of the above. And **2-1-1** or **239-433-3900** can find a faith-based or volunteer group that does small safety work such as grab bars and ramps when no government program is open.

## Rent, housing, emergency cash
**Start with a closure, because it is recent and it is the program most families are sent to first.** Lee County Human and Veteran Services states that **effective June 16, 2026, due to limited funding, it is no longer accepting applications for the Rent/Mortgage and Water Financial Assistance Program.** The county asks residents to check its website for future updates. If someone tells you to apply to the county for back rent, that is why nothing is happening. Do not spend a week on it.

## Transportation
**Lee County's bus discount starts at 65, and the savings are real, but you cannot just tell the driver your age.** LeeTran requires a reduced fare **TropiCard**, and you have to show it every single time you board.

The fare table is short. **Standard cash fare is $1.50** on fixed-route buses and **75 cents** on the beach trolley. **Discounted fares apply to seniors 65 and older, people with disabilities, and full-time students:**

- **Cash fare: 75 cents**
- **Senior/Disabled 7-Day Pass: $11.00**
- **Senior/Disabled 31-Day Pass: $23.00**
- **Senior/Disabled 12-Trip Pass: $6.50**
- **Trolley fare: 35 cents**

**Children 6 and under ride free** on fixed-route buses. Day passes run for **consecutive days**, and the countdown starts the first time the pass is tapped, so a 31-Day Pass bought and used on the first of the month runs out whether you rode 31 times or three. All fares paid on board require **exact change**, because drivers cannot make change.

**Getting the card is the step to plan for.** Bring **proof of age plus one form of photo ID** to the **Rosa Parks Transportation Center, 2250 Widman Way, Fort Myers**, staffed **Monday through Friday 8 a.m. to 5 p.m.** For a disability-based discount, acceptable proof includes a statement from a doctor, health care professional, government agency or approved social agency, **a red, white and blue Medicare card**, or a valid Disabled Persons Parking Identification Permit with Florida placard registration.

**If the regular bus will not work, that is Passport, and Lee County's rules are stricter than some neighboring counties. Age alone does not qualify you.**

- **Passport ADA service** requires that a **disability prevents you from using the fixed route bus**, and that **both the origin and destination of your trip fall within three quarters of a mile of a route**. Trips outside that corridor are not eligible for ADA service. LeeTran publishes an **interactive map** where you can enter an address and see whether it falls inside the blue corridor. Eligibility comes in three categories, including "conditional" eligibility for someone who can use the bus under some conditions and not others. Call the **Passport Office at 239-533-0300**, weekdays 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., for the application and medical certification form. **Visitors** with ADA documentation from another transit agency may use Passport while visiting, and those with documentation of a disability but no ADA determination may be eligible for up to **21 days**.
- **The Transportation Disadvantaged (TD) program is the other door, and it runs on income rather than age.** State law defines transportation disadvantaged to include people unable to transport themselves or to purchase transportation because of disability, income status **or age**, but LeeTran's screening is a two-step process where the practical test is money: **annual household income must be at or below 225 percent of the federal poverty guidelines**, which for **2026** LeeTran publishes as **$35,910 for one person, $48,690 for two, $61,470 for three, and $74,250 for four**. **Proof of income is required and self-declaration is not accepted.** Acceptable proof includes a tax return, a Social Security income letter (SSA, SSI or SSDI), a retirement, pension or investment statement including VA benefits, two recent pay stubs, or a SNAP, TANF or DCF benefits letter.
- **Two TD rules that surprise people.** **The origin or destination must be located outside the three-quarter-mile ADA corridor**, so TD is the program for the parts of the county the bus does not reach, not a general-purpose door-to-door service. And **the program does not transport a guest**. TD service runs **7 days a week, 6 a.m. to 7 p.m.**, reservations must be made **two days in advance**, and you may be asked to be ready **120 minutes before pickup**. You must **recertify annually**.
- **The free bus pass nobody mentions.** Income-eligible TD applicants also qualify for the **TD Fixed Route Bus Pass Program**, which provides a **30-day or 31-day LeeTran bus pass at no charge**, picked up at the **Rosa Parks Transfer Center, 2250 Widman Way**. Renew annually. Contact **Virginia Walters, Paratransit Senior Operations Supervisor, 239-533-0353**, or the Passport office at **239-533-0300**, mail to LeeTran TD Bus Program, 3401 Metro Parkway, Fort Myers, FL 33901.
- **Booking got easier in a way that matters for a family member helping from out of town.** Passport riders can now book, track and cancel trips through the **Passport app or at LeeTranPassport.App.RideWithVia.com**, 24 hours a day, up to **14 days in advance**, with real-time vehicle tracking. ADA riders must book at least one day ahead; TD riders at least two.
- Complaints about TD service go to the **Florida Commission for the Transportation Disadvantaged Ombudsman Hotline, 1-800-983-2435**.

## Caregiver support
- **The most useful thing in Lee County for a worn-out caregiver is free, and it happens at night.** Senior Friendship Centers runs **RELIEF, which stands for Respite for Elders Living in Everyday Families**, giving caregivers a few hours off. The detail that makes it work: **it is offered during nights and weekends, beyond the normal workday**, which is exactly when adult day programs are closed and exactly when a caregiver might want a dinner, a movie or a walk. **The service is provided free of charge.** Call **239-275-1881**.
- **Friendship at Home** is the answer to isolation, especially after a spouse dies. Volunteers provide **telephone reassurance** with a cheery call once or twice a week, a **friendly visitor program** matching a volunteer with a senior for conversation, errands or games, and **supportive intervention** helping people with limited mobility reach community resources. Same number, **239-275-1881**.
- **The Area Agency on Aging for Southwest Florida carries the structured caregiver programs**, all at **1-866-413-5337**. These are open to **caregivers of any age caring for an adult 60 or older, and to adults 60 or older caring for a person of any age**. **TCARE**, the Tailored Caregiver Assessment and Referral program, pairs you one-on-one with a specialist to build an actual plan rather than hand you a brochure. **Powerful Tools for Caregivers** is a six-session evidence-based series on confidence, self-care and managing emotions. **Savvy Caregiver** is a separate six-session series built specifically for people caring for someone with Alzheimer's or another dementia, shown to decrease caregiver distress, burden and depression. The agency also offers a **free caregiver support platform through a partnership with Caregiving.com**, and it will bring a class to your facility if you ask.
- **For diagnosis, Lee County has its own state-designated memory clinic, which many Florida counties do not.** Florida's legislature funds a network of **17 Memory Disorder Clinics across 13 service areas** providing comprehensive diagnostic and referral services for Alzheimer's disease and related dementia. The one serving this area is the **Lee Physicians Group Memory Care Clinic, 12600 Creekside Lane, Suite 7, Fort Myers, FL 33919, 239-343-9220**. If you are chasing a diagnosis and getting nowhere, that is the door.
- The **Alzheimer's Association 24/7 Helpline** (**1-800-272-3900**) is staffed at any hour, and 211 can point you to local support groups.

## Staying at home instead of a nursing home
- Florida Medicaid pays for care at home through the **Statewide Medicaid Managed Care Long-Term Care** program, for people **65 and older who are Medicaid eligible**, or **18 and older and Medicaid eligible by reason of a disability**, who are also determined by the state's **CARES** unit to meet a **nursing home level of care**. Plans must cover a long list at minimum, including adult companion care, adult day care, assisted living facility services, attendant care, caregiver training, case management, home accessibility adaptation, home delivered meals, homemaker services, hospice, skilled nursing, medical equipment and supplies, nursing facility care, occupational, physical, respiratory and speech therapy, personal care, personal emergency response systems, respite care and transportation. **Start with the Elder Helpline at 1-866-413-5337**, ask about the priority list on that first call, and start early.
- **The Area Agency also oversees the non-Medicaid home care programs, and they are worth asking about in the same call.** Funded through the Older Americans Act, the National Family Caregiver Support Program, Community Care for the Elderly, Home Care for the Elderly and the Alzheimer's Disease Initiative, these cover adult day care, personal care, case management, home delivered and congregate meals, transportation, caregiver training, support and respite, housekeeping, home health aide, legal assistance and shopping assistance. Eligibility is determined by the Florida Department of Elder Affairs, and **in Lee County the services are delivered by Senior Friendship Centers**. The agency makes the arithmetic plain: a typical in-home care plan runs about **$17,000 a year** against a nursing facility averaging **$177 a day**, close to $65,000 a year.
- **PACE, the Program of All-Inclusive Care for the Elderly**, wraps medical care, therapy, an adult day center, home care and prescriptions into one program for people **55 and older** who meet a nursing home level of care but can live at home with help. **Lee County has one of the strongest PACE programs in Florida, and the numbers are the opposite of what families usually find.**
- **Florida's Agency for Health Care Administration** monthly PACE report for **February 2026** lists **Lee County with Hope PACE, status Operational, and 437 enrollees present for the current month**, against **580 slots approved and 580 funded**, with initial AHCA approval on **January 22, 2008**. That is nearly two decades of operation and a program running at real scale. No other PACE organization is listed for Lee County.
- **Three of Hope PACE's five centers are inside Lee County**, which is unusual and matters when you are weighing a daily drive: **2668 Winkler Avenue, Fort Myers, FL 33901**; **1435 Santa Barbara Blvd., Cape Coral, FL 33991**; and **1201 Wings Way, Lehigh Acres, FL 33936**. The other two are in Port Charlotte and Naples. The program covers **primary and specialty medical care, medications, home care and socialization**. Call **239-985-6400** or toll-free **866-659-7223**, with **800-995-8771** for hearing-impaired callers. Hope Healthcare's main line is **800-835-1673** or **239-482-4673**.
- **One caution about a number you may find online.** Hope Healthcare publishes **239-482-6259** on its site as its **fax**, and it circulates in directories as though it were a phone line. Use the numbers above.
- **What should a family actually do with this?** Call, and also start the **Medicaid long-term care** track through the Elder Helpline at **1-866-413-5337** at the same time. With 437 of 580 slots filled, capacity is finite here in a way it is not in counties where PACE sits nearly empty, so an early call is worth more than a perfect one.
- **Do not confuse this PACE with the other one.** Across Florida, **Property Assessed Clean Energy** is a home improvement financing program for solar, impact windows, roofs and air conditioning, repaid as an assessment **on your property tax bill**. Same four letters, entirely different thing, and it is sold door to door to older homeowners. If someone knocks talking about PACE, find out which one they mean before anyone signs anything.
- The county's own infrastructure also helps someone stay home longer without any Medicaid determination: **Senior Friendship Centers case management, meals, RELIEF respite and Friendship at Home**, all starting at **239-275-1881**.

## Frequently asked questions
**Mom is 79, has owned her house in unincorporated Lee County since 1994, and it is worth about $210,000. What is the senior exemption actually worth to her?** Potentially the entire county portion of her bill, and this is where Lee County beats most of Florida. The county adopted **both** senior exemptions rather than one. The second one, the **long-term residency exemption**, is an exemption **equal to the assessed value of the property** for an owner **65 or older** who has kept permanent residence there **at least 25 years** on a home with **just value under $250,000**. She is at 32 years and under the value cap. She still has to clear the income test, **household adjusted gross income at or below $38,686 for 2026**, and she must already qualify for homestead. File the **DR-501SC by March 1** with the Property Appraiser at **239-533-6100**, then send the tax returns and W-2s for everyone in the household **by June 1**. And remember none of it touches school taxes.

**We live inside the City of Fort Myers. Do we get that same 25-year break?** Not against the city's portion. The Property Appraiser publishes the adoptions by jurisdiction, and the **City of Fort Myers adopted $25,000**, the same as Sanibel and Bonita Springs. **Cape Coral adopted $50,000.** Only **all Lee County millages, the Town of Fort Myers Beach, and the Village of Estero** adopted the $50,000 plus the up-to-$250,000 long-term residency version. So a City of Fort Myers homeowner still gets the county's full treatment against county millages, which is the larger share of most bills, plus $25,000 against the city levy. File anyway. Call **239-533-6100** with the exact street address and ask which authorities on that specific bill adopted which.

**I missed the March 1 deadline for the senior exemption. Is the year lost?** Not necessarily, and the Property Appraiser's own instruction is to act rather than wait. The office states that statutory provisions determine deadlines for late filing and income verification, and it tells applicants who think they may qualify to **file as soon as possible** so it can approve the application in accordance with Florida law. Call **239-533-6100**. Also, if nobody in the household files a tax return, the paperwork is different: complete the Sworn Statement, complete and sign **lines 1 through 4 of IRS Form 4506**, which costs nothing, submit those with proof of age, then send the **SSA-1099** by June 1.

**Dad is homebound in Lehigh Acres and cannot cook. Who brings him meals?** Two calls, in this order. **Community Cooperative runs Meals on Wheels of Southwest Florida countywide at 239-337-1055**, delivering daily with a safety and security check, and it will also supply food for his dog or cat. Then call **Senior Friendship Centers at 239-275-1881** and ask to be assessed through the Elder Helpline for home delivered meals, and ask which of its **10 Lee County dining sites** are open, since it has sites in Lehigh Acres and several have not returned to pre-pandemic operation. Also ask the **Salvation Army Lehigh Acres Service Center at 239-491-6071** about its monthly food bag and frozen meat, available Monday, Wednesday and Friday.

**The power bill is past due and my mother is 84 in Cape Coral. Who do I call first?** Because she is over 60, **EHEAP** first. The state's provider list names **Senior Friendship Center for Lee County** at **239-387-5642**, eheaplee@friendshipcenters.org, and you can also reach it through the **Elder Helpline at 1-866-413-5337**, which is the number FPL publishes for Lee County EHEAP. EHEAP needs a documented crisis such as a shut-off notice, and a household can receive more than one crisis benefit in a program year. In parallel, apply for **LIHEAP through Lee County Human and Veteran Services at 239-533-7900**, and know before you start that **Cape Coral is LCEC territory in most cases**, so have the bill in front of you when the application asks you to pick your utility. Also ask the **Salvation Army at 239-334-3745** about **FPL Care To Share**. Do not print any dollar figure you find for EHEAP into your plan, including the one on the provider's own website, which still carries a note that expired in 2021. Ask what the cap is this year.

**My father's roof is failing. He is 81 and owns his home free and clear. Where does he apply?** The answer is entirely his address, and this trips up more Lee County families than anything else on this page. **Unincorporated Lee County:** the county's Home Repair Assistance Program, **239-533-7900**, minimum $1,000, secured as a deferred mortgage, and you must select "Owner-Occupied Housing Rehabilitation" on the application. **City of Fort Myers:** the city's own program at **1825 Hendry St., 239-321-7970**, up to **$15,000 as a grant**, with emergency repairs prioritized. **Cape Coral:** **Rebuilding Together Greater Florida** at **RTGFL.org** or **239-229-6392** for no-cost repairs, and **Habitat for Humanity of Lee and Hendry Counties** at **239-652-4663** for the Emergency Owner Occupied program. All of them require being **current on mortgage and property taxes** and a **homesteaded** property, and the Habitat program excludes homes with a **reverse mortgage**, tax liens, IRS liens or judgments. Find out which of those applies before anyone schedules an inspection.

**Mom needs help with back rent. Someone told me to apply to the county.** That advice is out of date. **Lee County stopped accepting applications for its Rent/Mortgage and Water Financial Assistance Program effective June 16, 2026**, citing limited funding, and asks residents to watch its website for updates. Call **2-1-1** or **239-433-3900** instead, answered 24 hours a day, which keeps the live list of who has money this month. Then call the **Salvation Army Outreach Service Center at 239-334-3745**, where emergency rent and utility help requires **proof of a loss of income or a verifiable crisis**, with walk-ins Wednesday through Friday. If she is a **Cape Coral homeowner behind on a mortgage rather than rent**, and the lender has not yet filed, call **239-573-3164** about the city's **Foreclosure Prevention Program**, worth up to **$10,000**.

**My mother stopped driving. She is 72 and lives in North Fort Myers. What are her options?** If she can still ride a regular bus, **LeeTran costs her 75 cents a trip** instead of $1.50, and a **31-day pass is $23**. She needs a reduced fare **TropiCard**, obtained by bringing proof of age and a photo ID to the **Rosa Parks Transportation Center, 2250 Widman Way, Fort Myers**, weekdays 8 to 5, and she must show it every time she boards. If a regular bus will not work, be realistic about the two paratransit doors: **Passport ADA** requires a disability that prevents fixed-route use, with **both ends of the trip within three quarters of a mile of a route**, and the **Transportation Disadvantaged program requires household income at or below 225 percent of the federal poverty guidelines**, which for 2026 is **$35,910 for one person**. Age 72 by itself opens neither. Call **239-533-0300**. If she does qualify for TD on income, ask about the **free 30-day or 31-day bus pass** in the same call.

**My husband has dementia and I have not had an evening off in a year. What is available in Lee County?** Three things, in this order. Call **Senior Friendship Centers at 239-275-1881** and ask about **RELIEF**, which is free respite offered specifically **during nights and weekends** rather than only during business hours. Second, call the **Area Agency on Aging at 1-866-413-5337** and ask about **TCARE** and **Savvy Caregiver**, the six-session program built for dementia caregivers. Third, if the diagnosis itself is unclear or the care plan has stalled, the **Lee Physicians Group Memory Care Clinic at 12600 Creekside Lane, Suite 7, Fort Myers, 239-343-9220** is one of Florida's state-designated memory disorder clinics and it is right here. And if a full-day program is what you actually need, **Hope PACE** runs adult day centers in **Fort Myers, Cape Coral and Lehigh Acres**, at **239-985-6400**. The **Alzheimer's Association helpline at 1-800-272-3900** is answered at 3 a.m., which is usually when you need it.
