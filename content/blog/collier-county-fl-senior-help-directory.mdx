**Start with the one instruction the county itself gives you, because it saves a wasted phone call.** Collier County's Services for Seniors page opens with this: "To apply for Collier County Services for Seniors call the Area Agency on Aging Help Line at (866) 413-5337. The Help Line is the central telephone number for assistance to locate local services." That is unusually direct, and it matters. Collier County government delivers a lot of senior services on the ground here, but eligibility for the aging-network programs is determined through the regional Area Agency on Aging, which sits in **Fort Myers**, in the next county north. That agency is the state's designated **Aging and Disability Resource Center** for Southwest Florida and covers **Charlotte, Collier, DeSoto, Glades, Hendry, Lee and Sarasota** counties. One number, **1-866-413-5337**, is where SHINE Medicare counseling is arranged, where respite and caregiver programs live, where the Medicaid long-term care track begins, and where the elder energy crisis program is administered.

**The second thing to understand about Collier County is that it is really three places, and the programs know it even when the brochures do not.** There is coastal Naples and Marco Island, among the wealthiest addresses in Florida. There is Golden Gate and East Naples, where most of the working population lives. And there is **Immokalee**, a farmworking community roughly forty miles inland with its own food pantries, its own Salvation Army service center, its own housing authority headquarters and its own mobile pantry schedule. Several of the programs below have separate Immokalee locations, and a few, like the Collier County Housing Authority, are actually headquartered there rather than in Naples. When you call, say which part of the county you are in. It changes the answer more often than you would expect.

**The third thing is a pattern worth naming up front: several programs that serve Collier County are administered from outside it.** LIHEAP, the energy bill program, is run by an agency headquartered in Polk County with no service delivery site in Collier. Weatherization is run out of Florida City, on the far side of the state. The Area Agency on Aging is in Fort Myers. None of that means you cannot get help. It means the phone and the online application are your tools, and it means that "just go down to the office" is often not an option. Plan accordingly.

Two more numbers belong on the refrigerator. The **Florida Abuse Hotline at 1-800-962-2873** takes reports of suspected abuse, neglect or exploitation of a vulnerable adult, 24 hours a day. And the **Long-Term Care Ombudsman at 1-888-831-0404** is who you call about a nursing home, assisted living facility or adult family care home, rather than that facility's own management. Collier County also publishes a **Florida Public Guardian office at 239-417-1040** and an **Alzheimer's Support Network at 239-262-8388**.

## Property tax relief (apply with the Collier County Property Appraiser, 239-252-8141, 3950 Radio Road, Naples)
Florida seniors apply for property tax relief through the county **Property Appraiser**, not the Tax Collector. In Collier County that office is at **3950 Radio Road, Naples, FL 34104**, phone **239-252-8141**.

**Standard Homestead Exemption (statewide).** You must hold legal or equitable title on **January 1** and in good faith make the property your permanent residence. The Property Appraiser describes the benefit as "a reduction of up to $25,000 from the assessed value of the property for all taxing authorities, and an additional exemption on the assessed value above $50,000." The office states that **new applications for the current year must be made prior to March 1**. Filing also starts the **Save Our Homes** assessment cap, which over ten or fifteen years is usually worth more than any exemption on this page.

**Now the senior exemptions, and here is where Collier County is genuinely better than most of Florida, so it is worth the paperwork.** Florida law lets each county and each city decide by local ordinance whether to grant one or both additional homestead exemptions to low-income seniors. Plenty of Florida counties adopted only the smaller one, and some set it far below the maximum. **Collier adopted both.**

**Exemption one, up to $50,000.** The Property Appraiser's own page says it plainly: "Effective January 1, 2000, the Collier County Board of Commissioners and the Naples City Council approved, by ordinance, this additional homestead exemption. The amount of the exemption specified will not exceed $50,000." Note that two separate governments adopted it, the county and the City of Naples, which matters because of how these exemptions work.

**Exemption two, the whole assessed value, for long-term residents.** The same office describes the 2012 constitutional amendment this way: it "allows an additional homestead tax exemption that will be equal to the assessed value of homestead property with a just value less than $250,000, if the owner is over 65 years of age, has proven household income did not exceed the yearly income requirements and affirms their permanent residence has been on the same property for at least 25 years," and then states that "this amendment was adopted by Collier County to provide a further reduction in your portion of county taxes." For a long-tenured owner of a modest home, that is not a trim off the bill. That is the county portion gone.

**What both of them require.** You must own and reside on the property as of **January 1** of the tax roll year, meet the requirements for the regular homestead exemption, be **65 years of age on or before January 1** with proof of age, and meet the household income limit. For **2026 the limit is $38,686**, measured against **2025 adjusted gross income for the entire household**. The Florida Department of Revenue publishes that figure annually and adjusts it for the cost of living: it was **$37,694 for 2025** and **$36,614 for 2024**. You complete form **DR-501SC**, the Sworn Statement of Adjusted Gross Income of Household.

**Read this deadline twice, because it is not the one you have been told.** For the regular homestead exemption the deadline is March 1. For the **senior** exemption, the Collier County Property Appraiser states that the application "must be supported by copies of federal income tax returns for the prior year and any wage and earnings statements for each member of the household, and **must be submitted by June 1, of each year**." If you are past March and someone told you that you missed it, call **239-252-8141** before you accept that.

**Two mechanics that save people trouble later.** First, once you initially qualify, **annual re-application is not required** as long as you still meet the income limit. The office mails renewing recipients the state-determined income limit by the **end of January** each year, and if your household income goes over it you must notify the Property Appraiser **in writing by May 1** of that tax roll year. Random audits are conducted under Florida statute, so that written notice is not a formality. Second, if you do not file a federal tax return, you supply copies of all end-of-year income statements instead.

**The limit on all of it, and the question to ask about your specific address.** The Florida Department of Revenue states that these exemptions "apply only to the tax millage a county or city levies when it adopts a local ordinance and do not apply to the millage of school districts or other taxing units." So they never touch the school portion of your bill. The Property Appraiser names the **Collier County Board of Commissioners** and the **Naples City Council** as the adopting governments. It does not name Marco Island or Everglades City. Rather than guess whether your city adopted one, do this: give the Property Appraiser your **exact street address** and ask which taxing authorities appear on that specific bill and which of them granted a senior exemption. That is a two-minute call that decides whether the exemption is worth several hundred dollars or several thousand.

**One rumor to put down.** If you heard that Florida was about to exempt seniors from property taxes entirely, that was **SB 272 (2026)**, which would have provided a total exemption from ad valorem taxation other than school district levies for qualifying people 65 and older. It **died in the Senate Finance and Tax Committee on March 13, 2026**. The rules above are the rules that apply now.

**Finally, the program almost nobody mentions: tax deferral.** The **Collier County Tax Collector**, main office at the Government Center, **3291 Tamiami Trail East**, phone **239-252-8171**, administers homestead tax deferral. The office states that "any person entitled to claim Homestead Exemption may qualify to defer a portion of their property taxes or non-ad valorem assessments based on the applicant's household income," that taxes "may be deferred in their entirety" if household income is under $10,000, and that an applicant "over 70 years of age and has an annual income of less than $12,000" may defer the entire amount. **The deadline for filing is March 31 of each year.** Understand what you are signing, though. The Tax Collector states that an approved deferral "would become a first lien on the property and you MUST furnish fire insurance coverage with a loss payable clause to the Tax Collector." Deferral stops an immediate cash crisis. It is not forgiveness, and the balance is still owed. Ask the office to walk you through the current income thresholds before you file, since the underlying statute has been amended over the years.

## Energy and utility help
**First, know your utility.** Collier County households are served by **Florida Power and Light** or by **Lee County Electric Cooperative (LCEC)**, and the county's own energy assistance document checklist asks specifically for "FPL or LCEC Electric Bill." Which one you have changes who you call about hardship programs and payment arrangements.

**Second, and this is the part to read carefully, the elder energy program's local intake is genuinely unsettled right now.** Two Collier County pages currently say different things, and rather than pick one and hope, here is exactly what each says.

- Collier County's **Social Services Programs** page states: "The Emergency Home Energy Assistance for the Elderly Program (EHEAP) is no longer managed by Collier County. For utility assistance, please contact the Low-Income Home Energy Assistance Program (LIHEAP) administered by The Agricultural and Labor Program, Inc. (ALPI)."
- Collier County's **affordable housing** site still describes EHEAP in full, states it "is available to households who have a delinquent electric bill and an adult 60 years or older who resides in the home," adds that it "can also assist with the repair and replacement of air conditioning to an elderly household who is a homeowner," and gives in-person intake at the Community and Human Services office on **Airport Road South in Naples**, phone **239-450-2114** or dial **311**.
- The **Area Agency on Aging for Southwest Florida** lists EHEAP among its own programs across its seven counties, which include Collier, at **1-866-413-5337**.

**What to actually do:** call the **Elder Helpline at 1-866-413-5337** first, because the Area Agency is the entity that administers EHEAP regionally and will know who is taking Collier applications this month. Then call **239-450-2114**. If someone will take the application, the air conditioning benefit alone makes it worth pursuing in this climate.

**Regular LIHEAP for Collier County is run by The Agricultural and Labor Program, Inc. (ALPI)**, and this is where the "administered from outside the county" pattern bites hardest. ALPI's corporate office is at **300 Lynchburg Road, Lake Alfred, FL 33850**, phone **863-956-3491**. It states the program "is only available in the following counties: Collier, Glades, Hendry, Highlands, Martin, Polk and St. Lucie." But its published service delivery sites are in **Avon Park, Lake Alfred, Winter Haven and Fort Pierce**. There is no ALPI walk-in site listed in Collier County. The practical route is the **online application** through Florida Commerce, or the phone. Collier County also lists **Macedonia Baptist Church at 239-262-4877** as a local source of LIHEAP assistance, which is worth a call if you need a human in the room.

**Free weatherization is a separate program and it is not means-tested the same way.** **Centro Campesino** administers the Weatherization Assistance Program for Collier County along with thirteen other counties, from **35801 SW 186th Avenue, Florida City, FL 33034**, phone **305-245-7738**. Household income may not exceed 200 percent of the federal poverty level, and the program gives **priority to residents 60 and older**, people with disabilities, families with young children and households with a high energy burden. Covered work includes weatherstripping, caulking, insulation, window and door replacement, attic ventilation, solar screens, and repair or replacement of inefficient heating, cooling and water heating equipment. It is provided at no cost to eligible homeowners and tenants. In a county where the summer electric bill is the whole problem, a free air conditioning repair through weatherization is often the more permanent fix than a one-time bill payment.

**When the money has to come today,** call **2-1-1** or **239-263-4211**. Collier County's rent and utility assistance list also names **Catholic Charities Diocese of Venice at 239-455-2655**, **St. Vincent de Paul at 239-775-1667 or 239-775-2907**, and **The Salvation Army at 239-210-4016**. These funds move month to month, and 2-1-1 keeps the live list of which ones still have money.

## Medicare, Medicaid, prescriptions
**SHINE** is Florida's free Medicare counseling program, and in Collier County it runs through the **Area Agency on Aging for Southwest Florida**. Counselors are trained volunteers who are not affiliated with any insurance company or agency, which is the entire point of the program: nobody in that conversation earns a commission on what you choose. Call **1-866-413-5337** for a counselor, the current in-person schedule or an upcoming webinar. Collier County's own Services for Seniors page lists that same number for SHINE.

**Ask SHINE to screen you for the programs that pay the premiums, not just to compare plans.** The Area Agency separately administers **MIPPA**, the Medicare Improvements for Patients and Providers Act program, which exists specifically to find low-income seniors who are eligible for help with Medicare costs and are not getting it. The Medicare Savings Programs can cover the Part B premium, which is real money back in a monthly Social Security deposit, and Extra Help reduces prescription costs. A very large share of the people eligible for these never apply, usually because nobody told them the programs exist.

**Medicaid and long-term care.** Collier County publishes **239-332-2822** for Medicaid. For long-term care specifically, Florida splits the decision between two agencies, and knowing that saves months: the **Department of Elder Affairs** determines whether you meet the level of care through **CARES**, and the **Department of Children and Families** determines financial eligibility through **ACCESS**. For someone not already in a nursing home, the process starts with a call to the Aging and Disability Resource Center, which here is the **Elder Helpline at 1-866-413-5337**. The Area Agency lists the **Statewide Medicaid Managed Care Long Term Care Program** among the programs it helps people reach. Start that call earlier than feels necessary. The functional assessment and the financial determination run on their own timelines, and there is often a wait list between eligibility and enrollment.

**Medicare itself** is **1-800-633-4227**, 24 hours a day, TTY 1-877-486-2048.

## Home repair and safety
**Collier County's SHIP owner-occupied rehabilitation program is the main door, and its terms are better than most.** The county offers **interest-free, deferred loans up to $50,000** to eligible homeowners in **unincorporated Collier County, the City of Naples, the City of Marco Island or Everglades City** for repairs to single-family homes, townhomes or condominium units. **Deferred** is the word that matters: unlike emergency repair loans in some neighboring counties, this does not put a monthly payment on a fixed income. **Mobile homes built prior to July 13, 1994 do not qualify.** There is also a separate **roof replacement program**, also a deferred, interest-free loan, for Collier County residents whose primary home needs a new roof, subject to program and income criteria.

Contact **Collier County Community and Human Services**, **2671 Airport Road South, Naples, FL 34112**, phone **239-450-2114**, email housing.info@collier.gov. Funding runs in annual cycles, so if applications are closed when you call, ask when the next cycle opens and what you can gather in the meantime. The county's public notice for the 2025 to 2026 cycle put the state SHIP allocation at roughly **$3,026,000**, with homeowner strategies including purchase assistance, housing rehabilitation, and demolition and replacement of manufactured homes, serving households up to **120 percent of area median income**. That AMI ceiling is higher than people assume, and in a county with Collier's incomes it is worth checking rather than self-disqualifying.

**Free weatherization**, covered in the energy section above, is the other half of this. **Centro Campesino, 305-245-7738**, priority to applicants 60 and older, no cost.

**And the pay-off letter detail, for families settling an estate or selling:** if a Collier County loan is already on the property, the county directs payoff requests to a specific staff line at **239-252-2339**. That is the sort of thing that holds up a closing when nobody knows to ask.

## Rent, housing, emergency cash
**Collier County Housing Authority** is headquartered in **Immokalee**, not Naples, which surprises people. Main office: **1800 Farm Worker Way, Immokalee, FL 34142**, **239-657-3649**, Monday through Friday 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., closed noon to 1. TTY 800-955-8771. The **Section 8 office is separate and is in Naples**: **5286 Golden Gate Parkway, Suite 2, Naples, FL 34116**, **239-732-0732**. The authority runs Housing Choice Vouchers, farm worker housing, low-income housing and Horizon Village. Housing Choice Voucher wait lists in Florida open and close without much notice and are frequently closed, so ask specifically when the list is expected to open and whether there is a preference category you fall into, rather than assuming a closed list means never.

**For affordable rentals**, Collier County points residents to **Florida Housing Search at 1-877-428-8844**, and the county's own housing site publishes a rental apartment inventory.

**For emergency rent and utilities**, the county's published list is **Catholic Charities 239-455-2655**, **St. Vincent de Paul 239-775-1667 or 239-775-2907**, **The Salvation Army 239-210-4016**, and **United Way at 2-1-1 or 239-263-4211**.

**For county social services generally**, including medication assistance, Collier County Community and Human Services takes case-managed applications at **239-252-CARE (2273)**. Be ready for a real document list: four weeks of income proof or a benefits letter, Social Security cards and identification for everyone in the household, **two** documents proving Collier County residency, proof of assets, insurance policies and, where relevant, a letter from Medicaid stating denial of services. The county also states that to receive support "the resident must not be eligible for other assistance programs," so the case manager will screen you for everything else first. That is not a brush-off. It is how the program is written.

**One quiet program worth knowing about before you need it.** Collier County Human Services provides **indigent cremation** for low-income residents who cannot afford final disposition for a relative. The person must have died in Collier County, must not have had life insurance, and household income must not exceed 100 percent of the federal poverty guidelines. The program does not cover a funeral, viewing or memorial service. The county lists **239-252-8441** for cremation services. Nobody wants to make that call, and knowing it exists in advance is a kindness to whoever eventually has to.

## Staying at home instead of a nursing home
- **The Medicaid track** is the **Statewide Medicaid Managed Care Long-Term Care** program, and it covers care at home and in assisted living, not only in a nursing facility. Two separate determinations have to happen: **CARES**, under the Department of Elder Affairs, decides whether you meet the nursing-facility level of care, and **ACCESS**, under the Department of Children and Families, decides financial eligibility. For someone still living at home, the process starts with the **Elder Helpline at 1-866-413-5337**. Ask on that first call whether there is a wait list and where you stand on it.
- **The PACE track** is covered in its own section below and is the single most complete option for someone who qualifies.
- **The track that needs no Medicaid determination at all** is the Older Americans Act and state-funded network: home delivered meals, congregate meals, respite, caregiver support and home and community-based care, all reachable through the **Elder Helpline at 1-866-413-5337** and through **Collier County Community and Human Services at 239-252-2273**. These have their own eligibility and their own wait lists, but they do not require you to spend down assets. Start here while the Medicaid process runs in parallel, rather than waiting for one before beginning the other.
- **The private track** is **St. Vincent de Paul's Meals on Wheels at 239-775-1667** and **Baker Senior Center Naples at 239-325-4444**, neither of which is bound by the federal program rules. When the funded programs say no or say wait, these are the calls that sometimes say yes.

## PACE
**PACE, the Program of All-Inclusive Care for the Elderly, is real in Collier County and it has people in it.** That is not true everywhere in Florida, so it is worth stating plainly. Florida's **Agency for Health Care Administration**, in its **February 2026 PACE monthly report**, lists **Hope PACE** as **Operational** in Collier County, with an initial AHCA approval date of **April 12, 2012**, **120 slots approved and 120 funded**, and **89 enrollees** as of the first of the report month, plus **104 unique individuals** served across the fiscal year. Neighboring Glades and Hendry counties are listed with no PACE agency at all.

PACE combines Medicare and Medicaid into one program that provides medical care, an adult day center, therapy, prescriptions, transportation to appointments and in-home support, all coordinated by one team, for people who need a nursing-facility level of care but want to stay at home.

**The center serving this county is the Hope PACE Center at 13020 Livingston Road, Naples, FL 34105.** Hope PACE states the minimum age is **55**, that you must be a resident of **Charlotte, Collier or Lee County**, and that enrollees must be able to live safely at home with the additional services the program provides. Call the enrollment line at **239-985-6400**, Monday through Friday 8 a.m. to 5 p.m.

**Two practical notes.** First, with 120 funded slots and 89 people enrolled, there was room in this program as of the most recent published report, which is a much better position than a waiting list. Call rather than assume it is full. Second, PACE is usually a Medicare and Medicaid program, though private pay is possible, so start the **Medicaid long-term care** process through the Elder Helpline at **1-866-413-5337** at the same time you call Hope PACE, rather than finishing one before starting the other.

## Before hurricane season, do this one thing
Collier County Emergency Management maintains a **Special Needs Registry** for people who cannot safely stay home during an emergency and have no other sheltering options. You should pre-register if you are **medically stable but need help with daily tasks or rely on electronic medical equipment**. The registration criteria the county publishes are: individuals needing care beyond basic first aid, individuals dependent on electricity or oxygen, and medically stable individuals with disabilities requiring assistance. Registering also enrolls you in **ALERT Collier** for emergency notifications.

**Four details the county states that people routinely get wrong:**
1. **Registration must be completed annually.** A form filed three years ago is not a current registration.
2. **Completing the registry does not guarantee placement** in a special needs shelter. All registrations are reviewed by medical professionals and may be verified with your provider, and depending on your needs you may be directed to a general shelter, a special needs shelter or a medical facility.
3. **Residents of nursing homes or assisted living facilities should not register**, because those facilities are required to provide care during emergencies.
4. **Pets are allowed only in designated shelters.** Service animals are permitted at all shelters under ADA guidelines.

The county names **Palmetto Ridge High School** as the special needs shelter location and is candid about conditions: food and water provided though special diets may not be accommodated, basic medical monitoring only with no complex medical care, limited sleeping space that may be a cot or a chair, backup electricity for essential equipment, and conditions that may be crowded, noisy and short on privacy. Bring what you need.

Contact the **Special Needs Shelter Program, Collier County Emergency Management, 8075 Lely Cultural Pkwy, Suite 445, Naples, FL 34113**, phone **239-252-3603**. Registration forms are published in English, Spanish and Haitian Creole. Collier County's Services for Seniors page lists **239-252-3608** for emergency special needs disaster registration, so if one line does not answer, try the other.

## Frequently asked questions
**Dad is 78, has owned the same house in East Naples since 1994 and lives on Social Security. What is he actually entitled to?** Possibly the largest property tax break on this page, and he should call this week. He is over 65, he has been in the same permanent residence for more than 25 years, and if his household adjusted gross income for 2025 was **$38,686 or less** he may qualify for the **long-term residency exemption**, which the Collier County Property Appraiser describes as "equal to the assessed value of homestead property with a just value less than $250,000," adopted by Collier County "to provide a further reduction in your portion of county taxes." He may also qualify for the separate additional exemption of up to **$50,000**. Neither applies to school taxes. The just-value test is measured in the first tax year he applies and is eligible, which is one more reason not to wait a year. Call **239-252-8141** and ask for the senior exemption forms, and note the senior deadline is **June 1**, not March 1.

**Mom's air conditioner died in July and she is 82 on a fixed income. Who actually pays for that?** Ask three places, in this order. First, the **Elder Helpline at 1-866-413-5337**, because the Area Agency administers the elder energy crisis program for Collier County and can tell you who is taking applications now. Second, **239-450-2114**, because Collier County's housing site states EHEAP "can also assist with the repair and replacement of air conditioning to an elderly household who is a homeowner," even though a different county page says the county no longer manages the program. Third, **Centro Campesino at 305-245-7738** for free weatherization, which covers repair or replacement of inefficient cooling equipment at no cost, gives priority to applicants 60 and older, and is the more permanent fix. Do all three in the same week. They are different pots of money and applying to one does not disqualify her from another.

**My mother stopped driving. She is 71 and lives in Golden Gate.** Two options. **Collier Area Transit** cuts her fare in half at 65, so a one-way ride is **$1.00** and a 30-day pass is **$20**, but she has to go in person to a CAT transfer station with identification to get the reduced entitlement placed on her account, and CAT requires that ID check **in person every year** to keep it. If fixed-route service does not work for her, **CATConnect** paratransit at **239-252-7272** provides door-to-door service, but eligibility requires an application supported by a licensed medical professional, so start that paperwork before she needs it rather than the week she does.

**My husband has dementia and I have not had an afternoon to myself in months. What is in Collier County?** Two calls, and the first one is easy. **Baker Senior Center Naples, 239-325-4444**, runs a **Dementia Respite** program specifically designed to give the caregiver a **four-hour break**, plus free caregiver support groups on **Tuesday and Thursday from 1:00 to 2:25 p.m.** that require no advance registration. You can walk into the group this week. Then call the **Elder Helpline at 1-866-413-5337** and ask about **Alzheimer's Disease Initiative respite**, which funds in-home, facility-based and emergency respite through the Area Agency, and about the caregiver counseling and training that come with it. The funded programs may have a wait. The support group does not.

**Dad is a Vietnam-era veteran, 80, and we are about to start paying for aides out of pocket. Is there anything?** Yes, and it is free to pursue. Call **Collier County Veteran Services at 239-252-8387** and ask for an appointment specifically about **VA Pension with Aid and Attendance**. It is a needs-based benefit for wartime veterans with limited income, and it exists precisely to help pay for the care he is about to pay for himself. If your mother is the surviving spouse, ask about **Survivors Pension with Aid and Attendance** instead. The county publishes packets for both. Also ask about **Veteran-Directed Home and Community Based Services** through the Area Agency at **1-866-413-5337**, which lets a veteran receive services from a caregiver of their own choosing.

**We are looking at a nursing home for my mother and cannot afford it. Is there an alternative?** Ask about **PACE** before you tour another facility. **Hope PACE** is operational in Collier County with a center at **13020 Livingston Road, Naples**, and Florida's AHCA reported **89 people enrolled against 120 funded slots** as of its February 2026 report, meaning there was room. It bundles medical care, an adult day center, therapy, prescriptions, transportation and in-home support into one program for people who need nursing-facility-level care but want to stay home. Minimum age is 55 and you must live in Charlotte, Collier or Lee County. Call **239-985-6400**, and start the Medicaid long-term care process through the **Elder Helpline at 1-866-413-5337** in the same week rather than waiting.

**My father is behind on property taxes and terrified of losing the house.** Call the **Collier County Tax Collector at 239-252-8171** and ask about **homestead tax deferral**, and call the **Property Appraiser at 239-252-8141** to make sure he is receiving every exemption he qualifies for, which is the cheaper fix if he has been missing the senior exemption. The deferral filing deadline is **March 31**. Understand the trade: the Tax Collector states that an approved deferral becomes **a first lien on the property** and that fire insurance with a loss payable clause to the Tax Collector is required. It buys time, it does not erase the debt, and a family should make that decision together with clear eyes. Free legal advice on the question is available from the **Florida Senior Legal Helpline at 1-888-895-7873** and from **Legal Aid Service of Collier County at 239-775-4555**.

**Mom lives in Immokalee, not Naples. Does any of this apply to her?** All of it, and several programs are actually closer to her than to a Naples resident. The **Collier County Housing Authority** is headquartered in Immokalee at **1800 Farm Worker Way, 239-657-3649**. The county runs a nutrition site at **Beraca Church, 905 Roberts Avenue, 239-450-2331**. There is a **Salvation Army Immokalee Service Center at 415 Commerce Ave #3, 239-657-2199**, the **Amigos Center food distribution at 106 S 2nd St, 239-437-6727**, and a **Meals of Hope mobile pantry at 1800 Farm Worker Way on Thursdays from 3:00 to 5:00 p.m.** The Property Appraiser, the Elder Helpline and every state program apply identically. Mention Immokalee when you call, because the closest office is often not the Naples one.
