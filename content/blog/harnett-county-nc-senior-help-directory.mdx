## Property tax relief (apply with the Harnett County Tax Department, 910-893-7520)

**The office.** Senior and disability relief is handled by the **Harnett County Tax Department**, **305 W. Cornelius Harnett Boulevard, Suite 101, Lillington, NC 27546**, main line **910-893-7520**, email **taxinfo@harnett.org**. The **Tax Administrator is Christine Wallace**, and the state's official list of county assessors (revised September 23, 2026) shows the same address and number for Harnett's County Assessor. The department's **Personal Property Division** processes exemption and exclusion applications. The county does not post the office's hours on its tax pages, so call before you drive.

**The three programs.** All three are set by the state and filed on the state's **Form AV-9** with the county. Harnett takes them **on paper or online** through links on its Exemptions and Exclusions page, and says the application period is **January 1 to June 1 each year**.

- **Elderly or Disabled Exclusion.** It **excludes the greater of $25,000 or 50 percent of the appraised value of the home**. You must be **at least 65 as of January 1, or totally and permanently disabled**, and own the home where you live. Income for the prior year must be within the state limit: **$38,800 for the 2026 tax year, $39,900 for 2027**. The county says income means **all money received from every source** other than gifts or inheritances from a spouse, parent or child, and that for a married couple living together **both incomes count**, whether or not both names are on the deed. Two county rules to know before you go: **documentation of all income is required**, and **bank statements are not accepted as proof of income**. A disability claim uses **Form AV-9A**. The county's reappraisal FAQ says an approved exclusion **carries over** to later years; tell the office if income or ownership changes.
- **Circuit Breaker Tax Deferment.** Caps the tax at **4 percent of income up to $38,800** and **5 percent from $38,801 to $58,200** for 2026 (for 2027 the ceilings are **$39,900** and **$59,850**). The owner must be **65 or older or totally and permanently disabled**. The county repeats the state's warning in capital letters: **you must file a new application every year**. If there is more than one owner, **each owner files and all must qualify**. **This is a deferral, not forgiveness.** The county says the taxes over the cap **remain a lien on the property**, and **the last three years of deferred taxes become due, with interest,** when the owner dies, transfers the property, stops living there, or fails to reapply. It cannot be combined with either exclusion. Understand that before your parent signs, especially if the plan is to sell the house or leave it to family.
- **Disabled Veteran Exclusion.** **Excludes the first $45,000** of the appraised value for an honorably discharged veteran with a **permanent and total service-connected disability**, or an unmarried surviving spouse. The county says there is **no age requirement and no income requirement**. File the AV-9 with **Form NCDVA-9**, which the county says must **first be certified by your Veterans Service Office**. Harnett County **Veterans Services** (below) does that.

**Deadline.** **June 1.** Mark it every year for the Circuit Breaker, and once for the other two.

**Harnett reappraised in 2026, and the new value is already on the bill.** Harnett is on a **four-year reappraisal cycle**. Its last reappraisal took effect January 1, 2022, and the current one took effect **January 1, 2026**, with value notices mailed in March. Here is the part that trips families up, because it is different from several other North Carolina counties:

- In June 2026 the General Assembly passed **Session Law 2026-8** (Senate Bill 889), which told counties with 2026 reappraisals to bill one more year on their old values.
- On **July 7, 2026** the Governor signed **Session Law 2026-47** (Senate Bill 474), which changed the list of counties covered. Harnett's amended budget says that under the new law the county **"is permitted to utilize its recently completed 2026 property valuations for the current fiscal year."**
- So the bill mailed in August uses the **2026 value**. The county rate is **63 cents per $100**, up from **59.1 cents**, where it had held for four years. The budget says the rate first recommended, when the county expected to bill on 2022 values, was 75 cents.

**What that means for a parent on relief.** The Elderly or Disabled Exclusion takes off the greater of $25,000 or **half the appraised value**, so the excluded half grew along with the 2026 value. If your parent's income is under the limit and they are not yet on the exclusion, **June 1, 2027** is the date to circle, with income under **$39,900**.

**Appeals.** The county extended the deadline to appeal the 2026 value to **5 p.m. on May 22, 2026**, and that date has passed. The county's FAQ says valid reasons to appeal are that the value substantially exceeds market value or is out of line with similar properties; "taxes are too high" and the owner's ability to pay are not. If you think the value is wrong, ask the Real Property staff at **910-814-6920** what can still be reviewed for 2027. The county's FAQ says the **next reappraisal is in 2030**.

**Mobile homes were revalued too.** In 2026 the county also reassessed **mobile homes that are taxed as personal property** (usually homes on rented lots), using a new schedule for the first time since **1998**. If your parent owns a mobile home and the bill jumped, call **910-893-7520** and ask about both the value and the exclusion.

**Rates and bills.** The county's **2026** rate is **63 cents per $100**. Towns add their own: **Lillington 58 cents**, **Dunn 54 cents**, **Erwin 50 cents**, **Angier 49 cents** and **Coats 49 cents**. Fire districts add **8 to 15 cents**, and the county's rate sheet shows an **$85** solid waste and recycle fee. The county says **2026 bills were mailed August 28, 2026**. Taxes are due September 1 and can be paid **without interest through January 5, 2027**; interest is **2 percent on January 6** and three-quarters of a percent each month after.

**The payment plan has a hard stop.** The county accepts **partial payments in any amount** from the day the bill is mailed through January 5, and it takes **prepayments from February to August** toward the next bill. Write the bill number and "installment of payment" on the check. But the county says in capital letters that once a bill is delinquent it **does not make payment arrangements**. For a parent on a fixed income, the time to spread the bill out is now, not in January. Pay online, by phone at **1-866-580-9996** (a card fee applies), by mail or in person.

**Paperwork that protects the house.** The **Harnett County Register of Deeds**, **Matthew S. Willis**, is at **305 W. Cornelius Harnett Boulevard, Suite 200, Lillington**, **910-893-7540**. A power of attorney that will be used for real estate is recorded there. The office offers a **free Fraud Detection Notification Service** that sends an email notice when a document is recorded; sign your parent up from the Register of Deeds page.

## Energy and utility help

**Where to apply: Harnett County DSS, energy intake 910-814-6823.** The county says you can apply **in person, by paper application, telephone, fax, email or online** through ePASS, and that you do **not** need an appointment.

**LIEAP.** A **one-time payment** toward the heating bill. Both the state and the county say households with **someone 60 or older**, or a disabled person receiving services through the NC Division of Aging and Adult Services, can receive it starting **December 1**; everyone else from **January 1 to March 31** or until the money runs out. December 1 is three weeks away; get the paperwork together now. The county says you can receive LIEAP **once per season**.

**CIP.** The **Crisis Intervention Program** is for a heating or cooling emergency, taken **July 1 to June 30** or until the money runs out. The county says a CIP application can take **up to four days** depending on what has to be verified, and can sometimes be finished the same day. The state says a household with a member **60 or older** or disabled can have **resources at or below $4,500**.

**Two more funds, for Duke Energy Progress customers.** Harnett DSS also takes applications for **Share the Light** and the **NC Rate Settlement** fund, both paid for by Duke Energy Progress customers, employees and the company. Ask about them on the same call. Duke's **Payment Assistance Finder** on its website lists agencies by ZIP code.

**Too hot, not just too cold.** The state's **Operation Fan Heat Relief** list names the **Division on Aging, 910-893-7578**, as Harnett's provider. In 2026 the county gave free fans to residents **60 and older**, and to people of any age with a qualifying breathing condition, who had **no working air conditioning**; the contact was **Debra Weis at 910-814-6074**. The season has ended; note it for next June.

**The electric co-ops.** Parts of Harnett are served by **South River Electric Membership Corporation**, headquartered at **17494 US 421 South, Dunn**, **910-892-8071** or **800-338-5530**, and by **Central Electric Membership Corporation**, **128 Wilson Road, Sanford**, **919-774-4900** or **800-446-7752**. Both run **Operation Round Up** funds, but their own pages describe grants to **nonprofits, schools and community organizations**; neither page offers an application for an individual's bill. If your parent is behind on a co-op bill, call the co-op about a payment arrangement and call DSS about CIP.

## Medicare, Medicaid, prescriptions

**Free Medicare counseling, in Lillington.** North Carolina's **Seniors' Health Insurance Information Program (SHIIP)**, run by the NC Department of Insurance, gives free, unbiased Medicare counseling at **1-855-408-1212**, Monday to Friday 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Harnett County's SHIIP counselors work through the **Division on Aging**. The county says volunteer counselors are available **Monday through Friday** and to call **910-893-7578** for an appointment; the county's Medicare Coordinator is **LeAnn Blackmon, 910-814-6071**. Medicare open enrollment runs **October 15 to December 7**, so call this week. Bring the **Annual Notice of Change** that arrived in October.

**Help paying Medicare costs and prescriptions.** The **Medicare Savings Programs** can pay Medicare premiums, and **Extra Help** lowers Part D drug costs. The county says SHIIP also helps people enroll in prescription drug assistance programs, and Social Security (**1-800-772-1213**) handles Extra Help. **NC MedAssist (1-866-331-1348)** gives free prescription medicine to **uninsured** North Carolinians with household income at or below **300 percent of poverty**, and says it may help some Medicare patients case by case. We are not printing income limits for the Medicare or Medicaid programs; ask SHIIP or DSS to check them against your parent's numbers.

**Medicaid, and a reason to apply before December 31.** Apply online through ePASS, in person at DSS, or call the **Adult Medicaid** line at **910-814-6585**. The **NC Medicaid Contact Center** is **888-245-0179**, and the **NC Medicaid Ombudsman** is **1-877-201-3750**, Monday to Friday 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. NC Medicaid says that **if you apply by December 31, 2026, Medicaid will cover approved costs for up to 3 months before you applied**. Starting January 1, 2027, that window shrinks to **up to 2 months for adults 65 and older** and people with a disability. If a parent is heading toward a nursing home or already paying for care, that difference can be real money.

**Before anyone moves money or the house.** Harnett DSS says that for long-term care Medicaid, **all assets are reviewed for transfers five years before the application date**, and that giving assets to a family member "could potentially make the applicant ineligible." Talk to the Senior Legal Helpline (below) before a deed or a bank account changes hands.

**Staying home on Medicaid: CAP/DA.** The **Community Alternatives Program for Disabled Adults (CAP/DA)** is North Carolina's Medicaid waiver that brings case management, in-home aide help, respite, home mobility aids, a telephone alert and adult day health care to someone who would otherwise qualify for a nursing home. In Harnett the **Division on Aging runs it**: call **Marsha Johnson at 910-814-6079**. NC Medicaid's list of case management agencies for Harnett County also names **CAP of Cape Fear Valley Health System, 910-615-1841**; **Healthkeeperz, 910-522-0009**; **Primary Health Choice, 910-865-3500**; **Resources for Seniors, 919-872-7933**; and **RHA Management Services, 888-207-7828**. The county says to expect that there could be a waiting list.

**Special Assistance In-Home.** Harnett DSS says **Special Assistance In-Home** gives **cash payments** so an eligible adult can stay in their own home instead of moving to a facility, after an assessment by a social worker. Apply **in person at DSS**; a power of attorney or authorized representative can apply for the person.

**Mental health.** Harnett County's public mental health plan is **Alliance Health**. Its **Behavioral Health Crisis Line is 877-223-4617**; member and recipient services is **800-510-9132**.

## Home repair and safety

- **Weatherization and heating repair.** The same agency runs the **Weatherization Assistance Program**, which makes energy improvements **at no cost to the household**, and **HARRP**, which helps when a heating or air unit is not working. To confirm which agency takes your parent's address, use the state's **Find Your Agency** tool on the NC Department of Environmental Quality weatherization page, or call **919-934-2145**.

It does **not** do roofs, windows, HVAC or anything inside the home. - **Accessibility changes.** The **Independent Living Rehabilitation Program's Fayetteville office**, **910-912-2060**, is the state housing agency's **Displacement Prevention Partnership** partner for Harnett County; that program pays for accessibility changes that keep a person with a disability from having to move.

**Safety nets worth setting up.**

## Transportation

**HARTS (Harnett Area Rural Transit System).** **250 Alexander Drive, Lillington**, **910-814-4019**. HARTS is a shared, reserve-ahead van service, **curb to curb**, open to every county resident, running **Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m.**
- **Inside the county: $3 each way.** Payment is due when the van arrives, **in cash, exact change**.
- **Reserve two business days ahead**, between **8 a.m. and 3 p.m.** The county suggests calling back between 3 and 5 p.m. the day before to confirm the trip. The van can arrive **up to two hours before** the appointment, and drivers head back to the transit center by 4 p.m., so book morning appointments when you can.
- **Medical trips out of the county: $10 one way, $20 round trip**, on set days. **Mondays and Wednesdays:** Wake, Orange and Durham counties. **Tuesdays and Thursdays:** Cumberland, Lee, Moore and Chatham. **Mondays, Tuesdays and Thursdays:** Johnston and Sampson. Schedule the appointment **between 8:30 and 11 a.m.**; it must be finished by 1 p.m.
- **Help with the fare.** HARTS lists a **Council on Aging** transportation program for residents **60 and older**, for medical and general trips, and the **Elderly and Disabled Transportation Assistance Program (EDTAP)**, which needs an enrollment form signed by a doctor, medical professional or social worker. Ask HARTS for both applications.
- **Dialysis.** HARTS runs dialysis routes **six days a week** to seven centers, including Lillington, Dunn, Angier, Anderson Creek, Fuquay-Varina and Sanford. The county says to ask the dialysis center to request the ride.
- An **escort** can ride along if they help the passenger with walking, reading, lifting or a similar need. Riders may bring **two grocery bags**.

**Medicaid rides.** Harnett Medicaid members with full benefits call DSS at **910-814-6811**. The program uses HARTS and can also **reimburse mileage** when a family member drives to a Medicaid-covered appointment. If your parent is in a Medicaid health plan, the plan's own ride line also works.

## Frequently asked questions
**Mom is 74, lives in Dunn, and gets about $2,900 a month from Social Security and a small pension. Does she qualify for a tax break?** Likely. About $2,900 a month is about $34,800 a year, under the **$38,800** limit for 2026 and the **$39,900** limit for 2027, as long as that is the whole household's income, including a spouse's. The **Elderly or Disabled Exclusion** takes the greater of **$25,000 or half** her home's value off. Bring proof of every income source; the county does not accept bank statements. File Form AV-9 between January 1 and **June 1**. Call the Tax Department at **910-893-7520**.

**Should my father use the Circuit Breaker instead?** Go in with open eyes. It caps his tax at 4 or 5 percent of income, but the rest is **deferred, not forgiven**: on a sale, a death or a move, **the last three years of deferred taxes come due with interest**, and he must **file every year**. If his income is under the exclusion limit, the exclusion is usually simpler. Ask the Tax Department to run both, and talk to the **Senior Legal Helpline (1-877-579-7562)** if the house is part of an estate plan.

**Dad's tax bill went up this year. Is that the reappraisal?** Probably both the value and the rate. Harnett's 2026 reappraisal is on this year's bill, and the county rate went from 59.1 to 63 cents per $100. The appeal deadline for the 2026 value was May 22, 2026. What he can still do: check whether he qualifies for the exclusion by June 1, 2027, and start partial payments now so the bill is cleared by **January 5, 2027**. The county does not make arrangements after that date.

**Mom's savings are running out and she may need Medicaid for care. Does timing matter?** It can. NC Medicaid says applications filed **by December 31, 2026** can get approved costs covered for **up to 3 months before** the application; from January 1, 2027 that drops to **2 months** for adults 65 and older. Call Harnett DSS Adult Medicaid at **910-814-6585**, and talk to the Senior Legal Helpline before moving any assets, because DSS looks back five years.

**Dad has dementia and Mom is wearing out. Where does she get a break?** Call the Family Caregiver Support Program at **910-814-6075** about respite and the third-Wednesday support group. Call **Project C.A.R.E. at 910-408-6365** for dementia-specific help. Ask about **Walk By Faith Adult Health and Day Care in Dunn (910-891-2770)**, and if they live in Dunn, Erwin, Cameron or the Spring Lake side of the county, call **LIFE St. Joseph of the Pines at 910-429-7200** about PACE.

**Mom lives alone in Erwin and I live out of state. What can I set up today?** Call the Division on Aging (**910-893-7578**) about lunch at the **Erwin Nutrition Site**, home-delivered meals and an in-home aide. Set her up with **HARTS (910-814-4019)** and ask for the Council on Aging transportation application. Book a SHIIP Medicare review before December 7, sign her phone up for **Harnett Alerts**, sign her name up for the Register of Deeds **fraud notification**, and mark **December 1** for her LIEAP heating application at DSS (**910-814-6823**).
