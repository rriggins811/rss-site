*Free, plain-English help for seniors and families in Cumberland County, NC. Education, not advice. Verify before you rely on it.*

**Three local front doors:** **Cumberland County Council on Older Adults**, **339 Devers Street, Fayetteville, NC 28303**, **910-484-0111** | **Cumberland County Department of Social Services**, **1225 Ramsey Street, Fayetteville, NC 28301**, **910-323-1540** | **Mid-Carolina Area Agency on Aging (Mid-Carolina Regional Council)**, **6205 Raeford Road, Fayetteville, NC 28304**, **910-323-4191**. And **2-1-1**, or **1-888-892-1162**.

**If you only make one call from this page, make it the Council on Older Adults.** In Cumberland County the senior meals, Meals on Wheels and Medicare counseling do not run out of a county department. They run out of the **Cumberland County Council on Older Adults**, a nonprofit on Devers Street that offers its services **at no cost to seniors 60 and older who live in Cumberland County**. Its programs include **Meals on Wheels**, a **free congregate lunch**, **SHIIP Medicare counseling**, information and options counseling, in-home aide services, a home improvement program, **telephone reassurance**, a senior companion program, caregiver supports and respite, and dementia support. One number: **910-484-0111**.

**Social Services is the benefits office.** Medicaid, Food and Nutrition Services, energy assistance, Special Assistance, the homemaker program, guardianship and Adult Protective Services all run out of **1225 Ramsey Street, Fayetteville**, **Monday to Friday, 7:30 a.m. to 5 p.m.** Main line **910-323-1540**. There is a satellite office at the **Spring Lake Family Resource Center, 103 Laketree Boulevard**.

It plans and funds aging services across the three counties, runs the **Family Caregiver Support Program** and the **Long-Term Care Ombudsman**, and funds services such as meals, in-home aides and transportation for people 60 and older through the Home and Community Care Block Grant. Its office moved; older state lists still show 130 Gillespie Street, but the agency's own site gives **6205 Raeford Road**.

## Property tax relief (apply with Cumberland County Tax Administration, 910-678-7507)

**The office.** Senior and disability relief is handled by **Cumberland County Tax Administration**, in the **Cumberland County Courthouse, 117 Dick Street, Room 530, Fayetteville, NC 28301**, phone **910-678-7507**, **Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m.** The state's official list of county assessors gives the same phone and a mailing address of **P.O. Box 449, Fayetteville, NC 28302**. If you are paying a bill in person, that window is in **Room 527**.

**The three programs.** All three are set by the state, filed on the state's **Form AV-9**, and applied for with Tax Administration. The county says applications must be **filed in person or by mail**.

- **Elderly or Disabled Exclusion.** Takes the **greater of $25,000 or 50 percent** off the assessed value of your home and **up to one acre**. You must be **65 or older or totally and permanently disabled**, own and live in the home, and have **combined income for you and your spouse** within the limit: **$38,800 for 2026, $39,900 for 2027**. The county counts **all income before taxes**, not adjusted gross income. **It is a one-time application**, unless ownership changes, the home stops being your permanent residence, or income goes over the limit. For disability, the county wants **Form AV-9A** signed by a physician licensed in North Carolina.
- **Circuit Breaker Tax Deferment.** Limits the tax to a percentage of income for owners who meet the exclusion rules and **have owned and lived in the home for the past five years**, with incomes up to **$58,200 for 2026** or **$59,850 for 2027**. **This is a deferral, not forgiveness.** The county says the **three most recent years of deferred taxes become due, with interest**, when a disqualifying event happens, and **deferred taxes become a lien on the property**. And unlike the exclusion, **the Circuit Breaker needs a new application every year.** Understand the lien before your parent signs, especially if the plan is to sell the house or leave it to family.
- **Disabled Veteran Exclusion.** Takes **$45,000** off the assessed value of the home of an honorably discharged veteran with a **100 percent total and permanent service-connected disability**, or an unmarried surviving spouse. **No age or income limit**, one-time application, with **Form NCDVA-9**. **Cumberland County Veterans Services** (below) helps with that form.

**Deadline.** The county says applications **must be filed by June 1** of the year relief is requested.

**The 2025 revaluation did not cancel anyone's relief.** Cumberland County revalued every property **as of January 1, 2025**, its first countywide revaluation since 2017. The county says **previously approved tax relief applications are still valid**, with **no need to submit a new application**.

**Rates and this year's bill.** The county rate on **2026 tax bills is 49.9 cents per $100** of value. Inside Fayetteville the city adds **44.95 cents**, for a combined **94.85 cents**. Unincorporated areas add a recreation and a fire district rate, for a combined **69.9 cents**. Bills were due **September 1, 2026**, and the county says **the deadline to pay 2026 taxes is January 5, 2027**; interest starts January 6 at 2 percent, then 0.75 percent a month. You can make **partial payments in any amount** before then, or set up a **formal payment plan** before the delinquency date by calling **910-678-7507**. Options narrow after delinquency.

**Two protections worth ten minutes.**
- The **Register of Deeds** offers a free **Property Notification** service: register once and you get an **email alert when a document is recorded** in the name you enter. Sign up at ccrod.org under Records Search, then New Property Notification, or call **910-678-7775**. The office is in the courthouse, **117 Dick Street, Room 114**, Monday to Friday 8 a.m. to 5 p.m.
- The Register of Deeds also **records military discharges (DD-214s) free of charge for retired military.** A recorded DD-214 is much easier to find years later when a family is sorting out benefits.

## Food

**Meals on Wheels.** The **Council on Older Adults** delivers meals to Cumberland County residents who are **at least 60**, **homebound** (assessed through a **home visit** with program staff), and **not able to prepare meals with no one willing or able to provide them**. To make a referral, call **910-484-0111**. The Council does not publish a waitlist status, so ask when you call.

**Free lunch at 60.** The Council's **congregate lunch** is a **free lunchtime meal** for people **60 and older**. To enroll, go to the site you want **Monday to Friday between 9 and 11 a.m.** and fill out the registration. The Council lists these sites:
- **Falling Run Missionary Baptist Church**, 2852 Cedar Creek Road, Fayetteville 28312
- **Cumberland County Association for Indian People**, 2173 Downing Road, Fayetteville 28312
- **VFW Post 10630**, 3226 Davis Street, Hope Mills 28348
- **Mt. Calvary Missionary Baptist Church**, 4320 Doc Bennett Road, Fayetteville 28306
- **Spring Lake Senior Center**, 301 Ruth Street, Spring Lake 28390

A county transportation page still shows an older list of meal sites, so go by the Council's list and call **910-484-0111** before a first visit.

**The regional food bank.** **Second Harvest Food Bank of Southeast North Carolina**, part of Action Pathways, serves **Bladen, Cumberland, Duplin, Harnett, Hoke, Robeson and Sampson** counties from its distribution center at **406 Deep Creek Road, Fayetteville, 910-485-6923**. Its **Harvest of Hope** senior program offers food to **low-income seniors 55 and older** in the seven counties; it takes questions by email at **seniorsshfb@actionpathways.ngo**.

**Food and Nutrition Services (SNAP).** Apply online at **epass.nc.gov**, in person at 1225 Ramsey Street, or ask for an application by mail or email at **910-323-1540**.

## Energy and utility help

**Low Income Energy Assistance (LIEAP).** A once-a-year payment sent straight to the heating company. Cumberland County Social Services makes **December a priority month** for households with **someone 60 or older**, or a person getting services through the NC Division of Aging and Adult Services who also receives SSI, Social Security disability or VA disability. The state's page says the same, **December 1 to 31**. Everyone else applies **January, February and March**, while funds last. The county says income cannot exceed **130 percent** of the federal poverty level, with a **$2,250** household resource limit. The county's posted dollar chart looks older than this year's poverty guidelines, so ask for the current figure. Apply on **ePASS**, by fax to **910-677-2885**, by mail to P.O. Box 2429, Fayetteville, NC 28302, or drop it off at 1225 Ramsey Street. **Households that include a Native American adult 18 or older apply through the Lumbee Tribe**, not the county.

**Crisis Intervention Program (CIP).** For a **heating or cooling emergency**, such as a disconnect notice or no heat, when someone's health is at risk. Cumberland takes CIP applications **year round, July 1 to June 30, until the money runs out**. Call **910-323-1540**.

**Who sends the power bill matters.** Look at the name on the bill: the **Fayetteville Public Works Commission (PWC)**, **Duke Energy Progress**, or an electric cooperative such as **Lumbee River EMC**. Each has different help.
- **PWC.** PWC's **Medical Priority** program gives early notice and priority restoration to customers whose doctor completes the application, **renewed every year**. It does not reduce the bill. For help paying, PWC points customers to Social Services' CIP and LIEAP, **Operation Blessing (910-483-1119)**, **Fayetteville Urban Ministry (910-483-5944)** and 2-1-1. PWC customer service: **910-483-1382**.
- **Duke Energy Progress.** The **Share the Light Fund** is funded by Duke Energy Progress and handed out through county social services, **for Duke Energy Progress residential customers**. Ask Social Services at **910-323-1540**.
- **Lumbee River EMC.** Its **LREMC Cares Energy Assistance Program** gives eligible members **up to $150 once a year**. For Cumberland County members it runs through **Fayetteville Urban Ministry, 701 Whitfield Street, 910-483-5944**.
- **Any heating fuel.** Piedmont Natural Gas's **Share the Warmth** fund is handed out through county social services. State policy says a household **does not have to heat with gas or be a Piedmont customer**, and the benefit is capped at **$200 per state fiscal year**. Ask Social Services whether money is available this season.

## Medicare, Medicaid, prescriptions

**Free Medicare counseling.** North Carolina's **Seniors' Health Insurance Information Program (SHIIP)**, run by the NC Department of Insurance, gives free, unbiased Medicare counseling. In Cumberland County the counseling site is the **Council on Older Adults, 339 Devers Street**, **by appointment only**: call **910-484-0111, extension 221**, and ask for SHIIP help. The statewide SHIIP line is **1-855-408-1212**, Monday to Friday 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Call before any Medicare Advantage or Part D decision, especially during open enrollment, **October 15 to December 7, 2026**.

**Help paying Medicare costs and prescriptions.** The **Medicare Savings Programs** can pay Medicare premiums, and **Extra Help** lowers Part D drug costs. Apply for the Medicare Savings Programs through Social Services at **910-323-1540**, and for Extra Help through SHIIP or Social Security at **1-800-772-1213**. **NC MedAssist (1-866-331-1348)** is a free prescription program for uninsured North Carolinians with income under 300 percent of poverty; it says it may help **some Medicare patients on a case-by-case basis**. We are not printing income limits for the Medicare programs; ask SHIIP to check them against your parent's numbers.

**Medicaid, and a reason to apply before December 31.** Apply online, in person at the first floor reception of 1225 Ramsey Street (Monday to Friday 7:30 a.m. to 5 p.m.), by fax to **910-677-2885**, or ask for a paper application at **910-323-1540**. The **NC Medicaid Contact Center** is **888-245-0179**. NC Medicaid says that for **adults 65 and older and people with a disability**, if you **apply by December 31, 2026**, Medicaid will cover approved costs for **up to 3 months before you applied**. Starting **January 1, 2027**, that shrinks to **2 months**. If a parent is heading toward a nursing home or already paying for care, that difference can be real money.

**Staying home on Medicaid: CAP/DA.** The **Community Alternatives Program for Disabled Adults (CAP/DA)** is North Carolina's Medicaid waiver that brings in-home aide help, respite and case management so someone who would otherwise qualify for a nursing home can stay home. NC Medicaid's list of case management agencies for Cumberland County (dated May 2025) names **Bladen County Health Department/CAP, 910-872-6257**; **CAP of Cape Fear Valley Health System, 910-615-1841**; **Divine Home Care, 910-904-2377**; **Healthkeeperz Inc., 910-522-0009**; **Primary Health Choice, 910-865-3500**; **RBS Case Management, 910-768-0002**; **RHA Management Services, Inc., 888-207-7828**; and **Walkers Home Care CAPDA, 252-432-6308**. The list is more than a year old, so if a line does not answer, ask the Medicaid Contact Center.

**Special Assistance In-Home.** For someone who is eligible to live in a licensed residential care facility but wants to and can safely stay home, Social Services' **Special Assistance In-Home** program gives financial support. Call Adult Services at **910-677-2388** or **910-677-2389**.

**Mental health.** Cumberland County's public mental health plan is **Alliance Health**. Its **Behavioral Health Crisis Line is 877-223-4617, 24 hours a day**; member services is **800-510-9132**.

## Home repair and safety

- **County Housing Rehabilitation, outside Fayetteville city limits only.** **Cumberland County Community Development** offers a **deferred loan with no payments** for owner-occupied homes **outside the City of Fayetteville**, for households **below 80 percent** of the county median income. The note is at **3 percent over 15 years, payment deferred**, and it is forgiven **annually on a prorated basis beginning on the sixth anniversary** of the loan. Taxes must be current, there can be no outstanding liens or second mortgage, and **single-wide mobile homes are not eligible**. The loan amount is set case by case. **910-323-6112**, 707 Executive Place, Fayetteville.
- **City of Fayetteville Emergency Home Repair, inside city limits only.** Run by **Fayetteville Urban Ministry**, it covers **emergency repairs, not full rehabilitation**, for owners who have **lived in the home at least 12 months**, are **not facing foreclosure**, and have income at or below **80 percent** of area median, which the city lists as **$45,850 for one person and $52,400 for two**. Owners agree **not to sell for 12 months** after the repairs. **910-483-5944.**
- **Fayetteville Area Habitat for Humanity.** Its critical repair, **aging in place** and veteran repair programs serve low-income homeowners who are **veterans, elderly or disabled**, and homeowners must be able to pay project fees. **Applications are closed right now.** Call **910-483-0952** to ask when they reopen; the office is closed October 5 to 12, 2026.
- **Weatherization and heating repair.** North Carolina's weatherization provider for Cumberland County is **Action Pathways, 4525 Campground Road, Fayetteville, 910-485-6131**. Weatherization serves households **below 200 percent** of poverty, which the state lists as **$31,920 a year for one person and $43,280 for two**, and **prioritizes the elderly and people with disabilities**. Homes weatherized in the past 15 years do not qualify. Its **Heating and Air Repair or Replacement Program (HARRP)** inspects, repairs or replaces failing systems. Have ID for everyone 18 or older, proof of income (no bank statements) and a **12-month utility printout** ready.

**The emergency registry.** Cumberland County's new **Cumberland SAFE** (Safety for Access and Functional Needs in Emergencies) is a **confidential, voluntary registry** that explicitly includes **older adults living alone**. Sign up at cumberlandcountync.gov/alerts, pick up a paper form at **500 Executive Place, Fayetteville**, or call **910-321-6736**, Monday to Friday 8 a.m. to 5 p.m.

**Project Lifesaver.** The **Cumberland County Sheriff's Office** Community Policing Unit runs **Project Lifesaver**, which fits people who wander because of dementia or a similar condition with a tracking bracelet. Call Community Policing at **910-438-4015**, Monday to Friday 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Sheriff's Office main line **910-323-1500**, 131 Dick Street.

## Transportation

**Free county rides to medical visits and the pharmacy.** The **Cumberland County Community Transportation Program** gives **free rides to medical appointments and pharmacy pickups within Cumberland County** to residents **60 and older, or disabled with a doctor's verification**, who do **not live in a nursing or rest home** and are **not eligible for other transportation**, such as Medicaid or ADA rides. Requests must be made **by noon the business day before**; same-day rides cannot be scheduled. Call **910-678-7619**. The county's pages list slightly different call hours, so call in the morning. The office is at 130 Gillespie Street.

**Rides to lunch.** Through the Home and Community Care Block Grant, the same program takes people **60 and older** to the senior nutrition sites. **No fee is required**; contributions are asked for, and service is not denied for inability to pay.

**FAST buses.** FAST's regular fare is **$1.25**, and the **discount fare is 50 cents** with a **FAST Discount ID**. The city's application qualifies riders **65 or older** with a government photo ID, and the ID is issued at the **FAST Transit Center, 505 Franklin Street**, where a **Medicare card**, a Veterans service-connected health ID card or a valid government ID is accepted as proof. A 30-day pass is **$17** at the discount rate. FAST's main line is **910-433-1329**.

**FASTTrac!, FAST's paratransit.** For riders **certified as eligible** case by case, within **three quarters of a mile of a FAST bus route**. A one-way trip is **$2**. Book **up to 7 days ahead**, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., Monday to Sunday; **no same-day reservations**. Call **910-433-1232, option 2**.

**Rural rides for anyone.** The county's Rural General Public rides cost **$2.50 each way**, weekdays 5 a.m. to 8 p.m. Call **910-678-7675**.

**Medicaid rides to appointments.** Social Services' **medical transportation coordinator** is at **910-677-2529**. The county does not publish its notice rules online, so ask how far ahead to book when you call.

## Legal help

**Senior Legal Helpline.** **Legal Aid of North Carolina's Senior Legal Helpline** gives **free civil legal help to North Carolinians 60 and older**: **1-877-579-7562**, Monday to Friday **9 to 11 a.m. and 1 to 3 p.m.**

**Legal Aid, Fayetteville office.** **327 Dick Street, Suite 103, Fayetteville, 910-483-0400** or toll free **1-800-824-5340**, Monday to Friday 9 a.m. to 12:30 p.m. and 2 to 5 p.m., serves **Cumberland, Hoke, Robeson, Sampson and Scotland** counties. To apply, Legal Aid's general helpline is **1-866-219-5262**, Monday to Friday 9 a.m. to 3 p.m.

**Protecting an older adult.**
- **Cumberland County Adult Protective Services:** **910-677-2389** or **910-677-2388**. The intake line is answered **24 hours a day, seven days a week**. The county asks that you **not email or leave voicemail** about abuse or neglect. **In an emergency, call 911.** North Carolina law requires anyone who suspects an older adult is being abused or neglected to report it.
- **Long-Term Care Ombudsman at Mid-Carolina**, for problems in a nursing home or assisted living: **910-323-4191, extension 25**.
- **Guardianship questions**, Social Services Adult Services: **910-677-2389** or **910-677-2388**, ask for the intake social worker.
- **NC Attorney General Consumer Protection: 1-877-566-7226** (1-877-5-NO-SCAM).
- **National Elder Fraud Hotline: 1-833-372-8311.**

**Paperwork offices you will need.** A power of attorney that will be used for real estate is recorded at the **Cumberland County Register of Deeds, 117 Dick Street, Room 114, 910-678-7775**. Wills and estates are handled by the **Clerk of Superior Court** at the **Judge E. Maurice Braswell Cumberland County Courthouse, 117 Dick Street, Fayetteville, 910-475-3000**.

**For veterans and military retirees.**
- **Cumberland County Veterans Services**, **301 East Russell Street, Fayetteville, NC 28301, 910-677-2970**, **by appointment**, Monday to Thursday 8:30 a.m. to 4 p.m. and Friday 8:30 to 11 a.m. It signs the **NCDVA-9** for the Disabled Veteran property tax exclusion; bring the **VA award letter showing 100 percent permanent and total status**, or for a surviving spouse, the **DIC award letter and the veteran's death certificate**.
- **Fort Bragg Retirement Services Office.** The Army renamed Fort Liberty back to **Fort Bragg** on February 14, 2025. Its Retirement Services Office helps **retirees and their families** with benefits, entitlements and **Survivor Benefit Plan** questions: **910-396-5304** or **800-535-6067**, in the Soldier Support Center, Building 4-2843, Normandy Drive. It is open **Monday, Wednesday and Friday, 8:30 a.m. to 4 p.m.**, and closed Tuesday and Thursday.

- **Project C.A.R.E.** Dementia-specific support and respite for families caring for someone with dementia at home. The state's Southeastern office covers Cumberland: **910-408-6365**.
- **Adult day programs.** The state's April 2026 list of certified adult day programs in Cumberland includes **Cape Fear Adult Day Health Care Center, 920 Stamper Road, Fayetteville, 910-323-4424**; **RDL Therapeutic Adult Day Center, 6007 Morganton Road, Fayetteville, 910-676-8676**; and the day health center at **LIFE St. Joseph of the Pines** (below). ## PACE

**Cumberland County has a PACE program, and the state's list covers the whole county.**

PACE, the **Program of All-Inclusive Care for the Elderly**, takes over a person's whole care plan, doctors, a day center, therapy, personal care, prescriptions and transportation, so someone who needs a nursing home level of care can keep living at home. In North Carolina it is sometimes called LIFE. **LIFE St. Joseph of the Pines**, part of **Trinity Health PACE**, runs its center at **4900 Raeford Road, Fayetteville**: public line **910-483-4911**, referrals **910-429-7200**, TTY 711, 8 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. with 24/7 on-call support for participants.

**Who qualifies.** People **55 and older** who live in a service area ZIP code and **have been assessed by the state as needing nursing home care**. The program says it is **typically free** for people who have Medicare and Medicaid or Medicaid only, and people without Medicaid pay a **flat monthly fee**.

**Check the ZIP code.** NC Medicaid's official PACE list names LIFE St. Joseph of the Pines for **Cumberland County** and portions of Harnett, Robeson, Moore and Hoke. Its ZIP list includes **Fayetteville 28301 through 28312 and 28314**, **Fort Bragg 28307 and 28310**, **Falcon 28342**, **Godwin 28344**, **Hope Mills 28348**, **Linden 28356**, **Spring Lake 28390**, **Stedman 28391** and **Wade 28395**. The program's own site does not publish a ZIP list, so confirm your parent's address when you call. The state lists a different contact number, **910-429-7242**; start with the program's public line.

## Frequently asked questions
**Mom is 72, lives in Hope Mills, and gets about $3,000 a month from Social Security and a small pension. Does she qualify for a tax break?** Likely. About $3,000 a month is about $36,000 a year, under the **$38,800** limit for 2026 and the **$39,900** limit for 2027, as long as that is the combined income for her and any spouse. The **Elderly or Disabled Exclusion** takes the greater of **$25,000 or half** her home's value off the bill. It is a one-time application, due **by June 1**. Call Tax Administration at **910-678-7507**.

**Dad already had the exclusion. Does the 2025 revaluation mean he has to apply again?** No. The county says previously approved relief is still valid and there is no need to submit a new application. If he uses the **Circuit Breaker**, though, he reapplies every year no matter what.

**Should my father use the Circuit Breaker instead?** Go in with open eyes. It limits his tax to a percentage of income, but the rest is **deferred, not forgiven**. It becomes a lien, and when a disqualifying event happens, **the three most recent years of deferred taxes come due with interest**. He must **reapply every year** and needs five years of ownership and occupancy first. If his income is under the exclusion limit, the exclusion is usually simpler. Ask Tax Administration to run both, and talk to the **Senior Legal Helpline (1-877-579-7562)** if the house is part of an estate plan. If he is moving into a nursing home, ask Tax Administration directly how that affects either program.

**Dad is a retired soldier and Mom would be the surviving spouse. What should we do now?** Three things. Record his **DD-214** free at the **Register of Deeds (910-678-7775)**. Call the **Fort Bragg Retirement Services Office (910-396-5304)** to review his **Survivor Benefit Plan** election. And if he has a 100 percent permanent and total VA rating, have **Veterans Services (910-677-2970)** sign the **NCDVA-9** so the house gets the **$45,000 Disabled Veteran Exclusion**, and an unmarried surviving spouse can qualify for it too.

**Mom's savings are running out and she may need Medicaid for care. Does timing matter?** It can. NC Medicaid says people 65 and older who **apply by December 31, 2026** can get approved costs covered for **up to 3 months before they applied**; from January 1, 2027, the look-back is **2 months**. Call Social Services at **910-323-1540**, and talk to the Senior Legal Helpline before moving any assets.

**Mom lives alone in Spring Lake and I live out of state. What can I set up today?** Put her on **Cumberland SAFE** (**910-321-6736**) so emergency managers know she lives alone. Sign her house up for the **Register of Deeds Property Notification** alert. Ask the **Council on Older Adults (910-484-0111)** about **telephone reassurance** and the free lunch at the **Spring Lake Senior Center**. And if she needs a nursing home level of care, ask **LIFE St. Joseph of the Pines (910-483-4911)** whether PACE can keep her home.
