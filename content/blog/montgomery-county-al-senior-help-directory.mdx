*Free, plain-English help for seniors and families in Montgomery County, AL. Education, not advice. Verify before you rely on it.*

**Three local front doors:** **Central Alabama Aging Consortium (CAAC), Area Agency on Aging**, **400 Cotton Gin Road, Montgomery, AL 36117**, main **334-240-4680**, toll free **1-800-264-4680**, screening line **334-240-4670**, or the statewide **1-800-AGELINE (1-800-243-5463)** | **Meals on Wheels Montgomery AL Metro Area (Montgomery Area Council on Aging)**, **115 East Jefferson Street, Montgomery, AL 36104**, **334-263-0532** | **2-1-1**, also **888-421-1266**, or **text your ZIP code to 898-211**, available 24/7.

**Start with the office that holds most of this page: the Central Alabama Aging Consortium.** CAAC is the Area Agency on Aging for **Autauga, Elmore and Montgomery counties**, and in this county it is not just a referral line. It takes intake for the **Medicaid Elderly and Disabled Waiver**, runs **SHIP** Medicare counseling and the **Senior Medicare Patrol**, sponsors the senior centers where people gather for lunch, runs the **Alabama Cares** caregiver program, handles **SenioRx** prescription help, provides **Long-Term Care Ombudsman** advocacy, and offers **free legal assistance to people 60 and older**. It is open **Monday to Friday, 8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.**, and the email is **caac.adrc@caac-al.org**. If you only make one call from this page, make it **334-240-4680**.

**The second thing to understand is the line the bus draws.** Montgomery County is the capital city, the town of **Pike Road**, and a large rural stretch beyond them that includes communities like **Hope Hull**. The M's fixed routes and its paratransit service, **MAP**, are built for the city. MAP only operates **within a three quarter mile corridor of the fixed routes**. So a one dollar senior bus fare is a real answer in town and not an answer at all for a farmhouse off Federal Road. Meals, by contrast, reach well beyond the bus, through CAAC's senior centers and Meals on Wheels. Sort out which side of that line an address falls on before you build a plan around it.

**The third pattern is about the tax office, and it is partly a warning about what we could not confirm.** Montgomery County's property tax office is a **Revenue Commissioner**, currently **Janet Buskey**, and the county runs it from a **Downtown Revenue Office** and **two satellites**. The **East Satellite on Atlanta Highway closed in September 2025**, so if a parent used to pay there, that office is gone. The exemption amounts come from state law and are laid out below. What the county does **not** publish online, as far as we could find, is its own rule on whether the over-65 exemption has to be **reclaimed every year**. Other Alabama counties answer that question differently, so we are routing it to the office rather than guessing.

Three more numbers belong on the refrigerator. The **Alabama Adult Abuse Hotline at 1-800-458-7214** takes reports of suspected abuse, neglect or exploitation of an older or disabled adult. The **Long-Term Care Ombudsman**, reached through **CAAC at 334-240-4680** or the state office at **334-242-5753**, is who you call about a nursing home or assisted living facility rather than that facility's own management. And **2-1-1** will tell you which programs currently have money.

## Property tax relief (apply with the Montgomery County Revenue Commissioner, 334-832-1250)
**Get the office right first.** The Alabama Department of Revenue's official list of county property tax offices shows Montgomery County with a **Revenue Commissioner**, currently **Janet Buskey**, mailing address **P.O. Box 1667, Montgomery, AL 36102**. The county describes the job in one sentence worth knowing: the Revenue Commissioner **handles the mapping, appraising, assessing and collection of property taxes in Montgomery County**, and **is also responsible for granting exemptions from taxes**. One office, start to finish.

**Where to go, now that there are three offices instead of four.** In September 2025 the County Commission announced that the **East Satellite Revenue Office at 5449 Atlanta Highway** would **close effective at noon on Thursday, September 25, 2025**. The offices that remain:

- **Downtown Revenue Office**, **101 South Lawrence Street, Montgomery, AL 36104**, **334-832-1250**
- **South Satellite Revenue Office**, **3425 McGehee Road, Montgomery, AL 36111**, **334-284-4533**
- **West Satellite Revenue Office**, **3075 Mobile Highway, Montgomery, AL 36108**, **334-262-4546**

**The Appraisal Department has moved.** The Appraisal Department, which is part of the Revenue Commissioner's office and handles real property, business personal property and mapping, posts that it has **moved to 125 Washington Ave., Montgomery, AL 36104**, with mail still going to **P.O. Box 1667**. The state's county office list still shows an appraisal line of **334-832-1303** and a **Board of Equalization** line of **334-832-1327**. The county's appraisal page notes that **2026 valuation notices were scheduled to be mailed on or about April 15, 2026**, that notices go to **all property owners**, and that **if you are exempt no action need to be taken** on the notice. It also offers an **online appeal**. The county's property records portal is at **montgomery.capturecama.com**.

**What the exemptions are.** Alabama sets the homestead tiers statewide. In the Department of Revenue's own words:

- **H-1.** Taxpayers **under age 65 and not disabled**: **$4,000 assessed value** state and **$2,000 assessed value** county.
- **H-2.** Taxpayers **65 and older with an annual adjusted gross income of less than $12,000**, or **retired due to permanent and total disability regardless of age**: exempt from **all of the state portion** of the property taxes and **$5,000 of assessed value** on the county portion.
- **H-3, age 65 and older.** Taxpayers **65 and older with net taxable income of $12,000 or less on the combined taxpayer and spouse federal return**: **exempt from all ad valorem taxes**.
- **H-3, disabled.** A taxpayer who is **permanently and totally disabled** is **exempt from all ad valorem taxes**, and **there is no income limitation**.
- **H-4.** Taxpayers **65 and older with income greater than $12,000** on their most recent Alabama return: exempt from **all of the state portion**, plus the regular **$2,000** county homestead exemption.

The Department of Revenue adds a short answer to the question most families ask first. **If you are over 65, or permanently and totally disabled regardless of age, or blind regardless of age, you are exempt from the state portion of property tax.** County taxes may still be due, which is why the tier matters.

**The number that decides it.** The gap between H-4 and H-3 is the gap between trimming a bill and erasing it, and the deciding figure is **net taxable income on the federal return**, not gross income and not the size of the Social Security deposit. Take the actual return to the office before deciding a parent does not qualify.

**Who qualifies at all.** A homestead is **a single-family owner-occupied dwelling and the land thereto, not exceeding 160 acres**, and the owner must **own and occupy it as their primary residence on the first day of the tax year**. The state's instruction on applying is simple: **visit your local county office**.

**What we are routing rather than stating.** We looked for Montgomery County's own exemption guidance, the kind of page that spells out required documents, local deadlines and whether the senior exemptions must be reclaimed each year. The county's Revenue Commissioner page now forwards to the property records portal, and we did not find such a page anywhere on the county site. That matters because **Alabama counties do not handle renewal the same way**: some require the age and income exemptions to be reclaimed annually, others publish no annual rule at all. So do not assume either way. Call **334-832-1250** and ask four questions in one call: which exemption code am I on now, what documents do you need to change it, is there a deadline, and do I have to reclaim it every year. People are often on H-1 when they qualify for H-3.

**A statewide protection that helps whether or not you qualify on age.** Under **Act 2024-344**, Alabama limits the year-over-year increase in taxable assessed value for **Class II and Class III** real property, which includes owner-occupied residential property, to **seven percent**. The base year was **October 1, 2024**, for collections beginning **October 1, 2025**, and the limitation continues **through the fiscal year beginning October 1, 2027**. The cap comes off on a change of ownership, with exceptions for family transfers, transfers because of death and foreclosure redemptions, and also when the classification changes or significant improvements are added. The county's appraisal page links to the same 7 percent cap information.

## Food
**Meals on Wheels, and it is big here.** The **Montgomery Area Council on Aging**, which now does business as **Meals on Wheels Montgomery AL Metro Area**, has served seniors in Central Alabama **since 1972**. Its program is plain: **every Monday through Friday, volunteers deliver hot, nutritious lunchtime meals to 450 homebound seniors who are unable to prepare their own meals**, and its published eligibility is **homebound senior residents of Montgomery, AL, 60 years or older**. It also runs a **Frozen Meals Program**, a **Pet Food Program** that brings food for recipients' cats and dogs twice a month, and **Holiday Bags** of groceries and hygiene items every December. Call **334-263-0532**, email **mowmama@mowmama.org**, or use the **Refer A Client** form on its website. The office at **115 East Jefferson Street** is open **Monday to Thursday 8 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. and Friday 8 a.m. to noon**.

**Senior centers through the Area Agency on Aging.** CAAC **sponsors senior centers located in various community facilities throughout the tri-county area where seniors gather for lunch and enjoy recreational, educational, physical, and social activities**, and **home delivered lunches are available to home-bound individuals age 60 and older who are unable to attend a senior center**. The Montgomery County sites CAAC lists are **Antioch, Big Roxanna, Dublin, Freewill, New Home, Pilgrim Rest, St. Paul and True Divine Community**, along with a **MACOA pickup** site. CAAC publishes the names but not the addresses, so call **334-240-4680** and ask which is closest. CAAC also offers **nutrition counseling** for people 60 and older identified as at risk for poor nutrition.

**A city senior center with a hot lunch.** The City of Montgomery's **Crump Senior Center**, **1751 Cong. W. L. Dickinson Drive, Montgomery, AL 36109**, **334-625-4547**, is a fully accessible center with exercise classes, table games, tax assistance, a fitness area, a computer lab and a music room, and **members can enjoy a hot lunch for a small fee**. It is open **Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m.** For an older adult who is more isolated than unwell, this is often the better first call.

**The regional food bank.** The **Heart of Alabama Food Bank** is based at **521 Trade Center Street, Montgomery, AL 36108**, phone **334-263-3784**, and serves **35 counties**, **12 directly** and the rest through four affiliated food banks. Two programs are aimed at older adults. The **Commodity Supplemental Food Program** provides **nutritious USDA Foods** for **low-income seniors aged 60 and older**, and the **Senior Supplement Program** provides **monthly food boxes filled with 12 to 25 pounds of essential, nutritious foods**, with **fresh produce and high-protein foods**. The food bank does not publish the income limits on its program page, so ask when you call. For a pantry near a specific address, use its food map at **hafb.org/food-map** or dial **2-1-1**.

**SNAP, and the short form seniors should ask for by name.** The **Montgomery County Department of Human Resources** is at **3030 Mobile Highway, Montgomery, AL 36108**, mailing address **P.O. Box 250250, Montgomery, AL 36125-0250**, main line **334-293-3100**, open **8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.**, director **Kelly Pearson**. The state's food assistance information line is **334-242-0143 in the Montgomery area** and **800-382-0499** elsewhere. Ask about the **Alabama Elderly Simplified Application Project**. The current state form is for households where **everyone in the SNAP household is age 65 or older**, or where all members are 65 or older and **purchase and prepare food separately from the other people in the home**, and where **no SNAP household member receives earnings from work**. It says you **may file this application by completing at least your name, address, and signing the form**, and for help completing it the state lists **1-800-438-2958**. Many websites still describe this program at age 60, so ask for it by name, not by age.

## Energy and utility help
**LIHEAP goes through Montgomery Community Action.** ADECA assigns Montgomery County's **LIHEAP** to **Region 8**, **Montgomery Community Action Committee and CDC, Inc.**, **1066 Adams Avenue, Montgomery, AL 36104-4424**, with a **call center at 334-230-5259** and a **main office at 334-263-3474**. It serves **Montgomery County** alone. ADECA sets the income ceiling at **150 percent of the federal poverty level**, prioritizes households with **the elderly, persons with disabilities and young children**, and is blunt that **ADECA does not schedule appointments and does not accept or process applications**. The local agency is the only real door. One practical note: as of this writing, the agency's own website shows that it is **temporarily under construction** and directs anyone needing help to **call 334-263-3474**. So call rather than hunting for an online form.

**Weatherization is a different agency, and it is the county.** For weatherization, ADECA lists **Region 10** as the **Montgomery County Commission**, **430 South Court Street, Montgomery, AL 36104**, with the program delivered through the **Central Alabama Regional Planning and Development Commission (CARPDC)**, contact **Donny Barber**, **334-262-4300**. CARPDC's Weatherization Assistance Program states that **households at or below 200 percent of the poverty income guidelines are considered eligible**, as are households receiving **Supplemental Security Income**, and that it **gives priority to the elderly, families with one or more members with a disability, families with children, or high-energy users**. Its application asks for **proof of property ownership, such as a copy of deed or property tax notification**. Call **1-866-780-4945** toll free or **334-262-4300** and ask for the Weatherization office, or mail an application to **CARPDC Weatherization Program, 430 South Court St., Montgomery, AL 36104**. LIHEAP pays this month's bill. Weatherization is what lowers next winter's.

**Now look at the name on the electric bill, because not every home here is on the same utility.** If the bill says **Alabama Power**, **Project SHARE**, run in partnership with the Salvation Army, **helps low-income Alabamians who are 60+ or disabled pay winter energy bills**, and **in summer, those with medical emergencies may also qualify if funds are available**. Alabama Power directs applicants to **apply at your local Salvation Army office or call 205-328-2420**. In Montgomery, the Salvation Army's corps is at **3460 Carter Hill Road**, phone **334-265-0281**. But **Dixie Electric Cooperative**, a member-owned utility headquartered at **9100 Atlanta Highway, Montgomery, AL 36117**, serves **portions of eight Alabama counties including Montgomery**. Project SHARE is Alabama Power's program, so if the bill says Dixie Electric, call the co-op at **888-349-4332** and ask what help it offers. Its website references **energy audits** and **levelized billing**, which evens out seasonal spikes.

**Two more Alabama Power routes.** For Alabama Power customers, the utility publishes an **Energy Assistant** tool that identifies programs through anonymous questions, **low income assistance programs** for people receiving **SSI or Medicaid**, and **special needs** options including **alternate payment dates** and **Braille and large-print bills**. The **Alabama Business Charitable Trust Fund**, set up by Alabama Power stockholders, supports energy assistance through community action agencies, which in this county means back to **334-230-5259**.

## Medicare, Medicaid, prescriptions
**Free Medicare counseling, and nobody is selling anything.** CAAC runs **SHIP**, the State Health Insurance Assistance Program, and the **Senior Medicare Patrol**. Its counselors **address Medicare, Medicaid, supplemental insurance, and long-term insurance questions**. Call **334-240-4680** and ask for the ADRC. CAAC puts one number on its SHIP page that every family in this county should read twice: **nearly 60 percent of seniors in Autauga, Elmore and Montgomery counties are missing out on the Medicare Savings Program**. CAAC describes eligibility as based on income limits, and the one way to find out whether a parent fits is to ask.

**Medicaid, and a note about the office address.** Alabama Medicaid's statewide office table lists the **Montgomery District Office** at **200 Interstate Park Drive, Suite 207, Montgomery, AL 36109**, phone **866-621-6509**, serving **Autauga, Bullock, Butler, Chilton, Elmore, Lowndes and Montgomery** counties. The agency's own district map page, however, still describes the Montgomery office as located at the **Montgomery County Health Department, 3060 Mobile Highway, Montgomery, AL 36108**. Two official pages, two addresses. **Call 866-621-6509 before you go** and confirm where to bring paperwork.

**The Elderly and Disabled Waiver.** CAAC describes the **Medicaid Elderly and Disabled Waiver** as a program funded by Alabama Medicaid designed to **help older adults and people with disabilities stay in their own homes or community settings instead of needing a nursing facility**. To qualify, a person must qualify for **full Medicaid benefits, meaning income and assets are within Medicaid limits**, and must have **a level of disability or physical need similar to what would normally qualify you for a nursing home**. Services can include **homemaker services, personal care assistance, companion services, respite care, case management, home-delivered meals, adult day health activities, assistive technology, home modifications, pest control, and personal emergency response systems**. Call **334-240-4680** and **ask for the Intake Department**. CAAC also handles the **ACT Waiver**, for people **who live in a nursing facility but want to transition home or to a community setting**.

**Prescription help.** **SenioRx** is Alabama's prescription assistance program, and it has helped thousands of Alabamians receive **free or low-cost prescription medications from pharmaceutical companies**. You qualify at **age 55 or older** with **a chronic medical condition**, **no prescription drug insurance or limited prescription drug insurance**, and income within certain limits. Eligible clients **may receive a three-month supply of medication**, with **refills permitted as long as the participant remains eligible**. People with a disability qualify at **any age**, including those **in the 24-month Medicare waiting period**. CAAC runs it locally at **334-240-4680**, or call **1-800-AGELINE (1-800-243-5463)**.

## Home repair and safety
**First, the program you will find in searches that you cannot use.** The **Montgomery Thrive Critical Home Repair Program**, a Montgomery County Commission and City of Montgomery initiative funded through the American Rescue Plan Act and administered by CARPDC, offered free emergency repairs of up to **$25,000** per household, covering roofs, HVAC, plumbing, electrical and more. The county's own page now states that **the Montgomery Thrive Critical Home Repair Program is no longer accepting applications**, and CARPDC dates that to **November 9, 2023**. It still turns up in search results and older news stories. Do not build a plan around it. Questions about it go to **CARPDC at 334-262-4300**.

**What actually works now.** For an owner-occupied home, **weatherization** through CARPDC at **1-866-780-4945** or **334-262-4300** is the most dependable repair-adjacent program, and it gives priority to the elderly and people with disabilities. For someone who qualifies for the **Medicaid Elderly and Disabled Waiver**, CAAC lists **home modifications**, **assistive technology** and **personal emergency response systems** among the services, which is how a grab bar, a ramp or a fall alert button can get paid for; ask at **334-240-4680**. And **2-1-1** knows which church and civic repair ministries currently have volunteers and funds.

**Wandering protection through the Sheriff.** The **Montgomery County Sheriff's Office** runs **Project Lifesaver**, a search and rescue program for **at-risk individuals who are prone to wandering**, including people with **Alzheimer's, dementia, autism or other cognitive diseases**. The office states that through **multi-agency collaboration of monetary and personnel resources we plan to issue bracelets to any resident suffering from a memory-impaired illness**; each bracelet carries a personal PIN registered to the client, and once the Sheriff's Office is contacted, **we will notify the caregiver of their loved ones location**. **Sheriff Cunningham has a Deputy assigned to the program for registration.** The application is on the Sheriff's website, and the program line is **334-832-1369**. The page does not state a fee, so ask about cost when you call. Enroll before there is an incident, not after.

## Rent, housing, emergency cash
**Public housing and vouchers.** The **Montgomery Housing Authority** is at **525 South Lawrence Street, Montgomery, AL 36104**, phone **334-206-7200**, email **info@mhatoday.org**, open **8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.** Applications are made through an **online portal** where you set up an individual account and can use it for multiple applications. The authority's own website does not publish current waiting list status, so call and ask specifically about **public housing for seniors** as well as about vouchers, since those lists open and close on different schedules. Treat anything you read elsewhere about which lists are open as a reason to call, not a fact.

**A useful accident of geography.** The **Appraisal Department** and the **Montgomery/Bullock/Lowndes County Veterans Service Office** both list **125 Washington Avenue**, Montgomery County Courthouse Annex II. If a widow needs to sort out both a property question and a veterans claim, that is one trip.

**Emergency help.** **Montgomery Community Action at 334-263-3474** is the local community action agency. The **Salvation Army Montgomery Corps** is at **3460 Carter Hill Road, 334-265-0281**. And **2-1-1**, or **888-421-1266**, is the fastest way to find out who has money this month for rent, utilities or food.

## Transportation
**The M, and the senior fare is a dollar.** Montgomery's bus system, **The M**, is owned by the City of Montgomery and operated under contract by Transdev. The regular fare is **$2.00**. The senior and disabled fare is **$1.00 with valid ID**, and that category **applies to those 65 years of age and older, those with a disability, and those with a Medicare card**. That last group is worth noticing: a person under 65 on Medicare because of a disability rides at the reduced fare too. **Transfers are free with purchase of any fare.** A **7-day pass** is **$10.00** for seniors and disabled riders against **$18.00**, and a **31-day pass** is **$25.00** against **$45.00**. The reduced-fare ID is free: **present evidence of age and/or disability at the Transit System office, 2318 W. Fairview Ave.**, and a photo ID is issued. Customer service is **334-625-4012**.

**Door-to-door service, inside the corridor.** **Montgomery Area Paratransit**, known as **MAP**, is for people **age six and older** who cannot use the fixed route because of a **physical or cognitive disability**. It runs **the same hours as the fixed route buses** and **provides service within a 3/4 mile corridor of fixed routes**. The fare is **$4.00 one way**. To apply, send a **Registration Form** and a **Medical Information Form signed by a physician or social worker** to **MAP Eligibility Dept., 2318 West Fairview Ave., Montgomery, AL 36108**, or call **334-625-4691**. The transit system says a complete application will be processed **within 21 calendar days**, that certification for temporary disabilities is available, and that the longest certification is **three years**. Start before you need it.

**Outside the corridor.** For addresses beyond three quarters of a mile from a route, which covers much of rural Montgomery County, MAP is not an option. Call **CAAC at 334-240-4680** and **2-1-1** and ask what transportation currently operates for your specific community and purpose, whether that is medical appointments, a senior center or groceries. If the person qualifies for the Medicaid Elderly and Disabled Waiver, ask how services can be arranged around the fact that the person cannot get to them.

## Legal help
**Start with the Area Agency on Aging, because it covers most of what an older adult needs.** CAAC states that **for those individuals ages 60+, legal assistance is available with non-criminal matters such as wills, powers of attorney, advance directives, entitlement programs, housing, debt collection, exploitation, consumer issues, and defense of guardianship**. That list is close to a complete checklist for a family getting ahead of a parent's decline. Call **334-240-4680**.

**Legal Services Alabama, Montgomery office.** **2567 Fairlane Drive, Suite 200, Montgomery, AL 36116**, **local 334-832-4570**, **toll free 1-800-844-5342**, open **Monday to Thursday 8:30 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. and Friday 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.** It serves **Autauga, Bullock, Butler, Chambers, Chilton, Crenshaw, Elmore, Lee, Lowndes, Macon, Montgomery, Pike, Russell and Tallapoosa** counties, and the published income test is **at or below 125 percent of the federal poverty level**. The statewide intake line is **866-456-4995**, weekdays **8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.**

**Heirs property, which is a land problem as much as a legal one.** Legal Services Alabama states that **individuals affected by heirs property may qualify for free legal help**, and its heirs property work includes **estate and succession planning** and help documenting the **family tree**. Land that passes with no will becomes heirs' property, divided among more relatives each generation until any one of them can force a sale. A will drafted while a parent is still able to sign one prevents a problem that is very hard to fix afterward. Ask about heirs property by name at **866-456-4995**. We did not find a city-funded free will and probate program in Montgomery like the one some Alabama cities run, so the realistic free routes are CAAC for people 60 and older and Legal Services Alabama for households under its income limit.

**The volunteer lawyers.** The **Montgomery County Bar Volunteer Lawyers Program** is **a not-for-profit pro bono program supported by the Montgomery County Bar Foundation**, through which **area lawyers provide free legal assistance to low-income citizens of Montgomery County in certain civil matters**. The income test is **at or below 125 percent of the federal poverty level**. Call **334-265-0222, extension 2**, or write **P.O. Box 644, Montgomery, AL 36101**.

**When it is a safety question, not a legal one.** Suspected abuse, neglect or exploitation of an older or disabled adult goes to the **Alabama Adult Abuse Hotline at 1-800-458-7214**. Complaints about a nursing home, boarding home or assisted living facility go to the **Long-Term Care Ombudsman**, through CAAC, whose ombudsmen **advocate for residents of nursing homes, boarding homes, and assisted living facilities**, at **334-240-4680**, or the state ombudsman at **334-242-5753**. The state is explicit that **complainants do not have to give their name and all information is confidential**.

## Caregiver support
**Alabama Cares, through CAAC.** **Alabama Cares provides assistance to caregivers who are caring for elderly (60+) family members at home**, and its **services include respite, medical supplies, and education**. Ask for **respite** first, because it is the piece caregivers most need and ask for last. CAAC also runs a **homemaker** service, in which clients are **assisted with general household activities such as changing bed linens, cleaning and laundry**, with **donations encouraged**. Both start at **334-240-4680**.

**Dementia, at any hour.** The **Alzheimer's Association 24/7 Helpline at 1-800-272-3900** is free, staffed **around the clock, 365 days a year**, with interpreters in more than 200 languages. CAAC also lists **Dementia Friendly Alabama** among its projects, so ask about it when you call. If a loved one wanders, enroll in **Project Lifesaver** through the Sheriff at **334-832-1369**.

**For the caregiver who needs somewhere to go, or something to do.** The **Crump Senior Center** at **334-625-4547** offers exercise, games and a hot lunch for members for a small fee. Meals on Wheels Montgomery also runs **RSVP**, the **Retired and Senior Volunteer Program**, at **334-263-0532**, which gives an older adult who is still able a reason to get out of the house on a schedule.

## PACE
**There is no PACE program serving Montgomery County, and it is worth saying plainly so you stop looking.**

PACE, the **Program of All-Inclusive Care for the Elderly**, bundles **primary care including doctor and nursing services, hospital care, medical specialty services, prescription drugs, dentistry, nursing home care, personal care, physical therapy, adult day care, nutritional counseling, laboratory and x-ray services, social services, and transportation** into one program for people who need nursing-home-level care but want to stay at home. Alabama Medicaid lists four criteria, and an applicant must meet **each** of them: **meet the nursing home level of care**, be **able to live safely in the community at the time of enrollment**, be **55 years of age and older**, and **live in a designated PACE service area, currently Mobile and Baldwin counties**. Those are on the Gulf Coast. Nothing in the River Region qualifies.

**What to ask for instead.** The closest equivalent here is the **Medicaid Elderly and Disabled Waiver**, which assembles homemaker, personal care, respite, adult day, meals, home modifications and case management around a person at home. **CAAC takes the intake at 334-240-4680**; ask for the Intake Department. Then call the **Alabama Medicaid Montgomery District Office at 866-621-6509** about the eligibility paperwork. And ask CAAC the question that saves families weeks: what can start now, while the waiver application is pending.

## Frequently asked questions
**Dad is 79, has owned the same house near Carter Hill Road since the 1980s, and lives on Social Security and a small pension. What is he actually entitled to?** Possibly a zero property tax bill. Under Alabama's **H-3**, a taxpayer **65 and older with net taxable income of $12,000 or less on the combined federal return** is **exempt from all ad valorem taxes**. The deciding number is **taxable income on the return**, not gross income and not the Social Security deposit. Take the actual return to the **Downtown Revenue Office, 101 South Lawrence Street, 334-832-1250**, and ask which exemption code he is on now. If his income is over $12,000, **H-4** still exempts him from **all of the state portion** of the tax.

**Mom used to pay her taxes at the office on Atlanta Highway. Where does she go now?** That office closed. The **East Satellite Revenue Office at 5449 Atlanta Highway** closed at **noon on September 25, 2025**. The remaining offices are **Downtown, 101 South Lawrence Street, 334-832-1250**; **South, 3425 McGehee Road, 334-284-4533**; and **West, 3075 Mobile Highway, 334-262-4546**. Call before you drive, and while you have someone on the phone, ask whether her senior exemption needs to be renewed and when.

**We are being told Mom needs a nursing home, and we cannot afford it. Is there an alternative?** Ask about the **Medicaid Elderly and Disabled Waiver** through **CAAC at 334-240-4680**, Intake Department. It is for people whose needs are **similar to what would normally qualify you for a nursing home** but who want to stay at home, and it can cover **homemaker services, personal care, companion services, respite, home-delivered meals, adult day health, assistive technology, home modifications and emergency response systems**, with a case manager coordinating. She has to qualify for **full Medicaid**, so call the **Alabama Medicaid Montgomery District Office at 866-621-6509** as well. There is **no PACE program** in this county, so the waiver is the main alternative. If she is already in a facility and wants to come home, ask CAAC about the **ACT Waiver**.

**Mom's power bill is past due and she is 81 on a fixed income. Who actually helps?** Three calls. First, **Montgomery Community Action's LIHEAP call center at 334-230-5259**, or the main office at **334-263-3474**; its website is under construction, so call. Second, check the name on the bill. If it is **Alabama Power**, she is over 60, so ask about **Project SHARE** through the Salvation Army at **334-265-0281** or Alabama Power's listed line **205-328-2420**. If it is **Dixie Electric Cooperative**, call the co-op at **888-349-4332**. Third, ask **CARPDC** about **weatherization** at **334-262-4300**, since that is the one that lowers the bill for good, and she will need proof that she owns the home.

**My father lives out past Pike Road and stopped driving. How does he get to the doctor?** Probably not on the bus. **MAP**, the paratransit service, only runs **within a three quarter mile corridor of the fixed routes**, and much of the county sits outside that. Call **CAAC at 334-240-4680** and **2-1-1** and ask what transportation operates for his exact address and for medical trips specifically. For meals, he does not need to leave home: **Meals on Wheels Montgomery at 334-263-0532** and **CAAC's home delivered lunches** both serve homebound people 60 and older, subject to capacity and where he lives.

**My husband has dementia and I have not slept properly in months. What is in Montgomery County?** Four calls. **334-240-4680** for **Alabama Cares**, and ask directly for **respite**. **334-832-1369** to enroll him in the Sheriff's **Project Lifesaver** program before there is an incident. **1-800-272-3900**, the Alzheimer's Association's free 24/7 helpline, at whatever hour you are actually awake. And if his needs are at the level of a nursing home, ask CAAC about the **Medicaid Elderly and Disabled Waiver**, which can include respite and personal care at home.

**Mom is 74 and I think she qualifies for food stamps, but she will not sit through an interview.** Ask for the **Alabama Elderly Simplified Application Project** by name. The form is for households where **everyone in the SNAP household is age 65 or older** and **no SNAP household member receives earnings from work**, and it says you **may file this application by completing at least your name, address, and signing the form**. Take it to **Montgomery County DHR, 3030 Mobile Highway, 334-293-3100**, or call **1-800-438-2958** for help filling it out. While you are at it, ask the **Heart of Alabama Food Bank at 334-263-3784** about its **Commodity Supplemental Food Program** for low-income seniors 60 and older.

**My parents have family land in the county and there is no will. Is there anything free?** Yes, two routes. If either parent is **60 or older**, CAAC's legal assistance covers **wills, powers of attorney and advance directives** at **334-240-4680**. Separately, **Legal Services Alabama** states that **individuals affected by heirs property may qualify for free legal help**; call the Montgomery office at **334-832-4570** or statewide intake at **866-456-4995**. Land that passes with no will becomes heirs' property, split among more relatives each generation until any one of them can force a sale of the whole. A will signed now prevents a problem that is very hard to undo later.

**Is there anything for a Montgomery County veteran or a veteran's widow?** Yes. The **Montgomery/Bullock/Lowndes County Veterans Service Office** is at **Montgomery County Courthouse Annex II, 125 Washington Ave, Second Floor, Room 220**, phone **334-832-1392**. Call and ask what help it offers with filing a claim. The U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs also operates a **Montgomery Regional Benefit Office**, and the national **VA benefits hotline is 1-800-827-1000**, **Monday through Friday, 8:00 a.m. to 9:00 p.m. Eastern**. If a widow is living on very little and her husband served, ask by name about **survivor benefits and Aid and Attendance**.
